Vulnerable

/VUL-ner-uh-bul/ adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Vulnerable means open to being harmed, attacked, or damaged; exposed to real danger.

Show it in context

Vulnerable means open to being harmed, attacked, or damaged; exposed to real danger. Built on a low-lying floodplain, the entire riverside district remains vulnerable to flooding every spring when the snow melts upstream.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Newly hatched sea turtles are most vulnerable during their first crawl from the nest to the ocean, when gulls and crabs can pick them off in the open sand with nothing to shield them.

The hatchlings are exposed to real danger in the open with no protection at all, which is exactly what it means to be vulnerable.

Rootvulnerfrom Latin vulnus, a wound
Suffix-ableable to be

Vulnerable joins the Latin root vulnus, 'a wound,' to the suffix -able, 'able to be,' so a vulnerable target is literally one that 'can be wounded.' Outside this immediate word family, vulner- does not survive as a living root in common English words, so there is no root quiz for this word; instead, remember the wound at its heart, which is why vulnerable carries a more serious weight than everyday words for weakness.

Use the root in a new word: wound

A medieval knight who removed his armor could be wounded by any stray arrow, and vulnerable comes straight from Latin vulnus, 'a wound,' so a vulnerable target is literally one that can be wounded.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Vulnerable often takes 'to,' as in 'vulnerable to flooding,' and it is the serious choice for real exposure to harm, describing everything from a vulnerable population to a vulnerable position in battle. Vulnerability, its noun relative, is taught in this same unit and names the state of being vulnerable or the specific weak point itself.
