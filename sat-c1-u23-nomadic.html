Nomadic

/noh-MAD-ik/ adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Nomadic means wandering from place to place rather than settling permanently.

Show it in context

Nomadic means wandering from place to place rather than settling permanently. The young male wolf led a nomadic life for two years, crossing four mountain ranges without ever holding a territory.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Most songbirds of the boreal forest keep to the same few hectares all their lives. Crossbills do not. A flock ringed in Finland one autumn turned up 1,400 kilometres east the next year, then in Norway the year after, always where the spruce cones hung heaviest and never twice in the same wood. Because cone crops fail unpredictably, the birds can hold neither a fixed route nor a fixed home, and their nomadic habit is the only way they keep eating.

Birds that travel 1,400 kilometres to wherever the cones are heaviest, never return to the same wood, and hold no home range at all are wandering rather than settling, which is the heart of nomadic.

Rootnomad-a wandering herdsman (Greek nomas)
Suffix-ichaving the nature of

Greek nomas meant a herdsman who moved his animals on to fresh pasture, from nemein, 'to pasture.' English took the word in almost whole as nomad and added the adjective ending -ic. Unlike most roots in this unit, this one never spread through the language: apart from nomad and nomadism themselves there is no common English cousin built on it, so there is no honest root-check to offer here, and the word is best learned as a picture of the moving herd it names.

Use the root in a new word: nomadism

Historians use nomadism for the whole way of life a nomadic people follows, and it is very nearly the only common English relative this root ever produced.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral and descriptive

Nomadic describes a pattern of living rather than wildness or automatic behaviour, so keep it clear of feral (a domesticated animal now living wild) and instinctive (a single automatic behaviour) elsewhere in this unit. Note too that nomadic movement is unpredictable and homeless by definition, which is what separates it from a seasonal journey along a fixed route.
