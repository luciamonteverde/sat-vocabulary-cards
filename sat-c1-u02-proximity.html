Proximity

/prok-SIM-uh-tee/ — rhymes with anonymity · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Proximity means nearness or closeness in space, time, or relationship to something else.

Show it in context

She chose the apartment for its proximity to the hospital, close enough that she could walk to her night shifts in under ten minutes.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when proximity is the exact word.

Worked Example

The camera trap fired the instant the leopard's proximity to the watering hole broke the sensor's invisible beam, capturing the animal just a few steps from the water's edge.

Proximity names one thing specifically: the NEARNESS or CLOSENESS of one thing to another. The sensor did not care WHERE the leopard was in general — it cared only about how NEAR the animal had come to the water. That is why proximity is the right word and a looser word like LOCATION would miss the point: LOCATION only tells you the spot something occupies, not how CLOSE it sits to anything else. A leopard's location could be anywhere in the reserve, but its proximity is always measured RELATIVE to something — here, the watering hole. And DISTANCE is proximity's OPPOSITE: distance measures how FAR apart things are, while proximity measures how NEAR. Always ask, 'Near to WHAT?' — if the sentence answers that, proximity fits.

Your Turn

A family counselor observes that two sisters who live on opposite coasts still feel deeply connected: they speak every night, share every secret, and finish each other's sentences.

Which word best captures how CLOSE the two sisters feel to each other?

Choose the word that fits — then tap it to check.

proximity

✓ Correct — Correct. Proximity is not only spatial — it also describes closeness in a RELATIONSHIP. The sisters' nightly calls and shared secrets are exactly this kind of emotional nearness, even though they live far apart physically.

similarity

✗ Not quite — Incorrect. Similarity means being ALIKE, not being CLOSE. Two people can be extremely similar and still feel distant from each other; the scenario is about their closeness, which is proximity.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
nearness

The plainest synonym — the simple fact of being near. It carries no formality, whereas 'proximity' sounds slightly more technical or measured.

closeness

Stresses a small gap between two things and, like 'proximity,' extends comfortably to relationships and emotions ('closeness between friends'), not just physical space.

vicinity

Points to the general area NEAR something ('in the vicinity of the station'). It names the surrounding nearby zone rather than the direct one-to-one nearness that 'proximity' measures.

Antonyms · opposites

distance — Distance is the direct opposite: it measures how FAR apart two things are, while proximity measures how NEAR. As distance grows, proximity shrinks.

How Strong Is It?
remote→distant→proximate→adjacent

This spectrum tracks how CLOSE one thing is to another. 'Remote' is far off and hard to reach; 'distant' is clearly separated but less extreme; 'proximate' — the proximity range — means genuinely near, within easy reach of something; and 'adjacent' is the strongest, sitting right next to something with essentially no gap between them.

Build the Word

prox- / proxim- From Latin proximus ('nearest, next'), the superlative of prope ('near').

The proxim- root always signals NEARNESS. In 'proximity' it names the state of being near, and the same root powers related words like 'proximate' ('the nearest or most immediate') and 'approximate' ('to come near a value').

Without the final invoices, the accountant could only approximate the project's total cost, landing close to the real figure but not exactly on it.

'Approximate' shares a root with 'proximity.' How does it break down?

ad- ('to, toward') + proxim- ('nearest') → to bring a value TOWARD nearness, i.e., to come close to it

✓ Correct — Correct. The ad- assimilates to ap- before the p, and proxim- carries the 'near' meaning it shares with proximity — to approximate is to come NEAR the true figure.

a- ('without') + proxim- ('proof') → lacking solid proof

✗ Not quite — Incorrect. There is no 'proof' root here; proxim- means 'nearest,' from Latin proximus, and the prefix is ad- ('toward'), not a negative.

apex ('highest point') + -imate → reaching the top

✗ Not quite — Incorrect. 'Approximate' has nothing to do with 'apex.' Its root is proximus, 'nearest' — the same nearness found in proximity.

The root connection →

Both 'proximity' and 'approximate' grow from Latin proximus, 'nearest' (itself the superlative of prope, 'near'). Proximity is the STATE of being near something; to approximate is to bring a value NEAR the true one. The shared proxim- is the idea of nearness sitting at the heart of both words.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Neutral to slightly formal.

Proximity In everyday speech people usually say 'close to' or 'near,' but 'proximity' is the standard choice in reports, science writing, real-estate listings, and law (as in 'proximate cause'). It sounds precise and measured rather than casual.
