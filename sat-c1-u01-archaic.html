Archaic

/ahr-KAY-ik/ — rhymes with mosaic · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Archaic means belonging to an earlier period of time and no longer in current use.

Show it in context

Much of his vocabulary felt archaic, so tied to his own century that Priya sometimes had to guess at his meaning.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when archaic is the exact word.

Worked Example

In her grandmother's attic, Maya found archaic letters written in a spidery hand, words she'd never seen before because the language itself had changed so much.

Archaic means something is not just old, but specifically OLD AND NO LONGER IN USE. It's more precise than "ancient" (which emphasizes age but not disuse) or "old" (which says nothing about whether the thing is still used). These letters belong to a vanished era of language — that's exactly what archaic captures.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
ancient

Ancient means very, very old (usually thousands of years). Archaic is old AND out of use, but not necessarily ancient — archaic things can be only 100 years old.

obsolete

Obsolete means no longer useful or needed, often replaced by something newer. Archaic is old-fashioned with historical weight; obsolete things are just discarded.

outdated

Outdated means no longer current or fashionable. Archaic has a sense of belonging to a vanished era; outdated just means not trendy anymore.

Antonyms · opposites

modern — Modern means current and new. Archaic is the opposite: from the distant past and no longer in use.

Build the Word

arch- Greek: 'beginning, origin, chief'

The arch- root originally meant the 'beginning' or 'first origin' of something — archaic things are 'original' to an earlier era, making them old and leftover from that time.

The original supermarket layout became the archetype for every supermarket built after it.

Using the same root and the sentence above, what does archetype mean here?

the original form or first model of something

✓ Correct — arch- means 'original, beginning, chief.' Archaic describes something from the beginning of an era (thus old and unused); an archetype is the original model that others copy — same root, same idea of something primary.

a type of building that has been around for centuries

✗ Not quite — that reading misses the root. Arch- means 'original, beginning, chief,' so archetype must mean the first, original model that others are based on, not something old or discarded.

a design that is no longer used in modern buildings

✗ Not quite — that reading misses the root. Arch- means 'original, beginning, chief,' so archetype must mean the first, original model that others are based on, not something old or discarded.

The root connection →

Archaic and archetype share the root arch- ('original, beginning'). In archaic, that root gives us something from the beginning of a past era (thus out of use); in archetype, it gives us the first, original model others are built from.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Archaic is a scholarly word for things that belong to a vanished past. In everyday speech, say 'old-fashioned' or 'outdated.' Reach for archaic when describing vocabulary, customs, or styles that clearly belong to a historical era.
