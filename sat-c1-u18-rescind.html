Rescind

/rih-SIND/ verb

Hear It
What It Means

Rescind means to officially cancel a law, order, or agreement, usually through a deliberate, formal act by a governing body.

Show it in context

Rescind means to officially cancel a law, order, or agreement, usually through a deliberate, formal act by a governing body. Facing public outcry, the school board voted to rescind its new dress code just two weeks after adopting it.

Worked Example

In March, the village council approved an ordinance charging residents for overnight street parking. Complaints flooded in from nurses and factory workers who came home long after every garage had closed. At its next session, the council held a formal vote to rescind the ordinance, striking it from the books entirely. The fee disappeared, and the parking rules returned to exactly what they had been before March.

A governing body used a formal vote to cancel an order it had previously issued, which is exactly what rescind means.

Prefixre-back
Rootscindfrom Latin scindere, to cut or split

Rescind comes from Latin rescindere, 'to cut back' or 'cut away,' from re- (back) plus scindere (to cut, to split). To rescind an order is literally to cut it out of the law.

Use the root in a new word: rescission

The contract's rescission clause spelled out how either company could cut the deal apart, echoing the Latin root scindere, 'to cut.'

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal, legal/parliamentary register

Rescind is the standard verb for a council, board, or agency formally canceling its own ordinance, order, or offer (rescind an ordinance, rescind a job offer). Rescind is close to revoke, which specifically cancels a right, privilege, or official document, often as a punishment, and to invalidate, which makes something no longer legally binding, sometimes because of a flaw rather than a deliberate act; never treat the three as interchangeable.
