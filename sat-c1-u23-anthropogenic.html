Anthropogenic

/an-thruh-puh-JEN-ik/ adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Anthropogenic means caused or produced by human activity.

Show it in context

Anthropogenic means caused or produced by human activity. The sediment core showed a sudden anthropogenic lead spike beginning in the very year the smelter opened upriver.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Glaciologists drilling an ice cap read each annual layer like a page in a book. Layers older than 1750 contain sulfate only in the years of known volcanic eruptions. From 1850 onward, sulfate appears in every single layer, rising fastest through the decades when European coal use rose fastest and falling again after emissions laws passed in the 1970s. The team labelled that entire stretch of the record anthropogenic.

The sulfate rises in step with coal use and declines when emissions laws take effect, tying the change to human industry rather than to natural eruptions, which is exactly what anthropogenic means.

Rootanthropo-human being (Greek anthropos)
Root-gen-produced, brought forth (Greek -genes)
Suffix-icof the nature of

Greek anthropos, 'human being,' opens a long list of English words about people: anthropology studies them, philanthropy loves them, and a misanthrope avoids them. The second half, -genes, means 'produced' or 'brought forth,' the same element behind genesis and generate. Put them together and anthropogenic says plainly that whatever it describes was brought forth by humans.

Use the root in a new word: misanthrope

A misanthrope distrusts people in general, and the anthropos ('human being') at the heart of the word is the same root that makes anthropogenic mean 'human-caused.'

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

scientific and technical; neutral in tone

Anthropogenic is an adjective of cause, and it almost always modifies a noun naming an effect: anthropogenic warming, anthropogenic noise, anthropogenic runoff. It says nothing about what the change is, only who caused it, which is why it often sits beside acidification in this unit but never means the same thing.
