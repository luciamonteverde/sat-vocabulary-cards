Imitation

/im-ih-TAY-shuhn/ noun

Hear It
What It Means

Imitation means a copy or fake version of something, made to look like the real or original thing

Show it in context

The bracelet's stones turned out to be high-quality glass imitations, not the real emeralds the seller had claimed.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

When the museum's insurers demanded tighter security, curators quietly swapped the original 400-year-old necklace for a museum-grade imitation crafted from cubic zirconia, storing the genuine piece in an undisclosed vault while visitors admired the convincing fake on display.

The piece on display looks identical to the original but isn't — it's a deliberately crafted substitute, which is exactly what "imitation" names.

Just as "imitate" means "to copy," "imitation" names the result of that act — the copy itself, produced by the same imit- root taught earlier in this unit.

Use the root in a new word: imitator

The talent show's most popular act was an imitator who could reproduce six different singers' voices in under two minutes.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral to slightly negative

Imitation is often neutral (an 'imitation leather' couch is simply described honestly), but it can also carry a mildly negative edge when used to point out that something isn't genuine, as in 'a cheap imitation of the real thing.'
