Lengthy

/LENG-thee/ — rhymes with 'stealthy' · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Lengthy means very long in duration or extent, often longer than needed.

Show it in context

The lengthy debate stretched deep into the evening, and reporters in the gallery began to nod off in their seats.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when lengthy is the exact word.

Worked Example

The lecture was *lengthy*, stretching past everyone's patience with slides that repeated the same point over and over.

Lengthy is more precise than the plain word 'long' because it carries the sense that something is longer than necessary or wanted. The context — a lecture that goes on too much — captures that extra quality. 'Long' is neutral; 'lengthy' suggests unwanted or excessive duration.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
long

Simply describes a large span of time or distance, without judgment about whether it is excessive.

extended

Stretched out over a long period, but more neutral than lengthy — can be necessary or good.

prolonged

Stretched beyond the normal or desired endpoint, closer to lengthy's sense of going on too long.

Antonyms · opposites

Build the Word

length- Old English: 'to stretch or extend'

Lengthy comes from 'length,' which traces back to Old English 'lengthan' — meaning 'to stretch or make long.' So lengthy literally describes something that has been stretched out or extended in a way that feels like too much.

The root connection →

Lengthy and lengthen share the root length- ('to extend'). In lengthy, that root gives us the description of something stretched out and long; in lengthen, it gives us the action of making something longer.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Lengthy implies not just 'long' but 'longer than necessary or wanted.' Use lengthy when the duration is excessive or tedious, like a lengthy discussion or lengthy wait. For something that is simply long (like a lengthy novel), the word works, but 'long' alone is often enough unless you want to emphasize that the length is excessive.
