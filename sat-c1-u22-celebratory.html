Celebratory

/SEL-uh-bruh-tor-ee/ adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Celebratory means relating to or marking a celebration; festive in purpose.

Show it in context

Celebratory means relating to or marking a celebration; festive in purpose. The whole block turned out for a celebratory cookout the weekend the new park finally opened.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

The robotics team had eaten in the same quiet corner of the cafeteria all year. But the Monday after their machine finally cleared the qualifying round, their table looked different: someone had brought a grocery-store cake, someone else had tied a single balloon to a chair, and the coach gave a toast with a juice box. It was a small lunch, but an unmistakably celebratory one — every detail existed to mark the win.

The cake, balloon, and toast all exist specifically to mark the team's win, which is what makes the lunch celebratory rather than just cheerful.

Rootcelebrhonored by a great gathering; thronged (Latin celebrare)
Suffix-atoryrelating to; serving to

Celebratory builds on Latin celebrare, 'to honor with a great gathering' — originally from celeber, 'crowded, much-visited.' A Roman celebration was literally a thronged event. The suffix -atory makes it an adjective: anything — a dinner, a banner, a handshake offered at the right moment — whose purpose is to mark such an honoring.

Use the root in a new word: celebrate

We celebrate a victory by gathering to honor it — the base verb from Latin celebrare that celebratory simply turns into an adjective.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral; describes events, gestures, and atmospheres

Celebratory always points at a specific occasion being marked — a win, a launch, a milestone — while festive (also in this unit) can describe a merry, holiday-like atmosphere even when nothing particular is being commemorated.
