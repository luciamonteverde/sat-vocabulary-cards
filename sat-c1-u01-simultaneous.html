Simultaneous

/sim-ul-TAY-nee-us/ — stress on TAY · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Simultaneous means happening at exactly the same time as something else.

Show it in context

These simultaneous recordings, made from widely separated points on Earth, allowed researchers to measure the flare from two angles at once.

Here's how to decide when simultaneous is the exact word.

Worked Example

Two cameras positioned miles apart captured the solar flare simultaneously, recording the exact moment from different angles at the same time.

Simultaneous is more precise than simply saying 'at the same time' because it emphasizes that the timing is absolutely exact and coordinated — the flare happens as one unified moment rather than separate events that merely occur close together.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs

Happening at the same time but may feel more like overlapping periods; simultaneous stresses the exact moment.

synchronous

Coordinated to happen at the same time; often used for timed systems rather than natural events.

coincident

Occurring at the same time by chance or accident; simultaneous can be planned or intentional.

Build the Word

simul Latin: 'at the same time'

Simul is Latin for 'at the same time' — the same root that gives us 'similar' (at the same level) and 'simulate' (copy in the same way). Simultaneous combines simul + taneous, meaning 'happening-at-the-same-time-ly.'

The root connection →

Simultaneous and assimilate both share the root sim- ('same, together'). In simultaneous, that root gives us events happening at the same moment; in assimilate, it gives us the idea of becoming the same as or joining a group.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / technical

Simultaneous is the precise word when two or more events happen at exactly the same moment in perfect time coordination. For casual speech about things happening around the same time (but not necessarily exact), 'at the same time' or 'concurrent' often works better.
