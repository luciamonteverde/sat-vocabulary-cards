Simultaneous

/sim-ul-TAY-nee-us/ — stress on TAY adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Simultaneous means happening at exactly the same time as something else.

Show it in context

These simultaneous recordings, made from widely separated points on Earth, allowed researchers to measure the flare from two angles at once.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Two cameras positioned miles apart captured the solar flare simultaneously, recording the exact moment from different angles at the same time.

Simultaneous is more precise than simply saying 'at the same time' because it emphasizes that the timing is absolutely exact and coordinated — the flare happens as one unified moment rather than separate events that merely occur close together.

Rootsimulat the same time (Latin)

The Latin root simul means 'at the same time,' and it sits at the heart of simultaneous. When two events are simultaneous, they share one exact moment, as if time itself has pinned them together. The same idea of sameness echoes in relatives like similar and simulate, where things match or mirror one another — simultaneous simply applies that sameness to the clock.

Use the root in a new word: assimilate

When new students move to a school, it takes time for them to assimilate into the community and feel they truly belong.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / technical

Simultaneous is the precise word when two or more events happen at exactly the same moment in perfect time coordination. For casual speech about things happening around the same time (but not necessarily exact), 'at the same time' or 'concurrent' often works better.
