Stratification

/strat-uh-fih-KAY-shun/ · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Stratification means the arrangement of people or things into distinct, ranked layers or levels.

Show it in context

Beneath the polite dinner conversation lay a rigid stratification, in which the seating itself announced who stood above whom.

Here's how to decide when stratification is the exact word.

Worked Example

On the naval vessel, the crew was divided into sharply ranked layers, with senior officers berthed on the upper decks, petty officers just below them, and enlisted sailors packed into the cramped lower quarters near the hull.

Stratification means arrangement into DISTINCT, RANKED LAYERS stacked one above another by status or level. It is not just any grouping or sorting (you can separate people into equal categories that have no top and no bottom); stratification specifically requires LAYERS THAT SIT ABOVE AND BELOW ONE ANOTHER in a fixed order of rank. The crew split across higher and lower decks strictly by rank is the definition of stratification.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
hierarchy

A hierarchy is a system of ranked levels of authority; stratification emphasizes the LAYERED separation of a whole population into those levels, not just a top-down chain of command.

ranking

Ranking orders individual items along a single scale from top to bottom; stratification gathers them into distinct LAYERS or bands, each holding many members at a similar level.

layering

Layering captures the stacked, level-upon-level structure of stratification, but it can be purely physical or neutral, whereas stratification implies those layers carry differing RANK or status.

Antonyms · opposites

egalitarianism — Egalitarianism is the principle that all people are equal and belong to no ranked layer; it directly opposes stratification's division of a population into higher and lower levels.

How Strong Is It?
mixed→grouped→stratified→caste-bound

The list moves from no order at all (mixed), to separated-but-equal categories (grouped), to distinct ranked layers (stratified), up to caste-bound — where the layers are so rigid that no one can ever move from one level to another.

Build the Word

strat- From Latin 'stratum,' meaning 'a thing spread out, a layer,' from the past participle of the verb 'sternere,' 'to spread out or lay down' (from the ancient root meaning 'to spread').

The idea of layers LAID DOWN one atop another sits at the heart of stratification: a society (or a rock face, or anything else) is pictured as sheets stacked in levels, each spread over the one beneath it.

The weather balloon climbed higher and higher until it reached the stratosphere, the calm, layered band of atmosphere that sits far above the clouds.

In 'stratosphere,' what does the root 'strat-' tell you about that region of the atmosphere?

It is a LAYER of the atmosphere.

✓ Correct — Correct — 'strat-' means layer, and the stratosphere is one of the distinct layers of air stacked around the Earth.

It is the strongest part of the atmosphere.

✗ Not quite — No — 'strat-' means layer, not strength. It points to the layered structure of that band of air, not its force.

It is a round or spherical shape.

✗ Not quite — That meaning belongs to the '-sphere' part of the word; 'strat-' specifically means layer.

The root connection →

Both words are built on the Latin 'stratum' (a layer laid down, from 'sternere,' to spread out). In stratification, a society is imagined as layers of people stacked by rank; in the stratosphere, the sky is divided into layers of air stacked by altitude. Wherever you see 'strat-,' picture things arranged in LAYERS, one spread over another.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Formal / academic

Stratification Stratification is the precise term for arrangement into ranked layers, especially in sociology, geology, and ecology. In everyday speech, say 'the class system,' 'levels,' or 'pecking order,' but stratification is the exact word when you want to emphasize a whole system divided into distinct, ranked layers.
