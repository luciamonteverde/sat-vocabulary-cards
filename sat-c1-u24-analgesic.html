Analgesic

/an-ul-JEE-zik/ noun

Hear It
What It Means

Analgesic means a substance that relieves pain.

Show it in context

Analgesic means a substance that relieves pain. After the dental surgery, the oral surgeon recommended an over-the-counter analgesic to manage the soreness.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

By mile twenty, Priya's knees throbbed with every stride. At the finish-line medical tent, a volunteer offered her ice and a mild analgesic. Within an hour the sharp ache had faded to a dull background hum. Nothing about the pill fixed her knees — it simply quieted the pain they were sending.

The pill relieves Priya's pain without curing or treating anything else — doing exactly and only what an analgesic does.

Prefixan-without (Greek)
Rootalges-pain (Greek algos)
Suffix-icrelating to; a substance of this kind

Analgesic assembles Greek an-, 'without,' with algos, 'pain': literally 'without pain.' The same pain root hides in nostalgia (the ache of longing) and neuralgia (nerve pain). Don't confuse it with look-alikes such as analogy or allergy, which are built on different Greek roots.

Use the root in a new word: nostalgia

Nostalgia is literally the 'ache' of longing for the past — its -algia comes from the same Greek root algos, 'pain,' found in analgesic.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

medical, pharmaceutical

You'll meet analgesic on medicine labels and in doctors' offices. Keep it apart from antiseptic (another word in this unit): an analgesic relieves pain, while antiseptic describes something that prevents infection by keeping germs away — a well-stocked first-aid kit needs both, because neither does the other's job.
