Cohesive

/koh-HEE-siv/ adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Cohesive means sticking together as one well-connected whole; unified in a way that already holds a group or work together.

Show it in context

Cohesive means sticking together as one well-connected whole. The debate team was so cohesive that each speaker built seamlessly on the argument the one before had started.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Critics praised the album as remarkably cohesive, with every song sharing melodies and themes so that the whole record felt like one continuous piece.

The songs were written as separate tracks, but they hold together as a single artistic whole. Cohesive describes that quality of parts sticking together as one unit.

Cohesive literally means 'tending to stick together' — the members of a cohesive group cling to one another like well-glued parts of a single whole.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Neutral — academic

Cohesive describes the state of already holding together as one unit — a cohesive team, a cohesive argument, a cohesive neighborhood. Contrast with two process words taught separately in this unit: amalgamation and consolidation both name the process of combining separate things into one, while cohesive describes the quality a group or work has once it genuinely sticks together. A group can be merged by some process and still fail to be cohesive.
