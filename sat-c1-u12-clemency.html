Clemency

/KLEM-uhn-see/ noun

Hear It
What It Means

Clemency means mercy shown by someone in power toward a person who has earned a punishment, softening what they deserve

Show it in context

Clemency means mercy shown by someone in power toward a person who has earned a punishment, softening what they deserve. The judge granted clemency to the first-time offender who had repaid every stolen dollar before sentencing, reducing the term to probation.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

The school board showed clemency to the senior who reported his own grading-system prank, cutting the recommended expulsion to a two-week suspension.

The student really was guilty and really could have been expelled. The board's decision to soften the punishment out of mercy — while still acknowledging the wrong — is what clemency means.

Rootclemen-mild, gentle, merciful (Latin clemens)
Suffix-cystate or quality of

Latin clemens meant 'mild, gentle,' and -cy names a state or quality — so clemency is literally 'the quality of mildness': gentleness chosen by someone who had the power to be harsh.

Use the root in a new word: clement

The sailors hoped for clement weather — mild skies from the very root that gives clemency its gentleness toward wrongdoers.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Formal

Clemency flows downward from power — a governor, judge, board, or boss softens a punishment that someone genuinely deserves. Keep it distinct from its neighbors in this unit: a reprieve merely delays a punishment without reducing it, and vindication proves the person was never to blame at all. Clemency alone means mercy despite real guilt.
