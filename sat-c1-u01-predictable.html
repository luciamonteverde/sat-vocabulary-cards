Predictable

/pre-DIK-tuh-bul/ — emphasis on second syllable · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Predictable means able to be expected in advance because it follows a set pattern.

Show it in context

The movie was so predictable that the audience seemed to relax rather than worry, confident that no real surprise awaited them.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when predictable is the exact word.

Worked Example

The bully always waited by the flagpole at 3:15 p.m. to harass the younger kids—so predictable that the teachers stationed an aide there to stop him.

Predictable is more precise than a plain word like 'expected' or 'usual' because it emphasizes that we can foresee something BECAUSE it follows a set pattern.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
expected

Believed or anticipated to happen; predictable emphasizes the pattern that makes it foreseeable.

foreseeable

Able to be foreseen in advance; predictable emphasizes the pattern and regularity more.

regular

Following a repeated or consistent pattern; predictable emphasizes that the pattern allows prediction.

Antonyms · opposites

surprising — Unexpected, breaks the pattern; opposite of predictable.

Build the Word

dicere Latin: 'to say, speak'

The root dict- means 'to speak, say.' In predictable, you can 'speak beforehand'—foretell or foresee what will happen. In contradict, you 'speak against' something—disagree with it.

The root connection →

Predictable and contradict share the root dict- ('to speak, say'). In predictable, the root gives us something you can 'speak about beforehand'—foretell or predict. In contradict, it gives us 'speaking against' something—disagreeing with it.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral

Predictable describes something that's easy to foresee because it follows a clear pattern. Use it when you want to emphasize that a pattern makes something foreseeable. For simpler 'we saw it coming,' the plain word 'expected' works fine.
