Perpetual

/per-PECH-oo-ul/ — stress on PECH · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Perpetual means continuing endlessly, or for a very long time, without stopping.

Show it in context

Fishermen who pass nearby describe the roar as a perpetual growl, a sound that has echoed across those waters for as long as anyone can remember.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when perpetual is the exact word.

Worked Example

The lighthouse beam swept across the dark ocean, a perpetual light that had shone through countless storms and calm nights for over a hundred years.

Perpetual is more precise than just saying 'long-lasting' or 'enduring' because it emphasizes the idea of going on without end — a timeless, endless quality that suggests the light will continue forever, not just for a very long time.

Build the Word

per Latin: 'throughout' or 'through'

Per- is a Latin prefix meaning 'throughout' or 'through.' In perpetual, this root gives us the sense of something that goes throughout time or continues through all time without stopping. The same prefix appears in 'pervasive' (spreading through everything) and 'permanent' (lasting throughout or enduring).

The pervasive smell of smoke filled every room in the house, spreading throughout every corner.

Using the same root and the sentence above, what does pervasive mean here?

spreading throughout or present everywhere

✓ Correct — Correct — per- means 'throughout' or 'through.' Perpetual means lasting throughout time without end; pervasive means spreading/existing throughout a place — both use per- to express the idea of something extending through space or time.

rare or hard to find

✗ Not quite — Not quite — that reading misses the root. Per- means 'throughout' or 'through,' so pervasive can't mean that; it means spreading throughout or present everywhere.

pleasant and agreeable

✗ Not quite — Not quite — that reading misses the root. Per- means 'throughout' or 'through,' so pervasive can't mean that; it means spreading throughout or present everywhere.

The root connection →

Perpetual and pervasive both share the root per- ('through, throughout'). In perpetual, that root gives us something that continues throughout time without end; in pervasive, it gives us something that spreads throughout a space or is present everywhere.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / literary

Perpetual is the word to use when you want to emphasize endless duration or the sense of something continuing without end. For something that lasts a very long time but will eventually stop, 'lasting' or 'enduring' fits better. Perpetual is grander and suggests something timeless.
