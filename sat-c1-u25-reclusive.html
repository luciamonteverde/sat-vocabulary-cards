Reclusive

/rih-KLOO-siv/ adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Reclusive means living in deliberate solitude, avoiding the company of others.

Show it in context

Reclusive means living in deliberate solitude, avoiding the company of others. The reclusive game designer skipped his own launch party, streaming a brief thank-you from his cabin instead.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Fans of the puzzle column knew their setter only as 'Quill.' Quill declined every interview, sent a lawyer to accept awards, and had prize checks mailed to a forwarding service. When a journalist finally traced the byline to a farmhouse at the end of a private road, the neighbors could only report that the owner bought feed in the next county and waved from a distance, maybe once a year. Quill was not hiding anything, they insisted — just deeply, contentedly reclusive.

Quill refuses interviews, ceremonies, and even neighborly contact by choice — with no wrongdoing involved — which is the deliberate solitude that reclusive names.

Build the Word · tap each piece to reveal its meaning
Prefixre-back; away
Rootclusto shut (Latin claudere, clausus)
Suffix-ivetending to; having the nature of

Reclusive assembles cleanly: re- ('back, away') + clus ('to shut' — the root of exclude, include, and seclusion) + -ive ('inclined to'). A recluse is one who has 'shut themselves away,' and reclusive means 'characterized by shutting oneself away.' The closing root clus is one of Latin's most productive in English, so this word practically decodes itself once you spot the door swinging shut inside it.

Use the root in a new word: seclusion

The cabin's seclusion — se- (apart) + claudere (to shut) — is the shut-away state a reclusive person chooses, built on the same closing root clus.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral; descriptive rather than judgmental

Reclusive involves no secrecy or scheming at all — it simply means avoiding company. Contrast this unit's connive (secretly plotting with others) and furtive (acting in a sneaky, guilty-seeming manner): a reclusive person may be perfectly open about wanting to be left alone.
