Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Embryonic means in an early and undeveloped stage.

See the word in context

The peace talks remain embryonic — after a month, the two delegations have agreed only on the shape of the table.

In 1990 the country's democracy was embryonic: one independent newspaper run from a basement, a parliament that met in a gymnasium, a constitution that was still nine handwritten pages in a law professor's satchel. Foreign observers counted institutions and found almost nothing — which was the point. Almost nothing is what the earliest stage of anything looks like.

Embryonic is the exact word because it names what is in an early and undeveloped stage — formed enough to exist, too unformed to work at full size. It leans on biology's picture of the embryo: the design present, the parts barely sketched. Call a thing embryonic and you promise growth while admitting how little there is to show today.

Compare the words

Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

undeveloped: Undeveloped is the flat, factual word for what has not yet grown — no image behind it, no promise attached. Embryonic borrows the embryo's picture: the design already present, the parts barely sketched, growth clearly on the way. Call a district undeveloped and you describe it; call a plan embryonic and you bet on its future.

fledgling: A fledgling has just left the nest — young and wobbly, but already out in the air. A fledgling venture is likewise up and working while it finds its wings. Embryonic is earlier still: formed enough to exist, too unformed to do anything yet.

What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

full-grown: development complete

fully-formed: every part already in its final shape

Where it sits · how close to its finished form

embryonic → budding → full-blown

budding vs. embryonic: Budding goes past embryonic — the shape has broken the surface and anyone can watch it grow.

full-blown vs. embryonic: Full-blown is far stronger than embryonic: an embryonic plan is still a faint early sketch, while a full-blown one has gone all the way to its complete, finished form.
