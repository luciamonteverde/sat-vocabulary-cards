What It Means

Archaic means belonging to an earlier period of time and no longer in everyday use.

See the word in context

Much of his vocabulary felt archaic, so tied to his own century that Priya sometimes had to guess at his meaning.

In her grandmother's attic, Maya found archaic letters written in a spidery hand, words she'd never seen before because the language itself had changed so much.

Archaic means something is not just old, but old and no longer in everyday use — more precise than "ancient," which speaks to age alone. A thing can survive in one narrow corner, like a legal contract or an old hymn, and still be archaic: what matters is that it has left ordinary use. And archaic mostly describes language, customs and styles rather than objects still in service — a farmhouse people still live in is old, not archaic. These letters belong to a vanished era of language, and that is exactly what archaic captures.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    ancient: Ancient means extremely old — often thousands of years old — and speaks only to age. Archaic is about use, not age alone: an archaic word or custom belongs to an earlier era and has dropped out of current practice. A mountain is ancient, but it cannot be archaic, because it was never something people used.

    obsolete: Obsolete means discarded because something newer replaced it, like floppy disks. Archaic points to an earlier era of history rather than to replacement by an upgrade: archaic laws or spellings belong to a vanished period. A gadget from last decade can be obsolete without being archaic — nothing about it belongs to a distant age.

    antiquated: Antiquated is a disapproving word for what is old-fashioned and no longer works well, like an antiquated filing system that slows an office down. Archaic is more neutral and scholarly: it places a thing in a vanished historical era without complaining about it. Scholars prize archaic manuscripts; no one prizes an antiquated system.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    modern: new and belonging to the present day

    current: still in active, everyday use right now

    Where it sits · how far out of use it is

    outdated → old-fashioned → archaic

    outdated vs. archaic: An outdated bus schedule is much weaker than archaic — it is only a little behind the times, and one quick reprint makes it useful again.

    old-fashioned vs. archaic: An old-fashioned handwritten thank-you note is weaker than archaic — it feels quaint and charming, but people still happily write and receive them today.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / literary

      Archaic is a scholarly word for things that belong to a vanished past. In everyday speech, say 'old-fashioned' or 'outdated.' Use archaic mainly for vocabulary, customs, or styles that clearly belong to a historical era — not for an object, however old, that is still in service.
