Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Incremental means increasing or developing little by little, in small, regular steps.

See the word in context

The company's price increases were incremental, so small each month that customers barely noticed them add up.

The engineer improved the software in incremental steps, fixing one small bug per week, and after fifty weeks, the performance had nearly doubled.

Incremental means not just small changes, but small regular steps that add up over time. The engineer's weekly bug fixes are a perfect example: each fix is tiny, but the regularity and accumulation over fifty weeks created a major improvement. That precision — 'small, repeated steps that compound' — is exactly what incremental names.

Accuracy

“Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

Compare the words

Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

piecemeal: Piecemeal means done a piece at a time, with no master plan — repairs made piecemeal happen whenever something breaks. Incremental also moves in small steps, but the steps are regular and add up by design. A piecemeal renovation is patchwork; an incremental one follows a schedule of small, planned stages.

progressive: Progressive means moving forward, with each stage carrying the process further, but it says nothing about step size. Incremental insists the advance comes in small, measured steps. A progressive rollout might leap from ten users to ten thousand; an incremental one adds a hundred at a time.

continuous: Continuous means unbroken and flowing, with no pauses or seams at all. Incremental is the opposite texture: progress arrives in distinct little steps you could count. A dimmer that slides smoothly is continuous; one that clicks through ten brightness settings is incremental.

What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

sudden: happening all at once, without any warning

drastic: extreme, sweeping change in one stroke

Where it sits · how regular the progress is

sporadic → gradual → incremental

sporadic vs. incremental: A gardener who waters her seedlings sporadically — a splash whenever she happens to remember — watches them struggle, because sporadic effort lacks the regular, accumulating rhythm that makes improvement incremental.

gradual vs. incremental: Saying a glacier melted gradually tells us only that the change was slow; calling a reading habit incremental tells us more — progress arrives in small, countable steps, like one extra page added every night.
