Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Scurrilous means vulgar and insultingly abusive.

See the word in context

The morning show apologized after airing a scurrilous song about the retiring senator's family.

When the star striker asked for a transfer, one supporters' podcast spent forty minutes on his girlfriend, his mother's accent, and a chant too crude to print — nothing about football at all. The club cut every tie with the show by morning and called the segment what it was: scurrilous, abuse with nothing underneath it but filth.

Scurrilous is the exact word because it grades the attack's register, not its accuracy: coarse, vulgar, gleefully abusive. A critical piece argues; a defamatory one lies about facts. A scurrilous one goes for the gutter, and whether any of it is true is almost beside the point — the word smells of the tavern wall it was scrawled on.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    vulgar: Vulgar means coarse in any setting — the joke, the gesture, the word your grandmother forbade — and it needs no target at all. Scurrilous is coarseness weaponized at someone: the same filth aimed, personally and gleefully, at a name and a face. A limerick can be vulgar all by itself; it takes a victim to make it scurrilous.

    abusive: Abusive language attacks a person harshly — it wounds on purpose and keeps wounding. Scurrilous abuse adds the gutter's glee and filth: obscene, delighted with itself, scrawled at tavern-wall level. A tirade can be abusive in perfectly clean language; scurrilous means the attack has gone down into the dirt.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    tasteful: keeps within the lines of decency

    dignified: carries itself with self-respect

    Where it sits · how squarely the coarseness targets someone

    rude → crude → scurrilous

    rude vs. scurrilous: A rude remark forgets its manners — and can still be clean as a whistle; scurrilous goes far past rude, coarse to the bone and aimed, gleefully, at somebody in particular.

    crude vs. scurrilous: Crude talk is coarse without necessarily aiming at anyone; scurrilous goes further — coarseness sharpened into a personal attack and enjoying itself.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / disapproving

    Scurrilous marks the gutter register: vulgar, abusive, delighted with itself. It judges the style where its unit-mate defamatory judges the claims — a scurrilous rhyme may contain no checkable claim at all, while a defamatory paragraph can be primly polite. Tabloid feuds are scurrilous; sworn false statements are defamatory; some masterpieces of malice manage both.

    Elections in the port city of 1768 were fought less with speeches than with print. When the merchant Elias Voss stood for the assembly, his rivals hired the town's worst pen. The result was a scurrilous broadside: verses about Voss and his wife so obscene the printer refused to sign them, gleeful in their filth and pasted to every tavern door by morning. What made it more than ordinary coarseness was the aim. The dirt was not general; every filthy line pointed at one household by name. Voss lost the election, then won the libel suit — the court calling the verses an attack no decent quarrel could excuse.
