symbolism

/SIM-buh-liz-um/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Symbolism means the use of images, objects, or actions to stand for larger ideas — a wilting orchid carrying a family's decline, a storm carrying trouble to come.

See the word in context

Ms. Ito circled the wilting orchid in the story's first scene and wrote a single word in the margin: symbolism.

In the mural above the courthouse steps, the painter loaded every corner with symbolism: broken chains for the end of the old regime, an unhooded falcon for the freed press, and scales tipping toward a single feather for mercy outweighing gold.

Chains, falcon, scales — none of them matters as a mere object; each stands for a larger idea. Meaning carried by images in that way is exactly what symbolism is.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    allegory: Allegory means a story whose entire surface maps onto a second, hidden story — every character and event a stand-in from first page to last. Symbolism is more selective: certain images carry larger ideas, while the rest of the work stays simply itself.

    imagery: Imagery means language that appeals to the senses — it makes you see, hear, and smell the scene. Symbolism goes a step further: it takes what you see and makes it stand for something more.

    metaphor: Metaphor means a figure of speech that directly calls one thing another, made once in passing — 'the road was a ribbon.' Symbolism is the wider practice of loading objects and actions with meaning across a whole work.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    literalism: presenting things exactly as they are, with nothing standing for anything else

    reportage: plain factual telling, meant to inform rather than to suggest

    Where it sits · how much of the work stands for something else

    undertone → symbolism → allegory

    undertone vs. symbolism: An undertone is fainter than symbolism: it lets a second meaning hum quietly beneath a scene, while symbolism openly hands objects a larger idea to carry.

    allegory vs. symbolism: Allegory goes further than symbolism: symbolism loads certain images with larger meaning, but an allegory commits the entire story, start to finish, to standing for another one.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral, analytical — the English-class term

      Symbolism is the word for the practice as a whole; a single stand-in is a symbol. SAT passages reward keeping the family straight: imagery appeals to the senses, a metaphor makes one direct comparison, symbolism makes objects carry ideas, and an allegory commits the entire story to a second meaning. Outside literature the word covers gestures too — flying a flag at half-mast is pure symbolism, an action that exists to stand for grief.
