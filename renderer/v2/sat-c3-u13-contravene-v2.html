Meaning

What It Means

Contravene means to go against or violate a rule, law, or agreement.

See the word in context

Painting your fence purple would contravene the association's bylaws, the letter warned, citing page nine.

The team's star sat out a month after his new sponsorship was found to contravene league rules: players may endorse gear, never gambling sites — and his contract named one on every billboard.

Contravene is the formal word for conduct that collides with a rule, law, or agreement — the vocabulary of referees and official reports. It does not require intent: an act can contravene a regulation by accident, which is exactly why the word feels cooler than defy.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    violate: Violate is the everyday word for breaking a rule or law, blunt and morally loaded. Contravene is its cool, formal counterpart — the vocabulary of inspectors and official findings — and it carries no charge of intent: an act can contravene a regulation entirely by accident.

    breach: Breach breaks the terms of an agreement while it stands — a contract, a treaty, a lease. Contravene is wider: conduct measured against any rule, law, or agreement and found to collide with it. Every breach of contract contravenes something; plenty of contraventions involve no agreement at all.

    infringe: Infringe trespasses on a rule or a right, often bit by bit — each small step edging further over the line. Contravene names a conduct-versus-rule collision, whole and assessable in one finding. A fence infringes on a neighbor's land inch by inch; a banned pesticide contravenes the regulation outright.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    obey: to do what the rule requires

    honor: to keep faith with an agreement's terms

    Where it sits · how fully and openly the rule breaks

    bend → contravene → defy

    bend vs. contravene: To bend a rule is much milder than to contravene it — stretched, the rule still holds; contravened, it is broken.

    defy vs. contravene: Defy goes further than contravene: a rule can be contravened quietly, even by accident, but defiance is loud, deliberate, and aimed straight at the authority behind the rule.

      formal / official

      Contravene belongs to inspectors' reports and legal findings: conduct measured against a rule and found to collide with it, intent aside. To stress open rebellion, defy is the word; for the physical breaking of an agreement's terms, violate or breach.
