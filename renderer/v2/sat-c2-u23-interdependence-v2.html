Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Interdependence means a relationship in which each side relies on the other.

See the word in context

The ski town and the resort live in strict interdependence: the lifts bring the visitors, and the town houses everyone who runs them.

The climbing team practices interdependence by design: the belayer eats only after the leader checks her knots, and the leader climbs only on ropes the belayer has coiled. Neither job means anything alone.

Interdependence is stricter than cooperation and bigger than reliance: reliance can run one way, and cooperation can be quit. Interdependent parties are built into each other; neither half works — or even quite makes sense — without the other.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    reciprocity: Reciprocity is matched exchange — favor answered with favor, invitation with invitation, each side keeping the ledger roughly even. Interdependence runs deeper than trading: the two sides are built into each other's workings, function for function, so neither one operates without the other.

    symbiosis: Symbiosis is biology's word for two species locked in mutual need — the clownfish and the anemone, each surviving by the other. Interdependence is the same two-way need stated generally, for economies, teams, and towns as well as living things.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    independence: standing entirely on your own

    self-sufficiency: needing nothing from anyone — the condition interdependence rules out

    Where it sits · whether the need runs both ways

    cooperation → reliance → interdependence

    cooperation vs. interdependence: Cooperation stops short of interdependence — cooperating parties choose to work together and could each still walk away whole, while interdependent ones are built into each other.

    reliance vs. interdependence: Reliance stops short of interdependence — it can run one way only, the leaner and the leaned-on.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / analytic

      Interdependence is the analyst's word for two-way need — economies, ecosystems, teammates. Reliance and dependence run one way; cooperation is voluntary; symbiosis is the biology-specific cousin. Use interdependence when the point is mutual: each side is load-bearing for the other.

      Visitors to Cutler Sound photograph the fishing fleet, but the harbor's oldest partnership is less picturesque: the boats and the ice plant on the pier. Their interdependence is total. Without ice, the day's catch spoils before it reaches the mainland; without the fleet, the plant has no customer for a single block. The arrangement is not a favor either side could suspend. When the plant's compressor failed one August, the whole fleet tied up within two days. When a bad season kept the boats in port, the plant laid off half its crew by the second week. Neither business chose the other, exactly; the harbor built them into each other, and neither works alone.
