deregulation

/dee-reg-yuh-LAY-shun/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Deregulation means the reduction or removal of government rules and restrictions controlling an industry or activity.

See the word in context

After the deregulation of the telephone industry, dozens of new companies rushed in to compete for customers.

For forty years, federal rules dictated which routes long-haul trucking companies could drive and what they could charge. When Congress swept most of those rules away, the industry entered a period of rapid deregulation. New carriers flooded the market, shipping rates fell, and established companies suddenly had to compete on price and service. Economists still debate whether the change helped workers, but no one disputes how thoroughly the old rulebook was dismantled.

Removing the government rules that had controlled an entire industry is precisely what deregulation names.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    decontrol: Decontrol is the removal of one specific official control — most often a price cap or a quota — lifted one control at a time. Deregulation is broader: it strips away whole sections of the rulebook that governs an industry. Decontrol takes off a single control; deregulation shrinks the regime of rules itself.

    liberalization: Liberalization is the loosening of rules so markets can operate more freely, and it names a policy direction more than a single act — a market can be liberalized gradually over a decade. Deregulation is the concrete removal of the government rules themselves. Liberalization is where policy is heading; deregulation is the rulebook actually shrinking.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    regulation: the system of government rules controlling an industry or activity

    restriction: a rule that limits what is allowed

    Where it sits · how much of the rulebook is removed

    relaxation → deregulation → dismantling

    relaxation vs. deregulation: Relaxation is weaker than deregulation: relaxing the rules only eases a few requirements, while deregulation removes whole sections of the rulebook.

    dismantling vs. deregulation: Dismantling is stronger than deregulation: deregulation trims or removes many rules, while dismantling the regulatory system tears the entire structure down.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    economic and political register

    Deregulation almost always describes a policy applied to a whole industry or market, such as airlines, energy, banking, or trucking, rather than to one person. Its natural opposite is regulation, the building up of government rules, so the two words often appear together in debates about how free a market should be.

    Until 1982, running an intercity bus line in the United States meant living inside a thick federal rulebook. A company needed government permission for every route it drove, every fare it charged, and every city it added or dropped. Applications could take years. Then Congress passed a sweeping deregulation of the industry. Route approvals, fare filings, and entry permits disappeared from the rulebook together. New carriers flooded in within months. Fares fell on busy corridors, and discount lines appeared. Not every change was welcome -- some small towns lost service entirely, since no rule now required anyone to drive there -- but the industry had been remade by subtraction.
