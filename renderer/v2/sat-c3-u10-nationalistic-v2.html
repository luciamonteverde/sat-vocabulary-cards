nationalistic

/nash-uh-nuh-LIS-tik/ - the stress falls on LIS

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Nationalistic means marked by aggressive devotion to one's own nation — pride swollen into a claim of superiority over all the others.

See the word in context

Critics called the new stamp series nationalistic: every design showed the country's inventions towering over smaller, blurrier versions of everyone else's.

Reviews of the war epic split along a single line — what the director called patriotic, others called nationalistic, since every homegrown soldier in the film is a saint and every foreign one a coward.

Nationalistic is more precise than 'proud' because it names where the pride goes wrong. Patriotic love of country can sit happily beside respect for other nations; nationalistic feeling needs a ranking — my country first, and above, and at the expense of yours. The stacked suffixes are a hint that a belief system has moved in.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    chauvinistic: Chauvinistic is the wider disease — aggressive, blind conviction that your own group, whatever it is, outranks the rest. Nationalistic is chauvinism with a flag on it: the group is the nation, and the ranking is the point.

    flag-waving: Flag-waving is the show — anthems cranked up, banners on every porch, loyalty performed loudly for the crowd. Nationalistic goes deeper than display: it is the settled belief that the nation deserves the top spot, whether or not anyone is watching.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    cosmopolitan: at home among many nations and traditions, valuing them alike

    internationalist: favoring cooperation among nations over rivalry

    Where it sits · how far love of country tips into claims of superiority

    patriotic → nationalistic → jingoistic

    patriotic vs. nationalistic: Patriotic is milder than nationalistic — real love of country that needs no ranking and picks no fight with anyone else's.

    jingoistic vs. nationalistic: Jingoistic goes further than nationalistic: the superiority turns belligerent, itching for confrontation abroad to prove the point.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    political / critical

    Nationalistic is almost always an outsider's verdict — reporting and history writing use it to flag pride that has curdled into ranking. Call a crowd patriotic and you have complimented it; call it nationalistic and you have diagnosed it. Its government-shaped unit-mates — theocratic, plutocratic, oligarchic — describe who rules; nationalistic describes a mood that can grip any of them.

Every placard felt nationalistic: the sextant was not merely early, it was proof that this country's navigators outshone all rivals; the shipyard model closed by calling foreign hulls 'imitations.' Ana loves these collections and says so; her complaint is aimed at the framing, not the objects. A caption can honor its subject without scoring it against the world, she wrote. The exhibit, she concluded, treats history less as a record than as a ranking, with the home team permanently first.
