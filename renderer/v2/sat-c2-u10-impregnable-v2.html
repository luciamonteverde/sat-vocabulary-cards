Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impregnable means impossible to capture or break into.

See the word in context

Forty-one shots on goal, forty-one saves: by March the sports pages had declared the keeper's near post impregnable.

Analysts kept a running counter for it: break points against Ekaterin's serve. Across two full seasons the counter read zero — returners guessed, lunged, chipped, charged, and trudged back to receive the next one. Her service games were impregnable: not merely strong, but never once taken.

Impregnable is a ruling on results, not effort. Fortified says walls were raised; guarded says eyes are open; impregnable says the taking cannot be done, however clever or patient the attacker. It comes from Old French prenable, 'takeable,' with im- slamming the door — the g crept into the spelling later, by scribal habit. And no, pregnant is not a relative; that word grew from different Latin entirely.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unassailable: Unassailable says no attack can even be mounted — the position offers nothing to grab, which is why it usually describes arguments, reputations, and leads. Impregnable welcomes the assault and shrugs it off: the fortress word for defenses that are attacked, sometimes for years, and never once taken.

    invulnerable: Invulnerable means the thing cannot be wounded or harmed at all, attacked or not — no weak point exists anywhere on it. Impregnable makes a narrower promise about one outcome: nobody gets in, nobody takes it. An impregnable vault might come away scratched, dented, and scorched; it still holds.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    vulnerable: open to attack

    defenseless: without any protection whatever

    Where it sits · how surely it withstands any attack

    guarded → fortified → impregnable

    guarded vs. impregnable: A guarded gate has someone watching it, and that is all it promises; impregnable skips the watchman and promises the result — nothing gets in, ever.

    fortified vs. impregnable: Fortified says defenses were built; impregnable is the ruling after the battle — attack all you like, it cannot be taken.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / martial

    Impregnable belongs to defenses — fortresses, vaults, serves, alibis — and pronounces them untakeable. Its unit-mate inviolable guards a different treasure: rules and promises too sacred to break. You storm an impregnable wall; you betray an inviolable oath.

    Deep inside a mountain on the Arctic island of Spitsbergen, behind an airlock and a hundred meters of permafrost, sits a vault holding over a million seed samples from nearly every country on Earth. Its designers speak of it, carefully, as the world's most impregnable building, and the claim is specific. The vault is not beyond damage. In 2017, meltwater from an unusually warm winter flooded the entrance tunnel, and the doors have since been reinforced. But no flood, storm, or intruder has ever reached the seeds. The chamber itself, bored into rock kept frozen by the mountain, has never once been breached. Engineers like to put it plainly: the tunnel may scar and the ice may shift, but nothing gets past the inner door.
