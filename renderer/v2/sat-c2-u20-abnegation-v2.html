Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Abnegation means the denial of comforts or pleasures to oneself.

See the word in context

His abnegation puzzled the heirs: a fortune in the bank, yet he walked, mended his coats, and went without.

To finish her book by spring, Odile chose a season of abnegation. The streaming accounts went dark, dinners were declined, the theater tickets stayed in their envelope, and Saturday mornings belonged to the desk. Friends called the winter grim; she called it rent on the pages she wanted to write.

Abnegation is the exact word for what she practiced, because abnegation means the denial of comforts or pleasures to oneself — she imposed the lack, and she absorbed it, on purpose and for a purpose. Privation is the same bareness arriving uninvited; abnegation signs the lease itself.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    self-denial: Self-denial is the everyday word for refusing yourself pleasures — skipping dessert, passing on a purchase — and it can be casual, small, or brief. Abnegation is the graver, more deliberate practice: comforts given up in a sustained way, usually for a vow, a goal, or a principle with moral weight.

    going without: Going without is the plainest phrase for lacking something, and it says nothing about why — you can go without because the money ran out. Abnegation adds the choosing: you impose the lack on yourself, on purpose and for a purpose, which is what gives the word its moral weight.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    indulgence: granting yourself the pleasure

    extravagance: spending freely on your own comfort

    Where it sits · how much of oneself is given up

    restraint → abnegation → martyrdom

    restraint vs. abnegation: Restraint is far milder than abnegation — a single no, declining the second slice, skipping one sale — and it costs you an evening at most, while abnegation strings those refusals into a whole chosen season of bare living.

    martyrdom vs. abnegation: Martyrdom goes further than abnegation — abnegation gives up comforts; martyrdom gives up the whole self, and does not come back from it.

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Abnegation is for chosen bareness with moral weight: vows, training, a goal paid for in comforts. Plain self-denial does the everyday work. And keep it clear of this unit's privation, which is the same bareness imposed from outside — the fast is abnegation; the famine is privation.
