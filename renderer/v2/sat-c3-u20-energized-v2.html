energized

/EN-er-jyzed/ - the stress falls on EN

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Energized means filled with fresh energy and enthusiasm.

See the word in context

Energized by the first cold snap of October, the crew shingled the whole roof by early afternoon.

Marisol dragged herself to the pottery class after a dull workweek and left energized, sketching glaze ideas on the bus home and reorganizing her studio shelf before dinner.

Energized is more than rested or awake: it names a surge of usable enthusiasm, the state of wanting the work. Its en- is actually Greek — en ('in') plus ergon ('work'): energy is literally the at-work-ness inside a thing, and an energized person has it switched on.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    invigorated: Invigorated means braced and strengthened in body, usually by something physical — cold air, a swim, hard exercise. Energized points instead at a surge of usable enthusiasm, the appetite for doing something. A plunge can leave you invigorated from the skin in; good news leaves you energized to act.

    recharged: Recharged means your energy has been restored after running empty — a battery filled back up, a person topped off by a weekend of rest. Energized needs no prior emptiness: it can be brand-new drive arriving out of nowhere on top of an ordinary day.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    listless: too flat and drained to care about anything

    sluggish: moving slowly and heavily, as if through syrup

    Where it sits · how eager to spring into action

    rested → energized → electrified

    rested vs. energized: Rested stops short of energized — a rested worker can do the job; an energized one is itching to start it.

    electrified vs. energized: Electrified goes further than energized — an energized crew works briskly; an electrified one is out of its seats before the announcement ends.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    everyday / upbeat

    Energized is the go-to word for a fresh charge of drive — after good news, a break, a win. Invigorated leans physical; recharged implies you were empty first. And mind the trap on this unit's own list: enervated looks like a cousin and means the exact opposite — drained. Swapping those two reverses a sentence.
