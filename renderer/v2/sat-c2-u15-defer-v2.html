Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Defer means to put a matter off until a chosen later time — or, in the phrase defer to, to yield to another person's judgment or authority out of respect.

See the word in context

The zoning board voted to defer the rezoning question until the traffic study came back in the fall.

Three doctors disagreed about the climber's frostbitten hand, and the youngest of them had written the very paper the other two kept citing. After twenty minutes, the senior surgeon closed the debate: 'On this one, I defer to Dr. Okafor.' It cost him nothing to say and settled everything — the operating plan became hers, not because she outranked anyone, but because on this question her judgment did.

Defer is the precise verb for the surgeon's move: he set his own opinion aside and yielded, deliberately and respectfully, to a better-placed judge. Deferring is not losing an argument — it is choosing whose judgment should govern. And the same verb, without the 'to,' does calendar work: a committee defers a decision the way the surgeon deferred to a colleague, moving the matter into other hands — later ones or wiser ones.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    postpone: Postpone is the plain scheduling word: the game is postponed, the dentist appointment is postponed. Defer does the same calendar work in a more formal register — and it alone has the second life: defer to someone, yielding to their judgment.

    yield: To yield is to give way under pressure — a driver yields, an army yields ground. Defer to someone is yielding by choice: you set your own view aside out of respect for another's judgment or standing.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    expedite: push a matter forward faster

    overrule: set another's judgment aside in favor of your own

    Where it sits · how far from today the matter is pushed

    delay → defer → shelve

    delay vs. defer: Delay is milder than defer: a delayed vote slips by an hour or a week, often by accident, while deferring is a deliberate act that carries the matter to a chosen later date.

    shelve vs. defer: Shelve goes further than defer: a deferred question keeps a return date — October's agenda, next spring's budget — while a shelved one is set aside with no date at all.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal, procedural — boardrooms, courts, and committee minutes

      Defer is one spelling doing two jobs, and the little word 'to' tells you which. Defer a decision and you have moved it in time; defer to a person and you have moved it into their hands. Keep it clear of two sound-alikes: differ (to disagree or be unlike) and deflect (to turn something aside) — a witness who dodges a question deflects it; a committee that reschedules one defers it.
