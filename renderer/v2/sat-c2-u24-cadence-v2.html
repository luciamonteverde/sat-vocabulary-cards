Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Cadence means a rhythm or progression of sound.

See the word in context

You could set a watch by the auctioneer's cadence, numbers rising and dipping in the same rolling pattern all afternoon.

Double-dutch turners at the rec center keep a cadence you can hear from the parking lot — two slaps and a skip, two slaps and a skip — and the girls time their jumps to it without ever looking down at the ropes.

Cadence is the word because it means a rhythm or progression of sound — the slap-slap-skip repeats like a drummed sentence, and everything else organizes itself around that shape. Tempo would name only the speed; cadence names the rise and fall a body can ride.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    rhythm: Rhythm is the everyday, catch-all word for any repeating beat — a heartbeat, rain on a roof, a drum loop. A cadence is a more particular rhythm: the patterned rise and fall of a voice or a movement, a shape you could march to. Every cadence is a rhythm; most rhythms are not cadences.

    tempo: Tempo is only the speed of the music — how fast the beats come, something you could state as a number. A cadence is the shape of the pattern itself, the rise and fall a listener can ride. Doubling the tempo makes the waltz faster; it is still the same cadence.

    meter: Meter is the counted, formal grid of beats — four beats to a bar, five stresses to a line — the kind of pattern you write down and check. A cadence is the living pattern you hear, rising and falling as it moves. Meter is the ruled paper; cadence is the handwriting.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    monotone: one flat pitch with no rise or fall at all

    jumble: sounds with no pattern to find

    Where it sits · how much patterned movement the voice has

    monotone → lilt → cadence

    monotone vs. cadence: A monotone is the bare opposite of a cadence — every syllable on the same flat note, nothing rising, nothing falling, no pattern for a listener to ride.

    lilt vs. cadence: A lilt stops short of a full cadence — a hint of music in a voice, pleasant but loose, without the steady repeating pattern a drummer could follow.

      Word Choice

      neutral / musical

      Cadence is for the pattern in sound or motion — speech, drumming, rowing, marching. If only speed is at issue, tempo; if only the tune, melody. Reach for cadence when you can feel where the next beat will land.
