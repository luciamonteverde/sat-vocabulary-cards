Meaning

What It Means

Commensurate means corresponding in size or amount.

See the word in context

Ana's parents pay an allowance commensurate with the week's chores: mow the whole lawn and the number doubles.

On the expedition, water is rationed commensurate with body weight and load: the porter hauling forty kilos uphill draws two full bottles at each stop, while the surveyor carrying only a clipboard draws one.

Commensurate is tighter than 'fair' or 'matching' because it insists the two amounts move together in proportion — double one side and the other side doubles too, both measured with the same yardstick.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    proportional: Proportional is commensurate's plainest twin: two amounts rising and falling in step. The difference is register, not meaning — proportional lives in math class and everyday talk, while commensurate belongs to contracts, policies, and judgments, always paired with 'with'. Pay commensurate with experience is the formal promise; proportional pay is the plain one.

    matching: Matching means equal or alike — matching mugs are the same mug twice — and there is no scaling in it. Commensurate never means identical amounts; it means amounts that track each other, growing and shrinking together, so that a bigger contribution honestly earns a bigger reward.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    lopsided: tilted heavily toward one side

    mismatched: paired without regard to fit or size

    Where it sits · how exactly the two amounts match

    comparable → commensurate → identical

    comparable vs. commensurate: Comparable only claims two things are in the same neighborhood; commensurate makes the stricter promise that they rise and fall in exact proportion.

    identical vs. commensurate: Identical overshoots commensurate: commensurate bonuses scale with each worker's hours, while identical bonuses hand every worker the very same number.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / official

      Commensurate belongs to contracts, policies, and judgments: pay commensurate with experience, punishment commensurate with the offense — always with 'with.' In everyday speech, proportional or 'in line with' carries the same idea more lightly; reach for commensurate when you want the language of rules and measured fairness.

      After the flood, the relief office faced a hard question: how to divide one pool of money among four very different towns. Equal checks sounded fair until the mayors compared notes -- Marsh Creek had lost nine streets, Dunmore a single boat ramp. The final rule made aid commensurate with damage: assessors scored every parish, and each dollar followed the score. Marsh Creek received eleven times Dunmore's grant, and no one called it favoritism, because the ratio was printed beside the scores for anyone to check. The clerk who drafted the rule kept a copy framed: fairness, she liked to say, is not sameness but fit.
