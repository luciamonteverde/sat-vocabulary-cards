Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Idealistic means guided by high principles rather than practical concerns.

See the word in context

The new principal arrived idealistic, convinced that if lessons were engaging enough, the detention room would empty itself within a year.

The city's youth council drafted an idealistic charter: every park free, every bus electric, every teenager guaranteed a summer job. Veteran officials smiled at the arithmetic, but the young councilors argued that budgets should chase principles, not the other way around.

Idealistic describes judgment steered by how things should be rather than how they are. It is not an insult - idealistic people often move the world - but the word always hints that practical obstacles are being waved away.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    high-minded: High-minded praises the loftiness of someone's principles - noble aims, sincerely held. Idealistic adds a caution: those principles are steering real decisions while practical obstacles get waved aside. A high-minded judge applies noble standards to the case; an idealistic planner budgets as if the world already met them.

    starry-eyed: Starry-eyed is the gentle tease for dreamy, naive hopefulness - eyes shining, feet well off the ground. Idealistic is sturdier and more respectable: guided by genuine principles about how things should be, even while practicality is set aside. Starry-eyed mocks the dreamer; idealistic can still be praise.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    realistic: judging by how things actually are

    worldly: experienced in how the world really works

    Where it sits · how far the vision outruns the possible

    hopeful → idealistic → utopian

    hopeful vs. idealistic: Hopeful expects things may improve; idealistic goes further, steering choices by a vision of how things ought to be, practicality aside.

    utopian vs. idealistic: Utopian outruns idealistic: the idealistic reformer believes the city can be far better, while the utopian planner sketches a city with no conflict at all.

      Word Choice

      neutral, often gently critical

      Idealistic sits between compliment and caution: it credits noble principles while hinting they float above the facts. Its ladder-mate utopian makes the stronger charge of chasing outright perfection. Among this unit's words, idealistic is about the standards guiding effort; visionary, by contrast, praises imagination about the future that may well prove right.
