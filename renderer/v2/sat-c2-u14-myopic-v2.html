Meaning

What It Means

Myopic means lacking foresight; focused only on the short term.

See the word in context

Skipping breakfast to cram five more vocabulary words is myopic — the gain shows up now, the blur arrives by the second hour of the exam.

The eighty-dollar roof patch waited all autumn. There was always a nearer use for eighty dollars — a concert, a brake light, a weekend — and the stain on the ceiling grew politely, an inch a month, until a February thaw brought the bedroom ceiling down. The contractor's bill was four thousand dollars. Every myopic decision along the way had felt, at the time, like thrift.

Myopic is the exact word because each choice lacked foresight — focused only on the short term, the word's borrowed meaning from nearsightedness: near things sharp, far things blurred. A myopic decision isn't stupid and isn't reckless; next month simply crowds next year out of focus. The bill for the far blur always arrives.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    shortsighted: Shortsighted is the plain-English twin of myopic: both name the trade of far costs for near gains. The difference is register, not meaning — shortsighted scolds in everyday clothes, while myopic is the critic's word, at home in editorials about policies, budgets, and strategies.

    blinkered: Blinkered borrows the racehorse's eye-shields: unable to see outside one narrow view, deaf to other perspectives and inconvenient facts. Myopic runs along a different axis — time. A myopic planner may weigh every viewpoint in the room and still trade next year away for next month.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    farsighted: plans for what is coming long before it comes

    forward-thinking: builds today with tomorrow already priced in

    Where it sits · how far ahead the vision reaches

    narrow → myopic → blind

    narrow vs. myopic: A narrow view leaves things out to the sides; a myopic one loses everything past the near term.

    blind vs. myopic: Blind goes past myopic — the nearsighted planner still sees this quarter clearly; the blind one sees nothing coming from any distance.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / critical

      Myopic is the critic's word for decisions that can't see past the near term — policies, budgets, strategies. It is not stupidity: this unit's inept fails for lack of skill, but a myopic plan may be executed brilliantly toward a horizon two feet away. Its true opposite in this unit is judicious, which weighs the far term as heavily as the near. Shortsighted says the same thing in plainer clothes.
