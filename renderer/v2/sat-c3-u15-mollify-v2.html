mollify

/MOL-uh-fye/ - rhymes with qualify

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Mollify means to soothe someone's anger.

See the word in context

Nothing would mollify the twins after the trip was canceled until their father promised the beach in writing, with a date.

When the update deleted players' saved games, the studio moved fast to mollify them: a public apology, a rollback tool, and a month of free play for every affected account.

Mollify aims at anger specifically: you soothe pain and calm nerves, but you mollify someone who is furious with you, softening the fury until it can be reasoned with.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    calm: Calm is the plainest, broadest verb for settling agitation of any kind — nerves, a spooked horse, a crying child. Mollify is narrower and takes on anger specifically: you calm a startled friend, but you mollify an offended one, softening fury that a wrong has caused.

    placate: Placate means to quiet someone's anger, usually by handing over a concession, and it often buys only temporary peace. Mollify says the feeling itself was genuinely softened. A placated crowd stops shouting for now; a mollified crowd actually feels less wronged.

    appease: Appease means to buy off anger or hostility by yielding — often yielding more than you should, which gives the word its edge of weakness. Mollify carries no such judgment: it simply says someone's fury was softened, not that anything shameful was traded away.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    enrage: to make someone furious

    incense: to inflame with anger

    Where it sits · how far the calming has to go

    soothe → mollify → conciliate

    soothe vs. mollify: You can soothe a crying baby or a sunburn with gentleness alone; soothing is broader and milder than mollifying, which takes on genuine anger.

    conciliate vs. mollify: Conciliate goes further than mollify: mollifying softens the fury of the moment, while conciliating works to win the angry party back, often by giving ground.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / neutral

    Mollify is the precise verb when the object is an angry person (or the anger itself). 'Calm' and 'soothe' cover everything from babies to nerves; mollify announces that fury was there and was softened. Note the target: you mollify the person, not the problem.
