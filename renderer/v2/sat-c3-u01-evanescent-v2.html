evanescent

/ev-uh-NES-uhnt/ - the stress falls on NES

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Evanescent means lasting only a moment before vanishing.

See the word in context

An evanescent hush fell over the stadium between the kick and the roar, gone before anyone could name it.

The soap-bubble artist closes the children's show by filling the stage with a single bubble the size of a door. Its glory is evanescent: it trembles, throws rainbows across the front rows for four or five seconds, and is gone. No encore is possible — the next bubble is a different bubble — and the gasp in the dark is loudest at the exact instant there is nothing left to gasp at.

Evanescent is the precise word because the wonder is already dissolving as it appears; the audience is watching the disappearance itself. A short meeting is merely brief. Evanescent claims more: the thing fades before your eyes, and its going is part of what it is.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    vanishing: Vanishing reports a disappearance in progress — the ship is vanishing into the fog right now, whatever the ship is normally like. Evanescent makes the fading a built-in character: an evanescent glow was always going to dissolve almost as it formed. Vanishing describes an event; evanescent describes a nature.

    fleeting: Fleeting is about speed — a fleeting glance, a fleeting thought, here and gone almost at once. A fleeting moment simply ends fast. Evanescent adds a picture: the thing visibly dissolves as it appears, mist thinning while you watch, the fade itself on display before your eyes.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    enduring: lasting on and on, through wear and weather

    permanent: fixed in place for good

    Where it sits · how soon it is gone

    temporary → short-lived → evanescent

    temporary vs. evanescent: Temporary is far milder than evanescent — it promises only that a thing ends someday, maybe years out, while an evanescent hush is dissolving in the same breath that announces it.

    short-lived vs. evanescent: Short-lived stops short of evanescent: a short-lived fashion still gets its season, while an evanescent glow is dissolving in the very moment you notice it.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    literary / poetic

    Evanescent is for things caught dissolving — glows, hushes, scents, moods — and it carries a note of wistful admiration. Its neighbor in this unit, transient, is the cooler word: a transient guest still unpacks a bag and stays a while. Say transient for people, populations, and symptoms; save evanescent for what is fading even as you point at it.
