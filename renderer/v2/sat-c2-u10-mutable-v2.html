Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Mutable means prone to change; inconstant.

See the word in context

Case law treats 'reasonable' as mutable: what juries accepted in 1950 is not what they accept now.

The board game arrived with a rulebook and a promise: house rules encouraged. Within a year the family had voted in three new forfeits, retired the sand timer, and made the youngest player banker for life. Visiting cousins complain that it isn't the same game anymore; the family answers that it never was — the rules are mutable, and the voting is half the fun.

Mutable says only that change can happen; it schedules nothing. A mutable rule may sit unchanged for a decade — what matters is that nothing forbids Tuesday's vote from rewriting it. You met its opposite two units ago: immutable, the rule no vote can touch. Mutable is that word with the hinge oiled.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    changeable: Changeable reports that a thing keeps changing, and it can sound flighty — changeable weather, changeable moods. Mutable makes a calmer, deeper claim: the thing is open to change, whether or not any change is underway. A mutable law may sit untouched for a decade; changeable weather never sits at all.

    fluid: Fluid lies further along the same road: a fluid situation has no settled shape, its lines redrawn while you watch. Mutable is quieter — capable of being rewritten, not necessarily being rewritten now. A merger's first week is fluid; the club constitution is merely mutable.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    fixed: set in place, for now and until someone with authority moves it

    unchanging: staying exactly as it is

    Where it sits · how deeply change runs through it

    adjustable → mutable → ever-shifting

    adjustable vs. mutable: Adjustable means a person can change it on purpose, one dial at a time; mutable runs deeper — the thing itself is open to change through and through.

    ever-shifting vs. mutable: Ever-shifting outruns mutable: a mutable thing can change yet may sit still for years; the ever-shifting one never settles at all.

Then Priya read the handbook's last page and found a line nobody ever quoted: any rule herein may be revised by a two-thirds vote of the student council. The handbook, in other words, was mutable. It did not change often -- Rule 14 had outlasted six principals -- but nothing in it was fixed beyond reach. Priya gathered signatures, drafted a revision, and brought it to the council in March. The vote ran eleven to four in her favor. The next printing carried a new Rule 14. The rest stood exactly as before: unchanged, but no longer mistaken for permanent.
