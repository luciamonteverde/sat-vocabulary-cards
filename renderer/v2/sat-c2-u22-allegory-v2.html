allegory

/AL-uh-gor-ee/ - the stress falls on AL

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Allegory means a story in which the characters and events stand for deeper moral or political meanings.

See the word in context

On its surface the ballet is a nursery quarrel between wind-up toys; critics read it as an allegory of the museum's own funding wars.

The fourteenth-century tapestry seems to show a stag hunt, yet the guides unfold it as allegory: the stag is the city's independence, the hounds are its rival guilds, and the hunter wearing a foreign crown needs no explaining. Weavers who could not write politics wove it instead.

Allegory claims more than 'symbolic': an allegory sustains its second meaning across the whole work, so that nearly every character and event has a counterpart. One charged image is a symbol; one brief moral story is a parable; a work that keeps the mapping going from first scene to last is an allegory.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    symbolic tale: A symbolic tale is a story that leans on symbols here and there — an image or two carrying extra weight. An allegory goes much further: it keeps a sustained one-to-one mapping, so nearly every character and event has a counterpart from the first scene to the last.

    coded story: A coded story hides a second, intended meaning under its surface, often so that only insiders will find it. An allegory also carries a second meaning, but openly and completely: any attentive reader can unfold the mapping, and it runs through the whole work rather than staying buried.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    straight history: events told for what they were, standing for nothing beyond themselves

    factual report: an account whose only job is to state what happened

    Where it sits · how far the second meaning extends

    symbol → parable → allegory

    symbol vs. allegory: A symbol is a single charged image — one dove, one broken clock — while an allegory keeps a whole cast of symbols working together from the first page to the last.

    parable vs. allegory: A parable stops short of allegory: it builds one small story to make one point, while an allegory threads its second meaning through every scene of a larger work.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    literary / academic

    Reach for allegory only when a whole work sustains a second meaning — every major element standing for something else. A passing unnamed reference is an allusion; one brief moral story is a parable; and for imagery in general, figurative is the wider, plainer term.

    For three weeks the eighth graders read a slim novel about a lighthouse crew who ration candles while their keeper hoards the oil. On Friday, Ms. Arroyo projected a chart. The keeper, she said, matched the railroad baron who ran their town in 1890; the crew matched the strikers; even the storm matched the winter the mills closed. A student objected that the author never mentions the town. He does not have to, Arroyo answered -- the book is an allegory, and its mapping runs from the first chapter to the last. To prove it, she had each row of desks trace one character and report the historical figure behind him. By the bell, no character was left over: every sailor and every squall stood for someone or something in the town's record.
