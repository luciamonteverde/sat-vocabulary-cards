condone

/kuhn-DOHN/ - rhymes with alone

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Condone means to pardon or deliberately overlook wrongdoing.

See the word in context

Season after season the club fined no one for driving carts across the newly seeded greens, until members took the silence itself to condone the damage.

The academy's honor board found that a housemaster had known of the forged sick-notes for two full terms; by never reporting them he had condoned the forgery, the board ruled, and he was quietly retired.

Condone is subtler than 'approve': you may privately disapprove and still condone something by letting it pass without consequence. Its object is almost always wrongdoing — you condone cheating or cruelty, never a kindness.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    tolerate: To tolerate is to put up with something you dislike — openly, and often while saying so. Condone is quieter and closer to guilt: whoever condones a wrong sees it clearly, says nothing, and lets the silence stand as permission. You can tolerate noise blamelessly; condoning cheating stains you.

    excuse: To excuse is to accept a reason for a fault and let it go — the fault is admitted, weighed, and forgiven on its merits. Condone skips the weighing: it lets a wrong pass without consequence, and it belongs to onlookers and authorities whose silence gives the wrong its permission.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    forbid: to ban the behavior before it happens

    penalize: to make it cost something after

    Where it sits · how openly the wrong is endorsed

    overlook → condone → applaud

    overlook vs. condone: You can overlook a fault without ever knowing it was there; overlook stops short of condone, which sees the wrong clearly and lets it stand.

    applaud vs. condone: Applaud goes further than condone: to condone cheating is to let it pass in silence, while to applaud it is to celebrate the wrong out loud.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / formal

    Condone sits in the middle of a tidy trio in this unit: abet helps a wrong along, condone lets it pass, and condemn — its near-twin in sound and its opposite in sense — calls it out. Use condone for authorities and onlookers whose silence gives permission.
