caricature

/KAIR-ih-kuh-chur/ - the stress falls on KAIR

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A caricature is a portrayal that exaggerates its subject's most distinctive features, usually to amuse or to mock.

See the word in context

The courtroom artist kept her official sketches sober, but her notebook margins filled with caricatures — the judge all eyebrows, the prosecutor one enormous pointing finger.

For the band director's retirement, the seniors commissioned a caricature: a head twice life size, cheeks ballooned around a trumpet, a metronome for a heart — and he laughed hardest of anyone, because every exaggeration was his.

Caricature is the exact word because the drawing works by overloading real features: the cheeks, the trumpet, the obsession with tempo are all genuinely his, stretched until they turn comic. A faithful likeness would have kept the proportions; the caricature keeps the man and inflates the evidence.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    lampoon: A lampoon is a written or staged attack that ridicules its target, and it can work by any weapon — mock praise, parody, or plain scorn. A caricature has one signature move: it seizes the subject's own most distinctive features and stretches them until they turn comic.

    cartoon: A cartoon is the drawn form — most newspaper caricatures are cartoons. But a caricature can live in prose or in a comedian's act, and plenty of cartoons exaggerate no one; the two words overlap without matching.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    likeness: a portrayal faithful to how its subject really looks and acts

    tribute: a portrayal made to honor its subject rather than tease it

    Where it sits · how far the portrayal bends its subject

    impression → caricature → mockery

    impression vs. caricature: An impression is gentler than a caricature: a comic's impression borrows a voice and a favorite phrase, while a caricature seizes the subject's most distinctive features and stretches them past life size.

    mockery vs. caricature: Mockery goes further than caricature: a caricature bends its subject's features and keeps the likeness legible, while making a mockery of something abandons fidelity altogether — the original survives only as a target.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      mocking / critical

      A caricature needs a real subject: the humor comes from recognizing actual features pushed too far, whether in a drawing, a prose sketch, or an act. The extended use is worth owning for the SAT — an argument 'reduced to a caricature' has been distorted into its silliest version. Nearby: hyperbole, in this same unit, inflates a claim rather than a portrait, and a lampoon attacks by any comic means, not only by exaggerated features.

Her drawings were never cruel, but they were never neutral either: each teacher arrived as a caricature, one true feature hunted down and enlarged until it did all the talking.
