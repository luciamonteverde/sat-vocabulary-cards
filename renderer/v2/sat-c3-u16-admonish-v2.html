What It Means

Admonish means to scold someone for a fault in a firm but measured way.

See the word in context

The lifeguard admonished the boys for running on the wet tiles — once, quietly, and with the whistle still around her neck.

When the intern forwarded the draft outside the team, the editor admonished her behind a closed door: two firm sentences, a reminder of the rule, and back to work.

Admonish sits between a reminder and a scolding. It is firmer than a hint but calmer than berating: measured correction, usually from someone with the standing to give it — judges, editors, referees — and it aims to fix conduct, not to wound.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    caution: To caution is to warn someone about a danger or misstep before it happens, with no blame attached. A reminder can only re-tell what someone already knows; a caution can be brand-new information. And where caution looks forward, admonish looks back — it corrects, firmly but calmly, a line already crossed.

    reprimand: A reprimand is a formal, on-the-record scolding — the kind that ends up in a personnel file, and it is harsher than admonish. To admonish is milder and usually private: firm words meant to fix conduct, not to punish it. A manager admonishes you in the hallway; a board reprimands you in writing.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    praise: express approval of someone's conduct

    commend: praise formally, often to a superior

    Where it sits · how much heat the correction carries

    remind → admonish → scold

    remind vs. admonish: A reminder carries no blame at all — milder than admonish, which says plainly that a line was crossed.

    scold vs. admonish: Scold goes further than admonish: there is open anger in a scolding, where admonish stays level and measured.

    Word Choice

    formal / measured

    Admonish suits authority exercised with restraint — judges, doctors, referees. Within this unit's scolding family, chide is gentler, chastise harsher and more official, and berate louder and longer; admonish is the firm middle voice.
