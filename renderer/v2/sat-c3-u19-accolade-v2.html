Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Accolade means high praise, special distinction.

See the word in context

Being asked to carry the guild banner at the harvest parade is the highest accolade the growers can give one of their own.

At shift change, the head nurse pinned the little silver bee — the ward's oldest accolade — onto Amara's pocket, while two decades of past recipients clapped from the doorway.

An accolade is not just praise; it is praise made official — a nameable honor: a medal, a title, a prize. You can count accolades, and someone with standing has to confer each one. Acclaim (this unit) is the broad wash of public approval; an accolade is one bright drop of it, handed over with your name attached.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    honor: An honor is any mark of respect — an invitation to speak, a seat at the head table, a flag flown for you. An accolade is narrower: praise for merit made official and nameable. Every accolade is an honor, but many honors involve no praise at all.

    distinction: Distinction is the standing that sets you above your peers, built up over years and carried wherever you go — nobody hands it over in a box. An accolade is the opposite kind of thing: a single conferred token, the medal, the title, the prize presented on a particular day.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    insult: a direct blow to someone's dignity

    snub: the honor pointedly withheld — being passed over

    Where it sits · how high the recognition ranks

    mention → award → accolade

    mention vs. accolade: An honorable mention is the smallest coin of recognition — real, but a long way below an accolade.

    award vs. accolade: An award is solid recognition, yet accolade reaches higher still: it names the crowning distinction a whole field looks up to.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal

    Use accolade for a single, nameable honor — a medal, a title, a standing ovation with your name on it. It is more formal than award and far grander than a compliment. Beside its unit-mates: acclaim is the general roar of approval, plaudits are the bursts of praise, and an accolade is the trophy you can set on a shelf.
