prescient

Meaning

What It Means

Prescient means showing knowledge of events before they happen; able to foresee what is coming.

See the word in context

Her 2005 essay warning that phones would reshape childhood now looks remarkably prescient.

The city planner's prescient decision to reserve land for flood channels seemed wasteful at the time, but it spared thousands of homes when the record storms finally came.

The planner acted as if she already knew the floods were coming, and events proved her right — accurately foreseeing the future is exactly what prescient describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    visionary: A visionary imagines bold possible futures and works to bring them about — visionary founders, visionary architects. The claim is about imagination and ambition. Prescient is a verdict passed in hindsight: events proved the person right. A visionary dreams what could come; a prescient person turns out to have known what did.

    clairvoyant: Clairvoyant claims an outright supernatural power to see what cannot be seen — the word of psychics and crystal balls. Prescient stays inside the natural world: it credits insight so sharp that someone seemed to know what was coming, and we usually award it after events prove them right.

    shrewd: Shrewd means sharp and practical about the situation in front of you — a shrewd negotiator reads the room, the market, the moment. It is sharpness about the present. Prescient reaches past the present entirely: it credits someone with apparent knowledge of events that had not yet happened.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    shortsighted: failing to think ahead

    oblivious: unaware of what is happening or coming

    Where it sits · how surely what's coming is known

    farsighted → prescient → prophetic

    farsighted vs. prescient: Farsighted means planning sensibly for the future; prescient is stronger, suggesting someone seemed to actually know what was coming.

    prophetic vs. prescient: Prophetic goes beyond prescient — it implies foretelling the future with almost supernatural, oracle-like accuracy.

      Word Choice

      Formal / journalistic

      Prescient is usually a compliment paid in hindsight: we call a warning, essay, or decision prescient only after events prove it right. Common collocations: 'remarkably prescient,' 'a prescient warning,' 'proved prescient.'
