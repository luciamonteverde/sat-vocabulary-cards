deplete

/dih-PLEET/ - rhymes with complete

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Deplete means to reduce greatly the amount or supply of something; to use up.

See the word in context

By February the flu season had depleted the district's roster of fill-in teachers.

The homestead's yield book tells the story in three numbers: forty bushels an acre in 1900, thirty by 1930, eleven the year the family quit. Wheat after wheat after wheat had depleted the topsoil's nitrogen — every crop borrowing from an account into which nothing was ever paid back — until the field could no longer cover the cost of its own seed.

Deplete is the exact word for what those harvests did, because it means to reduce greatly the amount or supply of something — to draw a stock down toward empty without necessarily reaching bottom. Exhaust finishes the job; deplete names the drawing-down, which is why supplies, savings, and soils are its natural objects.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    drain: To drain is to draw a supply down the way liquid leaves a tank — and a drain can run all the way to empty. Deplete reports the level falling sharply without saying the tank is dry: harvests deplete soil, spending sprees deplete savings. Drain is the picture; deplete is the accountant's report.

    sap: To sap is to bleed strength away little by little — energy, morale, resolve. Deplete aims at stock instead of strength: supplies, savings, reserves, a roster. A long fever saps a patient; a long winter can deplete the woodpile.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    restock: fill the shelves back up

    refill: top the supply back to the line

    Where it sits · how much of the supply remains

    dent → deplete → exhaust

    dent vs. deplete: A dent is the first bite out of a stock — noticeable, and nothing more — far short of deplete, which reports the level drawn down toward empty.

    exhaust vs. deplete: Exhaust goes further than deplete — a depleted well still gives some water; an exhausted one gives none at all.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / quantitative

    Deplete wants a stock for its object: reserves, savings, soil, a roster. For a person worn out, say exhausted; for the last drop gone, exhaust or use up. Drain shares its picture but can finish the emptying; sap steals strength rather than stuff. Deplete is the accountant of the three — it reports the level falling.

Then the surveys began to agree: decades of record catches had depleted the cod stocks to a fraction of their old abundance. The fish were not gone. That was the strange difficulty of it — the nets still filled, just more slowly, and a good week could make the decline easy to doubt.
