paucity

/PAW-sih-tee/ - the stress falls on PAW

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Paucity means an amount of something so small it is strikingly close to none.

See the word in context

The museum's dinosaur hall is grand, but its paucity of actual fossils - four, by the docent's count - disappoints visitors expecting a herd.

The relief coordinator's inventory was blunt: nine hundred evacuees, forty cots. It was the paucity of blankets that worried her most, though - the forecast called for frost, and the shelf held eleven.

Paucity is 'too few' with the volume turned up: not merely 'less than we'd like' but strikingly, almost absurdly few. A shortage can be mild; paucity invites you to count on your fingers - forty cots, eleven blankets - and feel the gap.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    scarcity: Scarcity is the economist's neutral word for supply running short of demand, at any degree from mild to severe. Paucity is never mild: it insists the numbers are stark, strikingly close to none. A scarcity of taxis means a longer wait; a paucity means the street stays empty.

    shortfall: A shortfall is the measured amount by which a target is missed — it needs a number to miss. Paucity needs no target at all: it simply says that what is present is strikingly, countably small, whatever anyone planned or promised.

    dearth: A dearth is a serious lack of something needed — the word points at the unmet need. Paucity points at the count itself: not merely lacking, but strikingly, almost absurdly few. A dearth of volunteers means too few came; a paucity means you can name them all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    abundance: plentiful supply

    glut: so much that the excess itself becomes the problem

    Where it sits · how close to none the supply falls

    shortage → paucity → absence

    shortage vs. paucity: A shortage says supply has slipped below need and may recover next week; paucity is bleaker - strikingly, countably few.

    absence vs. paucity: Absence goes past paucity: paucity means almost none, while absence means none at all - even the last few are gone.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / academic

      Paucity is essay-and-report vocabulary, nearly always followed by 'of': a paucity of data, of witnesses, of options. In conversation, say 'hardly any.' Choose paucity over shortage when the fewness is stark and you want the reader to feel it, not just note it.
