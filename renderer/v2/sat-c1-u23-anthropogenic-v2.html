anthropogenic

/an-thruh-puh-JEN-ik/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Anthropogenic means caused or produced by human activity.

See the word in context

The sediment core showed a sudden anthropogenic lead spike beginning in the very year the smelter opened upriver.

Glaciologists drilling an ice cap read each annual layer like a page in a book. Layers older than 1750 contain sulfate only in the years of known volcanic eruptions. From 1850 onward, sulfate appears in every single layer, rising fastest through the decades when European coal use rose fastest and falling again after emissions laws passed in the 1970s. The team labelled that entire stretch of the record anthropogenic.

The sulfate rises in step with coal use and declines when emissions laws take effect, tying the change to human industry rather than to natural eruptions, which is exactly what anthropogenic means.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    human-caused: Human-caused is the everyday way of saying people brought a change about, and it fits any register. Anthropogenic is the scientific term for the same claim, the word of sediment cores and emissions reports, and both name only the cause of a change, never what the change is.

    man-made: Man-made describes objects people physically produced -- a man-made lake, a man-made fibre. Anthropogenic is broader and points at causation rather than manufacture: warming, noise, and runoff are anthropogenic because human activity drove them, though nobody set out to make them.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    natural: occurring without human involvement

    geological: produced by the earth's own long-term processes

    Where it sits · how directly people caused it

    human-influenced → anthropogenic → human-manufactured

    human-influenced vs. anthropogenic: Human-influenced is the milder claim, saying only that people shaped an outcome in part, while anthropogenic says the cause is human activity.

    human-manufactured vs. anthropogenic: Human-manufactured is narrower and stronger than anthropogenic, insisting something was physically built, while anthropogenic covers any human-driven cause, including emissions, farming, and land clearing.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      scientific and technical; neutral in tone

      Anthropogenic is an adjective of cause, and it almost always modifies a noun naming an effect: anthropogenic warming, anthropogenic noise, anthropogenic runoff. It says nothing about what the change is, only who caused it, which is why it often sits beside acidification in this unit but never means the same thing.

      The observatory on the ridge was built in 1908, when the valley below was farmland and the night sky above it was truly dark. A century later, astronomers there face a glow that no telescope filter fully removes. The valley's towns have multiplied, and their streetlights, parking lots, and signs scatter light upward all night. Researchers class this skyglow as anthropogenic: satellite images show it clinging to roads and settlements, brightening in step with population, while wilderness a hundred miles away stays black. No one set out to wash out the stars. The glow is a byproduct of ordinary life, which is precisely what the term captures. The observatory now publishes an annual brightness index, partly in hope that towns that can see the trend will begin shielding their lights.
