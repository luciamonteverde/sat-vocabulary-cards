flout

/FLOWT/ - rhymes with shout

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Flout means to disregard or disobey openly.

See the word in context

Patrons flouted the cafe's new no-laptop rule from day one, arriving with two screens apiece and asking for the wifi password.

The harbor posted new catch limits in April; by May the trawler crews had chosen to flout them outright, stacking the extra crates in plain view on the public dock and daring the warden's office to come count.

To flout a rule is to break it in the open, with a shrug or a grin — the disobedience is the display. That separates it from evade or skirt, which work quietly around a rule, and from defy, which can confront authority without any mockery in it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    defy: To defy is to resist authority to its face — a stare held, an order refused, open resistance. To flout adds mockery to the openness: flouting a rule treats it as beneath respect, broken with a grin. Defiance confronts the rule-maker; flouting laughs at the rule.

    disobey: To disobey is simply to break a rule you were given — quietly or openly, the word does not care. To flout is disobedience turned into display: the rule is broken where everyone can see, and the showing matters as much as the breaking.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    obey: to follow the rule as given

    heed: to take a warning seriously

    Where it sits · how proudly the violation is shown

    skirt → break → flout

    skirt vs. flout: To skirt a rule is far gentler than to flout it — you slip around the edge and hope no one asks.

    break vs. flout: Break stops short of flout: any violation breaks the rule, but flouting breaks it in the open, with a grin aimed at whoever made it.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / journalistic

      Flout is for rule-breaking as performance — laws, curfews, conventions disobeyed where everyone can see. Do not swap it with flaunt, which means to show something off: you flaunt a new watch and flout the dress code. Its unit neighbor brazen describes the spirit in which a flouter operates.
