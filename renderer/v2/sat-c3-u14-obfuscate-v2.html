obfuscate

/OB-fuh-skayt/ - the stress falls on OB

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Obfuscate means to make something deliberately hard to understand.

See the word in context

The professor accused the textbook of obfuscating: a rule any coach could teach in a minute took the authors nine symbol-choked pages.

When the collector asked where the atlas had been acquired, the dealer obfuscated — an anecdote about Lisbon, a digression on paper mills, a name that changed twice — until closing time arrived with the question still standing.

Obfuscate is deliberate fog. Blurring softens a detail, and muddling confuses by accident; to obfuscate is to bury the clear thing on purpose — under jargon, digressions, and qualifications — so that pursuit gives up before it arrives.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    muddy: Muddy is the informal cousin of obfuscate — stirring silt into water that was clear a moment ago. Anyone can muddy an issue with one careless sentence; to obfuscate is to build the fog deliberately and at length, under jargon and digressions. Muddy belongs to the kitchen table, obfuscate to the courtroom.

    befog: To befog is to wrap a subject in mist rather than burying it — the outline stays visible, just softened. Obfuscate goes further: it piles on jargon, digressions, and qualifications until no picture forms at all, and it always accuses the fog-maker of acting on purpose.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    clarify: clears the fog and shows the thing plainly

    spell out: state a thing step by step, leaving nothing implied

    Where it sits · how deliberate the confusion is

    blur → muddle → obfuscate

    blur vs. obfuscate: Blur is far milder than obfuscate — a blurred explanation softens the edges of one detail, often by accident.

    muddle vs. obfuscate: Muddle stops short of obfuscate: a muddled speaker confuses you without meaning to, while an obfuscating one builds the fog on purpose.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / critical

      Obfuscate is an accusation of intent — it says the fog was manufactured. Confusing writing may earn 'muddled'; only writing meant to defeat the reader earns obfuscate. Its unit-mates divide the territory: misrepresent plants a false picture, while obfuscate makes sure no picture forms at all.
