Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Parable means a short, simple story that teaches a moral lesson.

See the word in context

The recycling drive's poster is a one-panel parable: the village that shared one well never thirsted; the village that dug nine drank sand.

The safety trainer ends every session with the same parable — a sailor who patched the small leak the day he noticed it, and his dockmate who kept meaning to. She spells the lesson out, twice, because on an oil platform she would rather be didactic than subtle.

A parable is a short, simple story built to carry one moral lesson, usually with people in it — talking animals belong to fable. It differs from allegory in scale and function: an allegory sustains its second meaning across a whole work, while a parable makes its one point and stops.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    teaching story: A teaching story is any story told to carry a lesson — the label is broad and casual, happy to cover a rambling classroom anecdote. A parable is the tighter craft term: short, simple, human-scale, built to make one moral point and stop. Every parable is a teaching story; only the compact, pointed ones are parables.

    moral tale: A moral tale is a tale whose ending points at a rule for living, and it may run long and decorate freely on the way there. A parable keeps stricter discipline: one small, plain story carrying one lesson, nothing extra. Every parable is a moral tale; only the spare, single-point ones earn the name parable.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    plain statement: the lesson said outright, with no story around it

    idle yarn: a story spun purely for fun, pointing at nothing

    Where it sits · how much narrative carries the lesson

    proverb → parable → allegory

    proverb vs. parable: A proverb is slighter than a parable — one polished line of inherited wisdom, with no story built around it.

    allegory vs. parable: An allegory goes further than a parable: a parable tells one small story for one lesson, while an allegory threads its second meaning through an entire work.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / literary

    Parable is the word for a compact story with one moral aim — human-scale, often plain. If the cast is talking animals, fable is the sharper label; if the hidden meaning runs the length of a whole work, you want allegory; and a story told purely for delight is neither.

    When clients ask Ruth Okafor whether they should chase a hot stock, the retirement planner does not answer with charts. She answers with a parable she wrote years ago, and it takes her thirty seconds to tell. Two farmers find the same fast river. One builds a wheel to grind grain all year; the other dives for the gold he is sure the current carries. The diver drowns in spring. The miller eats in winter. That is the whole story: no names, no subplots, one point. Clients remember it, she says, precisely because it is small — a story short enough to carry, sharp enough to sting.
