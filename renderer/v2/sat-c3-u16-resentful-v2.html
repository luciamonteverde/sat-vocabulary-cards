Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Resentful means full of bitter indignation at being treated unfairly — nursing the sting of a wrong done to you and letting it color how you act.

See the word in context

Omar grew resentful of the weekend shifts that somehow always landed on him, saying nothing at the meetings but shutting the schedule drawer a little harder each Friday.

Passed over for the solo she had rehearsed all winter, the second violinist turned resentful: she still played every note beautifully, but she stopped sharing her bowings with the newcomer and left the hall the moment rehearsal ended.

Nothing erupts here — no shouting, no scene. Resentful names the quieter injury: the settled, smoldering sense of having been treated unfairly, aimed at a particular wrong and at whoever dealt it, and leaking out in withheld favors and early exits rather than open anger.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    bitter: Bitter is the broader, harder word — a person can turn bitter about life itself, with no single wrong left to point to. Resentful stays aimed: it smolders at a particular unfairness and at whoever dealt it, and it can still fade once the wrong is put right.

    aggrieved: Aggrieved means feeling wronged, often with a formal edge — the aggrieved party in a lawsuit has a stated injury. Resentful adds the brooding: not just registering the wrong but nursing it, replaying it, letting it sour every later dealing.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    forgiving: ready to let a wrong go without holding it against anyone

    content: satisfied with one's share, nursing no complaint

    Where it sits · how deep the sense of being wronged has sunk

    annoyed → resentful → embittered

    annoyed vs. resentful: Annoyed is far milder than resentful: annoyance flares at an inconvenience and fades by dinner, while resentful means the sense of being wronged has settled in to stay.

    embittered vs. resentful: Embittered goes further than resentful: a resentful person still nurses one particular wrong, but an embittered one has been soured all the way through, until the bitterness outlives its causes.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      everyday / psychological

      Resentful is the adjective for a person still carrying a wrong: it takes of, at, or toward, and the grammar wants a culprit — you are resentful of the decision, at the committee, toward the favorite. Keep it apart from envious, which merely wants what another has; resentful believes something was taken or denied unfairly. And where rancor (also in this unit) names the old, banked bitterness itself, resentful describes the person the bitterness lives in while it is still warm.
