exacerbate

/ig-ZAS-er-bayt/ - the stress falls on ZAS

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Exacerbate means to make an existing problem worse or more intense.

See the word in context

Scratching only exacerbates the rash, carrying the itch from wrist to elbow by nightfall.

The point guard felt his hamstring tighten during warm-ups and played anyway. Every sprint exacerbated the strain, every hard cut asked the muscle to do what it could not, and by halftime an injury that needed a week of rest had become a tear that needed a surgeon. The trainer's log spent five words on it: injury present, play made it worse.

Exacerbate is the exact word for what the minutes on court did, because it means to make an existing problem worse or more intense — and existing is the load-bearing idea. You cannot exacerbate a hamstring that was sound; the word requires trouble present first, then names the push that sharpens it. Worsen is the loose everyday verb; trigger starts something new; exacerbate pours vinegar into a cut already open.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    worsen: Worsen is the plain, everyday verb: anything at all can worsen, on its own or with help, whether or not trouble existed before. Exacerbate is choosier — it insists a problem was already there and names the push that made it sharper. Weather worsens; a curfew exacerbates a shortage.

    stoke: To stoke is to feed something so it flares — a fire, a fear, a rumor — and it works even on small embers. To exacerbate is to sharpen a problem already burning in the open. Stoking feeds a thing fuel; exacerbating makes existing trouble crueler.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    soothe: to calm it down

    ease: to make it milder

    Where it sits · how badly the situation is set back

    hamper → exacerbate → cripple

    hamper vs. exacerbate: Hamper is the mildest setback — a hampered effort is slowed and inconvenienced but still on course, well short of the sharpened trouble exacerbate names.

    cripple vs. exacerbate: Cripple goes further than exacerbate — an exacerbated supply line limps along in worse shape than before; a crippled one stops moving at all.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / analytic

    Exacerbate needs a problem already on the table — you exacerbate a shortage, a strain, a feud, never a blank slate. Trigger and cause start things; worsen is the plain verb when register does not matter. In this unit's terms: a blight destroys on its own, and careless handling exacerbates what the blight began.
