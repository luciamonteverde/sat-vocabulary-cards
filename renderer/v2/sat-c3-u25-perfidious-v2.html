Meaning

What It Means

Perfidious means treacherously disloyal.

See the word in context

Every spy thriller needs its perfidious insider — the smiling colleague with a second phone.

For three seasons the cycling domestique carried water bottles, blocked wind, and toasted his team leader at every banquet; on the decisive mountain stage he was revealed as perfidious—he had been feeding the leader's training data to a rival squad since spring.

Perfidious is darker than 'disloyal' because the betrayal happens under a mask of continuing loyalty—the perfidious friend keeps smiling at your table while quietly working against you.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    treacherous: Treacherous means betraying a trust — the guard who opens the gate to the enemy. Perfidious sharpens the charge: the perfidious traitor keeps performing loyalty the whole time, smiling at your table while working against you. Treachery names the betrayal itself; perfidious stresses the false show of faithfulness kept up meanwhile.

    traitorous: Traitorous is the word for betraying a country or public cause one is sworn to — it lives in politics and war. Perfidious is wider and more personal: any deep trust betrayed under a mask of continuing loyalty, by a friend, a manager, or an ally as easily as a soldier.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    loyal: faithful to the people and causes one is bound to

    faithful: keeping trust steadily, without wavering

    Where it sits · how deep the betrayal of trust cuts

    unreliable → disloyal → perfidious

    unreliable vs. perfidious: Unreliable stops far short of perfidious — the unreliable friend forgets your birthday, not your trust.

    disloyal vs. perfidious: Disloyal stops short of perfidious — disloyalty drifts away from you, while perfidy smiles at your table and sells your secrets.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / literary

      Perfidious is the storyteller's word for betrayal from inside a trust — sworn guards, old friends, allies. 'Disloyal' covers everyday lapses; save perfidious for treachery wearing loyalty's uniform. Its unit-mate mendacious accuses lying words; perfidious accuses a false heart.
