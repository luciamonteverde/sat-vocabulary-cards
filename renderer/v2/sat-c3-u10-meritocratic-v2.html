Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Meritocratic means rewarding ability and achievement rather than wealth or birth.

See the word in context

The climbing gym is quietly meritocratic: route-setters are chosen from whoever tops the monthly competition, members and staff alike.

The astronomy club's telescope time is allocated on meritocratic terms — the best observation proposal wins the dark-sky weekend, whether it comes from a professor or a ninth grader.

Meritocratic is more precise than 'fair' because fairness only promises no cheating; a meritocratic system goes further and names what does decide — measured ability and achievement, rather than wealth, birth, or friends in the right rooms.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    merit-based: Merit-based is the plain, everyday label for the same idea: rewards follow demonstrated ability. Meritocratic means nothing more, but it is the analyst's word — the one essays use to characterize a whole system of exams, promotions, or admissions. Choose by register; the meaning is shared.

    evenhanded: Evenhanded means playing no favorites — every entrant faces the same treatment and the same scrutiny. Meritocratic goes further and names what decides once favoritism is gone: measured ability and achievement. A coin flip is perfectly evenhanded, but only a contest of skill can be meritocratic.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    nepotistic: hands the good jobs to relatives and favorites

    dynastic: passes position down bloodlines, earned or not

    Where it sits · how much ability alone decides the outcome

    open → fair → meritocratic

    open vs. meritocratic: An open contest lets anyone enter — a low bar that stops far short of meritocratic, since the door can be open while connections still pick the winner.

    fair vs. meritocratic: A fair process bans cheating and favoritism, but it stops short of meritocratic until one more thing is true: that measured ability is what actually decides.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / analytical

      Meritocratic evaluates systems — exams, promotions, auditions — so it lives in essays and editorials. 'Fair' praises the absence of cheating; meritocratic makes the stronger claim that ability alone decides. Keep its unit-mates straight by their first roots: a plutocratic system answers to wealth, a theocratic one to religious authority, an oligarchic one to a small circle — and a meritocratic one, at least on paper, to skill.
