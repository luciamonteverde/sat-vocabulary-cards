Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Indomitable means impossible to defeat or discourage.

See the word in context

Losing her sight at fifty slowed Judge Rivera for exactly one season; the indomitable jurist learned braille within the year and never missed another term of court.

A hand injury ended cellist Ana Marin's concert career at thirty. She retrained as a conductor, was turned away from two academies for lacking experience, founded her own chamber orchestra instead, and sold out its first season. Critics who once pitied her now reach for one word: indomitable.

Indomitable is built on 'cannot be tamed': it describes a spirit that defeat cannot break and discouragement cannot reach. Tireless people keep working; indomitable people keep coming back.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unconquerable: Unconquerable says no outside force can defeat you - the fortress, the army, the champion team. Indomitable moves the claim inside: a spirit that defeat cannot discourage, whatever the scoreboard says. An unconquerable team never loses; an indomitable one can lose repeatedly and come back undiminished.

    invincible: Invincible means unbeatable in the contest itself - the champion no rival can touch. Indomitable makes no promise about winning: it describes the will, not the record. Invincible people never taste defeat; indomitable people taste it and refuse to let it discourage them.

    unstoppable: Unstoppable describes motion and momentum - once something is moving, nothing halts it. Indomitable is about what happens after a stop: knocked down, turned away, rejected, the indomitable spirit simply cannot be discouraged. Unstoppable never meets the wall; indomitable hits it and comes back.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    submissive: yielding readily to others' will

    defeatist: expecting and accepting defeat

    Where it sits · how unbreakable the will is

    persistent → tenacious → indomitable

    persistent vs. indomitable: A persistent salesman calls back twice; indomitable belongs to people whom no string of failures can discourage at all.

    tenacious vs. indomitable: Tenacious grips hard and refuses to let go, but even tenacity can be worn down; indomitable says the spirit cannot be broken, period.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal, admiring

    Indomitable is the strongest refusal-to-quit word in this unit and always admiring. Resolute praises firmness about a decision; assiduous praises unflagging care; indomitable goes further than either, claiming that no defeat can discourage the person at all. Save it for spirits, wills, and characters - not for mild stubbornness.
