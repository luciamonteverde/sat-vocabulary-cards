ratification

/rat-uh-fih-KAY-shun/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Ratification means the act of formally approving an agreement, treaty, or set of rules so that it becomes official and legally binding.

See the word in context

The constitutional amendment sat unfinished for years until ratification by three-quarters of the states finally made it law.

The student council's new constitution was only a proposal until a schoolwide vote delivered its ratification in the final week of classes.

The document existed before the vote, but it had no official power. Ratification names the formal group approval that switched it from a draft into binding rules.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    confirmation: A confirmation is an official statement that something is accepted, true, or settled — a senate confirms a judge, a clerk confirms a booking. Ratification does more than state acceptance: it is the formal vote that gives a drafted document binding legal force.

    authorization: Authorization is official permission for someone to act — it points forward, licensing steps not yet taken. Ratification points at a finished text: the formal approval that turns an already-written treaty, charter, or amendment into binding law.

    validation: Validation is the act of declaring something officially acceptable or properly done — a parking stamp, a checked form, an approved result. Ratification is reserved for founding documents: the solemn vote that makes a constitution, treaty, or amendment legally binding.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    rejection: a refusal to accept something

    veto: an official block that stops a decision from taking effect

    Where it sits · how much legal force it carries

    approval → ratification → enactment

    approval vs. ratification: A show of hands can signal approval, but only formal ratification makes an agreement legally binding.

    enactment vs. ratification: Ratification makes the new charter official; enactment goes one step further, putting its rules into daily force.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Formal — legal and political

    Use ratification for the official, usually voted, approval that makes a treaty, contract, constitution, or amendment legally binding — not for everyday agreement ('we ratified our lunch plans' would sound comically formal). The related verb is ratify. Ratification is the final step that turns a finished draft into a document with real force.
