quell

/KWEL/ - rhymes with bell

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Quell means to put down or calm a disturbance by force or authority.

See the word in context

Two senior stewards moved through the grandstand to quell the scuffle before it could spread beyond one row.

It took the head zookeeper four minutes to quell the chaos in the aviary — nets lowered, lights dimmed, one recorded owl call — before a single feather was harmed.

Quell is not gentle soothing: it comes with force or authority behind it. Disturbances are quelled from above — an uproar, a mutiny, a wave of fear — by someone with the standing to put them down.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    subdue: To subdue is to bring a person or group under control by force — the rioters, the intruder, the crowd itself are wrestled down. Quell points at the disturbance rather than the people: an uproar, a mutiny, a wave of fear put down by someone with authority. You subdue the mob; you quell the riot.

    quash: To quash is to stamp something out decisively with official power — an indictment, a subpoena, a proposed rule can all be quashed by whoever has the authority to kill it on paper. Quell belongs to live disturbances: riots, mutinies, panics put down while they are still moving. Quash ends measures; quell ends trouble.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    foment: to stir up trouble deliberately

    stoke: to feed a fire — or a feeling — until it grows

    Where it sits · how forcefully the trouble is ended

    calm → quell → crush

    calm vs. quell: Calming a crowd is gentler than quelling it — soothing words and patience, with no force or authority behind them.

    crush vs. quell: Crushing goes even further than quelling — a quelled disturbance is over, but a crushed one is destroyed so thoroughly that nothing dares stir again.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Quell pairs an authority with a disturbance: guards quell riots, captains quell mutinies, statements quell rumors and fears. Use 'calm' or 'settle' when there's no force in play, and quash — its chip on this card — when what's being killed is official, like a ruling or an indictment. In this unit's family, quell handles trouble already burning, while forestall stops it from ever igniting.
