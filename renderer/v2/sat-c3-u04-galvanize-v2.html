galvanize

/GAL-vuh-nyz/ - the stress falls on GAL

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Galvanize means to shock or stir someone into sudden action.

See the word in context

One glimpse of her rival's finished sculpture galvanized Rosa, who had been circling her own block of marble for weeks.

When the burst pipe flooded the archive, the loss galvanized the historical society: membership doubled in a month, and the digitization drive finished five years of scanning by spring.

Inspire and motivate can warm a person slowly; galvanize keeps its electric origin. It is the jolt — sudden, sharp, impossible to ignore — that flips people from stillness into immediate motion.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    electrify: To electrify is to thrill a crowd intensely — the gasp, the standing ovation, excitement that may never leave the seats. To galvanize turns that current into motion: the shock lands and people act at once. An electrified audience feels; a galvanized one moves.

    energize: To energize is to fill people with fresh energy and drive — a good night's sleep or a pep talk can energize a team for the whole season. To galvanize is sharper and sudden: one shock flips people from stillness into immediate action, right now.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    lull: to soothe into stillness or inaction

    demoralize: to drain the confidence needed to act

    Where it sits · how much energy the push delivers

    prompt → rouse → galvanize

    prompt vs. galvanize: Prompting someone is far milder than galvanizing them — a quiet cue that suggests action without supplying any of the energy.

    rouse vs. galvanize: Rousing is weaker than galvanizing — you stir people awake, but galvanizing sends the current through them: they don't just wake, they leap.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      vivid / journalistic

      Galvanize is the headline writer's favorite for the moment a sleepy public suddenly moves — a scandal galvanizes voters, a defeat galvanizes a team. Use 'motivate' for slow-burning drive and 'inspire' for lifted spirits without urgency. Where its unit-mate exhort works by cheering and goad works by needling, galvanize works by shock.
