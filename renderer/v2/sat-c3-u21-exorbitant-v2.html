/ig-ZOR-bih-tuhnt/ - the stress falls on ZOR

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Exorbitant means excessive, unreasonably high.

See the word in context

The beach kiosk wanted an exorbitant twelve dollars for a single scoop of ice cream, and somehow the line never shortened.

When the ferry broke down, the island's one taxi driver quoted stranded visitors an exorbitant two hundred dollars for the six-mile ride to the hotel — a figure with no relation to fuel, to time, or to any fair rate.

Exorbitant does more than say a price is high — it says the price has left the orbit of reason entirely, so far beyond fair that naming it out loud feels like an insult.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    steep: Steep is the informal word for a price that stings — what you mutter at the register before paying anyway. Exorbitant is the formal accusation: the number has lost any relation to fairness, and calling it that is a charge, not a grumble. A steep price still gets paid; an exorbitant one gets protested.

    outrageous: Outrageous is the all-purpose cry of protest at anything that violates decency — prices, behavior, decisions alike. Exorbitant is narrower and more exact: it belongs to prices, rates, and demands, and its specific charge is that the number sits unreasonably far past fair. Every exorbitant bill is outrageous; most outrageous things involve no bill at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    reasonable: priced about where fairness would put it

    affordable: within an ordinary budget's reach

    Where it sits · how far past reason the price

    overpriced → exorbitant → extortionate

    overpriced vs. exorbitant: Overpriced stops short of exorbitant — an overpriced ticket costs somewhat more than it should, while an exorbitant one has left the orbit of fair pricing altogether.

    extortionate vs. exorbitant: Extortionate goes further than exorbitant — it adds an accusation: the seller is treating your desperation as the price tag.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / indignant

    Exorbitant is the complaint you file in writing; steep is what you mutter at the register. Both mean overpriced, but exorbitant carries indignation and a claim of unreasonableness — use it when the number is not just high but indefensible.
