impede

/im-PEED/ - rhymes with speed

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impede means to slow or hinder the progress of something without stopping it altogether.

See the word in context

Fog impeded the search all night without ever ending it; the boats simply moved at a crawl between calls.

Halfway across the Atlantic, the clipper's log begins complaining about weed. Barnacles by the thousand had settled along her hull, each one a knuckle of drag, and together they impeded her: the same wind that had driven her nine knots in April now managed barely six. Nothing was broken. Nothing was blocked. The ship was simply towing a reef of passengers the rest of the way to port.

Impede is the exact word for what the barnacles did, because to impede is to slow or obstruct the progress of something — the voyage continues, dragging. A paralyzed ship would sit dead in the water, and a delayed one merely arrives late; impede names the friction itself, the going made heavy while the going goes on.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    bog down: To bog down is the mud picture of impeding: progress that labors on under the weight, wheels spinning and gaining a little less each hour. Impede is the wider, cooler verb — any drag at all, fog or paperwork or friction — the word an engineer's report uses where a complaint says bogged down.

    obstruct: Obstruct means to put a thing in the path — a fallen tree, a parked truck, a filed objection. Impede needs no obstacle at all: fog or friction can impede a voyage that nothing actually blocks. Obstruct names the thing in the way; impede measures the drag.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    hasten: clears the road and quickens the pace

    ease: takes the friction out of the going

    Where it sits · how much forward motion survives

    delay → impede → paralyze

    delay vs. impede: Delay is milder than impede — the shipment still arrives whole, just later than the paper promised; nothing about the going itself was made heavier.

    paralyze vs. impede: Paralyze goes further than impede — impeded traffic still crawls forward; paralyzed traffic stands dead where it is.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / technical

    Impede is the engineer's and referee's verb: progress continues, slower. If movement stops outright, you want halt or paralyze; if someone is bodily held, that is this unit's restrain; and repress is heavier still — force from above holding a thing down. Impede judges nobody. It just measures the drag.
