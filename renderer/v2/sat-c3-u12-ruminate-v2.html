ruminate

/ROO-muh-nayt/ - the stress falls on ROO

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Ruminate means to contemplate or reflect on something deeply and at length.

See the word in context

The chess master lost in June and ruminated on the same twelve moves until October.

For weeks after the interview, Amara ruminated on the one question she had fumbled — turning it over on the bus, over the dishes, in the dark — until she finally wrote out the answer she wished she had given and taped it inside her planner for next time.

Ruminate fits because the thought is deep, long, and returned to again and again — the mental version of a cow chewing its cud, which is exactly the word's root. Mull would be lighter; obsess would mean the thought had taken over. And unlike this unit's outward-looking verbs, ruminating needs no evidence but memory.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    mull: To mull something over is to turn it over casually, usually briefly — a choice weighed on the walk home and settled by dinner. Ruminate is the long version: the same question chewed slowly and returned to for days or weeks. Mulling passes quickly; ruminating keeps coming back.

    contemplate: To contemplate is to consider something steadily and calmly, in one sustained look, without the circling back. Ruminate adds the return trips: a question you ruminate on gets picked up again and again, long after a contemplative mind would have set it down.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    rush: to push past a question without sitting with it

    shelve: to set a question aside unexamined

    Where it sits · how much the thought takes over

    ponder → ruminate → obsess

    ponder vs. ruminate: Ponder is milder than ruminate: you weigh the question once, deliberately, and set it down, while ruminating circles back to it for days.

    obsess vs. ruminate: Obsess goes further than ruminate — the thought stops being chosen at all and starts running the person, crowding out everything else.

    Word Choice

    literary

    Ruminate is slow, inward, and repeated — thought chewed like cud, true to its root. Unlike scrutinize and interrogate, this unit's outward-pointing verbs, ruminating happens entirely behind the eyes. For quick everyday thinking, mull or think over is the better fit.

    Captain Iversen retired with a spotless record and one exception he never mentioned at parties. In 1987, with a storm building, he had turned his ferry back to port. No one drowned; nothing was lost; the passengers caught the morning crossing. And yet for years he would ruminate on that turn -- at the wheel of his car, in checkout lines, on the dock at low tide -- reopening the decision the way a tongue finds a chipped tooth. Had the storm really been that bad? The barometer said yes. Some nights he trusted it. Other nights he re-argued the whole crossing from memory. His wife pointed out, correctly, that he had been right. That was never the itch. Settled questions can still refuse to stay put.
