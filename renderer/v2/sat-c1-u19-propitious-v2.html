propitious

/pruh-PISH-us/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Propitious means favorable; showing signs that something about to be attempted is likely to succeed.

See the word in context

With calm winds and a rising tide, the harbor master declared it a propitious morning for the young crew's first sail.

A pastry chef had dreamed of opening her own shop for years. One spring, everything lined up at once: her lease buyout came through, a busy farmers market relocated to the block she liked, and the city announced a grant program for first-time food businesses. Reading these signs, she judged the moment propitious and signed the papers that same week.

Every sign pointed toward success for the venture she was about to attempt, which is exactly what a propitious moment is.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    favorable: Favorable means giving a good chance of success, and it covers anything that helps — favorable odds, favorable reviews, a favorable wind. Propitious is grander and more forward-looking: the signs themselves seem to line up, promising success for what is about to be attempted. A forecast is favorable; the morning of the launch feels propitious.

    auspicious: Auspicious means showing signs of future success, and it is the word for beginnings — an auspicious debut, an auspicious start. Propitious describes the conditions around an attempt: a propitious moment or season favors whatever is about to be tried, beginning or not. A first concert is auspicious; a third straight week of calm seas is propitious for the crossing.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    inauspicious: showing signs of likely failure

    ominous: suggesting that something bad is coming

    Where it sits · how strongly conditions point to success

    suitable → propitious → providential

    suitable vs. propitious: A suitable time merely works well enough, while a propitious time actively favors success, with the signs lining up in your favor.

    providential vs. propitious: Propitious conditions favor a good outcome, but a providential event seems almost heaven-sent, arriving exactly when it is needed most.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal, literary register

      Propitious describes conditions or timing that favor something about to be attempted (a propitious moment, propitious weather). Keep it distinct from fortuitous: a propitious sign points forward toward likely success, while a fortuitous event is simply a lucky accident that already happened, with no omen-like promise about what comes next.
