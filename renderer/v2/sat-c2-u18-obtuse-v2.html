Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Obtuse means slow to understand or catch on.

See the word in context

The sitcom runs on an obtuse landlord who hears 'the ceiling leaks' as small talk about weather.

For a week the interns assumed Bo was joking. Told the fire drill was 'a dress rehearsal,' he asked where the costumes were kept; handed a box marked THIS SIDE UP upside down, he rotated the sticker toward himself and kept walking. He isn't careless — he's obtuse: hints, irony, and arrows all land a beat late.

Obtuse is the exact word because it means slow to perceive or understand — the signal arrives and takes the long way in. Geometry uses the same word for the angle past ninety degrees — the corner worn round — and that's the picture: not a missing mind, but an edge rounded off.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    slow-witted: Slow-witted is the plain insult: it blames the wits themselves, claiming the whole mind runs slowly — arithmetic, directions, jokes, all of it. Obtuse is narrower and a shade kinder: the obtuse person can be brilliant elsewhere while hints, irony, and subtext take the long way in.

    thick-headed: Thick-headed is the schoolyard picture — a wall the message bounces off, blunt and jeering. Obtuse says nearly the same thing in adult, written English, and it alone carries the accusation of pretending: telling someone not to be obtuse means you suspect they understand perfectly well.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    quick-witted: the signal lands before the sentence ends

    sharp: the all-purpose opposite: edges intact

    Where it sits · how hard meaning is to get across

    distracted → obtuse → impenetrable

    distracted vs. obtuse: Distracted misses the hint only because attention is elsewhere — call the name and the point lands fine — while the obtuse listener is looking right at you and still takes the long way in.

    impenetrable vs. obtuse: Impenetrable goes further than obtuse — the obtuse listener gets there late; with an impenetrable one, the hint never arrives at all.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    critical / wry

    Obtuse criticizes uptake — hints, warnings, and subtext bouncing off. Keep it apart from its unit-mate facile: the obtuse listener can't see the point; the facile thinker sees one instantly and digs no further. And 'don't be obtuse' accuses someone of pretending not to understand — the word's most common live use.

    For two weeks the whole office worked around Dev. The party planning happened in plain sight, because Dev had a reputation for being magnificently obtuse about hints. Colleagues asked what cake he liked 'for a cousin's birthday.' They borrowed his guest list 'for a seating study.' He answered every question helpfully and suspected nothing. It was not that his mind was slow. Dev could quote contract clauses from memory and untangle a budget in an afternoon. Nor was he inattentive; he looked each schemer in the eye, listened closely, and missed the point anyway. When the lights came on and the room shouted his name, Dev laughed hardest of all. 'You could have just told me,' he said. 'Subtext takes the scenic route with me.'
