retrospective

/reh-truh-SPEK-tiv/

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Retrospective means looking back on or dealing with past events and situations.

See the word in context

The clinic's retrospective review of five years of patient charts revealed which treatments had actually helped.

In a retrospective essay for the paper's final issue, the graduating editor looked back on four years of late-night deadlines and hard-won lessons.

The essay examines events that already happened — four years of the editor's own past — which is precisely what makes it retrospective.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    backward-looking: Backward-looking means focused on the past, and it often carries a sniff of criticism — a backward-looking policy resists whatever comes next. Retrospective is neutral and deliberate: a retrospective review examines the past on purpose to learn from it. Backward-looking is an orientation; retrospective is an activity.

    reflective: Reflective means thoughtfully reviewing what has happened — a quiet, musing frame of mind a person falls into. Retrospective is more structured: it names a deliberate look back across a defined stretch of the past, like a report or exhibit. A person feels reflective; a review is retrospective.

    nostalgic: Nostalgic means feeling a bittersweet, affectionate longing for a past time — it names an emotion. Retrospective is cool and workmanlike: a retrospective report examines the past without necessarily missing it at all. Nostalgic is how looking back can feel; retrospective names the act of reviewing.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    forward-looking: focused on the future

    prospective: concerned with what is to come

    Where it sits · how deeply it engages with the past

    reminiscent → retrospective → elegiac

    reminiscent vs. retrospective: Reminiscent is gentler than retrospective — usually something is reminiscent of the past, evoking a few memories in passing, while a retrospective review deliberately examines a whole stretch of it.

    elegiac vs. retrospective: Elegiac is more intense than retrospective — an elegiac poem doesn't just look back at what happened, it openly mourns what is gone.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Formal / analytical

    This course teaches retrospective as an adjective ('a retrospective report,' 'a retrospective glance'). You may also meet it as a noun — 'a retrospective' is an exhibit reviewing an artist's whole career. Watch the near-twin retroactive: a retroactive rule reaches back to take effect from a past date, while something retrospective looks back to examine the past. Reviewing versus rule-changing — keep them separate.
