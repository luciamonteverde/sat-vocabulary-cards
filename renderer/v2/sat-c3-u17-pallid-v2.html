Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Pallid means lacking color.

See the word in context

The motel's pallid wallpaper had faded to the ghost of a pattern.

When the star's voice cracked mid-aria, the understudy in the wings went pallid — her cue was suddenly, terrifyingly real — and the stage manager pressed a water bottle into her shaking hand.

Pallid is the right word because her face lost the color it ought to have: fear drained it. Pale can be a natural, healthy fairness; pallid always implies color that is missing — from fright, sickness, or exhaustion.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    wan: Wan is the faint, drained look of long tiredness or illness, and it nearly always describes a person and hints at the fatigue behind the fading. Pallid is broader in what it can describe: color missing from anything that ought to have some — a face, a sky, old wallpaper — whether fright, sickness, or age did the draining.

    colorless: Colorless is the plainest statement of fact: no color is present at all. Pallid adds a judgment — the color is missing from something that ought to have it, drained by sickness, fright, or age. Water is simply colorless; a colorless face would be pallid, because a face should have some.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    ruddy: flushed with healthy red

    rosy: pink with warmth and health

    Where it sits · how drained of healthy color it looks

    pale → pallid → cadaverous

    pale vs. pallid: Pale can be natural and healthy — fair skin, a light sky; it stops short of pallid, which looks drained of color it ought to have.

    cadaverous vs. pallid: Cadaverous goes further than pallid — past unhealthy paleness to looking like the dead.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral to critical

    Pallid does two jobs: literally, color drained from what should have some — a face, a winter sky, old wallpaper; figuratively, feeble and lifeless, as in a pallid remake of a great film. Pale is neutral and can be lovely; pallid always diagnoses something missing. For shock-gray in an instant, ashen is the sharper word.
