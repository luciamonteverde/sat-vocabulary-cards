What It Means

Cleave means to divide something into parts.

See the word in context

The butcher's heavy blade can cleave a whole side of beef into matched halves with a single stroke.

At the quarry bench, the splitter set his chisel to the hidden seam and cleaved the slate block into roofing sheets, each tap opening a clean new face.

Cleave is more exact than break or chop because it names a clean division into parts, often along a natural line and often at one decisive blow — the pieces come apart whole, not in fragments.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    split: Split is the everyday word for dividing something along a line — you split firewood, a bill, a sandwich. Cleave is the vivid, slightly literary cousin: it suggests one decisive blow, often along a natural seam, leaving clean whole pieces. Anything cleaved is split, but cleave adds drama and precision.

    sunder: Sunder is literary and violent: to tear something apart, often people or bonds, and usually for good. Cleave is calmer and more exact — a clean division into parts, often at a single stroke along a natural line. Sundering wrenches things apart; cleaving splits them cleanly.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    join: bring separate parts together

    fuse: melt or merge pieces into a single whole

    Where it sits · how completely the object breaks apart

    crack → cleave → shatter

    crack vs. cleave: Crack is milder than cleave — a cracked block carries a line of damage but still holds together as one piece, while a cleaved one has come apart into clean, separate parts.

    shatter vs. cleave: Shatter goes further than cleave: not two clean halves but a hundred useless fragments flying apart.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      vivid / physical

      Cleave is vivid and slightly literary: reach for it when something is divided cleanly into parts at one decisive blow, or when a group splits for good. One warning — English has a second, unrelated verb cleave meaning to cling ('she cleaved to her beliefs'), so let context make clear you mean splitting. Compare truncate, this unit's word for cutting short: cleaving divides a thing into parts; truncating merely lops off its end.
