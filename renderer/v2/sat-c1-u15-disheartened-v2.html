disheartened

/dis-HAR-tnd/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Disheartened means having lost hope, courage, morale, or enthusiasm.

See the word in context

Missing the qualifying time by nine seconds left Amara so disheartened that she almost skipped her next training run.

Three months after opening, Tomas counted another slow morning at his bakery and felt thoroughly disheartened. He had imagined lines out the door, not trays of unsold bread, and it took real effort to preheat the ovens the next day.

Tomas has lost hope and enthusiasm after his expectations collapsed, which is exactly the deflated feeling that disheartened describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    discouraged: Discouraged means your confidence about succeeding has slipped - the task now looks bigger than your ability. Disheartened is a broader deflation: hope, courage, and enthusiasm all drain away together after a setback. A discouraged student doubts she can pass; a disheartened one has trouble caring enough to open the book.

    dejected: Dejected describes visible sadness - slumped shoulders and a downcast face right after a blow. It is about low spirits in the moment. Disheartened reaches forward: the setback has drained your hope and enthusiasm for continuing. You look dejected at the result; you feel disheartened about trying again.

    demoralized: Demoralized means morale has collapsed, and it usually describes a group ground down under sustained pressure - a losing team, an exhausted staff. Disheartened is the gentler, more personal word for one person's hope and enthusiasm draining away after a setback. An army is demoralized; a lone baker counting slow mornings is disheartened.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    encouraged: given hope or confidence

    hopeful: feeling optimistic about the future

    Where it sits · how far the spirits have sunk

    disappointed → disheartened → despairing

    disappointed vs. disheartened: A disappointed person feels let down for a moment, while a disheartened person has lost real hope and enthusiasm for continuing.

    despairing vs. disheartened: Disheartened spirits droop but can recover, while a despairing person has given up hope entirely.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      Neutral / everyday

      Disheartened is about morale: hope, courage, and enthusiasm draining away after a setback. Unlike disaffected, which means a person or group has lost loyalty to an authority and turned rebellious, disheartened carries no rebellion at all; a disheartened person may still be completely loyal, just deflated.
