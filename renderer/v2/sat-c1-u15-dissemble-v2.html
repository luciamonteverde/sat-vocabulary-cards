dissemble

/dih-SEM-bul/

Meaning

What It Means

Dissemble means to hide your true feelings, motives, or intentions behind a false appearance.

See the word in context

Though the diplomat privately distrusted the visiting officials, she chose to dissemble, greeting them with warm smiles and easy conversation.

When her brother asked why she kept sneaking off to make phone calls, Priya dissembled, claiming she was checking homework answers with a classmate. In reality, she was coordinating every detail of his surprise graduation party.

Priya hides her true purpose behind a false but believable explanation, which is precisely what it means to dissemble.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    feign: To feign is to fake one specific thing - you feign surprise, illness, or delight, putting on a single false display, and the word wants an object. To dissemble is broader and quieter: hiding your real feelings or intentions behind a whole false front, often across an entire conversation.

    dissimulate: To dissimulate is the closest twin: it also means hiding your true feelings behind a false appearance. Dissimulate stresses covering up what is really there, while dissemble is the more common word and covers both masking what is real and acting out what is false - the diplomat's whole warm performance.

    pretend: To pretend is the plain, everyday word for acting as if something false were true, and children do it openly in games. To dissemble is deliberate adult concealment: hiding real motives behind a composed, believable front. Pretending can be innocent play; dissembling is calculated cover.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    confess: to admit the truth openly

    avow: to declare something openly and honestly

    Where it sits · how far the dishonesty goes

    hedge → dissemble → defraud

    hedge vs. dissemble: Someone who hedges merely avoids giving a straight answer, while someone who dissembles actively wears a false front.

    defraud vs. dissemble: To dissemble is to hide your true feelings, but to defraud is to use deception to actually cheat someone out of something.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal

      Dissemble is usually used without an object, as in 'he dissembled when asked about his plans.' Do not confuse it with disassemble, which means to take something apart piece by piece.
