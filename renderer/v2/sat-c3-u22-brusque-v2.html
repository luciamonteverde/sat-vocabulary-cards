Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Brusque means short, abrupt, dismissive.

See the word in context

The concierge answered every question with a brusque wave toward the elevators, and guests wondered what they had done to offend him.

Reviews of the mountain guesthouse agreed on two points: the views were glorious and the owner was brusque — questions at breakfast got one-syllable answers, and requests for extra towels got the towels, flung, with no reply at all.

Brusque describes the surface of someone's manner — short, abrupt, dismissive — not what drives it. That keeps it milder than surly, which carries real hostility: a brusque host has run out of words; a surly one wants you gone.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    gruff: Gruff is roughness of voice and manner — a low bark that can hide a kind heart underneath. Brusque is about brevity, not sound: a brusque reply is short to the point of rudeness, however warm or smooth the voice that delivers it.

    offhand: Offhand means tossed out casually, without ceremony or much thought — an offhand remark can even be friendly. Brusque has a sting the casualness lacks: a brusque answer is short in a way that dismisses you, and it lands as rude.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    courteous: taking time to be polite

    warm: friendly and welcoming in manner

    Where it sits · how unfriendly the shortness is

    direct → brusque → surly

    direct vs. brusque: Direct is milder than brusque: a direct answer skips the padding but keeps its manners.

    surly vs. brusque: Surly is harsher than brusque — brusque is short with you; surly is short with you and hostile about it.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral

      Use brusque for a manner that is short to the point of rudeness — service-counter shortness, not open hostility. Its unit neighbor curt does the same work for a single reply (a curt nod, a curt 'No'); brusque more often paints the whole manner. When the shortness turns hostile, you have left brusque behind for surly.
