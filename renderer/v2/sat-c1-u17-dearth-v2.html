Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Dearth means a scarcity or lack of something.

See the word in context

City council members admitted that the dearth of affordable apartments was quietly pushing young families out of the neighborhoods where they grew up.

When the youth orchestra held spring auditions, forty violinists showed up but not a single oboist. Facing such a dearth of oboe players, the conductor joked that he might have to rewrite the entire symphony around the instruments he actually had.

The orchestra has plenty of some musicians but a serious scarcity of oboists, and that shortage of something needed is exactly what a dearth is.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    scarcity: Scarcity is the general condition in which something is hard to obtain at all: water is scarce in a desert, for everyone, all the time. A dearth is a serious, noticeable lack of something needed, felt in one particular place or situation rather than everywhere.

    paucity: Paucity means a smallness of amount or number, and it usually measures abstract things like evidence, ideas, or data. A dearth is the more urgent word: a serious lack of something needed. A study can show a paucity of data, while a region suffers a dearth of doctors.

    lack: A lack is the plain, neutral state of being without something, whether or not that absence matters much. A dearth is never neutral: it names a serious scarcity of something genuinely needed, one that people feel and complain about. Any missing thing is a lack; a dearth hurts.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    abundance: a very large, plentiful amount

    surplus: an amount beyond what is needed

    Where it sits · how little of it remains

    shortage → dearth → absence

    shortage vs. dearth: A shortage means there is not quite enough to go around, while a dearth is a serious, noticeable scarcity of something needed.

    absence vs. dearth: A dearth means very little of something exists, but an absence means there is none of it at all.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal

      Dearth almost always appears in the pattern 'a dearth of' followed by a noun, as in 'a dearth of evidence' or 'a dearth of options.' Despite growing out of the word dear, it has nothing to do with affection today; its natural opposite is a glut or surplus.
