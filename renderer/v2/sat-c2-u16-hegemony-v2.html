Meaning

What It Means

Hegemony means the controlling dominance of one power over the rest.

See the word in context

Nobody voted for English's hegemony over the sciences; laboratories in forty countries simply stopped publishing in anything else.

Nine of every ten questions typed anywhere on earth pass through one company's search box. It owns none of the newspapers, none of the shops, none of the encyclopedias it points to — and yet its hegemony is everywhere: headlines are rewritten to please its ranking, storefronts redesign themselves for its crawler, and a page it cannot find might as well not have been written.

Hegemony is the exact word for that position, because hegemony means the controlling dominance of one power over the rest — dominance, not ownership. A monopoly would have to own the market it rules; a hegemon rules markets it never bought, because everyone else has arranged their business around its lead.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    dominance: Dominance is the plain word for being on top, and it can be won one contest at a time — a team can dominate a season and be gone the next. Hegemony adds the standing arrangement: the others steer by the leader's lead and reshape their own plans around it.

    supremacy: Supremacy is the title for holding top rank — a claim about position, the name engraved on the trophy. Hegemony is the working arrangement underneath: one power setting terms that its supposed equals follow. Supremacy can be asserted in a slogan; hegemony shows up in everyone else's behavior.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    rivalry: a contest still undecided — nobody yet sets the terms

    equal footing: powers facing each other at the same height

    Where it sits · how far influence hardens into rule

    leverage → hegemony → empire

    leverage vs. hegemony: Leverage is far short of hegemony — strength for one negotiation, a good hand in a single game, spent when the deal closes; hegemony is a standing position the whole field steers by.

    empire vs. hegemony: Empire goes further than hegemony — the hegemon's rivals keep their own flags and follow its lead; an empire hauls the flags down and rules directly.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / academic

    Hegemony belongs to history seminars and foreign-affairs columns: one power, many followers, no purchase receipt. Dominance is the everyday word; supremacy the trophy word. Inside this unit, keep the lanes: centralization gathers control within one system, a despot is one person holding it, and hegemony is one power towering over its supposed equals.

Its fleet had not won a race in years, and smaller companies still beat it on price along a dozen routes. The line issued no orders and needed none; the entire coast simply organized itself around the company's lead.
