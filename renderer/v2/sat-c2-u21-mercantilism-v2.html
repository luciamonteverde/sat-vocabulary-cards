What It Means

Mercantilism means an economic doctrine of maximizing exports and hoarding precious metals.

See the word in context

The colony's tobacco could sail only to home ports — mercantilism written into every bill of lading.

The strategy game's veterans all play the same opening. Sell your kingdom's wool to every neighbor, buy nothing back, slap harbor taxes on any rival's ship, and watch your treasury tokens stack while everyone else's dwindle. New players call it hoarding; the rulebook's designers, who studied their history, printed the strategy card's true name: Mercantilism.

Mercantilism is the exact word on that card, because mercantilism means an economic doctrine of maximizing exports and hoarding precious metals — trade as a contest the state must win, with gold as the score. It is not simple stinginess: the mercantilist kingdom trades constantly and eagerly, but only ever in one direction, out.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    economic nationalism: Economic nationalism is the modern textbook cousin — trade policy bent to serve national power, whatever the tools. Mercantilism is one historical recipe for it: exports forced up, imports squeezed, gold banked as the score. Every mercantilist state is an economic nationalist; the reverse is not true.

    gold-hoarding: Gold-hoarding is the blunt nickname for the doctrine's scorekeeping — the piled-up bullion by which mercantilism measures a nation's winnings. But mercantilism is the whole game, not just the score: the export drive, the squeezed imports, the taxed rivals. Hoarding names the trophy shelf; mercantilism names the contest.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    free trade: goods crossing borders untaxed, in both directions

    open markets: commerce steered by buyers and sellers, not the crown

    Where it sits · how closed the economy is to trade

    thrift → mercantilism → autarky

    thrift vs. mercantilism: Thrift is the household shadow of mercantilism — spend less than comes in and tuck away the difference; mercantilism plays that same hoarding instinct with a whole kingdom's trade.

    autarky vs. mercantilism: Autarky goes further than mercantilism — the mercantilist nation still trades busily so long as gold flows in; the autarkic one bolts the door on trade altogether.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      formal / historical

      Mercantilism belongs to history essays and economics classes, and it names a complete doctrine: exports forced up, imports squeezed, bullion banked as the measure of national strength. Tariffs alone are just protection; mercantilism adds the one-way flow of gold. Its unit-mates mark the other corners — laissez-faire sends the state home, corporatism seats industries inside it, consumerism keeps the households buying.
