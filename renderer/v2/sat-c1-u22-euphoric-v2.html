euphoric

/yoo-FOR-ik/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Euphoric means in a sustained state of intense elation and well-being, often almost dreamlike.

See the word in context

For a week after her first book was accepted, Dana walked around euphoric, grinning at strangers on the bus.

Back on Earth after six months on the space station, Commander Reyes kept catching herself smiling at nothing. Gravity felt like a gift, grocery stores seemed miraculous, and even a traffic jam delighted her. The flight surgeon told her that returning crews often spend their first weeks euphoric — riding a long, dreamy high that no single moment can explain.

Reyes's weeks-long, free-floating glow — attached to everything and nothing in particular — is the sustained elevated state that euphoric names.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    blissful: Blissful means full of serene, complete happiness -- calm, untroubled, wanting nothing more. Euphoric is also a lasting state, but it is a high: intense, soaring elation that colors everything around it. A blissful afternoon is peaceful and still; a euphoric week runs high on everything at once. Bliss rests; euphoria soars.

    exhilarated: Exhilarated means thrillingly energized -- heart pounding, senses sharpened, the jolt you get from a fast ski run or a cold plunge. Euphoric is a sustained, dreamlike glow of well-being that floats on for days. Exhilaration spikes with the moment that causes it and fades; euphoria lingers long after any single cause.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    miserable: deeply unhappy

    gloomy: dark and low in mood

    Where it sits · how high the elation soars

    content → euphoric → rapturous

    content vs. euphoric: Content is quiet satisfaction; euphoric is a soaring, intense high of well-being that colors everything around it.

    rapturous vs. euphoric: Rapturous pushes past even euphoric — joy so consuming that a person seems swept entirely away by it.

      Word Choice

      clinical-to-literary; describes a sustained state

      Euphoric describes a lasting, dreamlike high, while ecstatic (also in this unit) is a sudden, explosive burst of joy triggered by one specific piece of news — you're ecstatic at the moment you win, and euphoric for the week that follows.
