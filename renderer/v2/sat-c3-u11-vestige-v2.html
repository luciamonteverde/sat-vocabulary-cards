Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Vestige means a mark or trace of something lost or vanished.

See the word in context

The only vestige of the estate's formal gardens is a line of box hedge running straight through the parking lot.

Every October the schoolhouse bell rings at noon for 'harvest recess,' a vestige of the years when classes emptied so children could help bring in the crop. No working farm remains within twenty miles, but the bell still gets its holiday.

A vestige is a footprint — the word is Latin vestigium, a track. It is not a usable piece of the vanished thing, only the faint mark proving the thing once passed this way: a custom, a scar, a name on a map.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    trace: A trace is the faintest sign that anything was present — a trace of smoke, of an accent, of doubt — and the thing itself may still exist nearby. A vestige is a trace with a history: the mark left by something otherwise vanished, proof that it once passed this way.

    relic: A relic is an object from the past that people deliberately keep — in a case, a shrine, a drawer — often with reverence. A vestige is not kept at all: it is the accidental mark a vanished thing left behind, a footprint rather than a possession.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    entirety: the complete thing, nothing lost

    abundance: plenty of it, everywhere you look

    Where it sits · how little of the original survives

    leftover → remnant → vestige

    leftover vs. vestige: A leftover is far milder than a vestige — plenty still remains, and it is still perfectly usable.

    remnant vs. vestige: A remnant is weaker than a vestige — a remnant is still a real piece of the original, while a vestige is only the faintest mark that it ever existed.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal

    Vestige is precise about scale: the barest trace of something otherwise vanished — a vestige of an accent, of a custom, of a road. If a usable piece survives, call it a remnant instead. Science keeps the root honest: vestigial organs are the body's own footprints of its past.
