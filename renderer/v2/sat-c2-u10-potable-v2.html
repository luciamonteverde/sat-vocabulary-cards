Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Potable means safe to drink.

See the word in context

The trail map marks two springs but stars only the northern one as potable; the other is for soaking tired feet.

The hardware store sells two grades of hose, and the difference matters more than the price. The white one is rated potable, so the marina uses it to fill the boats' drinking tanks; the green garden hose leaches enough from its lining that its water is for washing decks and topping off the dinghy — never the kettle.

Potable is a certification, not praise: it says nothing about taste. Chalky well water can be perfectly potable, and a lovely-looking creek can fail the test by a mile. The word draws one line — safe to swallow — and stops there; for everything short of the line, English has usable, treated, filtered.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    drinkable: Drinkable is the everyday twin you'd use across a campsite — water you can drink, said casually. Potable is the same verdict in official dress, the word signs, plumbing codes, and ships' logs reach for when the ruling must be exact. Ask a friend if it's drinkable; ask the county if it's potable.

    purified: Purified reports a process: the water was cleaned by treatment — boiled, chlorinated, run through membranes. Potable reports a result, safe to swallow, however that result was reached. A mountain spring can be potable with no treatment at all, and sloppily purified water can still fail the test.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    contaminated: fouled by something harmful

    undrinkable: unfit to swallow, whatever the reason

    Where it sits · how fit the water is to swallow

    usable → filtered → potable

    usable vs. potable: Usable water will wash a deck or rinse a tool, and that is all it claims; potable sits well above it on this scale — water that has earned the ruling safe to swallow.

    filtered vs. potable: Filtered says the water went through a purifier, not that it passed the test; potable is the ruling itself — safe to swallow.
