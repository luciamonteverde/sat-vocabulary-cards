exponential

/ek-spuh-NEN-shul/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Exponential describes growth that multiplies rather than adds: because each gain enlarges the base for the next, the pace itself keeps accelerating.

See the word in context

One infected laptop became an exponential outbreak, the virus doubling its reach with every hour the office network stayed open.

A student posted a short tutorial that drew twenty views on its first day. Each viewer, on average, shared it with two others: forty views the next day, then eighty, then a hundred sixty — the opening arc of an exponential curve. By the end of two weeks the counter was jumping by hundreds of thousands a day — not because the video had changed, but because every new audience recruited a still bigger audience after it.

The growth multiplied instead of merely adding: each gain enlarged the base from which the next gain grew, so the pace itself kept accelerating — the compounding curve that exponential names.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    snowballing: Snowballing means growing larger and faster as it rolls, each gain feeding the next — the everyday picture. Exponential is the precise claim behind it: the rate itself keeps multiplying, so equal stretches of time bring ever-bigger jumps.

    meteoric: Meteoric means dazzlingly rapid and sudden, like a meteor's streak — one spectacular leap. Exponential says nothing about drama at the start: it can begin almost invisibly, and its signature is that the growth keeps compounding, doubling and redoubling.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    linear: increasing by the same fixed amount each step, never speeding up

    flat: holding level, with no growth at all

    Where it sits · how sharply the growth accelerates

    linear → exponential → explosive

    linear vs. exponential: Linear growth is weaker than exponential: a linear climb adds the same amount at every step, while exponential growth multiplies, so each step's gain outgrows the last.

    explosive vs. exponential: Explosive goes further than exponential: exponential growth compounds along a curve you can still chart, while explosive growth seems to detonate, the whole surge arriving at once.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    technical in math and science; often loosely emphatic in journalism

    Strictly, exponential describes growth whose rate multiplies as the quantity grows — doubling and redoubling. Journalists stretch it to mean merely 'very fast' or 'very large,' a looseness the SAT is likelier to test than reward: on the test, look for compounding, where each gain enlarges the next. Note the family's other job, too: an exponent of an idea is its public advocate — the one who 'puts it out' before an audience.

    Putting it all together

    A famous French riddle asks children to picture a lily pad that doubles in size every day until, on the thirtieth day, it covers the entire pond. On what day, the riddle asks, did it cover half? Most people guess the fifteenth. The answer is the twenty-ninth. The trap works because human intuition expects steady growth, and the pond is anything but steady: its growth is exponential, each day's gain doubling the day before's. For twenty-five days the lily patch looks harmless, a green coin on the water; the astonishing part arrives almost all at once, at the end. Ecologists use the riddle to explain why invasive species seem to appear from nowhere: by the time the problem is visible, the doubling has been running for weeks.
