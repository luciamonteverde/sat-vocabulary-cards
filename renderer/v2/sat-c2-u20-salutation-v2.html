What It Means

Salutation means a greeting, especially one that opens a letter.

See the word in context

Every letter in the tin box opens with the same salutation — 'Dear little brother' — even after fifty years.

The new consul's first cable home came back with one correction. Not the analysis — the salutation: she had opened to the foreign minister with the formula reserved for deputies, one honorific short. Her predecessor phoned from retirement to reassure her. Everyone does it once, he said; no one does it twice. The ministry keeps a whole desk that does nothing but check how letters begin.

Salutation is the exact word for what that desk checks, because a salutation is a greeting, especially one that opens a letter — the formal handshake a document performs before it says anything. A hello is any greeting at all; a salutation is the composed, calibrated one, and its closing twin is the valediction, the formal goodbye.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    hello: Hello is the greeting word at its plainest — a single word that works anywhere, from a hallway to a phone call. A salutation is the composed, calibrated version: the formal opening a letter or a ceremony performs. Every salutation greets; hello would be underdressed where one is required.

    welcome: A welcome is a greeting that also opens the door — it receives someone into a place or a group, and it cares about their arrival. A salutation opens a text or an occasion instead: the formal address before the substance. You give guests a welcome; you write readers a salutation.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    farewell: the closing counterpart

    send-off: the goodbye with ceremony added

    Where it sits · how formal the acknowledgment is

    nod → greeting → salutation

    nod vs. salutation: A nod falls far short of a salutation — the smallest acknowledgment there is: wordless, over in half a second, with none of the composed formality a salutation carries.

    greeting vs. salutation: A greeting goes further than a nod — friendly words exchanged in passing — but it stops short of a salutation, the composed, formal opening that letters and ceremonies require.

      Word Choice

      formal / old-fashioned outside letters

      Salutation is the letter-writer's and protocol officer's word; in speech it tends toward the tongue-in-cheek — 'Greetings and salutations!' Greeting covers everything casual. Its closing twin is the valediction — and mind the sound-alike trap: salvation is no relative at all.
