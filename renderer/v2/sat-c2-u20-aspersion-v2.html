Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Aspersion means a remark meant to damage someone's reputation.

See the word in context

One aspersion in a trade paper — 'convenient bookkeeping' — cost the firm two clients by Friday.

Nobody could fault Dr. Reyes's trial results, so the rival lab tried something cheaper: a conference-hall murmur that her sample sizes were 'curiously tidy.' No data, no named error — just an aspersion, shaped to make listeners squint at work they had trusted the day before.

Aspersion is the exact word for that murmur, because an aspersion is a remark meant to damage someone's reputation — it does not argue, it stains. A slur insults outright; an aspersion plants a doubt and lets the listener grow it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    smear: A smear is the blunt journalistic word for a reputation attack, and it usually names the whole coordinated effort — a smear campaign of planted stories and repetition. An aspersion is a single remark inside that kind of effort: one insinuation cast, one doubt left where a reputation used to be solid.

    slur: A slur is a one-word insult thrown openly to wound, and everyone in the room hears it as an attack. An aspersion works more quietly: it insinuates something to be doubted, so listeners do the suspecting themselves. The slur strikes in the open; the aspersion stains from the side.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    endorsement: a public vote of confidence

    praise: open admiration — the attack's plain opposite

    Where it sits · how seriously the words wound a reputation

    dig → aspersion → slander

    dig vs. aspersion: A dig is far gentler than an aspersion — a little elbow in conversation that stings for a minute and draws no blood, while an aspersion is aimed squarely at a reputation and leaves a mark there.

    slander vs. aspersion: Slander goes further than an aspersion — an aspersion plants a doubt; slander states a false, damaging fact outright, the kind courts get involved in.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / journalistic

    Aspersion nearly always travels in the phrase cast aspersions, and its target is a reputation. Slander makes a false factual claim; a smear is the whole campaign; an aspersion is one planted doubt. You met innuendo earlier in this course — innuendo is the sly wording; the aspersion is the damage it aims at. For ill will spoken as a curse, see this unit's malediction.

    Lena's water-purity sensor won the regional science fair on a Saturday; by Monday, half the school doubted she had built it. The trouble began with one sentence. As the judges pinned the ribbon, a rival's parent said, just loudly enough, 'Her uncle does work at the county lab, you know.' It was an aspersion, not an accusation — nothing anyone could rebut, only a seed of suspicion left to sprout on its own. It sprouted. Students repeated it; a teacher hesitated before congratulating her; the fair's committee quietly requested her notebooks. The notebooks showed eleven months of dated entries, and the committee apologized. The parent had never said Lena cheated — and had never needed to.
