penchant

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Penchant means a tendency, partiality, preference.

See the word in context

The station manager's penchant for rearranging furniture means no desk is ever where you left it on a Friday.

Chef Adeyemi has a penchant for smoked paprika — it turns up in the stew, the cornbread, and once, memorably, the lemonade.

A penchant is a standing pull toward something — stronger than a taste, more personal than a habit, and always aimed by 'for': a penchant for puns, for risky shortcuts, for paprika. It needn't be chosen, and its owner is often the last to notice it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fondness: A fondness is a warm liking you know you have — you would name it if asked. A penchant can steer you unnoticed: it is the standing tilt in your choices, visible to everyone but its owner. Fondness lives in the feelings; a penchant shows up in the pattern.

    weakness: A weakness is a fondness you indulge a little guiltily — you know the pull is there and you give in anyway. A penchant needs neither guilt nor awareness: it simply tilts you the same way, time after time, whether or not you ever catch yourself doing it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    aversion: a strong pull away from something

    dislike: the plain opposite of a liking

    Where it sits · how hard the desire pushes you

    liking → penchant → craving

    liking vs. penchant: A liking is milder than a penchant — pleasant when it's satisfied, quiet when it isn't.

    craving vs. penchant: A craving goes further than a penchant — a penchant leans you toward the paprika; a craving marches you to the store at midnight.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / polished

    Penchant dresses up 'liking' — use it when a leaning is habitual and worth remarking: a penchant for drama, for hats, for the last word. It always takes 'for.' A fondness is warmer, a weakness more indulgent, a craving more urgent; a penchant simply tilts you, again and again, the same way.
