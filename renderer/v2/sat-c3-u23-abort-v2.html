Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Abort means to give up on a half-finished project or effort.

See the word in context

When the crosswind gusted past the safety limit, the pilot chose to abort the landing and climb away for another circuit.

The mission director aborted the balloon launch at two minutes to release, with the envelope half filled, after the telemetry link dropped for the third time.

Abort is sharper than stop or cancel because it means ending an effort that is already underway — the launch had begun and was deliberately cut off mid-course, not merely rescheduled before it started.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    scrap: To scrap is to cancel something at any stage — even a plan still on the drawing board that nobody has begun. Abort is stricter: it ends an effort already underway, cut off mid-course on purpose. You can scrap what never started; you can only abort what is live.

    discontinue: To discontinue is to stop making or offering something, often winding it down gradually — a product line, a bus route. Abort is abrupt and mid-action: a live effort deliberately cut off at once. Discontinuing phases a thing out; aborting stops it dead.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    complete: carry an effort through to the finish

    resume: take a halted effort back up again

    Where it sits · how final the stop is

    pause → shelve → abort

    pause vs. abort: Pause is the gentlest stop, far short of abort — paused work waits a moment and then goes on where it left off, while an aborted effort is deliberately cut off mid-course, for good.

    shelve vs. abort: Shelve is milder than abort: a shelved project sits unfinished but alive, waiting for a better season, while an aborted one is ended for good, mid-course.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / technical

      Abort is the crisp word pilots, surgeons and engineers use for stopping something already in motion — an approach, a launch, a rollout. It insists the effort was live and was ended mid-course, on purpose. For casual plans, 'call off' sounds more natural, and scrap covers plans killed before they ever start — which abort, strictly, does not.
