disruptive

/dis-RUHP-tiv/

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Disruptive means causing disruption; disturbing the normal course or flow of something.

See the word in context

The month-long roadwork on Main Street was so disruptive that the bus company had to reroute six lines and print entirely new schedules.

Midway through the second act, a ringing phone and its owner's loud apology proved so disruptive that the actors had to stop the play and begin the scene again.

The interruption breaks the normal flow of the performance and actually forces it to stop and restart. Disturbing the ordinary course of an activity in this way is exactly what disruptive describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    disturbing: Disturbing means interfering with someone's peace, order, or concentration, like a disturbing racket from the apartment upstairs; in everyday use it also means troubling. Disruptive is more concrete: it breaks the normal flow of an activity, knocking a class, meeting, or performance off course.

    disorderly: Disorderly means breaking the normal order of things, as in disorderly conduct or a disorderly queue; it describes behavior or a state that ignores the rules of order. Disruptive aims at an ongoing activity: a disruptive thing interrupts what a group is doing.

    obstructive: Obstructive means getting in the way of progress, often deliberately, the way an obstructive official slows every request; it blocks movement toward a goal. Disruptive breaks the flow of what is already happening: a disruptive noise shatters a meeting's rhythm rather than blocking its goal.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    orderly: well-arranged and well-behaved

    peaceful: calm and free of disturbance

    Where it sits · how badly the normal flow breaks

    distracting → disruptive → chaotic

    distracting vs. disruptive: A distracting noise pulls your attention away for a moment, while a disruptive one actually breaks the flow of what a group is doing.

    chaotic vs. disruptive: A disruptive event interrupts the normal routine, but a chaotic one destroys order completely, leaving no routine at all.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Neutral / everyday and business

    Disruptive commonly appears in 'disruptive behavior' and 'disruptive noise,' and in business writing 'disruptive technology' can even carry a positive spin of shaking up an industry. Keep it distinct from divisive: disruptive means interrupting the normal flow or functioning of something, while divisive means causing people to take opposing sides.
