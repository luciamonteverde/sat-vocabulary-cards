conciliatory

/kun-SIL-ee-uh-tor-ee/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Conciliatory means intended to soothe someone's anger and bring a dispute to an end — describing a tone, gesture, or statement that reaches out to make peace.

See the word in context

After the product recall sparked furious reviews, the CEO sent a conciliatory email admitting the mistake and promising free replacements to every customer.

On the second night of the bunk feud, the camp counselor gave a conciliatory talk, praising both sides' loyalty to their friends and proposing a shared campfire to clear the air.

The counselor's words are aimed squarely at cooling tempers and ending the conflict — crediting both sides and offering a peace gesture — which is what makes the talk conciliatory rather than merely friendly.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    placating: Placating means quieting one particular person's anger in the moment, often by giving them whatever stops the shouting. A conciliatory move looks past the moment: it reaches out to settle the dispute itself, and it can come from either side of a two-way quarrel, dignity intact.

    peacemaking: Peacemaking is working to end a conflict between other people — a mediator's or a diplomat's role, standing outside the fight. A conciliatory word or gesture comes from inside the quarrel: one of the parties itself extends the olive branch.

    appeasing: Appeasing means buying calm by handing an angry party what it demands, and the word hints the yielding goes too far. A conciliatory offer seeks peace without caving in — it cools anger while keeping the offerer's own position and dignity.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    confrontational: seeking out conflict rather than calming it

    inflammatory: likely to stir up anger even further

    Where it sits · how far you bend to make peace

    polite → conciliatory → groveling

    polite vs. conciliatory: Polite is weaker: a polite reply is merely courteous, while a conciliatory one actively works to cool anger and end the dispute.

    groveling vs. conciliatory: Groveling is stronger: a conciliatory speaker offers peace while keeping some dignity, but a groveling one abandons every position and simply begs forgiveness.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      Formal — diplomacy, workplace, and news writing

      Conciliatory is not generic friendliness: it specifically describes a tone, gesture, or statement aimed at cooling someone's anger and ending a dispute — a conciliatory letter, offer, or handshake. The related verb, reconcile (taught elsewhere in this unit), names the broader act of restoring harmony; conciliatory describes the peace-seeking manner of one particular message or move.
