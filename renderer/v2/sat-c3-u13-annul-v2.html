annul

/uh-NUL/ - rhymes with dull

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Annul means to make void or invalid.

See the word in context

Because the officiant's license had lapsed years before, the county annulled all thirty weddings he had performed that spring.

The land office annulled the deed the moment surveyors proved the seller had never owned the parcel: in law the sale had simply never happened, and the buyer's money came back to the last cent.

Annul goes further than cancel: a canceled deal stops running forward, but an annulled one is declared void from the beginning — treated as though it never existed. Courts and officials annul marriages, contracts, elections, and results.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    void: To void something is to stamp it no-longer-valid — the ticket, permit, or warranty simply stops counting from that point on. Annul digs deeper: a court declares the thing invalid from the very beginning, as though it had never existed. Voiding kills the future; annulment erases the past too.

    quash: Quash is what a court does to a decision, order, or indictment — it forcefully throws the ruling out. Annul aims at the thing itself, a marriage or contract, and declares it void from the start. You quash what another authority decided; you annul what the parties made.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    uphold: to confirm that a thing stands and still binds

    ratify: to approve formally and give full force

    Where it sits · how far back the undoing reaches

    contest → overturn → annul

    contest vs. annul: To contest a result is much milder than to annul it — the challenge questions the result while it goes on standing.

    overturn vs. annul: Overturn stops just short of annul: an overturned ruling is reversed going forward, while an annulled one is erased as if it had never been.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / legal

    Annul is the word for officially declaring something void — marriages, contracts, elections, results — as if it had never been. Cancel merely stops a thing going forward. Beside its unit-mates: abrogate kills a law or pact by official authority; nullified reports that a thing's legal force is gone.
