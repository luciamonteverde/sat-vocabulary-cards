syndicalism

/SIN-dih-kuh-liz-uhm/ - the stress falls on SIN

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Syndicalism means the belief that workers' unions should own and run industry.

See the word in context

The cannery co-op's newsletter preached syndicalism plainly: the boats, the nets, and the packing lines should belong to the crews' own union.

In the port strike of 1911, syndicalism went from theory to demonstration: for eleven days the longshoremen's union ran the harbor itself — assigning berths, paying crews from union books, and turning the owners' managers away at the gate.

Syndicalism is specific among the -isms: not state ownership (that is socialism, from this unit's reference list) and not bargaining inside the owners' system (plain unionism), but the industries themselves owned and run by the unions of those who work them.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    socialism: Socialism puts industry in the hands of the state or the public at large. Syndicalism agrees the private owners must go but hands the keys elsewhere: to the workers' own unions, industry by industry, with no ministry above them. The two -isms quarrel over who inherits.

    collectivism: Collectivism is shared ownership in general — by a state, a commune, a co-op, any collective at all. Syndicalism is one specific version: the unions of the workers in each industry own and run that industry themselves, answering to no authority above.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    privatization: moves industry into private owners' hands — the reverse direction

    free enterprise: owners compete in open markets, workers hired by them

    Where it sits · how much control the workers claim

    consultation → unionism → syndicalism

    consultation vs. syndicalism: Consultation is the mildest claim — workers are heard before the owners decide; syndicalism would hand workers the deciding itself.

    unionism vs. syndicalism: Unionism stops well short of syndicalism: the union bargains over wages and hours inside the owners' system, while syndicalism would give the union the keys to the works.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    historical / political

    Syndicalism is a historian's precision tool: workers' unions owning and running industry themselves. Do not blur it into socialism (this unit's reference list), where the state or public owns, or into plain unionism, which bargains within the owners' system. The word names the boldest of the three claims.

    Putting it all together

    In 1912, delegates from forty trades met above a printing shop to argue about who should own the future. The older men wanted the state to buy the mills and mines; the younger ones went further in a different direction. Their program, syndicalism, held that each industry belonged in the hands of its own workers' union -- railways to the railwaymen, mines to the miners -- with no ministry over any of them. Skeptics in the hall asked who would settle disputes between industries if no authority stood above the unions. The delegates never fully answered, and the movement faded, but its one big idea -- workers running their own industries -- kept resurfacing for a century.
