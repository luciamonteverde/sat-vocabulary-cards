proliferate

/pruh-LIF-uh-rayt/ - the stress falls on LIF

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Proliferate means to increase rapidly in number — to multiply, with new ones appearing faster and faster.

See the word in context

Ten years ago the block had one coffee shop; today espresso bars proliferate on every corner, sometimes three to an intersection.

When the city dropped its food-cart licensing fee, carts proliferated overnight — four the first spring, forty by the next — until the health department begged for more inspectors.

Proliferate is more exact than 'grow' or 'spread': it says the number of separate things is climbing fast — four carts becoming forty — as if the things were breeding. One thing getting larger never proliferates; only a rising count does.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    burgeon: To burgeon is to grow and flourish quickly — a bud swelling into bloom. Burgeoning is about vigorous growth in size or strength; proliferate is strictly about number: new units appearing, the count climbing. A single business can burgeon; only many of something can proliferate.

    abound: To abound is to exist in great plenty — it names the crowded state after the growth has happened. Proliferate names the process itself: things abound once they have proliferated. If the count is still climbing, proliferate is the word.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    dwindle: shrinks steadily in number until little is left

    wane: fades and diminishes in size, strength, or number

    Where it sits · how fast the count climbs

    multiply → proliferate → mushroom

    multiply vs. proliferate: Multiply is milder than proliferate: numbers multiply at any pace, even a stately one, while to proliferate is to multiply with the throttle open — fast, everywhere, and hard to stop.

    mushroom vs. proliferate: Mushroom goes further than proliferate — what proliferates climbs steeply, but what mushrooms seems to double overnight, springing up whole before anyone has counted the first batch.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / wary

      Proliferate needs a head-count: many separate things multiplying, the tally climbing. One thing growing bigger or fiercer doesn't proliferate — it swells, spreads, or escalates. The word often carries a wary edge, used for things multiplying faster than anyone quite wants: committees, regulations, weapons programs.
