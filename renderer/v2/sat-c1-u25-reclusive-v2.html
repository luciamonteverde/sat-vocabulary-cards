reclusive

/rih-KLOO-siv/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Reclusive means living in deliberate solitude, avoiding the company of others.

See the word in context

The reclusive game designer skipped his own launch party, streaming a brief thank-you from his cabin instead.

Fans of the puzzle column knew their setter only as 'Quill.' Quill declined every interview, sent a lawyer to accept awards, and had prize checks mailed to a forwarding service. When a journalist finally traced the byline to a farmhouse at the end of a private road, the neighbors could only report that the owner bought feed in the next county and waved from a distance, maybe once a year. Quill was not hiding anything, they insisted — just deeply, contentedly reclusive.

Quill refuses interviews, ceremonies, and even neighborly contact by choice — with no wrongdoing involved — which is the deliberate solitude that reclusive names.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    solitary: Solitary states the neutral fact of being alone, whether chosen or not — a solitary lighthouse keeper may wish for visitors who never come. Reclusive adds the deliberate choice: a reclusive person arranges life on purpose to avoid the company of others.

    withdrawn: Withdrawn describes a pulling back that is often temporary and emotional — someone quiet and unreachable after a loss, frequently while still living among people. Reclusive is a settled way of life: deliberately organizing existence to keep human company away altogether.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    sociable: enjoying the company of others

    outgoing: eagerly seeking social contact

    Where it sits · how fully human company is shut out

    private → reclusive → hermitic

    private vs. reclusive: Private is weaker than reclusive: a private person simply keeps personal matters to themselves, while a reclusive one avoids human company itself.

    hermitic vs. reclusive: Hermitic is stronger than reclusive — a hermitic life is organized entirely around total isolation, while a reclusive person may still tolerate the occasional distant wave.

      Word Choice

      neutral; descriptive rather than judgmental

      Reclusive involves no secrecy or scheming at all — it simply means avoiding company. Contrast this unit's connive (secretly plotting with others) and furtive (acting in a sneaky, guilty-seeming manner): a reclusive person may be perfectly open about wanting to be left alone.
