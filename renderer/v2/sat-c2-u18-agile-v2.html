Meaning

What It Means

Agile means quick and nimble.

See the word in context

Backstage, the dresser stayed agile between scenes, ducking scenery and vaulting cable runs to reach the quick-change booth in forty seconds.

Rush hour tests the bicycle courier: cabs drift lanes without signaling, doors swing open, a bus noses in. Teo is agile enough to thread all of it — a feint left, a hop over the drain grate, a slide past the gap a mirror leaves — and still brake softly at the crosswalk.

Agile is the exact word because agile means quick and nimble — speed plus the lightness to change direction inside it. Fast alone is a straight line; agile is fast around corners. The word stretches to minds as well: an agile thinker pivots mid-argument the way Teo pivots mid-block.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    spry: Spry is nimbleness where you least expect it — the eighty-year-old who pops up from the garden bed unaided. Agile is the same lightness with no surprise attached, and it adds real speed: quick feet or a quick mind changing direction at pace. Spry earns a smile; agile wins the rally.

    quick: Quick is the speed alone — how little time a thing takes — and it can run in a dead straight line. Agile is quickness plus steering: the lightness to change direction without losing pace. A quick reply is merely fast; an agile one turns mid-argument the moment the question shifts.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    lumbering: heavy, swaying movement

    stiff: joints that fight every bend

    Where it sits · how freely the body changes direction

    swift → agile → acrobatic

    swift vs. agile: Swift is weaker than agile on this scale — an express train is swift, and it cannot dodge anything; agile adds the lightness to swerve inside the speed.

    acrobatic vs. agile: Acrobatic goes further than agile — the agile skater weaves the cones; the acrobatic one is airborne and upside down between them.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / physical

    Agile is for quickness with change of direction — bodies first, minds by extension. Its unit-mates divide the ground: deft is small motions of the hands, adroit is the tricky moment handled cleverly, adept is overall mastery. If the praise is about moving lightly at speed, agile is the word.

    The mountain goats of the Rockies spend their lives on ledges narrower than a bookshelf. Biologists who film them describe the same astonishing moment again and again: a goat bolting from a cougar does not simply run fast -- it turns. Mid-leap, it can plant two hooves on a knuckle of rock, swap directions, and land facing the way it came, all without losing speed. That is what makes the animal agile rather than merely fast: pace it can steer, on ground where one blunt straight line would mean a fall. Racehorses beat goats badly on the flat. On a cliff, where survival belongs to whatever can change course between heartbeats, the goat has no rival.
