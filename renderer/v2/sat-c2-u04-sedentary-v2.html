sedentary

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Sedentary means involving much sitting; settled and inactive.

See the word in context

Doctors classify long-haul trucking as sedentary work despite the miles: the body sits still while the odometer runs.

After the concert season moved online, the choir director noticed her days had gone entirely sedentary: podium to piano bench to sofa, four thousand steps on a good day where twelve thousand used to be. Nothing about her work had gotten easier — only stiller.

Sedentary is the honest word because it measures sitting, not character: it is not lazy. A surgeon bent over a microscope for ten hours is doing grueling, exacting work and having a perfectly sedentary day — the word counts hours in the chair, not effort.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    desk-bound: Desk-bound means kept at a desk by the nature of the work — the job itself pins you to the chair. It describes workers and their posts. Sedentary is wider: it counts all the sitting in a day or a life, at desks, in trucks, on sofas, however it came about.

    stationary: Stationary means fixed in one place, and it applies to things as readily as to people — a parked truck with the brake set, a weather front that refuses to move. Sedentary belongs to bodies and lifestyles, counting hours spent sitting; a machine can be stationary, but only someone with a chair can be sedentary.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    mobile: moving about freely

    roving: ranging from place to place by habit

    Where it sits · how small the range of movement gets

    inactive → sedentary → housebound → bedridden

    inactive vs. sedentary: Inactive just means not getting much exercise; sedentary is more specific and a shade stronger — the day is actually built around sitting.

    housebound vs. sedentary: Housebound goes beyond sedentary: the sedentary clerk still commutes and runs errands, while a housebound patient rarely passes the front door.

    bedridden vs. sedentary: Bedridden goes far beyond sedentary: a sedentary life is built around sitting by habit or by work, while the bedridden patient has no choice left and cannot leave the bed at all.

      neutral / technical

      Sedentary is the health report's word: it counts chair-hours without judging character, so prefer it to lazy when you mean sitting, not shirking. Its cluster-mate inert belongs to objects and total stillness — a sedentary analyst still types, travels, and talks; an inert machine does nothing at all.
