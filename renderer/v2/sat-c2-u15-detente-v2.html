Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Detente means an easing of hostility or strained relations, especially between nations.

See the word in context

After ten years of quarreling over the hedge, the two households settled into detente: the hedge stays, and the leaf blower keeps office hours.

For six years the two noodle shops on Perry Street fought like small nations: poached cooks, copied menus, a sign war that ended with one awning reading WE INVENTED LUNCH. The detente, when it came, was unwritten and instantly understood — separate closing days, no more hiring away each other's staff, nods across the street at the fish delivery. Nobody apologized, and the feud was not over; it was resting, which both owners could live with.

Detente is the exact word for that arrangement, because it means an easing of hostility or strained relations — an easing, not an ending of them. A truce stops the shooting; peace settles the quarrel; detente is the middle distance, where both sides breathe out and keep their guard half up.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    thaw: A thaw is the weather picture for relations warming — small gestures returning after a freeze, often unplanned and easily refrozen. Detente is the settled, deliberate version between longtime foes: both sides ease the strain on purpose and keep it eased.

    cooling-off: A cooling-off is a short, deliberate pause taken so tempers can settle — days or weeks, with the quarrel picked back up or patched up afterward. Detente is a lasting arrangement: hostility eased for the long haul while the dispute still stands.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    standoff: both sides frozen, weapons up

    escalation: the strain wound tighter instead

    Where it sits · how fully the quarrel is resolved

    ceasefire → detente → peace

    ceasefire vs. detente: A ceasefire only stops the firing — the quarrel and the aim both remain — while detente goes further and loosens the strain itself.

    peace vs. detente: Peace goes further than detente — detente eases the strain while the dispute still stands; peace ends the dispute itself.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / diplomatic

      You may meet this word spelled two ways — détente, with the French accent, or detente, plain — and both are standard English; American writing often drops the accent. Either way it names a state, not a document: nations reach detente, they don't sign one. Truce and ceasefire stop active fighting; thaw is its everyday cousin; detente merely lowers the temperature while rivals stay rivals. Don't confuse either spelling with detent, a machine part's catch — a different word entirely.

      For forty years the two ferry families of Lake Ondawa ran their boats like rival navies: undercutting each other's fares, racing for the same dock, suing and countersuing over the original crossing rights. The feud ended nothing and settled nothing — it just got expensive. So the summer both patriarchs retired, their daughters negotiated a detente. Neither side surrendered its claim to the crossing rights; the lawsuits were simply shelved, unresolved. The Marsh boats took the north dock, the Oyelaran boats the south; fares were published each May and left alone; and every June the two crews shared the cost of dredging the channel both depended on. Visitors sometimes mistake the arrangement for friendship. It is nothing so warm — only hostility deliberately eased, year after year, because easing it works.
