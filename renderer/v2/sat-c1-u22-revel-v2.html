revel

/REV-ul/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Revel means to enjoy something with unrestrained, exuberant enthusiasm — often used as 'revel in.'

See the word in context

The whole third grade reveled in the surprise snow day, sledding until their mittens froze solid.

Closing night ended, the curtain fell, and the cast simply refused to let the evening die. They dragged the prop couch to the middle of the stage, blasted the show's soundtrack, reenacted every flubbed line at triple speed, and danced in their costumes until the janitor flicked the lights. After eleven weeks of rehearsal, they had earned the right to revel — and they held nothing back.

The blasting music, wild reenactments, and dancing until lights-out show enjoyment with all restraint thrown off — that unrestrained abandon is what it means to revel.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    bask: To bask is to soak quietly in a pleasant glow that comes from outside you — praise, good news, sunshine — the way a cat lies in a warm patch of light. To revel is the loud opposite: enjoyment with every restraint thrown off. Basking is still and content; reveling climbs onto the furniture.

    luxuriate: To luxuriate is to sink into comfort or pleasure and stretch it out at length, self-indulgently, like staying in a hot bath after the water cools. To revel is more public and more explosive — unrestrained celebration rather than slow, private indulgence. You luxuriate at leisure; you revel out loud.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    mope: to sulk in gloomy silence

    grieve: to feel deep sorrow

    Where it sits · how wild and free the fun gets

    enjoy → revel → carouse

    enjoy vs. revel: To enjoy something is simply to take pleasure in it; to revel in it is to enjoy it loudly, freely, and without holding anything back.

    carouse vs. revel: To carouse goes past reveling — boisterous merrymaking stretched into a long, noisy spree.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      lively, informal-to-literary; usually 'revel in'

      Revel implies open, unrestrained abandon — you revel in a victory or even in chaos — while relish (also in this unit) is quieter and more contained, a savoring appreciation of one particular thing, like relishing the irony or relishing a good meal.
