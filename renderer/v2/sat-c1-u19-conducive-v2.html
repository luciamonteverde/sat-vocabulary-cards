conducive

/kun-DOO-siv/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Conducive means helping to bring about a certain result; making an outcome likely or possible, almost always followed by 'to.'

See the word in context

The cafe's soft lighting and quiet corners proved conducive to long, unhurried conversations.

A violin teacher noticed her students improved fastest in her smallest studio room. The carpet swallowed echoes, the window faced a quiet courtyard, and the music stand sat at exactly eye level. Nothing in the room played the violin for anyone, but every detail made focused practice easier and more likely. The room was, in a word, conducive to learning.

The room's conditions made focused practice more likely without guaranteeing it, and creating conditions that help bring an outcome about is exactly what conducive means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    favorable: Favorable means providing good conditions in a general way — favorable weather, a favorable climate for small businesses. Conducive is more pointed: it names the specific outcome the conditions help produce, and it is nearly always followed by 'to' that outcome. A morning can be favorable; a dim, quiet room is conducive to sleep.

    advantageous: Advantageous means giving someone a benefit or an edge, and it centers on who gains. Conducive centers on the outcome instead: conditions that help a particular result come about, whoever benefits. A rule change is advantageous to one team; a silent library is conducive to study for everyone in it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unfavorable: creating poor conditions for something

    obstructive: standing in the way of progress

    Where it sits · how much it pushes the result along

    harmless → conducive → essential

    harmless vs. conducive: A harmless condition simply does no damage, while a conducive one actively helps an outcome along.

    essential vs. conducive: Conducive conditions make a result easier and more likely, but essential conditions are ones without which the result cannot happen at all.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral, academic register

    Conducive is nearly always followed by 'to' plus the outcome it helps produce: conducive to sleep, conducive to healing, an atmosphere conducive to trust. It credits the surrounding conditions rather than the person acting, so use it for environments, habits, and settings, not for direct actions.

    The garden club's first seedlings failed two years running, and members blamed the seeds, the rain, and finally each other. Then a retired botanist toured the plot and pointed at the ground instead: heavy clay, packed hard, cold long into spring. Nothing about such soil was conducive to germination, she explained; a seed there was not harmed so much as never helped. The club spent October forking in sand and leaf mold, and by April the bed drained fast and warmed early. That spring nearly every seed came up, and the botanist's lesson stuck: conditions do not have to attack a seedling to fail it -- they have to push it along.
