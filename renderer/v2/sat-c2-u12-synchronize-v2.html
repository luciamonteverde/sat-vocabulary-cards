Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Synchronize means to make two or more separate things happen at the same time or run at the same rate — to bring their timing into exact agreement.

See the word in context

Each June along the park's creek, thousands of fireflies synchronize their flashing, whole hillsides blinking on and off in one shared rhythm.

Bedridden with a fever in 1665, the Dutch scientist Christiaan Huygens lay watching the two pendulum clocks that hung from the same wooden beam across his room, and noticed something eerie: their pendulums had fallen into perfect step, swing for swing. However he disturbed them, within half an hour the pair would synchronize again, as if the clocks had agreed on a beat behind his back.

Synchronize is the exact word because what changed was timing and nothing else: two separate clocks, each running on its own works, ended up beating at the same rate and the same moment. Huygens finally traced the cause to tiny vibrations traveling through the shared beam — each clock was nudging the other into step. Neither clock became the other; they simply stopped keeping separate time, and that agreement of timing is precisely what synchronize names.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    coordinate: Coordinate means to organize separate people or parts so they work together smoothly — each can keep its own pace and its own schedule. Synchronize is stricter: it matches the timing itself, the same moment, the same beat, the same rate.

    align: Align means to bring things into a shared line or a shared position — wheels, policies, interests — a match of direction or stance. Synchronize is a match of time: aligned things point the same way, while synchronized things move at the same instant.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    stagger: to space things out on purpose so they do not happen at the same time

    alternate: to make things take turns, one after another, never together

    Where it sits · how completely separate things are made to run as one

    overlap → synchronize → merge

    overlap vs. synchronize: Overlap is weaker than synchronize: two shifts that overlap merely share a slice of the same hour, while synchronized shifts start, run, and end together, beat for beat.

    merge vs. synchronize: Merge goes further than synchronize: synchronized orchestras are still two orchestras playing to one beat, but merged orchestras stop being two things at all.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral — the engineer's, editor's, and choreographer's word

      Synchronize is a verb of deliberate timing: swimmers synchronize their strokes, film editors synchronize sound to picture, and satellites synchronize their clocks with ground stations. On the test, expect the noun as often as the verb — synchronization names the matched-timing state that all of that adjusting produces. Reach for coordinate when separate parts are organized to work together but each keeps its own pace, and for align when the match is one of position or stance rather than time. Keep it apart from this unit's contemporaneous, which only says two things existed in the same period, and coincidental, which says a time-match happened by chance — synchronization is no accident: someone, or something, makes the timing agree.
