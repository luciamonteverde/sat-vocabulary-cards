impeccable

/im-PEK-uh-buhl/ - the stress falls on PEK

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impeccable means so flawless as to be beyond criticism.

See the word in context

Strangers assumed the thrift-store suit was bespoke; her tailoring was that impeccable.

Odile's manners were impeccable in four languages. She never reached across a table, never interrupted, never let a name slip her mind twice; her thank-you notes were postmarked before her hosts had finished the dishes. Diplomats' wives studied her; one swore she had once watched Odile turn down a live eel, at a state dinner, in a way that flattered the chef.

Impeccable is the exact word because Odile's manners are so flawless as to be beyond criticism — not merely good, but offering criticism no toehold at all. The word admits no degrees. Polished manners can survive a lapse; impeccable ones, by definition, haven't had one. Save it for the performance that gives even an unfriendly eye nothing to hold.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    flawless: Flawless reports an inspection of the thing itself: not one defect anywhere in it. Impeccable makes the further boast — so flawless that criticism is left with nothing to say, no toehold for even a hostile judge. A gem can be flawless in fact; impeccable manners defeat the fault-finders.

    faultless: Faultless is the plain zero-defect finding — nothing to fix, nothing to apologize for — and it stops at that arithmetic. Impeccable carries the same zero with a flourish: it pictures the sternest critic circling the work and walking away empty-handed. Faultless counts; impeccable defies you to count.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    flawed: carrying at least one real defect

    sloppy: careless enough that flaws are guaranteed

    Where it sits · how few faults remain to find

    presentable → polished → impeccable

    presentable vs. impeccable: Presentable merely clears the lowest bar — fit to be seen, no more; impeccable sits at the other end of the scale, where even a hostile eye finds nothing to hold.

    polished vs. impeccable: Polished work has been buffed until it shines, yet a sharp eye can still find the smudge; impeccable goes further and leaves the sharp eye nothing at all.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / admiring

    Impeccable is a finding of zero: no fault found, none findable — reserve it for the one performance in a hundred that survives unfriendly inspection. This unit's meticulous describes the worker's care; impeccable describes the finished work's state. A meticulous tailor may still deliver a crooked seam; when the seam offers criticism nothing to hold, the suit is impeccable.
