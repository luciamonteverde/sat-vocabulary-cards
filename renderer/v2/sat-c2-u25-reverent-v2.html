Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Reverent means showing deep, awed respect — treating something as near-sacred in how you speak, move, or stay silent before it.

See the word in context

The apprentice lifted the old luthier's violin with reverent care, as though a careless breath could crack it.

No sign at the trailhead asks for quiet, and none is needed: a dozen paces into the grove of two-thousand-year-old redwoods, the loudest tour group of the morning went reverent all at once — voices dropped mid-sentence, footsteps slowed, and one hiker reached out to touch the bark, then let his hand fall without making contact, as if the tree had a right to refuse.

Reverent is the exact word for that hush, because it means showing deep, awed respect — treating something as near-sacred in how you speak, move, or stay silent before it. Nobody instructed the group; the awe did. A respectful group would have kept the trail rules and the noise down, and thought no more about it; a reverent one lowers its voice the way people do in front of something they cannot quite measure.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    respectful: Respectful is the everyday, all-purpose word — polite and considerate toward anyone, in any setting — and it asks nothing more of you than good manners. Reverent goes much further: the respect deepens into awe, is saved for what feels close to sacred, and shows in hushed voices and careful hands.

    solemn: Solemn describes the weight of a mood or occasion — grave, formal, no lightness anywhere — without pointing at anything. Reverent is aimed: deep respect directed at one particular thing held in awe. A judge can be solemn all day out of habit; a person is reverent only before something.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    flippant: treating what deserves seriousness as a joke

    scornful: looking down on it with open contempt

    Where it sits · how much awe the respect carries

    respectful → reverent → worshipful

    respectful vs. reverent: Respectful is milder than reverent: a respectful visitor follows the rules and keeps a polite tone, while a reverent one is quieted by genuine awe.

    worshipful vs. reverent: Worshipful goes further than reverent — reverent respect keeps a human distance, while worshipful devotion treats its object as divine.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / solemn, warmly positive

    Reverent belongs to the one offering the respect, never to the thing receiving it: the pilgrims are reverent, the shrine they walk to is revered. Keep its neighbors straight — respectful is everyday courtesy with no awe in it; solemn is gravity of mood that points at nothing in particular; reverent is respect deepened into awe and aimed at something held near-sacred. Two traps ride along with this word: the sound-alike reverie is a daydream, no relation at all, and the re- up front is an intensifier — a reverent hush is not a hush that happens again.
