largess

/lar-JESS/ - the stress falls on JESS

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Largess means the generous giving of lavish gifts.

See the word in context

The wedding was remembered less for its vows than for the groom's largess: cab fare home for every guest and tips that made the waiters blink.

The lottery winner's first month passed in a blur of largess. He paid off every layaway ticket at the toy store, rented the roller rink for the whole town on a Tuesday, and pressed hundred-dollar bills on carolers, crossing guards, and one astonished meter reader.

Largess is the exact word for that month, because largess means the generous giving of lavish gifts — gifts, plural, arriving in a shower. Generosity is quieter and needs no audience; largess likes a crowd and a little astonishment, giving on a scale people will talk about.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    bounty: Bounty is generous abundance handed out — the word carries harvest overtones, plenty flowing to people from a rich source, a good season, a full treasury. Largess is more theatrical: not just plenty, but lavish gifts showered on a crowd, given at a scale meant to be talked about.

    generosity: Generosity is the everyday virtue of giving freely, at any size and often in private — it asks for no audience and makes no headline. Largess is generosity turned into performance: lavish gifts, plural, arriving in a shower, on a scale people will remember and repeat.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    stinginess: giving as little as possible

    miserliness: hoarding every coin as if it were the last

    Where it sits · how unrestrained the spending becomes

    openhandedness → largess → extravagance

    openhandedness vs. largess: Openhandedness is milder than largess — the openhanded giver gives freely, quietly and without spectacle, while largess showers lavish gifts on a scale meant to be noticed and talked about.

    extravagance vs. largess: Extravagance goes further than largess — largess at least showers its lavish gifts on people, while extravagance is spending run wild for its own sake.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / a little glittering

      Largess is generosity in performance: lavish, plural, and usually watched. Munificence, its unit-mate, is the graver word for giving of extraordinary scale; a benefactor may practice either. If the giving is small or private, plain generosity tells the truth better.

      When the copper baron finally sold his mines, the town braced for a quiet exit. Instead came a season of largess no one had asked for. He paid off the mortgages on an entire street in one morning. Schoolchildren found new instruments waiting in the band room, and every teacher received a bonus that made the news two counties over. The local paper ran out of ways to describe the giving, and the baron, by all accounts, enjoyed the coverage as much as the gratitude. His critics noted that the gifts arrived in showers, never in secret, and always with photographers nearby. Whatever else it was, it was giving built to be witnessed — and remembered long after the mines changed hands.
