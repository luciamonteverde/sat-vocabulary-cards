pretense

/PREE-tens/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Pretense means a false appearance or action intended to deceive — a pretending.

See the word in context

The salesman got inside dozens of homes under the pretense of offering a free safety inspection.

The reporter arranged a job interview at the warehouse, but the interview was a pretense — her real goal was to document the unsafe working conditions inside.

The interview looked genuine from the outside, but it was staged to conceal her true purpose. That false front — an act put on to deceive — is exactly what a pretense is.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    guise: A guise is the deceptive outer form something wears — reforms passed under the guise of progress, a spy in the guise of a florist. It points at the costume, the surface itself. A pretense is the pretending: the false front a person keeps up to deceive. The guise is what is worn; the pretense is the performance behind it.

    charade: A charade is a false display kept up for show — often an empty performance nobody quite believes, so hollow that onlookers can see through it. A pretense is built to actually deceive: the audience is meant to buy it. Call it a charade to sneer at its emptiness; call it a pretense when the deception is meant to work.

    sham: A sham is a fake thing passed off as genuine — a sham marriage, a sham election, a sham trial. The word points at the counterfeit object or institution itself. A pretense is the act of pretending a person keeps up — the false appearance, not the fake article.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    candor: frank, open honesty

    sincerity: genuineness of feeling or intent

    Where it sits · how far the false front extends

    act → pretense → masquerade

    act vs. pretense: Putting on an act can be as small as hiding a bad mood; a pretense is a false front built specifically to deceive someone.

    masquerade vs. pretense: A masquerade goes further than a pretense — an entire false identity, worn head to toe for as long as the deception needs to last, not just one false front.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      Neutral — watch the idiom and the unit-mate

      The most common idiom is 'under false pretenses' — gaining something through deception. And keep pretense apart from its unit-mate pretext (also taught in this unit): a pretext is the fake reason you give for doing something, while a pretense is the fake appearance or act you keep up.
