eloquent

/EL-uh-kwent/ - the stress falls on EL

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Eloquent means using language so fluently and movingly that it stirs the audience.

See the word in context

Nobody expected the quiet groundskeeper to deliver the evening's most eloquent speech, but by the end even the caterers had stopped to listen.

Asked to say a few words at the bridge dedication, the retired ferry captain proved unexpectedly eloquent: he spoke of thirty years reading the river's moods, of couples who met aboard and married ashore, and when he finished, the applause waited a beat — people were wiping their eyes first.

Eloquent is the exact word because the speech did more than come out clearly: it moved people, and the pause before the applause is the measure. Articulate would credit the clarity alone; eloquent credits language with power — fluency in the service of feeling.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    articulate: Articulate praises clarity: thoughts put into words cleanly and in good order. Eloquent starts there and adds power — eloquent language does not just inform its audience, it moves them.

    silver-tongued: Silver-tongued admires persuasive charm, often with a raised eyebrow — the word hints the speaker could sell anything. Eloquent is admiration without the suspicion: moving language offered in earnest.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    tongue-tied: unable to find words at all when the moment comes

    halting: speech that stumbles, stalls, and restarts

    Where it sits · how much power the language has to move its audience

    fluent → eloquent → spellbinding

    fluent vs. eloquent: Fluent is milder than eloquent: fluent speech flows without a stumble, but flow alone moves no one — eloquent speech has the ease and the power to stir its audience.

    spellbinding vs. eloquent: Spellbinding goes beyond eloquent: an eloquent speaker moves the room, while a spellbinding one suspends it — nobody shifts, coughs, or checks the time until the voice stops.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      admiring

      Eloquent is praise, and it can land on more than speeches: an eloquent letter, an eloquent silence, an eloquent shrug — anything that expresses powerfully. Keep it apart from elegant, which praises how something looks, not how it speaks; the SAT likes that near-miss. Beside its neighbors: articulate is clear, fluent is smooth, and eloquent is the one that moves people.
