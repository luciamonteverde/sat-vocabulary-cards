satiate

/SAY-shee-ayt/ - the stress falls on SAY

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Satiate means to satisfy fully or to excess.

See the word in context

The tasting portions were tiny on purpose: the chef wanted guests intrigued, not satiated, when the main course arrived.

Success finally satiated the collector: after the missing 1913 coin arrived, he stopped bidding entirely and let the auction catalogs pile up unopened.

Satiate goes past 'satisfy' — a satisfied appetite is content, a satiated one is finished, filled to the brim or beyond. That's why advertisers promise to satisfy your cravings, never to satiate them: satiation is where wanting stops.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sate: Sate is the blunt old sibling of satiate — to fill someone completely, full stop. Satiate does the same work in a longer robe, and it more readily tips past fullness into too much: a sated guest is done eating; a satiated one may be groaning.

    quench: Quench answers thirst specifically — water on a fire, the drink that puts wanting out. Satiate answers hunger and appetite in the broad sense: for food, novelty, or success, filling the want completely until nothing more is asked.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    starve: to deny food or fuel until the need turns desperate

    deprive: to withhold what is wanted or needed

    Where it sits · how far past hunger it fills

    feed → satiate → glut

    feed vs. satiate: Feed stops short of satiate — feeding answers hunger for now, without promising fullness.

    glut vs. satiate: Glut goes further than satiate — to satiate is to fill completely; to glut is to stuff past all pleasure, into queasy excess.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / precise

    Satiate is satisfaction taken to its limit, and often a shade beyond — the word can carry a groan. Satisfy a curiosity you plan to keep; satiate a hunger you want ended. Its cousin sate is blunter and older; satiated is the polite word for 'completely full' at a dinner party.
