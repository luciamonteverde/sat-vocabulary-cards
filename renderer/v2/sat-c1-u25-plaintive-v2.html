Meaning

What It Means

Plaintive means sounding sad or mournful, especially describing a sound, voice, or cry.

See the word in context

The loon's plaintive call carried across the misty lake long after sunset.

The shelter's newest arrival, an old beagle, would not eat the first night. Instead he sat facing the door and let out a long, low howl every few minutes — not loud, not demanding, just unmistakably sorrowful. Volunteers came out of the back office one by one, unable to work through it. 'That,' the manager said quietly, 'is the most plaintive sound in this building.'

The beagle's low, sorrowful howling — sad rather than loud or demanding — is exactly the mournful quality of sound that plaintive describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    mournful: Mournful means full of sorrow or grief, as if grieving an actual loss — mournful faces, a mournful procession, black crepe on the door. Plaintive lives in sound and asks less: a plaintive cry or melody simply sounds sad and appealing, with no real bereavement required behind it.

    sorrowful: Sorrowful means expressing sadness, and it can describe almost anything — eyes, news, a whole chapter. Plaintive is the sound-specific cousin: a thin, sad, touching quality in a voice, cry, or tune. Sorrowful states the emotion; plaintive is how that emotion reaches the ear.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    cheerful: bright and happy in tone

    jubilant: ringing with triumphant joy

    Where it sits · how much sorrow the sound carries

    wistful → plaintive → anguished

    wistful vs. plaintive: Wistful is weaker than plaintive: a wistful tone carries gentle longing, while a plaintive one is openly sad and mournful.

    anguished vs. plaintive: Anguished is stronger than plaintive — an anguished cry expresses raw, unbearable pain, while a plaintive one is sorrowful without that intensity.

    Word Choice

    literary; used of sounds, voices, music

    Plaintive nearly always attaches to something heard — a cry, a melody, a whistle. It is the emotional opposite of this unit's effusive: a plaintive tone is quiet and mournful, where an effusive one overflows with gushing enthusiasm.

Most weeks she played reels and show tunes, quick bright things for the commuter rush. But when the last express pulled away she would end with an old air, and the melody turned plaintive — thin, sad, and strangely appealing as it drifted down the empty platform. A transit worker once asked if the tune had been written for a funeral. She laughed and said no; nothing sad had ever happened to it, or to her. The sorrow lived in the sound itself, she explained — in the slides and the minor turns — and that was exactly why strangers stopped to listen.
