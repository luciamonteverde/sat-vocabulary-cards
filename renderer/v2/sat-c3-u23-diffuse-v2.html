diffuse

/dih-FYOOZ/ - the stress falls on FYOOZ, and the verb ends in a 'z' sound

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Diffuse means to spread something widely through a space, thinning it out as it goes.

See the word in context

Word of the strike diffused through the port by nightfall, carried from pier to pier faster than any bulletin.

When the projectionist swapped the clear bulb for a frosted one, the harsh glare diffused into a soft, even glow that reached every corner of the little theater.

Nothing was blocked and nothing switched off — all the same light is still there, only spread thin across the room instead of concentrated in one hot point. That spreading-and-thinning is exactly what diffuse describes, and it is why the glow feels gentler without the bulb being any dimmer.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    disperse: To disperse is to drive or send things off in different directions — a crowd, seeds, a fleet — and the pieces stay themselves, just farther apart. Diffuse adds thinning: what spreads becomes fainter and more evenly mixed as it goes, like scent through a room.

    dissipate: To dissipate is to scatter something until it is effectively gone — fog dissipates and the sky is simply clear. Diffuse spreads and thins without promising disappearance: the diffused light or warmth is everywhere at once, only gentler.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    concentrate: gather something into one strong, dense point

    contain: keep something from spreading at all

    Where it sits · how far and how thin the spreading goes

    seep → diffuse → saturate

    seep vs. diffuse: Seep is weaker than diffuse: a seeping smell creeps out slowly and stays near its source, while a diffusing one spreads wide and thins through the whole space.

    saturate vs. diffuse: Saturate goes further than diffuse — a diffused scent drifts thin through the air of a room, while a saturated room has soaked it up, so the curtains and the coats now carry it themselves.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / scientific and figurative alike

    Diffuse spreads a thing thin — light, heat, scent, news — and the thing survives everywhere at lower strength. Don't reach for it when you mean defuse, its sound-alike: you defuse a bomb, or a quarrel, by taking the fuse out. And unlike efface and expunge, taught in this unit, diffusing removes nothing at all; everything is still there, just spread wide. You will also meet the adjective, said with a soft 's' (dih-FYOOSS): a diffuse argument is one that has undergone the verb — spread thin, and hard to pin down.

    Under the floor of a Roman bathhouse ran a low open chamber called a hypocaust, and beneath one corner of it, a single wood furnace. The fire itself heated only the bricks directly above it. If the system had stopped there, bathers would have scalded their feet in one room and shivered in the next. Instead, the builders left the whole underfloor connected, so the hot air could diffuse through the entire chamber, thinning and evening out as it traveled. By the time the warmth rose up through the tiles, no spot was fierce and no spot was cold; the far rooms were simply a little gentler than the near ones. Archaeologists mapping the soot say the design worked for centuries -- one fire, spread thin, warming forty rooms at once.
