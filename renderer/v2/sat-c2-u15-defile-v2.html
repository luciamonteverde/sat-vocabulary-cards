defile

/dih-FYL/ - rhymes with mile

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Defile means to make something unclean or impure.

See the word in context

A single afternoon of dye-dumping upstream defiled the pools where the town has always swum.

The beekeeper's deal with the co-op turns on one word: pure. Her hives sit two miles from the nearest field for a reason. The summer a neighbor's sprayer drifted, the tests came back speaking of traces — nothing you could see, nothing you could taste — and the whole season's honey was refused. What drift defiles, no filter recovers, because what entered is in the thing now, not on it.

Defile is the exact word for what happened to that honey, because it means to make something unclean or impure — and it carries a note of offense, as if the purity had been owed protection. Contaminate states the laboratory fact; defile adds the sense that something kept clean has been wronged.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    pollute: Pollute is defiling at environmental scale — rivers, air, whole coastlines fouled, usually measured in tons. Defile can strike one spring or one jar of honey, and it adds a note of offense that the laboratory report leaves out.

    taint: A taint is the smallest dose of the same uncleanness — one drop of dirt, or of doubt, in something otherwise clean, and a trace is all it takes. To defile goes all the way: the whole thing made impure, and the word rings with offense.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    purify: drive the uncleanness back out

    cleanse: wash clean, body or soul

    Where it sits · how deep the fouling goes

    dirty → defile → poison

    dirty vs. defile: Dirty is far milder than defile — mud on boots, dust on sills, and a rinse undoes it; what defiles gets into the thing itself and does not rinse away.

    poison vs. defile: Poison goes further than defile — defiled water is unfit to drink; poisoned water does harm with every swallow.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / moral

      Defile is charged: it treats cleanness as something owed protection, which is why it suits springs, snowfields, and honey jars better than lab benches — there, contaminate is the plain term. Its unit mates split the territory: deface attacks a surface's look, desecrate a place's sacredness, debase a thing's worth. And despite its spelling, defile has nothing to do with filing anything.
