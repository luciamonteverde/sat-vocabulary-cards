Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A mastermind is the person whose intelligence designs and directs a complex operation, often from behind the scenes.

See the word in context

Every profile of the campaign names the same mastermind: the strategist who plotted the county-by-county upset from a rented back office.

The seniors' farewell prank ran like a moon launch: at 8:00 sharp, three thousand balloons rolled out of every hallway closet at once, each closet unlocked with a borrowed key, each key returned before homeroom. Administrators grilled the loudest boys in the class and got nowhere. The mastermind, it came out years later, was quiet Lena from the AV club, who had charted the janitors' rounds for a month and never touched a single balloon herself.

Mastermind fits Lena exactly because the word names the intelligence behind an operation, not the hands that carry it out. She designed the whole machine — keys, closets, timing — and directed it invisibly; never touching a balloon is practically the job description. A helper executes and a ringleader stirs the group up and fronts it, but the mastermind is the one mind the entire scheme comes from.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    architect: The architect of a plan designs its structure — the word looks at the drawing board. A mastermind designs and then runs the operation, often unseen; the architect's job can end where the mastermind's is just beginning.

    ringleader: A ringleader stirs a group into mischief and fronts it — the loudest voice in the ring. A mastermind may never be seen at all: the brains behind the operation, supplying the design rather than the noise.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    pawn: a person moved around by someone else's plan

    bystander: someone with no hand in the scheme at all

    Where it sits · how much of the scheme one mind controls

    organizer → mastermind → puppeteer

    organizer vs. mastermind: An organizer is milder than a mastermind: an organizer books the rooms and keeps the schedule, while a mastermind supplies the entire design the schedule serves.

    puppeteer vs. mastermind: Puppeteer goes further than mastermind — a mastermind directs the operation, but a puppeteer controls the people themselves, tugging players who may never know whose strings they're on.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    admiring / conspiratorial

    Mastermind carries a hint of the heist even in innocent settings — call someone the bake sale's mastermind and you've made the cupcakes sound like a caper. It also works as a verb: to mastermind a merger is to design and run it. This unit's canny praises sharp practical judgment in the moment; mastermind is bigger and colder — the one mind a whole operation answers to.
