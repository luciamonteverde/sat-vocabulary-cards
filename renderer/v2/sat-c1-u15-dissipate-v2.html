dissipate

/DIS-uh-payt/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Dissipate means to scatter and drift apart until it is gone, the way fog, smoke, or a strong feeling gradually thins into nothing; it can also mean to waste money or resources through careless living.

See the word in context

By mid-morning, the thick fog over the harbor had begun to dissipate, and the fishing boats slowly came into view.

Dissipate also has a second, older sense: to waste money or resources through careless overindulgence. The young heir dissipated his entire inheritance in barely three years, pouring it into gambling trips and lavish parties. By the time he turned thirty, a fortune built over generations had been thrown away completely.

This example shows the word's separate 'waste or squander' sense, which is different from the fading-fog sense: here nothing fades on its own; instead, a person carelessly scatters wealth until nothing is left.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    evaporate: To evaporate is literally to turn from liquid into vapor, and figuratively to disappear as if into thin air - savings or confidence can evaporate. Dissipate keeps the scattering visible: fog, smoke, or tension thins and drifts apart until nothing is left. Evaporation is the vanishing trick; dissipation is the slow drift apart.

    dwindle: To dwindle is to shrink steadily in size or number - a crowd dwindles, supplies dwindle - and what dwindles is still there, only smaller. To dissipate is to thin out and scatter until it is entirely gone. A dwindling fog has shrunk; a dissipated fog is gone from the sky altogether.

    squander: To squander is to waste money, time, or chances through carelessness, and it is always about resources. Dissipate shares that second sense - the heir dissipated his fortune - but adds the image of scattering it to nothing. And unlike squander, dissipate's main job is describing fog, smoke, and feelings thinning away.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    accumulate: to build up or gather over time

    intensify: to grow stronger or more concentrated

    Where it sits · how completely it goes away

    fade → dissipate → vanish

    fade vs. dissipate: Fade simply means to grow fainter, but dissipate goes further, suggesting something thins out until it is entirely gone.

    vanish vs. dissipate: Vanish is stronger and more sudden than dissipate, which describes a slow, gradual disappearance rather than an instant one.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Neutral; academic and literary

    Unlike dispel, which implies someone actively banishes a feeling or myth on purpose, dissipate suggests something fades away gradually on its own, like fog burning off in the sun. It is also distinct from disperse, which describes a crowd or group physically scattering in different directions. Watch for the second sense too: to dissipate a fortune is to waste it through careless living.
