/muh-NOT-uh-nuhs/ - the stress falls on NOT

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Monotonous means unchanging and repetitious to the point of being dull.

See the word in context

The hold music is monotonous — the same eight bars looping, softly, for as long as you can stand to wait.

The garrison's cook had one kettle and one order: boiled barley, every meal, for two years. Soldiers' letters home stopped mentioning food altogether by the fourth month — no news was left in it that the last letter had not carried. The menu was monotonous in the exact, grinding sense: not bad, not short, just the same, endlessly.

Monotonous is the exact word because it names what is unchanging and repetitious to the point of being dull — sameness that has begun to wear on its audience. The root picture is one musical note held forever. A thing can come around again and stay charming; it turns monotonous at the moment the sameness itself is the hardship.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    one-note: One-note is the informal jab for a performance or argument stuck on a single idea, as if a musician played the same note all night. It mocks a lack of range. Monotonous is the broader, more formal complaint that sameness of any kind — a sound, a menu, a routine — has turned dull.

    unvarying: Unvarying states the sameness flatly, as a plain fact with no complaint attached: an unvarying bedtime, an unvarying gray sky. Monotonous adds the verdict that the sameness bores. A surgeon wants an unvarying pulse on the monitor; nobody wants a monotonous one — the word itself carries the groan.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    varied: full of differences

    lively: active and engaging — the cure for dullness

    Where it sits · how heavily the sameness wears on you

    unvaried → monotonous → mind-numbing

    unvaried vs. monotonous: Unvaried just states the fact of no change; monotonous adds the ache — the sameness has started to bore.

    mind-numbing vs. monotonous: Mind-numbing goes past monotonous: the sameness is so total it blanks thought altogether.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral, disapproving

    Monotonous hands down a judgment: the sameness bores. Keep it apart from this unit's incessant, which is about never stopping rather than never changing — a drill can be monotonous yet mercifully brief, and a varied racket can be incessant. For the same fact without the complaint, unvarying is the flat alternative.

    The summer Jonah turned sixteen, he took a job in the exit booth of a downtown parking garage. At first the novelty carried him: the gate arm, the till, the little sliding window, the ceremony of making change. By July the shift had gone monotonous — the same three questions from drivers, the same beep of the scanner, hour after hour, until he started counting ceiling tiles to stay awake. His sister worked the aquarium's ticket desk, just as repetitive on paper, but she swore it never wore on her. 'Different faces every minute,' she said. That, Jonah decided, was the whole difference. Repetition alone was survivable; it was repetition with nothing new inside it that ground him down.
