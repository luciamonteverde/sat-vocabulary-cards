Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Calibrate means to adjust precisely against a known standard.

See the word in context

Divers calibrate their depth gauges against the pool's marked ten-meter line before any open-water trip.

On the first Monday of each month, the pharmacist sets a certified brass weight — exactly one hundred grams, sealed and stamped — on the dispensary scale. If the display strays by even a tenth of a gram, she calibrates the instrument, working the adjustment screw until scale and weight agree, then hangs the brass back in its case until next month.

Calibrate is the exact word for that monthly ritual, because calibrate means to adjust precisely against a known standard — the stamped weight is the standard, and the scale is bent to match it. Adjust alone covers any nudge in any direction; calibrating requires something true to aim at, and it ends only when the instrument agrees with the truth.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    align: Align means to bring things into agreement or into line with each other — two schedules, a policy and a mission, a wheel and an axle. Calibrate is narrower and more exacting: to calibrate is to true one instrument against a known standard, and the job ends only when the two agree.

    standardize: Standardize means to make many things match one agreed norm — forms, sizes, procedures across a whole system. Calibrate works on one instrument at a time: you calibrate a scale against a known standard until it reads true. Standardizing writes the rule; calibrating brings a single tool into line with it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    skew: push the readings off true

    throw off: knock an instrument out of agreement with the truth

    Where it sits · whether changes answer to a set standard

    adjust → fine-tune → calibrate

    adjust vs. calibrate: Adjust is far looser than calibrate — turn it until it seems right, with nothing to answer to, while calibrating must answer to a known standard.

    fine-tune vs. calibrate: Fine-tune stops short of calibrate — the touches are small and careful, but only calibrating checks them against a known standard.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      technical / neutral

      Calibrate is bench language — instruments, sensors, scales — and it always implies a standard somewhere in the room. Adjust covers every looser case; fine-tune sits between. Figuratively you can calibrate a speech to its audience, and the standard is still there: the audience is the reference the wording is trued against.

      The stream-monitoring station above the old mill reports water acidity every hour, and the town's safety alerts depend on those numbers being right. So at the start of each season, a technician hikes up to calibrate the station's pH meter. She carries two bottles of buffer solution, each prepared to a known, exact acidity: one should read 7.00 and the other 4.00 on any instrument telling the truth. She dips the probe into the first bottle and adjusts the meter until the display settles at 7.00, then repeats the whole routine with the second bottle. Only when both readings match the bottles' true values does she pack up — a meter left to drift could miss the difference between a healthy stream and a poisoned one.
