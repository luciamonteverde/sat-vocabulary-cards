exhort

/ig-ZORT/ - rhymes with sort

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Exhort means to urge someone strongly and encouragingly to act.

See the word in context

In her last assembly before retiring, the principal exhorted the students to guard their curiosity like a campfire in the rain.

Pacing the volunteer tent at dawn, the crew chief exhorted the sandbaggers: the river would crest by noon, the levee was half built, and every pair of hands mattered.

Exhort sits between ordering and begging: it does not command, because no authority is invoked, and it does not grovel, because the urging stays encouraging. An exhorter pushes you toward action with energy and belief, not with rank or tears.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    implore: To implore is to beg desperately — pleading, not rallying, with the asker's need laid bare. To exhort never grovels: it pushes people toward action with energy, belief, and a raised voice, asking from strength rather than from need.

    rally: To rally is to gather scattered people and lift them into a shared effort — the word covers the assembling as much as the lifting. To exhort is the urging itself: strong, public, encouraging speech that pushes listeners toward action, whether or not anyone gathers.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    dishearten: to drain someone's will to act

    caution: to advise someone away from acting

    Where it sits · how forceful and public the push is

    encourage → urge → exhort

    encourage vs. exhort: Encouraging someone is gentler than exhorting them — a warm word of support that asks for nothing in particular.

    urge vs. exhort: Urging is milder than exhorting — you press for action, but exhorting adds public fire: the raised voice, the rallying speech, the pointed finger.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / rhetorical

      Exhort is for speeches, sermons, and sideline shouting — strong, public, encouraging urging. Use 'urge' for everyday pressing and 'implore' when the asking turns desperate. Its unit-mates cover the darker routes to the same goal: goad stings someone into acting, and galvanize shocks them into it, while exhort cheers them there.
