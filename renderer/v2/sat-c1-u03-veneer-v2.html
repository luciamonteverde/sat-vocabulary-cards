veneer

/vuh-NEER/ - rhymes with sincere

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Veneer means a superficial or deceptively attractive appearance, facade.

See the word in context

Fresh sod gave the abandoned mine's entrance a veneer of parkland.

Furniture makers slice walnut into sheets thinner than a coin and glue that veneer over cheap pine — and the workshop word became the perfect metaphor, so that by 1860 novelists were writing of a scoundrel's veneer of respectability: handsome surface, bargain material beneath.

Veneer is exact because it insists on two layers: an attractive face deliberately applied, and something quite different underneath. Mere shine is a sheen; a false face constructed to cover the truth is a veneer.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    gloss: A gloss is a thin shine of respectability brushed over something — a quick coat that improves how it reads without changing what it is. A veneer is more constructed: a whole false surface deliberately laid on to hide a different reality. A press release puts a gloss on bad news; staged charity builds a veneer of virtue.

    front: A front is the false outward show a person performs — the brave face, the respectable act kept up for an audience. A veneer keeps the carpentry image: a thin attractive layer applied onto a thing, a business, or a life. People put up fronts; buildings and reputations wear veneers.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    core: the true center of a thing

    bedrock: the solid reality everything rests on

    Where it sits · how far the pretense goes

    sheen → veneer → charade

    sheen vs. veneer: A sheen is only a surface impression — it stops short of a veneer, which is a false layer deliberately laid on.

    charade vs. veneer: A charade goes further than a veneer: not a thin covering over the truth but a whole performance in place of it.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    critical / figurative

    Veneer is the word for an attractive surface you suspect: civility, luxury, or legality laid thin over something else. A front is what a person performs; a gloss is shallower still; veneer keeps the carpentry image of a layer glued on. It is the natural opposite of this unit's manifest — what is manifest stands in plain view, what wears a veneer is dressed to avoid it.

Reporters who had covered the dispute for years recognized the performance at once. The civility was a veneer, one wrote — a thin, polished layer of protocol laid carefully over a negotiation that had already failed in private. The tells were small: interpreters left waiting, a banquet shortened by an hour, two ministers who never once sat at the same table. When the talks collapsed on the fourth day, nothing about the collapse was new. Only the surface had ever been intact.
