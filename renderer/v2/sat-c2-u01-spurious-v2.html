Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Spurious means false but made to seem genuine or plausible.

See the word in context

The signed jersey proved spurious: the 'autograph' had been applied by machine, ink, smudge, and all.

The county fair's 'moon rock' drew crowds until a geologist borrowed it for a weekend. Her report ran three sentences: the specimen was spurious—quarry basalt, tumbled smooth—and its certificate of authenticity had been issued by an agency that does not exist.

Spurious does a job that 'fake' does only loosely: it stresses that the falseness is dressed to pass inspection. A toy banana is fake; a lab result invented to look like real data is spurious.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    counterfeit: Counterfeit is for physical fakes manufactured to pass as the real article — bills, designer bags, forged papers you can hold. Spurious lives one level up, in claims and content: a quotation or a result that is false but dressed to look sound. If the fake is an object, counterfeit is the sharper word.

    bogus: Bogus is the casual, hallway word for fake — a bogus excuse, a bogus phone number — and it passes no judgment on craftsmanship. Spurious is its formal cousin and adds a charge: the falsehood was dressed up to survive inspection. A bogus claim may fool no one; a spurious one is built to.

    phony: Phony is informal and aims mostly at people and their manners — a phony smile, a phony accent. Spurious rarely describes a person at all: it targets claims, credentials, and evidence that are false but made to seem genuine. Call the salesman phony and his miracle testimonials spurious.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    genuine: really being what it claims to be

    authentic: verified as real, with the pedigree to prove it

    Where it sits · how deliberately false the claim is

    questionable → spurious → fraudulent

    questionable vs. spurious: A questionable claim merely invites doubt—it may yet check out; a spurious one is actually false, dressed up to look sound.

    fraudulent vs. spurious: Fraudulent moves past spurious into crime: a spurious quote may fool people innocently, while a fraudulent document is deployed to cheat someone out of something.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / academic

    Spurious is the scholar's word for fakes built to pass: spurious quotations, statistics, pedigrees. Keep it apart from its unit-mates on the truth-and-falsehood shelf: fallacious condemns reasoning, distortion names real facts twisted out of shape, and veracity is the truthfulness all of them offend against. If the fake is physical merchandise, counterfeit is more exact.
