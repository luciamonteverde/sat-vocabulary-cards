What It Means

Tome means a large, heavy book.

See the word in context

Somewhere in every law office lobby sits a leather tome no one has opened since the firm bought it by the yard.

The print shop's pride is a tome of Victorian wallpaper samples: varnished pages by the thousand, boards thick as cutting blocks, and a shelf that bows visibly beneath it. Apprentices roll it out on a cart built for the purpose and are forbidden, by long tradition, to move it alone.

Tome is the word because it means a large, heavy book — the size is the point, and affection and mockery ride along with it. Volume is neutral shelf-talk; call something a tome and you can feel the wrists complaining.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    volume: A volume is any single bound book, big or small — the librarian's neutral counting word, as happy with a slim yearbook as with a monster. Tome refuses to be neutral: it insists on size and heft. Every tome is a volume; only the back-straining ones are tomes.

    doorstop: Doorstop is the joke name for a book this size — pure teasing, with the affection optional. Tome carries the same wink more gently: it respects the book's weight even while smiling at it. Call a dictionary a tome and you admire it; call it a doorstop and you're done reading.

    opus: An opus is a creator's major work, measured in importance rather than pounds — a symphony can be an opus and weigh nothing at all. A tome is measured at the shelf: physical size and heft, whatever the contents amount to. Opus praises the achievement; tome weighs the object.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    leaflet: a single folded sheet

    booklet: slim and stapled — a pocket swallows it

    Where it sits · how much physical heft it has

    booklet → textbook → tome

    booklet vs. tome: A booklet is the featherweight end of the line — stapled pages you read standing up and forget in a coat pocket, as far from a tome as print gets.

    textbook vs. tome: A textbook stops short of a tome — a schoolbag staple, heavy enough to notice on your shoulder, while a tome is heft you plan a whole shelf around.

    Word Choice

    informal-wry / bookish

    Tome carries a wink: it praises a book's weight while teasing it. For neutral counting, volume; for a systematic study whatever its size, this unit's treatise. If it fits in a pocket, it is not a tome, however serious it feels.
