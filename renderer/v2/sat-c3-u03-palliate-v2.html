palliate

/PAL-ee-ayt/ - the stress falls on PAL

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Palliate means to reduce the severity of something without curing it.

See the word in context

The vet was frank: the pills would palliate the old dog's arthritis — easier stairs, longer walks — but the joints themselves would keep wearing.

The bridge's corrosion runs too deep to repair this decade, the county admits. Meanwhile it palliates: paint over the worst rust, lower weight limits, a yearly inspection — measures that slow nothing underneath but keep the crossing usable while the replacement is designed.

Palliate is the exact verb because it promises only surface relief: the severity drops while the disease — here, the corrosion — stays. Mitigate would suggest the harm itself had been cut down; palliate concedes that underneath, nothing is cured.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    soothe: To soothe is to calm how something feels right now — a cool cloth, a kind voice, relief that lasts only as long as the soothing does. Palliate is more clinical and more sustained: it manages a symptom's severity over time while openly admitting the cause goes untreated.

    comfort: Comfort aims at the person's spirits — company, reassurance, a warmer blanket — and it may never touch the symptom at all. Palliate works on the symptom itself, cutting its severity for real stretches even though the disease underneath goes on uncured.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    cure: to remove the cause — the very thing palliating admits it cannot do

    sharpen: to give the pain a keener edge

    Where it sits · how far the relief reaches

    dull → palliate → heal

    dull vs. palliate: Dulling is the smallest mercy — the edge of the pain fades for a while — and it stops short of palliating, which manages the whole symptom for real stretches of time.

    heal vs. palliate: Heal goes further than palliate — palliation keeps the patient comfortable while the illness stays, but healing ends the illness itself.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / clinical

      Palliate is the most candid of this unit's relief verbs: built into it is the admission that nothing is being cured. Medicine uses it exactly that way — palliative care aims at comfort, not cure. Alleviate and mitigate leave that question open; palliate answers it. Outside medicine it can even sound evasive: to palliate a scandal is to soften how it looks.
