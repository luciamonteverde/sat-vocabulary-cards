Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Arid means extremely dry, with very little rainfall.

See the word in context

The atlas colors the country's whole interior ochre for arid: under ten inches of rain a year, most of it in one violent week.

Two thousand years ago, engineers in one of the most arid uplands on earth solved a problem their descendants still live by. Rain would not come, but snow sat on the far mountains, so they dug — gently sloping tunnels, mile after mile, that walk mountain water underground to the villages without losing it to the sun. Some of those tunnels still run, and the orchards at their mouths are the only green for a day's travel.

Arid is the exact word for that upland because it means extremely dry, with very little rainfall — not a bad summer but the standing condition of the place, its settled character. Dryness you can wait out is a drought; dryness you must engineer your whole civilization around is arid.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    bone-dry: Bone-dry is the everyday exaggeration for a thing with no moisture left in it right now — a creek bed, a roast, a sponge. It describes a momentary state, and a total one. Arid is a climate verdict: a place where serious rain almost never comes, decade after decade.

    rainless: Rainless is a plain fact about a stretch of weather — a rainless month is simply one in which no rain fell. Arid turns that fact into the character of a whole place: rainfall so scarce, year after year, that life there must be built around the lack.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    humid: air heavy with moisture

    rain-soaked: drenched by weather an arid region never sees

    Where it sits · how lasting the lack of rain is

    dry → parched → arid

    dry vs. arid: Dry is the mildest word on this scale: a lawn is dry after one rainless week, and the next shower undoes it entirely — while arid weather is the kind that never really breaks.

    parched vs. arid: Parched goes further than dry: a drought's depth, cracked earth and gasping plants — but one hard storm can still end it; arid claims the most, dryness as the settled character of a place, counted in decades rather than seasons.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / scientific

      Arid is the climate scientist's word: it describes places, not weeks, and it measures rainfall, not temperature. Keep it apart from this unit's torrid, which measures heat — a Gulf coast summer can be torrid and dripping wet at once, and a high cold desert is arid under frost. For a dry spell that will someday break, parched or drought-stricken is the honest choice.
