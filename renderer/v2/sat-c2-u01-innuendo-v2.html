Meaning

What It Means

Innuendo means an indirect hint that implies something damaging.

See the word in context

Her congratulations arrived wrapped in innuendo: 'Amazing what some people accomplish when the head judge is a family friend.'

Nobody on the team accused Dev of anything. There was only innuendo: 'Funny how the equipment fund came up short the same season somebody's got new cleats.' The remark named no one and claimed nothing—yet by Friday half the locker room was glancing at Dev's feet.

Innuendo does its damage while staying deniable: an accusation can be tested and answered, but an innuendo never quite says the thing, so there is nothing to disprove and no one to confront.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    dig: A dig is an informal jab — a quick, teasing remark aimed at someone, usually delivered openly enough that everyone catches it. An innuendo works under cover: the damaging suggestion is planted indirectly, never quite said, so its maker can deny meaning anything at all.

    aspersion: An aspersion is a remark that attacks someone's honesty or reputation, classically in the phrase 'to cast aspersions,' and it can be made openly and plainly. An innuendo inflicts the same damage sideways: the charge is only implied, wrapped in innocent-sounding words, and stays deniable.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    accusation: a direct charge, stated openly where it can be answered

    declaration: a plain, public statement

    Where it sits · how open and hostile the suggestion grows

    hint → innuendo → smear

    hint vs. innuendo: A hint merely points somewhere without saying it; an innuendo points at somebody, and the somewhere it points is always unflattering.

    smear vs. innuendo: A smear is louder than innuendo: innuendo damages through one deniable hint, while a smear is an open campaign of ugly claims.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / journalistic

    Innuendo is the noun for the damaging hint itself; insinuate, its verb partner in this unit, names the act of planting one. Use innuendo when reporting or judging the tactic—'a campaign of innuendo'—and keep plain hint for suggestions that carry no sting.
