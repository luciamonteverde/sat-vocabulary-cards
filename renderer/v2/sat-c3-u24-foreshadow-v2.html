What It Means

Foreshadow means to hint at events that will come later in a story.

See the word in context

The video game's quiet first level foreshadows its finale: the tutorial teaches you to mend a bridge you will need, hours later, to escape across.

The playwright plants her hints early: a fire marshal inspects the theater in scene one, chatting about frayed wiring nobody fixes. Nothing burns until act three — but when it does, the audience realizes the play foreshadowed its own blaze all along.

Foreshadow is a storyteller's verb: the early detail gestures at what is coming without announcing it. A prophecy or a spoiler states the future outright; foreshadowing works sideways, so the ending arrives as a surprise the reader somehow already expected.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    portend: To portend is what an omen or event does all on its own — dark clouds portend a storm, falling sales portend layoffs. To foreshadow is an author's deliberate craft: a writer plants the early hint so the ending can pay it off.

    augur: To augur is to be a sign of things to come, good or bad — rising sales augur a strong year. Foreshadow keeps that forward pointing but moves it into storytelling: a crafted hint, placed on purpose, that the plot will cash in.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    recall: to look backward at what has already happened

    recap: to run through past events again — foreshadowing runs the story forward instead

    Where it sits · how plainly the coming twist is signaled

    hint → foreshadow → telegraph

    hint vs. foreshadow: A hint is the lightest touch — one stray detail a reader may never notice; it stops short of foreshadow, which builds a pattern the ending will cash in.

    telegraph vs. foreshadow: Telegraph goes further than a hint ever does — a telegraphed twist is signaled so plainly that the audience sees it coming a mile off, spoiling the surprise foreshadowing protects.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / literary

      Foreshadow belongs to storytelling: writers, composers and directors foreshadow. When events themselves are the sign — dark clouds, falling sales — portend or augur fit better; when a person simply announces the future, that is predicting, not foreshadowing. Save the word for crafted hints that pay off later.

By chapter nine, the misdelivered mail had become the whole case, and the barking closet gave up the missing will.
