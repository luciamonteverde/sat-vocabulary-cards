Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Itinerant means traveling from place to place, especially for work.

See the word in context

For thirty years the island churches shared one itinerant organist, who crossed with the Tuesday ferry and played six services in four days.

The farrier's truck is a forge on wheels, and her week is a wheel too: Monday the racing yard in the valley, Wednesday the trail-riding outfit up the pass, Friday whichever small barns have called in. Horses need shoeing where horses live, so the anvil goes to them. She has no shop, no storefront, no fixed address between dawn and dark — an itinerant smith with no shortage of appointments, ever.

Itinerant is the exact word for that working life, because it means traveling from place to place, especially for work. It is the opposite of drifting: her route is planned to the hour and every stop expects her. What the word rules out is not order but a fixed workplace — the work itself is what moves.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    peripatetic: Peripatetic is the bookish cousin of itinerant — Aristotle's students learned while strolling, and the word still smiles a little at a life spent moving from post to post. Itinerant is the plainer working word, the one histories use for judges, preachers, and laborers on a circuit.

    nomadic: Nomadic describes a whole way of life that moves — herders and households shifting camp with the seasons, home and all. Itinerant is narrower and about work: the itinerant farrier or judge makes a living by rounds, while the home, if there is one, stays put.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    resident: living and working in one fixed place

    settled: rooted, with no plans to move on

    Where it sits · how untethered the life becomes

    traveling → itinerant → rootless

    traveling vs. itinerant: Traveling is weaker than itinerant: anyone on a single trip is traveling, while an itinerant worker goes further, making constant movement from place to place the shape of a working life.

    rootless vs. itinerant: Rootless goes past itinerant — the itinerant worker keeps a route and is awaited somewhere next Tuesday; the rootless have no route, no rounds, and nowhere they are due.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / historical

    Itinerant dignifies a working life on the move — itinerant judges, preachers, laborers — and histories lean on it. It implies a circuit, not aimlessness: for roaming with no route at all, rootless or drifting is honest. Keep it apart from this unit's migrate: migration is a population's move between regions with the seasons, while the itinerant worker makes a living by rounds, in any season. And mind the sound-alike itinerary, which is the paper, not the person.
