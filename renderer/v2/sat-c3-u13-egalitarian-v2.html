egalitarian

/ih-gal-ih-TAIR-ee-un/ - the stressed syllable rhymes with 'air'

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Egalitarian means holding that all people deserve equal rights, status, and opportunity — or built so that they actually get them.

See the word in context

The chess club stayed stubbornly egalitarian: grandmaster or beginner, every member waited in the same signup line for a board.

The new director made the orchestra egalitarian in a single season: auditions moved behind a screen, every chair rotated monthly, and the last stand of the violas drew the same rehearsal solos as the concertmaster.

Notice what the word claims: not that the players are identical in skill, but that the institution levels their standing — equal access, equal voice, equal treatment. An egalitarian arrangement is about the worth people are granted, not the sameness of what they can do.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    even-handed: treating the parties in front of you without favoritism, case by case — a referee can be even-handed for one match. Egalitarian is the standing creed behind such conduct: everyone, everywhere in the system, deserves the same footing.

    democratic: run by the vote of the many — a way of making decisions. Egalitarian is a claim about worth rather than procedure: a democracy can still rank its citizens sharply, while an egalitarian order levels their standing.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    elitist: holding that a superior few deserve to lead and to have more

    hierarchical: arranged in ranks, with some standing above others

    Where it sits · how far the drive for equality reaches

    fair-minded → egalitarian → leveling

    fair-minded vs. egalitarian: Fair-minded is milder than egalitarian: a fair-minded person plays no favorites in the matter at hand, while an egalitarian holds the sweeping conviction that everyone deserves equal standing everywhere.

    leveling vs. egalitarian: Leveling goes further than egalitarian: an egalitarian wants equal rights and equal worth recognized, while a leveling program sets out to erase distinctions of rank and wealth altogether.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    principled / political

    Egalitarian pledges more than niceness: it asserts that people deserve equal standing, and it usually describes arrangements — charters, pay scales, institutions — or the people who fight for them. It is not a synonym for generous, and it is not about matching objects: a gift can be lavish without being egalitarian, and identical houses make a street uniform, not equal. When rank is earned and celebrated, the right word is meritocratic — and an egalitarian would still object.

Investors warned her that talent would flee, but she held to the egalitarian design. Desks were assigned by lottery, and the intern's opinion in hiring counted like anyone's.
