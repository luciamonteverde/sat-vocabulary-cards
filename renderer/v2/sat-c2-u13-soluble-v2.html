Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Soluble means able to dissolve in a liquid.

See the word in context

Washable-marker ink is soluble by design, which is why the art smock survives the school year.

Rock candy begins with a fact children refuse to believe: sugar is so soluble that half a kilo will disappear into a single cup of near-boiling water. The syrup looks like nothing. Then a string goes in, the jar sits on a shelf, and for two weeks the trick runs backward — as the water cools and departs, the sugar has nowhere to stay, and it climbs out of hiding one glittering crystal at a time.

Soluble is the exact word for the sugar, because it means able to dissolve in a liquid — to break up into particles too small to see and vanish evenly into it. The property belongs to the solid: sugar is soluble in water the way iron is not, and the whole candy trick is just that ability, lent and then taken back.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    dissolvable: Dissolvable says only that a thing can be dissolved — the plain, do-it-yourself word. Soluble is the term chemistry and labels prefer, the one that comes with conditions attached: soluble in what, at what temperature, how completely.

    dispersible: Dispersible means breaking into particles fine enough to spread evenly through a liquid without truly dissolving — the near-miss printed on medicine labels. Soluble goes the whole way: the solid passes into the liquid itself, invisibly, and stays gone.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    undissolvable: stays solid no matter how long the stirring goes on

    waterproof: made to shed liquid entirely — waterproof ink is exactly the ink that will not wash away

    Where it sits · how completely it disappears into liquid

    mixable → soluble → instant

    mixable vs. soluble: Flour is mixable — beaten through water it makes a cloudy suspension, and by morning it has settled back out; soluble goes further than mixable: the solid truly disappears into the liquid and stays gone.

    instant vs. soluble: Instant goes past soluble — a plain soluble powder still wants a stir and a little patience; an instant one is engineered to vanish the moment it lands in the liquid.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      technical / scientific

      Soluble describes the solid that vanishes; this unit's solvent names the liquid that swallows it — keep the pair pointed in opposite directions, since a chemistry answer that swaps them is wrong twice. Labels lean on soluble (water-soluble vitamins, soluble aspirin); in looser prose a soluble problem is one that can be solved, the same Latin loosening applied to a knot of a different kind.
