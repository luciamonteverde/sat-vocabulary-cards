insurgent

/in-SUR-jent/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Insurgent means a person who rebels against established authority, especially as part of an armed group.

See the word in context

Soldiers at the border crossing detained a suspected insurgent who was carrying hand-drawn maps of the army's fuel depots.

After the military council seized his village's fishing fleet, a former schoolteacher became an insurgent, ferrying food and ammunition at night to fighters hidden in the coastal swamps.

The schoolteacher does not just resent the government; he actively joins and supplies an armed rebellion against it. A person who takes part in that kind of armed resistance to authority is an insurgent.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    rebel: A rebel is anyone who fights against their government or resists control, and the word stretches from armed fighters to a rebellious teenager; it can even sound admiring. An insurgent is specific and military: an armed member of an organized uprising, the word of war reporting.

    mutineer: A mutineer rises against his own commanding officers; the word belongs to ships, barracks, and crews, where rebellion turns on the chain of command from inside it. An insurgent fights the established government or occupying authority itself, as part of an armed movement.

    guerrilla: A guerrilla is a fighter defined by method: small, irregular bands relying on ambush and hit-and-run raids. The word tells you how someone fights. Insurgent tells you why: the fighter belongs to an armed rebellion against established authority, whatever tactics that rebellion uses.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    loyalist: a person who stays faithful to the ruler or government

    defender: someone who protects the established order

    Where it sits · how far defiance of authority goes

    dissenter → insurgent → revolutionary

    dissenter vs. insurgent: A dissenter openly disagrees with those in power, but an insurgent takes up arms to fight against them.

    revolutionary vs. insurgent: An insurgent fights to bring down the current authority, but a revolutionary aims to sweep away the whole system and build a new one.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Neutral / journalistic

    Most common in war reporting, usually in the plural: 'insurgents attacked,' 'armed insurgents.' An insurgent is the person who rebels, while an insurrection, from the same Latin root surgere ('to rise'), is the uprising itself; keep the person and the event distinct. The word can also work as an adjective ('insurgent forces'), but this unit teaches the noun.

    Covering the mountain war for a wire service, Dana Reyes learned that her hardest choices were words, not photographs. Her editors used insurgent for the armed men of the northern movement, and they used it carefully. The word marked a fighter as part of an organized, armed uprising against the provincial government -- no more, no less. A farmer who cursed the governor was not one; neither was a boy throwing stones, whatever the checkpoint soldiers muttered. When a colonel demanded her outlet call the movement's fighters bandits instead, Reyes refused: a bandit steals for himself, she wrote, while these men fought, however brutally, for a cause that wanted the government gone.
