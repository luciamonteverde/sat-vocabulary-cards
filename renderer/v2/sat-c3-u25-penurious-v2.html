penurious

/puh-NYOOR-ee-uhs/ - rhymes with curious

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Penurious means extremely unwilling to spend money.

See the word in context

Our penurious landlord swapped the lobby bulbs for ones so dim that reading the mail required a flashlight.

Aunt Verna, penurious to the bone, re-wrapped every present she received for future re-giving, logged the household's rubber bands in a ledger, and counted exact bus fare from a jar labeled 'fare, not tips.'

Penurious describes the grip on the purse, not the size of it—an impecunious person has no money to spend, while a penurious one may be rich and still refuse to spend a cent. It is stinginess worn as a way of life.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    miserly: Miserly means hoarding money for its own sake — the miser loves the pile and suffers to keep it whole. Penurious describes the same shut wallet as a way of life: an extreme unwillingness to spend, whatever the motive behind it. The miserly cherish the hoard; the penurious simply refuse to open it.

    stingy: Stingy means giving or spending less than a situation fairly calls for — skimping on the tip, the portion, the heating. Penurious goes further and hardens it into character: the penurious refuse to spend at all, as a settled way of life. Stingy shortchanges an occasion; penurious padlocks the wallet.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    openhanded: giving freely, without being asked twice

    freehanded: spending and giving with easy generosity

    Where it sits · how fiercely the wallet stays shut

    thrifty → tightfisted → penurious

    thrifty vs. penurious: Thrifty stops far short of penurious — thrift is sensible saving and a virtue.

    tightfisted vs. penurious: Tightfisted stops short of penurious — the tightfisted hate opening the wallet, while the penurious act as if it were padlocked.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / disapproving

    Penurious condemns refusal to spend — the padlocked wallet. 'Thrifty' and 'frugal' praise the same instinct in sensible amounts, so choose by verdict. And keep the unit's money pair straight: impecunious means the money is missing; penurious means the willingness is.
