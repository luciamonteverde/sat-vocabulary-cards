cohesive

/koh-HEE-siv/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Cohesive means sticking together as one well-connected whole; unified in a way that already holds a group or work together.

See the word in context

The debate team was so cohesive that each speaker built seamlessly on the argument the one before had started.

Critics praised the album as remarkably cohesive, with every song sharing melodies and themes so that the whole record felt like one continuous piece.

The songs were written as separate tracks, but they hold together as a single artistic whole. Cohesive describes that quality of parts sticking together as one unit.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unified: Unified means joined and acting as one. A group can also act unified for an audience — a coordinated public display of agreement staged for one occasion — even while quarreling inside. Cohesive makes the stronger claim that the group genuinely holds together as one connected unit, no staging required.

    tight-knit: Tight-knit describes people bound by close personal relationships — a tight-knit family or village where everyone knows everyone. Cohesive is broader and cooler: a team, argument, or album is cohesive when its parts connect into one whole, with or without personal warmth.

    interconnected: Interconnected just says the parts are linked to one another, the way roads or computer networks are. Cohesive adds that the linked parts hold together as one whole — a cohesive essay does not merely cross-reference itself; it reads as a single connected unit.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    fragmented: broken into disconnected pieces

    divided: split into disagreeing parts

    Where it sits · how close the bond is

    compatible → cohesive → inseparable

    compatible vs. cohesive: Compatible teammates merely get along; a cohesive team moves and thinks as a single unit.

    inseparable vs. cohesive: A cohesive group works as one, but an inseparable pair goes even further — you never find one without the other.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Neutral — academic

    Cohesive describes the state of already holding together as one unit — a cohesive team, a cohesive argument, a cohesive neighborhood. Contrast with two process words taught separately in this unit: amalgamation and consolidation both name the process of combining separate things into one, while cohesive describes the quality a group or work has once it genuinely sticks together. A group can be merged by some process and still fail to be cohesive.
