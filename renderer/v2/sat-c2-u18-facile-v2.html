Meaning

What It Means

Facile means superficial; achieved with too little thought or care.

See the word in context

The seminar's rule, posted on the door, was that facile answers — the ones that fit on a bumper sticker — earn a follow-up question.

The tutoring app promised a facile fix for the district's math scores: fifteen gamified minutes a day. Two years and one audit later, scores were flat, and the teachers who had warned that fractions don't yield to streaks and badges were finally given the budget for smaller classes.

Facile is the right verdict because facile means superficial — achieved with too little thought or care. The word's sting is that it concedes the surface appeal: a facile fix looks neat, sells well, and dissolves on contact with the actual problem. Simple is a virtue; facile is simplicity that skipped the work.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    shallow: Shallow measures depth and nothing else: little lies beneath the surface, whether of a person, a friendship, or an idea. Facile points at the workmanship — an answer achieved with too little thought, its surface appeal intact. Shallow describes what a thing is; facile blames how it was made.

    glib: Glib describes a speaker — fluent, quick, and persuasive on the surface, with the thought missing underneath. Facile judges the product instead: the argument or fix achieved with too little care. A glib salesman talks past hard questions; a facile plan simply never met one.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    probing: digging past the first answer

    thorough: the work actually done, corner to corner

    Where it sits · how little real substance lies underneath

    pat → facile → hollow

    pat vs. facile: A pat answer is suspiciously neat — rehearsed-sounding, a little too smooth — but pat stays milder than facile: pat may still be true, while facile means the thinking was skipped.

    hollow vs. facile: Hollow goes further than facile — the facile argument is thin but holds something; the hollow one collapses at the first press.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    critical / intellectual

    Facile is reviewer's vocabulary — a dismissal of arguments, fixes, and endings that bought neatness with skipped thought. Keep it apart from its unit-mate obtuse: the obtuse listener is slow to see the point; the facile thinker sees one too quickly and stops. Beware the compliment trap: in older books a facile pen just meant a fluent one.

But transportation engineers have measured what actually happens for seventy years: new lanes fill within months, because easier driving invites more drivers. The op-ed never mentioned this, never asked why the last three widenings had failed, and never priced its own proposal. The appeal of the piece was real. The work behind it was not.
