caustic

/KAW-stik/ - the stress falls on KAW

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Caustic means biting and bitterly sharp — criticism or wit that burns like the acid the word originally named.

See the word in context

Renata's caustic thank-you note — 'the fruitcake arrived intact, more's the pity' — cost her two invitations the following year.

The retiring theater critic's farewell column was caustic to the last line: he thanked one director for 'thirty years of intermissions that always came as a relief,' and the town's stages felt the burn of it for a season.

The compliment is the vehicle and the acid is the cargo: the line is witty enough to laugh at, sharp enough to wound, and corrosive enough that the damage outlasts the laughter. Caustic — literally 'burning,' the word chemists still use for substances that eat through whatever they touch — describes exactly that kind of remark.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    biting: Biting describes a remark sharp enough to sting the moment it lands, though the smart soon fades. Caustic keeps eating at its target long after the laughter stops — the acid works slower and deeper.

    scathing: Scathing is brutally severe and openly condemning — no jokes, no disguise. Caustic does its damage dressed as wit: the insult arrives smiling.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    gentle: mild and kind, meant not to hurt

    soothing: calming — easing a sting rather than causing one

    Where it sits · how deep the words burn

    sharp → caustic → vitriolic

    sharp vs. caustic: Sharp is weaker than caustic: a sharp remark pricks and is done, while a caustic one burns on like the acid it is named for.

    vitriolic vs. caustic: Vitriolic is stronger than caustic: caustic wit still earns a laugh even as it burns, while vitriol is hatred poured out too pure and undiluted for anyone to laugh at.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      critical / wry

      Caustic began in the chemistry lab and never lost the burn: caustic soda corrodes grease, and caustic wit corrodes reputations. Reserve it for sharpness with acid in it — mockery meant to wound. Sarcastic covers any mocking edge; caustic promises damage that lingers after the joke.

      The playwright answered his critics by telegram, and collectors now trade the telegrams like rare stamps. The most famous, sent to a rival who had panned his opening night, read simply: 'Saw your play too. Get well soon.' Audiences at the time roared; the rival reportedly kept the telegram in a drawer and reread it, wincing, for years. Biographers call the playwright's humor caustic for exactly this reason — the joke landed lightly and then went on burning. Even his targets admitted the wit; what they could not do, they said, was ever quite scrub it off.
