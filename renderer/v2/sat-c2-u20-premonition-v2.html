Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

A premonition is a strong feeling, arriving before any evidence, that something specific — usually something bad — is about to happen.

See the word in context

Leena's premonition had no shape she could point to — only that the tour bus should leave without her — and by evening the mountain road was on the news.

Halfway through the third act, the stage manager was seized by a premonition about the chandelier — no creak, no sway, nothing an inspector could have flagged. She moved the chorus six feet downstage anyway, and felt foolish right up until the counterweight let go at the curtain call.

Premonition is the word for that unreasoned certainty of what is coming: a warning felt in advance of any evidence, about one specific thing ahead. It is not a deduction and not a sign — nothing in the theater pointed to the fall. The warning simply arrived first, from nowhere the stage manager could name.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    foreboding: Foreboding is the same dark weather without the address: a general heaviness that something bad is on its way. A premonition points — at the ferry, the chandelier, the old tree — a feeling with one specific event in mind.

    hunch: A hunch is a quick intuitive guess about how things stand — who did it, which drawer, which stock — and it carries no dread and no timetable. A premonition is the hunch's graver cousin: it concerns what is about to happen, and it usually arrives cold.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    hindsight: understanding that arrives only after the event

    obliviousness: no inkling at all of what is coming

    Where it sits · how forcefully the future announces itself

    inkling → premonition → prophecy

    inkling vs. premonition: An inkling is far fainter than a premonition — the first faint suspicion that something may be coming, easy to shrug off, while a premonition grips hard enough to change what you do.

    prophecy vs. premonition: A prophecy goes further than a premonition: a premonition is a private feeling about what is coming, while a prophecy is declared aloud as authority — a claim that the future is already written.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      atmospheric / narrative

      Premonition keeps three promises: the feeling comes before the event, it comes without evidence, and it points at something specific — usually something bad. Its neighbors each break one: a forecast is reasoned from data, an omen is an outward sign waiting to be read, and foreboding is dread without an address. On the SAT it appears most often in fiction; read it as characterization — what the character fears — never as the passage's proof that the event will come.
