Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Enmity means ill will, hatred, hostility.

See the word in context

By closing night the enmity between the play's two leads was real enough that the director took her bows standing between them.

The two racing team principals have not spoken since the contested finish of 2009. Their motorhomes park at opposite ends of the paddock; their engineers are banned from sharing so much as a tire gauge; and when one stepped away from the sport, the other came back the same week, seemingly to keep the feud alive. The paddock calls their enmity the sport's coldest war.

Enmity is the exact word because it names ill will that has settled in and made itself at home — hatred as a standing condition between two parties. A quarrel is an event and an insult is a moment, but enmity is weather: it was there last season and it will be there next. The word's own bones say it plainly — un-friendship, made to last.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    hatred: Hatred is the broad word for the feeling itself, and it can attach to anything — a food, an idea, a stranger — with no relationship behind it. Enmity is hatred settled between particular parties: two sides who know each other, return the feeling, and have carried it long enough for it to become the relationship.

    animosity: Animosity is strong ill will that can flare in a moment — two drivers and one parking space can produce it by lunchtime. Enmity has moved in for years: durable, usually mutual, with a history behind it. Animosity names the heat of the feeling; enmity names the standing state of un-friendship.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    goodwill: friendly, wishing-you-well feeling

    friendship: the bond enmity un-makes

    Where it sits · how far bad blood spreads and acts

    grudge → enmity → vendetta

    grudge vs. enmity: A grudge is one nursed grievance — a single splinter kept; enmity is the whole relationship gone dark.

    vendetta vs. enmity: Vendetta goes past enmity: the hatred has a to-do list, acted on and often inherited.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Enmity is for hatred that has settled between two parties and stayed — feuds, cold wars, estranged partners. Its neighbors in this unit divide the work: hostility is the open unfriendliness you can feel in a room today, and antipathy is one party's deep dislike, which the other side may never return. Enmity is mutual, durable, and usually has a history.

    The two villages face each other across a narrow alpine valley, close enough to share weather and church bells. Their enmity is nearly four hundred years old, older than any grievance anyone can document. It is scrupulously mutual: each village schedules its festival on the other's, and neither cheese may be sold at the other's market. Historians who ask about the original insult get a shrug; the feeling no longer needs a cause. When a flood closed the valley road, both mayors sent rescue crews -- and both denied it afterward, unwilling to let four centuries of habit be spoiled by one good deed.
