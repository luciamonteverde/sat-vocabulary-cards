parasitic

/par-uh-SIT-ik/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Parasitic means living on or in a host organism and benefiting at that host's expense.

See the word in context

The parasitic mistletoe taps the oak's own sap channels and gives the tree nothing in return.

A cuckoo lays one egg in a reed warbler's nest and flies away. The warbler incubates it, and the cuckoo chick, larger and quicker to hatch, pushes the warbler's own eggs over the rim. For six weeks the pair of warblers hunt insects from dawn to dusk to feed a chick three times their size, and raise none of their own young. The cuckoo's parasitic strategy costs its host an entire breeding season.

The cuckoo gains a whole season of free care while the warblers lose every one of their own young, and benefit to one at the direct expense of the host is what parasitic means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    leeching: Leeching is the vivid, accusing word for draining a person or system a little at a time, the way a leech draws blood. Parasitic is the biologist's term for the whole arrangement: one organism living on or in a host and profiting at that host's cost.

    exploitative: Exploitative means taking unfair advantage of another for gain, and it belongs to human dealings -- an exploitative contract or employer. Parasitic names a biological relationship: an organism physically living on or in its host and feeding at the host's expense.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    mutualistic: benefiting both partners in the relationship

    self-sustaining: able to feed and support itself without a host

    Where it sits · how much the host suffers

    dependent → parasitic → ruinous

    dependent vs. parasitic: Dependent says only that one organism needs another, while parasitic adds that the host pays the cost of that need.

    ruinous vs. parasitic: Ruinous is stronger than parasitic, since many parasites weaken a host that survives for years, while a ruinous infestation destroys it outright.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      scientific; also figurative and insulting

      In biology a parasite takes from a living host over time and usually does not kill it outright, which separates parasitic from predatory in this unit: a predator kills and eats its prey, while a parasite feeds on a host kept alive to go on feeding it.

      To an aquarium visitor, the green crab looks perfectly ordinary: it feeds, scuttles, and defends its corner as any crab would. Hidden inside its shell, the barnacle Sacculina has spread a network of fine tendrils through the crab's body, siphoning off a share of every meal the crab catches. Marine biologists class the arrangement as parasitic, and the crab's condition explains the label. An invaded crab stops molting and stops growing; the energy that would have built new shell goes to the barnacle instead. Sacculina, for its part, supplies nothing -- no defense, no cleaning, no food -- and can live off the same crab for years.
