constrain

/kuhn-STRAYN/ - rhymes with train

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Constrain means to forcibly restrict.

See the word in context

A sprained wrist constrained the pianist to pieces written for the left hand alone.

The old bridge's weight limit constrains the whole island economy: delivery trucks cross half-loaded, the quarry ships stone in small batches, and no builder can pour a foundation in a single day.

Constrain is limit with force behind it: something outside your control — a rule, an injury, a weight limit — presses your options into a narrower space. Notice its frames: constrained by the rule, constrained to the smaller set of choices it leaves.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    cramp: Cramp squeezes the room a thing has to work or grow in — a tiny kitchen cramps a cook's style. Constrain is force from outside pressing your options into a narrower set: a rule, an injury, a budget. Cramped, you work awkwardly; constrained, some choices are simply gone.

    bind: Bind ties someone down with obligation — a contract, a promise, a duty they cannot shrug off. Constrain presses from outside with no promise involved: weather, budgets, and injuries constrain people who never agreed to anything. You are bound by your word; you are constrained by your circumstances.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unleash: to release something to run at full force

    loosen: to slacken the grip and give more room

    Where it sits · how little room for movement remains

    limit → constrain → immobilize

    limit vs. constrain: Limit is the milder, everyday word — a ceiling you bump against; constrain adds the feel of force pressing the bounds in on you.

    immobilize vs. constrain: Immobilize goes further than constrain: what constrains you leaves some room to move, while what immobilizes you leaves none.

      neutral / formal

      Constrain is the working word for force pressing options into a narrower space — budgets, rules, injuries. Circumscribe (this unit) is more formal and draws the boundary line around a whole scope; fetter (this unit) is more literary and hangs chains on the picture.
