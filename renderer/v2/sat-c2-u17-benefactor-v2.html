Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Benefactor means a person who gives money or help to a person or cause.

See the word in context

A brass plaque in the lobby thanks the benefactor whose gift built the skating rink and whose visits kept it open.

The town band owned two dented trumpets until Mr. Aoki retired from his orchard. He bought the band a full set of instruments, then kept showing up — a benefactor who drove the tuba to away games, paid for repairs, and taught the youngest players to oil their valves. Money was only half of what he gave.

Benefactor is the exact word for Mr. Aoki, because a benefactor is a person who gives money or help to a person or cause — the definition holds both halves of what he did. Donor is narrower: a donor writes the check, and one check is enough; a benefactor's support tends to keep arriving.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    patron: A patron backs artists or institutions, usually with money and often expecting a little credit — the named gallery, the program note. A benefactor may want no billing at all: the word simply names the person whose money or help a cause could thank.

    philanthropist: A philanthropist gives to better life for people in general — hospitals, scholarships, whole causes — and the word stays large in scale and public in feel. A benefactor can work at any scale and usually stands behind one person or cause: the student, the band, the rink that could thank him.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    beneficiary: the receiver of the gift — the mirror image

    freeloader: takes support endlessly and gives nothing back

    Where it sits · how substantial and sustained the giving is

    supporter → donor → benefactor

    supporter vs. benefactor: A supporter is far weaker than a benefactor — a supporter may give nothing but applause, signing petitions and cheering from the sidelines, while a benefactor keeps the cause itself afloat.

    donor vs. benefactor: A donor goes further than a supporter — money actually changes hands — but a single gift is enough to be a donor, while a benefactor keeps a person or cause afloat.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / appreciative

      Benefactor names the person behind the giving. Keep the family straight: benevolent describes the kindly disposition, largess and munificence describe the giving itself, and bequeath is how a benefactor gives one last time — through a will. Use benefactor when you mean the human being a cause could thank.
