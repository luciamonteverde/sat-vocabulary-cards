Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Adept means extremely skilled.

See the word in context

Long winters made Aunt Vera adept at mending nets, her shuttle moving faster than the eye could follow.

The station's traffic reporter is adept at reading the city: give her three camera feeds and a rainfall report, and she will tell you which bridge will jam eight minutes before it does, rerouting half the morning's drivers around a stall they never see.

Adept is the right word because adept means extremely skilled — not merely able to do the job, but so practiced that the hard parts look routine. Skilled says you clear the bar; adept says you clear it every time, without visible effort, usually after years of repetition.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    proficient: Proficient means solidly capable: you meet the standard reliably, and nobody worries about the result. Adept sits a rung higher — the skill repeated until the difficult parts stop showing. Every adept sailor is proficient; plenty of proficient sailors still sweat the tricky moorings.

    practiced: Practiced tells you where the skill came from — repetition, hours logged — without saying how far it climbed; a practiced apology can still be a clumsy one. Adept names the height itself: skill so complete the work looks easy. You can be practiced and mediocre, never adept and mediocre.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    incompetent: unable to do the job at all

    amateurish: the rough edges showing

    Where it sits · how high the skill level runs

    capable → adept → masterly

    capable vs. adept: Capable is milder than adept — the job gets done correctly, and that is all capable promises; adept does the same job with a polish that years of repetition buy.

    masterly vs. adept: Masterly goes further than adept — the adept sailor wins the regatta; a masterly one seems to teach the wind itself how the race should go.
