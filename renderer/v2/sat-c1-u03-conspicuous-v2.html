What It Means

Conspicuous means easy to notice; standing out clearly from the surroundings in a way that attracts attention.

See the word in context

Wearing a bright orange raincoat in the middle of a gray, rainy city made her conspicuous from a block away.

During the stakeout, the rookie detective parked his shiny red convertible directly across from the suspect's building, a choice so conspicuous that the suspect noticed him within the hour and slipped out the back.

The bright, unusual car practically announced the detective's presence — nothing about it blended in, which is exactly what "conspicuous" means: impossible to miss.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    prominent: Prominent means standing out because of importance, rank, or position — a prominent lawyer, a prominent role. Conspicuous is about the eye: something stands out visually from its surroundings. A prominent citizen may dress plainly and never look conspicuous at all.

    eye-catching: Eye-catching describes appealing looks that draw a pleased second glance — a poster or a dress designed to attract. Conspicuous is neutral about appeal: a stain, a blunder, or a bright coat can be conspicuous, standing out whether or not anyone enjoys looking.

    glaring: Glaring describes what stands out harshly and unpleasantly, almost always a fault — a glaring error, a glaring omission. Conspicuous is milder and more general: it says something is easy to notice, without insisting the thing is bad.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    inconspicuous: the direct opposite — deliberately blending in

    camouflaged: specifically disguised to match the surroundings

    Where it sits · how strongly it draws the eye

    noticeable → obvious → conspicuous

    noticeable vs. conspicuous: A noticeable stain might catch your eye if you're looking closely; a conspicuous one draws attention on its own.

    obvious vs. conspicuous: An obvious stain is impossible to miss even at a glance, and conspicuous goes a step further: it doesn't just fail to hide, it actively pulls the eye toward itself.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral, slightly formal

    Conspicuous is a fairly neutral, versatile word — it can describe something admirably eye-catching or something embarrassingly obvious, depending on context. 'Conspicuous by its absence' is a common set phrase for something so expected that its absence is itself noticeable.
