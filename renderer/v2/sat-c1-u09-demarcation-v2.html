demarcation

/dee-mar-KAY-shun/

Meaning

What It Means

Demarcation means the marking of a clear boundary or dividing line between two things.

See the word in context

The surveyor drove bright orange stakes along the property line, and that demarcation finally ended the neighbors' argument over where one yard stopped and the other began.

The textbook uses the invention of the printing press as its demarcation between the medieval period and the early modern era.

Demarcation fits because the printing press serves as a clear dividing line between two categories of history — everything before it belongs to one era, everything after it to another, with the boundary explicitly marked.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    delineation: Delineation is the precise outlining or describing of something's limits — a job description delineates duties, a sketch delineates a coastline. It is the act of drawing or spelling out. Demarcation is the established dividing line itself, fixed and marked between two things. You produce a delineation on paper; a demarcation stands on the ground.

    boundary: A boundary is any line that marks the limit of an area or category — a fence, a county line, a personal limit — whether or not anyone marked it deliberately. Demarcation stresses the deliberate act and its result: the line is explicitly drawn and marked, so the two sides are set apart beyond argument.

    borderline: The borderline is the line where one thing ends and another begins, and the word often shades into 'almost' — a borderline case sits right at the edge of a category. Demarcation is firmer and more formal: a dividing line explicitly drawn and marked out so the two sides cannot be confused.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    blurring: making a line or difference unclear

    merging: combining two things into one

    Where it sits · how firmly the two are set apart

    distinction → demarcation → gulf

    distinction vs. demarcation: A distinction is weaker — it notes a difference between two things without demarcation's sense of an explicitly drawn, marked-out line.

    gulf vs. demarcation: A gulf is stronger than a demarcation — it implies a vast, hard-to-bridge separation between two things, far beyond a line that has simply been drawn and labeled.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      Formal / technical

      Demarcation shows up in law, labor, geography, and formal writing — often in the set phrases 'line of demarcation' and 'demarcation dispute' (a labor term for arguments over which workers may do which jobs). In casual speech, people just say 'dividing line' or 'boundary.'
