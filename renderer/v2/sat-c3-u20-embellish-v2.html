embellish

/em-BEL-ish/ - rhymes with relish

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Embellish means to decorate or adorn with ornamental details.

See the word in context

The luthier embellished the guitar's soundhole with a ring of inlaid mother-of-pearl.

For the mayor's centennial visit, the ironworker embellished the plain park gates with curling vines, oak leaves, and a pair of hammered copper wrens — by unveiling day, visitors were photographing the gates instead of the park.

Embellish is more particular than 'decorate': it means adding ornamental extras to something already complete, purely to make it richer to look at. The gates worked fine before; the vines and wrens are embellishment. The word stretches to stories, too — a fisherman embellishes a tale with details that were never there.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    gild: Gild means to coat something with a thin layer of gold — one exact, literal treatment. Embellish is the umbrella above it: adding ornamental extras of any kind for beauty's sake. Every gilded edge is embellished, but you can embellish with thread, paint, or iron and never touch gold.

    festoon: Festoon means to drape something with garlands, chains, or streamers — ornament that hangs and swings. Embellish is broader and quieter: it covers any decorative addition, including detail stitched, carved, or painted flat onto a surface, where festooning always leaves something dangling.

    garnish: Garnish is the kitchen's word for ornament: a sprig of parsley or a twist of lemon set on a finished plate. Embellish does the same job everywhere else — gowns, gates, manuscripts — and it can pile on far more than a single finishing touch.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    strip: to pull every ornament off, down to the bare surface

    simplify: to remove detail until only the essentials remain

    Where it sits · how heavily the flourishes pile on

    accent → embellish → encrust

    accent vs. embellish: To accent something is to add one small touch — a single ribbon on a plain box; it stops well short of embellish.

    encrust vs. embellish: Encrust goes further than embellish — ornament piled on ornament until the original surface disappears beneath the crust.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / slightly formal

      Use embellish when extras are added for beauty's sake — needlework, ironwork, the margins of a manuscript — or when a storyteller improves on the truth. Plain decorate does everyday duty; gild and festoon name particular kinds of embellishing. And note the quiet warning in the story sense: an embellished account is prettier than what actually happened.

She chose to embellish the border with tiny emblems of local life -- a rowboat, a thimble, a line of geese -- each no bigger than a thumbprint.
