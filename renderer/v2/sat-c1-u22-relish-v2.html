relish

/REL-ish/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Relish means to enjoy something greatly, savoring it with real appreciation.

See the word in context

The debate captain openly relished the toughest cross-examination questions, leaning into each one like a favorite song.

Grandpa Miro tells the story of the runaway tractor exactly once per family dinner — never twice, never rushed. He sets down his fork, waits for the table to quiet, and stretches every pause like taffy, savoring the groans when everyone realizes which story is coming. He has told it two hundred times, and he relishes telling number two hundred one just as much.

Miro's unhurried pauses and evident savoring of every groan show him wringing full, appreciative enjoyment from the telling — that is what it means to relish something.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    savor: To savor something is to take it in slowly and attentively, dwelling on every detail — a technique of noticing more than a feeling. To relish it adds hearty delight: real, felt enjoyment on top of the attention. You can savor a broth while merely studying it; you relish it when it makes you happy.

    delight in: To delight in something is simply to take great pleasure in it — even at a distance, in news you merely heard about. Relishing is closer and slower: you turn the particular thing over and enjoy it in detail. Delight is the pleasure itself; relish is pleasure taken slowly, up close.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    dread: to anticipate with fear or distaste

    loathe: to feel intense dislike for

    Where it sits · how intense the enjoyment gets

    like → relish → devour

    like vs. relish: To like something is mild approval; to relish it is to enjoy it deeply, savoring every part.

    devour vs. relish: To devour something — a book, a meal, a challenge — is even hungrier than relishing it: enjoyment turned into fast, eager consumption.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    warm, versatile; everyday-to-literary

    Relish is contained, savoring appreciation — you relish one particular thing, like the irony of a moment or a well-made meal — while revel (also in this unit) throws off all restraint: revelers fill the streets, but a person can relish a sentence in perfect silence.
