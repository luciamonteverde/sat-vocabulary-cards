Meaning

What It Means

Indoctrination means the teaching of a set of beliefs so thoroughly that they are accepted without question.

See the word in context

The sect's catechism ran to ninety questions, and indoctrination began with the first: every answer given word for word, or repeated until it was.

The movement's summer camps photographed beautifully: lakes, campfires, hiking badges. The schedule told another story — indoctrination by the hour. A song at reveille, a song before meals, an oath before swimming; every badge stitched with a slogan, every ghost story ending in the founder's rescue. Veterans of those summers could still sing every verse fifty years on — and could not recall one evening when anyone had asked what the verses meant.

Indoctrination is the exact word for those camps, because indoctrination means the teaching of a set of beliefs so thoroughly that they are accepted without question — the missing questions are the tell. Education hands you the beliefs and the tools to test them; indoctrination hands you the beliefs and quietly takes the tools.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    conditioning: Conditioning trains automatic responses — the reflex, the habit, the flinch — answers the body gives before thought gets a vote. Indoctrination works one level up: it drills the thoughts themselves, until the beliefs feel like reflexes and questioning them no longer occurs to anyone.

    propaganda: Propaganda is the outward-facing campaign — posters and broadcasts aimed at strangers a movement hopes to sway. Indoctrination points the same machinery inward, drilling the movement's own members until the beliefs are held without question. Propaganda persuades outsiders; indoctrination trains insiders.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    education: hands over the tools of doubt along with the facts

    inquiry: starts from the very questions indoctrination removes

    Where it sits · how much independent questioning survives

    instruction → indoctrination → brainwashing

    instruction vs. indoctrination: Instruction is far milder than indoctrination: it simply hands the material over — the manual, the drill, the theorem — and welcomes a raised hand; indoctrination teaches so thoroughly that the raised hands stop.

    brainwashing vs. indoctrination: Brainwashing goes further than indoctrination — the indoctrinated student has stopped asking questions; the brainwashed one can no longer form them.

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / critical

    Indoctrination is an accusation about method: the beliefs may even be true, but they were installed, not examined. Propaganda is its outward-facing cousin — posters aimed at strangers, while indoctrination drills its own. And do not let the spelling pull you toward this unit's induce: induce leads (Latin ducere), indoctrination teaches (docere) — look-alike prefixes on unrelated roots.
