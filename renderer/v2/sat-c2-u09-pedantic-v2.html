Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Pedantic means overly focused on minor rules and details, often to show off knowledge.

See the word in context

The wine club's newest member turned every toast pedantic, pausing mid-sip to correct the vintage by a single year.

The hiking club's message board asked a simple question: which trailhead has parking? Its resident expert answered in nine paragraphs — the surveyed elevation of each lot to the decimal, the Forest Service's numbering conventions since 1962, and a closing correction of the asker's spelling of 'chaparral.' The parking question died somewhere in paragraph two. That performance — knowledge deployed to show itself off on details nobody needed — is pedantic in full flower.

Pedantic is the exact word because it captures both halves: minor rules and details, plus the showing off. An exacting editor sweats details in service of the work; the pedant sweats them in service of being seen knowing. The tell is the audience — the exacting writer keeps the reader in mind; the pedant keeps the mirror.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    nitpicking: Nitpicking faults every small thing it can reach — a comma here, a rounding error there — but it stays a habit of criticism. Pedantic behavior needs a further ingredient: the parade of learning — details policed chiefly so the expert's knowledge gets seen.

    hairsplitting: Hairsplitting draws distinctions too fine to matter — differences real only under a magnifying glass. A pedantic performance often splits hairs, but the word adds a motive: minor rules pressed on an audience mainly to show off what the presser knows.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    easygoing: relaxed about the small stuff

    relaxed: untroubled by minor slips

    Where it sits · whom the strictness really serves

    careful → exacting → pedantic

    careful vs. pedantic: A careful proofreader catches what matters and stops there; pedantic strictness drifts past that point — off the work itself and onto trifling rules, with the reader left behind.

    exacting vs. pedantic: An exacting reviewer demands much but honestly serves the work; pedantic goes further — the demands drift to the smallest rules, and the point quietly becomes the demonstrator's own learning.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      dry / critical

      Pedantic is never praise: it says the details have stopped serving the work and started serving the speaker. Reach for it when someone polices trifles to show off learning — and notice what it is not: an exacting surgeon or a careful auditor is paid precisely for that rigor. The pedant's rigor is unpaid, unasked for, and aimed at an audience.
