onerous

/OH-nuh-ruhs/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Onerous means burdensome; describing a duty, task, or obligation that weighs on someone as a heavy, oppressive load.

See the word in context

The lease came with onerous conditions, including weekly inspections and a security deposit worth three months' rent.

Counting the warehouse's entire inventory by hand fell to Priya's crew, an onerous assignment that kept them scanning shelves through three straight overnight shifts.

The assignment demands an enormous, exhausting amount of effort that weighs heavily on the crew for nights on end. A duty that presses down on people like a heavy load is exactly what onerous describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    burdensome: Burdensome means loading someone down with heavy work or worry, and it is the closest everyday twin of onerous, usually interchangeable with it. Onerous is the more formal word and clings to duties and obligations: contracts, conditions, and requirements are onerous when their weight feels oppressive.

    taxing: Taxing means draining: a taxing day makes heavy demands on your energy, patience, or strength while it lasts, and the word measures the toll on you in the moment. Onerous measures the standing weight of an obligation that oppresses you between the hard days too.

    laborious: Laborious means requiring long, hard work; the sweat is the point, hours of effort in the doing. Onerous is about weight rather than work: a duty can be onerous even when each task is easy, because it is imposed, endless, and oppressive to carry.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    effortless: requiring no work at all

    light: easy to bear or carry out

    Where it sits · how hard it is to bear

    demanding → onerous → crushing

    demanding vs. onerous: A demanding job asks a lot of you; an onerous one weighs on you like a heavy burden you can barely carry.

    crushing vs. onerous: A crushing workload goes beyond onerous, so overwhelming that it flattens any hope of keeping up.

    Apply

    Word Choice

    Formal / legal

    Onerous appears often in formal and legal contexts, in collocations like onerous duties, onerous obligations, and onerous terms of a contract. Distinguish it from arduous, which stresses the strenuous physical effort a task takes; onerous stresses the oppressive weight of a duty or obligation on the person stuck with it.
