anthology

/an-THOL-uh-jee/

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Anthology means a collection of selected writings, poems, or songs by various authors, published together.

See the word in context

For her brother's birthday, Amara chose an anthology of science-fiction stories, so he could sample twelve different writers in a single book.

For the museum's new exhibit on the transcontinental railroad, curators placed a leather-bound anthology in the reading corner — letters, articles, and memoirs by dozens of different writers who had watched the railroad being built, gathered into one published volume.

One volume gathering selected writings by many different authors — letters, articles, and memoirs from dozens of writers — is precisely what an anthology is.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    collection: Collection is the broadest word for works gathered together — one author's essays, one museum's paintings, anything assembled at all. An anthology is a specific kind of collection: selected writings by many different authors, published together in one volume. Every anthology is a collection; a collection needs no variety of voices.

    compilation: Compilation stresses the labor of assembling material from many sources, and the material can be anything — statistics, film clips, songs. An anthology is narrower: a published volume of selected writings by various authors. Call the spreadsheet of election results a compilation; call the book of thirty writers' stories an anthology.

    treasury: Treasury presents gathered works as precious — a treasury of verse promises gems worth keeping. An anthology makes no claim about value; it is simply selected writings by various authors published in one volume. Treasury adds a tone of celebration that a plain classroom anthology does not carry.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    monograph: a scholarly work on one subject by a single author — one voice, not many

    excerpt: a single passage standing alone, rather than many works gathered together

    Where it sits · how complete the collection aims to be

    sampler → anthology → compendium

    sampler vs. anthology: A sampler offers just a few short pieces to give readers a quick taste — slimmer than an anthology, which brings together a full selection of writings by many authors.

    compendium vs. anthology: A compendium goes further than an anthology: it tries to gather everything important on a subject in exhaustive detail, not just a chosen selection.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral, literary

    Anthology is a neutral word you will meet constantly in English classes — most literature textbooks are anthologies. The key idea is many authors between two covers: if one person wrote the whole book, it is not an anthology. A work included in one is said to be 'anthologized.'
