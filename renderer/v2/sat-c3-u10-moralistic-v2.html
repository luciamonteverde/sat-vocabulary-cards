moralistic

/mor-uh-LIS-tik/ - the stress falls on LIS

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Moralistic means quick to lecture others about right and wrong; preachy.

See the word in context

The hiking forum's most moralistic member answers every gear thread with a short sermon on the vanity of ultralight equipment and the decay of self-reliance.

What began as a cooking show turned moralistic by season three: each recipe now arrives with a little homily on thankfulness, waste, and the character of people who buy pre-chopped onions.

Moralistic is more precise than 'strict' or 'serious' because it points at the lecturing itself: a moralistic person turns every topic into a lesson about right and wrong, invited or not. Being moral is living by principles; being moralistic is preaching them at the table.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    judgmental: Judgmental means quick to condemn — the verdict arrives fast, sticks, and needs no words at all. Moralistic adds the sermon on top of the verdict: the moralistic person must also deliver the lecture about right and wrong. Every moralistic uncle is judgmental, but a judgmental one might only glare.

    sermonizing: Sermonizing is the act itself — delivering little moral lectures no one requested, one speech at a time. Moralistic names the habit of mind behind them: the standing urge to grade everyone's conduct as right or wrong. Sermonizing is what a moralistic person is caught doing out loud.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    permissive: lets choices slide without comment

    tolerant: lives comfortably beside habits it doesn't share

    Where it sits · how hard standards are pushed on others

    principled → moralistic → self-righteous

    principled vs. moralistic: A principled person holds firm standards quietly — well short of moralistic, which cannot hold a standard without delivering it as a lecture.

    self-righteous vs. moralistic: Self-righteous goes further than moralistic: the lecturing continues, and the lecturer has privately concluded that no one else could possibly measure up.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      critical / wry

      Moralistic is almost always a complaint — about narrators, uncles, and editorials that lecture. Don't confuse it with moral (actually good) or with careful ethical reasoning. Among its unit-mates, solipsistic and sophomoric describe other ways a speaker wears on you: the solipsistic one sees only himself, the sophomoric one shows off, and the moralistic one grades your soul over dessert.
