Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Veracity means truthfulness; conformity to fact.

See the word in context

The hiring manager doubted the veracity of a resume listing 'fluent in eleven languages' beside a misspelled hometown.

Historians prize the sea captain's log for its veracity: wherever an entry can be checked—against port records, tide tables, another ship's account—it holds, down to the hour of each storm. A record that survives that much cross-examination earns trust even on the pages no one can check.

Veracity is narrower than 'honesty': honesty is a person's overall virtue, while veracity is the truth-to-fact quality of statements and accounts. A kind, honest man can still write a log with poor veracity if his memory embroiders.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    truthfulness: Truthfulness is the everyday virtue of telling the truth, and it lives in a person's intentions. Veracity is the formal, testable version that courts and editors invoke: whether an account actually matches the facts once checked. A truthful narrator means every word; veracity asks whether the entries hold up.

    accuracy: Accuracy is correctness of detail, and it needs no one behind it — a map can be accurate, and so can a guess. Veracity belongs to tellers and their accounts: it is truthfulness measured against fact, so it can be doubted only where someone is making a claim.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    falsehood: an untrue statement

    dishonesty: the habit or practice of deceiving

    Where it sits · how strictly words must match reality

    sincerity → veracity → infallibility

    sincerity vs. veracity: Sincerity means you believe what you say; veracity sets the higher bar—what you say must also match the facts.

    infallibility vs. veracity: Infallibility towers over veracity: a witness of perfect veracity tells the truth as the facts show it, while an infallible one could never be wrong at all—a standard no witness meets.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / legal

      Veracity is the courtroom-and-editing-desk word for truthfulness—of witnesses, documents, and claims. It is the standard the rest of this unit offends against: spurious papers fake it, fallacious arguments cannot deliver it, and a distortion bends it. Use honesty for a person's broad character; save veracity for whether an account matches the facts.

      In 1936, a climber named Edda Voss returned from the Karakoram claiming a solo first ascent of a peak called the White Horn. Reporters loved her; rival climbers did not, and they questioned the veracity of her account in every alpine journal that would print them. Voss never argued. She handed the editors her negatives, her barometric log, and the torn page of a tea-house register from the far side of the pass. A panel spent two winters checking: the shadows in the photographs matched the date, the pressure readings matched the altitude, the register matched the route. The claim held. What her rivals had treated as a story to doubt turned out to be an account that agreed, point by point, with the facts.
