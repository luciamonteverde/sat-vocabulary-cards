Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Scrupulous means painstakingly careful and honest.

See the word in context

The scrupulous landlord itemized the deposit deductions to the penny — and waived the scratch he couldn't prove was new.

When the miracle result made headlines, the scrupulous Dr. Paz spent her week of fame correcting people. She told the radio host the effect was small; she published the two failed trials alongside the famous one; she wrote to a senator's office to un-promise what a press release had over-promised. Nothing obliged her to do any of it. Her conscience kept books no auditor would ever ask to see.

Scrupulous is the exact word because Paz is painstakingly careful and honest — the care and the honesty fused into one habit. The Latin scrupulus was a sharp pebble in the shoe: the nagging small discomfort that will not let you walk on. A scrupulous person feels that pebble at the first shading of the truth, and stops.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    upright: Upright describes the broad moral posture — a person who deals straight and stands where honor stands. Scrupulous zooms in: the same honesty applied at fine grain, down to errors too small for anyone else to notice. An upright reputation is earned over years; a scrupulous habit shows in every small entry.

    conscientious: Conscientious means dutiful and careful in work: assignments done fully, deadlines honored, standards kept without being watched. Scrupulous adds honesty as the point of the care — the conscience itself does the auditing. A conscientious employee finishes the report properly; a scrupulous one also flags the numbers that flatter her.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    sloppy: careless in the details, cutting corners

    unprincipled: no inner rules to consult at all

    Where it sits · how far the moral exactness goes

    honest → scrupulous → saintly

    honest vs. scrupulous: An honest clerk tells the truth when asked; a scrupulous one hunts down the five-cent error nobody would ever have found.

    saintly vs. scrupulous: Saintly goes past scrupulous — beyond exact honesty into self-sacrifice no ledger could require.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / approving

      Scrupulous lives on the moral axis: care whose fine grain is honesty. This unit's meticulous measures the same fineness with no conscience attached — a meticulous forger is a professional description; a scrupulous forger is a contradiction in terms. Fastidious, meanwhile, is about taste and thresholds. When the detail being sweated is right versus wrong, the word is scrupulous.

Instead he spent a weekend being scrupulous: he traced every stub, matched each one to the cash box, and found the missing eight dollars in a mislabeled envelope. Then he did something stranger. He reported an extra forty cents nobody had noticed, overpaid by some parent at the door, and taped it to the ledger. Nobody would have caught either error. The club's adviser had already offered to write off the difference. Osei refused. Money that is almost right, he said, is a habit that ends somewhere worse.
