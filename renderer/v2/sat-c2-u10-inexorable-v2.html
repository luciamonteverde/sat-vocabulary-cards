Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Inexorable means impossible to persuade or hold back; deaf to every plea, appeal, or delay.

See the word in context

The tide was inexorable; the children's seawall of sand, engineered all afternoon, was gone in four unhurried waves.

The scholarship committee's chair listened to every appeal with perfect courtesy — the injured athlete, the family that had moved twice in a year, the teacher who drove eighty miles to testify — and granted none of them. Deadlines, she repeated, were deadlines. By spring the applicants had a private name for her office: the department of the inexorable.

Inexorable names what pleading cannot reach. The Romans built it from orare, to plead or pray: an inexorable judge was one no prayer could bend. The word now stretches to impersonal forces — an inexorable tide, the inexorable arithmetic of a budget — and the stretch keeps the original point intact: argue, beg, or bargain, the outcome does not move.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    relentless: Relentless measures pressure that never lets up — relentless rain, a relentless interviewer — and it says nothing about persuasion, because nobody argues with rain. Inexorable adds the shut door: appeals were possible, appeals were made, and appeals moved nothing.

    unyielding: Unyielding describes the stance: a person or position that will not bend under pressure. Inexorable describes the outcome of every possible approach — not just firm against force, but deaf to pleading, unmoved by the best case anyone can make.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    flexible: willing to bend or adjust

    merciful: moved by appeals to soften a penalty

    Where it sits · how deaf it stands to appeal

    firm → adamant → inexorable

    firm vs. inexorable: Firm is far weaker than inexorable: a firm official holds the line today but can still be brought around by a strong enough case, while inexorable means no case will ever be strong enough.

    adamant vs. inexorable: Adamant is strong but still one rung below inexorable: an adamant refusal announces that persuasion is unwelcome, while inexorable means persuasion is impossible — the tide does not attend the hearing.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / grave

      Inexorable carries a note of doom — the word for verdicts, tides, and arithmetic that will not hear you out. Keep it apart from implacable, taught in this same unit: implacable is anger that nothing soothes, while inexorable is broader — any will or process, angry or serene, that no appeal can slow. A committee can be perfectly inexorable without a flicker of hostility.
