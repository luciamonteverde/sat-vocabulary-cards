equitable

/EK-wih-tuh-bul/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Equitable means fair and just in a way that accounts for people's different needs or circumstances.

See the word in context

The scholarship fund uses an equitable formula, so students facing the greatest obstacles receive the most support.

Principal Ramos had one pot of tutoring money and two very different schools to serve. Instead of splitting it fifty-fifty, she funded three extra tutors at the school where most students had no internet at home, and one at the school where most families already paid for private help. Some parents grumbled that the split was unequal. Ramos explained that identical shares would have left the students who needed the most help with the least support, and the board agreed her plan was the more equitable one.

The funding split is equitable because it accounts for the two schools' different needs to reach a genuinely fair outcome, even though the shares are not identical.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fair: Fair is the everyday, all-purpose word for treating people in a reasonable, acceptable way, and it fits any situation from board games to courtrooms. Equitable is fair's formal cousin with a sharper edge: an equitable arrangement takes people's different needs and circumstances into account. Every equitable plan is fair, but casual fairness needs no such calculation.

    balanced: Balanced means giving appropriate weight to all sides — a balanced news report, a balanced committee. Equitable is about outcomes for people: whether a distribution genuinely serves those with different needs and circumstances. A report can be balanced between two views; only a policy that answers people's unequal situations is equitable.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unjust: morally wrong or unfair

    lopsided: heavily uneven, favoring one side

    Where it sits · how high a standard of fairness

    acceptable → equitable → just

    acceptable vs. equitable: An acceptable arrangement is merely good enough, while an equitable one is genuinely fair to people with different needs.

    just vs. equitable: An equitable policy treats people fairly in practice, while a just one meets the highest standard of moral rightness.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal register

      Use equitable for policies, distributions, and outcomes that are genuinely fair once people's different needs are taken into account; equal shares are not always equitable shares. Equitable focuses on fair outcomes, unlike parity, which measures equal levels between two things, or impartial, which describes an unbiased judge or process.
