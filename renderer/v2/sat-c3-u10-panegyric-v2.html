panegyric

/pan-uh-JEER-ik/ - the stress falls on JEER

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Panegyric means a formal speech or piece of writing that praises someone highly.

See the word in context

The stadium program opens with a panegyric for the club's oldest season-ticket holder — sixty years of loyalty recounted without one word of reproach.

For the ship's christening the company commissioned a formal panegyric in verse — six stanzas honoring the naval architect's every decision, to be read from the bow before the champagne.

Panegyric is more precise than 'speech' or 'tribute' because it names praise at full volume and full length — formal, public, and unmixed with criticism. A toast admires for thirty seconds; a panegyric builds its subject a monument out of sentences.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    encomium: An encomium is a composed, formal piece of glowing praise — a panegyric's nearest twin, and like a panegyric it is a deliberate production rather than a passing remark. Where the two differ is scale: an encomium can live comfortably in a page, while a panegyric builds the full public monument, delivered at length.

    eulogy: A eulogy is formal praise for someone who has died, delivered at a funeral or memorial. A panegyric is every bit as formal, but its subject is typically alive to blush — often seated in the front row. If the honoree can hear it, panegyric is the word.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    diatribe: a bitter verbal attack — praise's exact reverse

    takedown: a piece written to dismantle someone's reputation

    Where it sits · how formal and elaborate the tribute is

    praise → toast → panegyric

    praise vs. panegyric: Plain praise is the everyday material — a kind word at any volume — and it stops far short of a panegyric, which builds praise into formal architecture.

    toast vs. panegyric: A toast is warmer and more public than a stray kind word, but it stops short of a panegyric: one raised glass and a minute of affection, not an hour of organized glory.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / ceremonial

    Panegyric is the word for praise as a formal production — commissioned, public, and total. Use 'tribute' for everyday honors and 'toast' for a raised glass. Writers often deploy it with an eyebrow raised: calling a review 'a panegyric' hints the critic forgot to criticize. Its shadow-twin in this unit is vitriolic — total praise and total acid, two ways of abandoning balance.
