Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Reticent means hesitant to speak up; keeping one's thoughts to oneself.

See the word in context

Grandpa stayed reticent about his years at sea; asked directly, he would nod at the horizon and change the subject.

Three lunches into her first week, all anyone at the workshop knew about Imani was that she kept bees — and that only because a jar of honey had appeared on the break table with a small handwritten label. Reticent but warm, she answered questions briefly, asked better ones back, and returned to her sandwich. The crew liked her enormously and knew almost nothing about her.

Reticent is the exact word for Imani, because it means hesitant to speak up, keeping one's thoughts to oneself — the silence is chosen, comfortable, and aimed mostly at her own affairs. Guarded would imply wariness, something protected; reticence needs no threat at all, only a preference.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    tight-lipped: Tight-lipped is the informal twin of reticent, and it usually clamps shut on one subject in particular — the coach goes tight-lipped about the starting lineup. Reticent describes a person's whole manner: answers short, opinions unoffered, on most topics and most days.

    guarded: Guarded adds wariness to the quiet — a guarded speaker weighs every word for risk, protecting something from listeners who might use it. Reticent needs no threat at all: the silence is a comfortable preference, chosen and kept even among people who are trusted.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    talkative: the chatter flows freely on any topic

    forthcoming: offers information before being pressed

    Where it sits · how much of oneself stays held back

    private → reticent → walled-off

    private vs. reticent: Private is milder than reticent — a few doors politely closed, plenty still shared — while the reticent keep most of their thoughts unspoken as a general habit.

    walled-off vs. reticent: Walled-off goes further than reticent — a reticent colleague still answers when pressed, while a walled-off one lets nothing through at all.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / restrained

      Reticent describes people, and their manner, when thoughts stay unspoken: answers short, opinions unoffered. Tight-lipped is the informal twin, usually about one topic; guarded adds wariness. Two boundaries matter: reluctant — a cousin in this unit's reference list — is unwillingness to act, while reticence is unwillingness to speak; and recalcitrant, this unit's louder mate, refuses authority outright. The reticent refuse nothing; they simply are not saying.
