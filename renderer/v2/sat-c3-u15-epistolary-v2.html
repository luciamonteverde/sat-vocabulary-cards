Meaning

What It Means

Epistolary means carried on through or consisting of letters.

See the word in context

The playwright built the second act as an epistolary duet: two soldiers reading aloud the letters they exchanged across enemy lines.

Their courtship was entirely epistolary: he was posted to a lighthouse and she taught in the capital, so for three years everything — jokes, quarrels, the proposal itself — traveled by the weekly mail boat.

Epistolary is the exact word for something carried on through letters, from a friendship to a novel's whole structure; 'written' is too broad, and 'postal' describes the mail system rather than the relationship or the form.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    postal: Postal describes the mail system that moves the letters — stamps, carriers, sorting offices. Epistolary describes what the letters add up to: a friendship, feud, or novel carried on through them. A postal strike is about delivery; an epistolary novel is about letters doing the telling.

    handwritten: Handwritten says only that a pen, not a keyboard, produced the words — a grocery list qualifies. Epistolary is about form, not penmanship: an exchange conducted through letters, sent and answered. A typed love letter is epistolary but not handwritten.

    textual: Textual describes words as text on a page — textual evidence, a textual error — and belongs to editing and analysis. Epistolary names a form instead: an exchange carried on through letters. A scholar's textual notes may study an epistolary novel; the notes concern wording, the novel is made of letters.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    oral: carried by the spoken voice

    face-to-face: conducted in person, with no letters at all

    Where it sits · how much the conversation travels by mail

    spoken → written → epistolary

    spoken vs. epistolary: A spoken exchange commits nothing to paper, stopping far short of an epistolary one, which lives entirely in letters.

    written vs. epistolary: Written covers anything set down on paper, but it stops short of epistolary, which says the exchange itself was conducted through letters, mailed and answered.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / literary

      Epistolary is a book-review and history-seminar word. Use it for novels told in letters and for friendships, feuds, or courtships conducted by mail; for anything less specific, 'written' or 'by mail' is plainer and usually better.
