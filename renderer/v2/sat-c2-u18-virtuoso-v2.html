/vur-choo-OH-soh/ - four beats, stress on OH

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A virtuoso is a performer of dazzling, masterly skill — one whose command of an art, especially music, goes far beyond ordinary competence.

See the word in context

By the second encore even the stagehands had crowded into the wings to watch the virtuoso, a cellist who made the concerto's impossible passages sound like breathing.

The festival's closing set belonged to a virtuoso: the pianist pulled the tune apart mid-chorus, ran it backward through three keys, and reassembled it so cleanly that the bassist laughed out loud and the crowd was on its feet before the final chord.

Virtuoso is the precise word because a virtuoso's skill is dazzling, earned mastery on display — not merely playing well, but commanding the instrument so completely that the hardest moves look effortless. Skilled would undersell the moment, and talented would miss the years of training behind it; virtuoso names the performer whose command you can hear.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    maestro: Maestro is a title of authority — the revered master, most often the conductor or teacher the whole orchestra defers to. A virtuoso is named for the playing itself: the dazzling command an audience can hear. A maestro may never touch an instrument all evening; a virtuoso earns the word mid-performance.

    prodigy: A prodigy astonishes early — talent arriving so young it seems unearned, and the word says nothing about polish. A virtuoso's mastery is finished and trained, judged by the performance rather than the birthday. A ten-year-old prodigy may grow into a virtuoso; the second word has to be earned.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    amateur: one who plays for love, without professional mastery

    hack: a performer grinding out routine, uninspired work

    Where it sits · how dazzling the command of the art is

    competent → masterly → virtuoso

    competent vs. virtuoso: Competent is far milder than virtuoso: competent playing hits the right notes and keeps the tempo, while a virtuoso commands the instrument so completely that the hardest passages sound easy.

    masterly vs. virtuoso: Masterly is strong but still sits below virtuoso: masterly playing shows full command of the craft, while a virtuoso adds the dazzle — difficulty dispatched so brilliantly that audiences come just to watch it done.
