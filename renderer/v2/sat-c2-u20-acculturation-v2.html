Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Acculturation is the gradual taking-on of another culture's customs and habits through long contact with it — new ways absorbed while much of one's own culture stays.

See the word in context

Her first year abroad was one long acculturation: new greetings, new queue manners, new small talk — and her grandmother's Sunday recipes untouched.

When the Duarte family moved from Lisbon to Winnipeg, acculturation crept in through the small things. Within two winters the children were talking hockey at breakfast and packing maple cookies beside the custard tarts; Mr. Duarte learned to shovel a neighbor's walk without being asked. And still, every Christmas Eve, the table filled with salt cod, exactly as it had at home.

Acculturation is the exact word for that slow shift, because it names the process of absorbing a new culture's ways through steady contact — greetings, foods, and habits picked up year by year. It is not erasure: the Duartes take on Canadian customs without surrendering their Portuguese ones. When the old identity dissolves entirely, the word is assimilation.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    immersion: Immersion is the setting, not the change: you can be immersed in a culture — surrounded by it, schooled in it — for a season and come home unchanged. Acculturation is what long immersion can produce: the new ways actually take root in your habits and speech.

    integration: Integration is the civic, two-way word — a community and its newcomers adjusting institutions until both fit. Acculturation happens inside the newcomers themselves: it names the customs and manners one group's members absorb from another.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    insularity: keeping to one's own culture, sealed off from contact

    resistance: refusing the new culture's ways outright

    Where it sits · how much of the original culture gives way

    adjustment → acculturation → assimilation

    adjustment vs. acculturation: Adjustment is milder than acculturation: an adjustment swaps a schedule or a phrase to get through the week, while acculturation reworks habits, manners, and speech through years of contact.

    assimilation vs. acculturation: Assimilation goes further than acculturation — acculturation adds a second culture's ways alongside your own, while assimilation absorbs the original culture until little of it remains visible.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / social-science

    Acculturation is the social scientist's word for culture rubbing off through contact, and it keeps both cultures in the frame — the new one arriving, the old one persisting. When the original identity is swallowed outright, the word is assimilation; when a body rather than a culture adapts — to altitude, to heat — the word is acclimatization. On the SAT it lives in history and social-science passages about migration and empire, always as a process of years, never a single event.
