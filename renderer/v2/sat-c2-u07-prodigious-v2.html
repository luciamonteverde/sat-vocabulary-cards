Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Prodigious means remarkably large or impressive in size or amount.

See the word in context

The eruption threw a prodigious column of ash nine miles up — crews could see it from three countries.

The port's new gantry crane hoists a prodigious load: three locomotives in a single swing, with dockworkers of thirty years stopping mid-shift to watch it happen. The harbor has bigger machines on paper. It has nothing else that makes the whole pier put down its coffee.

Prodigious is 'big' with a gasp built in. Immense states a size; prodigious reports the astonishment — the root is Latin prodigium, an omen or marvel. A prodigious load isn't merely heavy. It is heavy enough to stop the pier.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    immense: Immense states plain hugeness and stops there — an immense warehouse, an immense debt — with no gasp included. Prodigious is size that astonishes: scale remarkable enough to stop onlookers where they stand. Everything prodigious is immense; only the immense thing that makes people stare earns prodigious.

    staggering: Staggering reports the onlooker's reaction: a number or size so large it stuns whoever meets it. Prodigious praises the thing itself for astonishing scale. The two often trade places in a sentence, but staggering keeps the camera on the stunned witness, while prodigious keeps it on the marvel doing the stunning.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    slight: small enough to overlook

    puny: weakly small — almost laughably so

    Where it sits · how much wonder the scale stirs

    sizable → impressive → prodigious

    sizable vs. prodigious: Sizable is worth a note in the minutes; prodigious empties the room onto the sidewalk to look.

    impressive vs. prodigious: An impressive feat earns a long look and a low whistle; prodigious goes further — whole crowds stop mid-errand to stare.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / admiring

      Prodigious praises size until jaws drop — appetites, memories, outputs, loads. Its unit-mates carve up the territory: copious for quantities that pour, voluminous for sheer bulk, immense (its own synonym chip) for plain hugeness. One caution: prodigious is not prodigal, the spendthrift's word — a prodigious fortune is astonishing; a prodigal heir is broke.

      To drive one of London's black cabs, an applicant must first pass an examination known simply as the Knowledge. The requirement is prodigious: candidates memorize some twenty-five thousand streets and twenty thousand landmarks, then prove they can link any two of them from memory alone. Preparation swallows three to four years. Candidates circle the city on mopeds, maps clipped to the handlebars, running the routes until the whole map lives in their heads. Examiners, hardly strangers to good memories, still describe watching a candidate recite a perfect route across the entire city as something close to a magic trick. Neuroscientists who scanned veteran cabbies found the memory centers of their brains measurably enlarged -- the mark of a feat that continues to astonish even the people who study it.
