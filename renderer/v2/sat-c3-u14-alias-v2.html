Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Alias means a false name used in place of one's real one.

See the word in context

The fugitive rented rooms under a different alias in every town, paying cash so that no register could tie the names together.

After testifying against the cartel, the accountant was relocated under an alias; the name on her new license appeared in no file her old life could reach.

Alias is sharper than nickname or pen name because an alias exists to conceal. A nickname is affectionate and public, and a pen name is an open convention of publishing — but an alias is a false name doing the work of hiding who you really are.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    pseudonym: A pseudonym is a false name adopted openly, often by a writer or artist — readers know the pseudonym hides a legal name and accept the game. An alias exists to conceal identity itself: the whole point of an alias is that someone is hiding.

    assumed name: An assumed name is the law's neutral term for any name a person takes on, honest or otherwise — a business can register one openly. An alias is the shadier cousin: a false name whose job is to keep its owner from ever being traced.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    legal name: the name the records actually show

    true identity: who a person really is, names aside

    Where it sits · how thoroughly the true identity is hidden

    nickname → pen name → alias

    nickname vs. alias: A nickname is far milder than an alias — it is affectionate, public, and hides nothing; half the point of a nickname is that everyone knows exactly who it belongs to.

    pen name vs. alias: A pen name stops short of an alias: it screens an author's name as an open convention that publishers and readers are in on, while an alias is built so identity cannot be traced at all.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / journalistic

      Alias is the plain, slightly legal-flavored word for a concealing false name — police blotters and court records say 'alias.' Pseudonym suggests an open artistic convention, and nickname is pure affection. Reach for alias when the point of the false name is that someone is hiding.
