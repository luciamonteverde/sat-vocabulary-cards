amenity

/uh-MEN-ih-tee/ - the stress falls on MEN

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Amenity means a feature that adds comfort or convenience.

See the word in context

The listing for the tiny apartment made the most of its one amenity: a washing machine tucked behind the kitchen door.

The commuter ferry's upper deck offers a small amenity that regulars guard jealously: a shelf of loaner umbrellas by the gangway, so that no one has to walk from the dock to the office soaked.

Amenity is more precise than feature or luxury because it names an extra whose whole job is comfort or convenience. The umbrellas are not part of getting the boat across the harbor, and they are not extravagant - they simply make the trip pleasanter, which is exactly what an amenity does.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    convenience: A convenience is anything that saves you time or effort - a drive-through window, an online form - wherever it happens to live. An amenity is a built-in feature of a place, and it can please without saving any effort at all: a lobby fireplace saves nothing, yet it is a classic amenity.

    perk: A perk is a small informal extra that comes with a job or a membership, granted to sweeten the deal - free coffee, an employee discount. An amenity belongs to a place rather than a position: it is a feature the building, park, or hotel itself offers everyone who uses it.

    comfort: Comfort is the pleasant, at-ease feeling itself - being warm, rested, and unbothered. An amenity is the concrete feature that produces that feeling: the heated pool, the shuttle, the in-unit washer. You feel comfort; you list, install, or advertise an amenity.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    drawback: a feature that counts against a place

    inconvenience: something that costs you effort instead of saving it

    Where it sits · how far beyond the basics it goes

    staple → amenity → luxury

    staple vs. amenity: A staple like hot water or a working stove is simply expected; calling it an amenity would be odd, because an amenity is a step beyond the basics.

    luxury vs. amenity: A luxury - a private rooftop pool, a personal chef - goes further than any amenity; amenities add everyday comfort, while luxuries announce indulgence.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral

      Amenity is the standard word of travel listings, real-estate ads, and campus brochures. Say 'perk' among friends; write amenity when describing what a building, park, or hotel offers - it signals a built-in comfort rather than a favor.

      When the Hotel Alcazar reopened after its restoration, the owners published a list of everything the building now offers its guests. Some entries were expected: a heated courtyard pool, a reading room stocked by the town library, a shuttle to the train station. Others were stranger. The hotel kept its 1920s pneumatic mail tubes, and guests may send letters hissing between floors. None of this is necessity. Hot water and clean rooms go unmentioned, because a guest simply assumes them; an amenity begins where the basics end. The tubes cost a fortune to maintain and save nobody a single minute. The manager keeps them anyway. People, she says, do not write home about the plumbing.
