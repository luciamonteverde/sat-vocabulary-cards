Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Fervent means burning with intense feeling — belief, hope, or devotion that keeps its heat.

See the word in context

The bakery's regulars are fervent defenders of the Tuesday rye — question it and see what happens.

In a fervent midnight email to the entire staff, the chef vowed that not one shortcut would survive the reopening — three hundred words, four exclamation points, one unmistakable flame.

Fervent is belief or feeling at a boil — from the same Latin root that makes this unit's effervescent fizz. A sincere supporter means it; a fervent supporter burns with it. The word loves nouns of feeling: fervent hope, fervent prayer, fervent devotion.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    passionate: Passionate is the everyday word for strong feeling of any kind — you can be passionate about soccer, a person, or pizza. Fervent is narrower and hotter: it belongs to belief, hope, prayer, and devotion, feeling that burns steadily. A passionate fan yells for a night; a fervent believer keeps the flame lit for years.

    heartfelt: Heartfelt means deeply and sincerely meant — warmth without the blaze. Fervent takes the same sincerity and sets it alight: fervent hope burns, while heartfelt thanks simply glow. When feeling is genuine but calm, heartfelt is the right word; fervent requires heat.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    halfhearted: effort with most of the heart missing

    lukewarm: neither hot nor cold — enthusiasm at room temperature

    Where it sits · how feverish the devotion burns

    wholehearted → fervent → fanatical

    wholehearted vs. fervent: Wholehearted commitment is complete and sincere, but it is calmer than fervent — all of the heart, none of the fever.

    fanatical vs. fervent: Fanatical goes further than fervent — fervent devotion still listens to reason; fanatical devotion has hung up the phone.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / emotional

    Fervent turns the heat up on sincere: use it for belief, hope, prayer, support — feeling that burns. Its noun in this unit is ardor (and ardent, in the reference list, is its near-twin adjective); its fizzy cousin is effervescent. For feeling that is deep but calm, heartfelt is the honest choice.
