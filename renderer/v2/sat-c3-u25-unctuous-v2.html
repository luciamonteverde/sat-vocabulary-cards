What It Means

Unctuous means excessively flattering in an insincere, oily way.

See the word in context

The talent scout turned unctuous the moment the girl missed her note, praising her 'bold interpretation.'

At the open house, the listing agent was unctuous from the doorbell on: our coats were 'couture,' our questions 'brilliant,' the sagging porch 'a craftsman's opportunity'—each compliment slicked on to move us one room closer to an offer.

Unctuous keeps the oil in the flattery: where 'flattering' can be warm and 'fawning' can be sincere, unctuous praise coats its target for a purpose, and everyone but the flatterer can feel the grease.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    oily: Oily is the everyday word for the same slick insincerity — compliments that slide out too smoothly to trust. Unctuous is its formal cousin and says a little more: the flattery is laid on thick for a purpose, and everyone can feel the grease. Oily names the texture; unctuous adds the agenda.

    smarmy: Smarmy is the informal jab for ingratiating behavior that makes the skin crawl — the too-wide smile, the too-warm handshake. Unctuous is the dressier insult and centers on the praise itself: compliment after compliment slicked on to serve the flatterer's purpose. Smarmy is how the person feels to you; unctuous is what their flattery is doing.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    sincere: meaning every word, with nothing performed

    plainspoken: saying it straight, without polish or flattery

    Where it sits · how calculated the flattery has become

    fawning → unctuous → groveling

    fawning vs. unctuous: Fawning stops short of unctuous — the fawning admirer is at least sincerely starstruck, while unctuous praise is oil applied with a purpose.

    groveling vs. unctuous: Groveling goes further than unctuous — groveling drops the polish entirely and simply begs.
