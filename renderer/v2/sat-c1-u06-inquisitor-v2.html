inquisitor

/in-KWIZ-ih-ter/

Meaning

What It Means

Inquisitor means a person who questions others aggressively or in a hostile, relentless manner.

See the word in context

By the third round of rapid-fire questions about her weekend, Dana told her roommate to stop playing inquisitor.

In the story's darkest chapters, the palace inquisitor summoned the frightened servants one by one and twisted each of their answers into a new accusation.

The inquisitor is not asking questions to learn; he summons frightened servants and turns their own answers into accusations. That hostile, relentless style of questioning is exactly what the word names.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    cross-examiner: A cross-examiner tests testimony in court, probing sharply but within legal rules and limits. An inquisitor recognizes no such limits: the questioning is hostile and accusing wherever it happens, courtroom or kitchen. A cross-examiner's sharpness serves procedure; an inquisitor's serves zeal.

    examiner: An examiner is a neutral person who tests or inspects — grading papers, checking eyesight — with no hostility implied at all. An inquisitor is never neutral: the word accuses someone of grilling with hostile, relentless persistence. Examiners assess; inquisitors accuse.

    prosecutor: A prosecutor is a courtroom official whose job is to accuse and argue the case against a defendant, inside the law. An inquisitor may hold no office at all: the word names a manner — hostile, relentless questioning that presumes guilt. Prosecutor names a role; inquisitor names a style.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    defender: a person who shields someone from attack or accusation

    advocate: a person who speaks up on someone's behalf

    Where it sits · how fierce the grilling gets

    questioner → interrogator → inquisitor

    questioner vs. inquisitor: A questioner simply wants information, while an inquisitor hammers at you as if you were on trial.

    interrogator vs. inquisitor: An interrogator questions you intensely and officially, but an inquisitor adds a relentless, accusing zeal.

    formal / historical-literary
