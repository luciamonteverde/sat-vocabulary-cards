Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Officious means offering unwanted advice or services.

See the word in context

An officious passenger stood at the carousel directing everyone's luggage onto 'the correct side of the belt.'

The new hire meant well but arrived officious: by Friday he had reorganized a supply closet nobody had asked about, taped laminated instructions above the coffee maker, and 'corrected' three colleagues' voicemail greetings.

Officious is not 'official': an official acts with real authority, while an officious person supplies direction nobody requested—help so insistent that it becomes interference.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    meddlesome: Meddlesome means poking into other people's private affairs — reading the mail, prying into the argument, asking questions that are none of your business. Officious intrusion is bossier: it hands out instructions and 'help' nobody requested. The meddlesome want to know your business; the officious want to run it.

    interfering: Interfering means getting in the way of what others are doing — blocking or disrupting it, whether or not any 'help' is offered. Officious is a particular style of interfering: interference dressed up as assistance, from someone acting as if they had authority. All officious people interfere; not every interferer is officious.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unobtrusive: present without pushing in, easy to overlook

    hands-off: staying out of things and letting others manage alone

    Where it sits · how far the helper oversteps

    attentive → officious → overbearing

    attentive vs. officious: Attentive stops short of officious — the attentive waiter notices what you need, while the officious one decides it for you.

    overbearing vs. officious: Overbearing goes further than officious — the officious meddler pushes unwanted help, while the overbearing one takes charge of your affairs outright.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / critical

    Officious is the precise word for uninvited supervision — the self-appointed expert straightening everyone else's work. Do not let its look fool you: official means having real authority, officious means acting as if you did. Its unit-mate punctilious is fussy about their own conduct; the officious are fussy about yours.
