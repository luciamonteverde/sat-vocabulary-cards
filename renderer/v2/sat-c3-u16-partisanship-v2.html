Meaning

What It Means

Partisanship means strong, one-sided loyalty to a party or faction.

See the word in context

Pure partisanship decides the family's game nights now: the Ramirez cousins vote as a bloc, whatever the question.

Partisanship swallowed the model-railway club whole: the diesel faction and the steam faction now publish rival newsletters and hold separate picnics.

Partisanship is loyalty running ahead of judgment — not one unfair favor (that is favoritism) but a standing allegiance to a side that settles every question in advance. Ask a partisan anything, and the side answers for them.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    tribalism: Tribalism is group loyalty so strong it overrides individual judgment — my people, right or wrong. Partisanship is the same surrender aimed at a party, faction, or bloc: the side does the judging. Tribalism names the instinct; partisanship gives it a party badge.

    factionalism: Factionalism is infighting: rival blocs inside one organization, each loyal to itself first. Partisanship is the loyalty a single member gives one of those sides. A club suffering factionalism has split into camps; a member showing partisanship votes with a camp every time.

    bias: A bias is a tilt in judgment, and it can be unconscious — a judge may not know she has one. Partisanship is declared and worn openly: standing allegiance to a side that settles every question in advance. Bias leans; partisanship has already signed up.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    impartiality: fairness that takes no side

    evenhandedness: treating all sides by the same measure

    Where it sits · how fully one side owns the judgment

    favoritism → partisanship → zealotry

    favoritism vs. partisanship: Favoritism is one unfair preference at a time — smaller than partisanship, which pledges the whole judgment to a side.

    zealotry vs. partisanship: Zealotry goes further than partisanship: loyalty hardened into fanaticism that admits no question at all.

      formal / political
