Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Profligate means dissolute, extravagantly wasteful.

See the word in context

The startup was profligate from day one, leasing three floors it never filled and catering breakfast for a staff of nine.

The duchess's household books read like a fever chart: two hundred beeswax candles a night, peaches forced in winter hothouses, a new carriage whenever mud splashed the old one — profligate spending that outran even a duchy's rents.

Profligate is harsher than 'extravagant' — extravagance overspends, but profligacy wastes with abandon, and the word's older sense of moral ruin still shadows it: a profligate life squanders more than money.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    wasteful: Wasteful is the everyday, neutral word for using more than a task needs — long showers, lights left burning. Profligate raises waste to an indictment: value poured away headlong, past caring, with a hint of vice. A habit is wasteful; a squandered fortune is profligate.

    prodigal: Prodigal means wastefully lavish with what was given — the runaway who burns through the inheritance. The word mourns the waste more than it blames. Profligate turns the same squandering into an accusation, adding the note of moral abandon that prodigal's sad story lacks.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    thrifty: careful to make every dollar work

    economical: gets the most from the least

    Where it sits · how ruinous the outlay becomes

    free-spending → extravagant → profligate

    free-spending vs. profligate: Free-spending stops short of profligate — the free-spending pick up every check and enjoy it; nothing is yet being ruined.

    extravagant vs. profligate: Extravagant stops short of profligate — extravagance buys too much, while profligacy pours the whole fortune out and calls for more.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / condemning

    Profligate is an indictment — waste with a hint of vice. Extravagant teases; prodigal mourns the waste; profligate condemns the waster. It also survives in public-finance scolding: a profligate agency, a profligate decade.
