causation

Meaning

What It Means

Causation means the relationship in which one thing actually produces another; the act of causing an effect.

See the word in context

The safety board's report established causation, tracing the citywide blackout directly to a single overloaded transformer.

A data analyst in a seaside town noticed that sunscreen sales and jellyfish stings rose and fell together all summer long. Sunscreen was not causing the stings; hot beach days simply drew more swimmers into the water, driving both numbers up at once. To claim causation, the analyst would have to show that one thing actually produced the other, not merely that the two moved together. Her report was careful to label the pattern a correlation, not causation.

The two measures moved together without one producing the other, so the analyst correctly refused to claim causation, which requires proof that one thing genuinely brings about the other.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    causality: Causality means the relation between cause and effect, and in many sentences it can swap with causation. The shade of difference: causality tends to name the abstract principle that philosophers and physicists discuss, while causation is what a lawyer or a safety board sets out to prove in one particular case.

    cause: A cause is the specific thing that produces an effect — the spark, the overloaded transformer, the missed inspection. Causation is the relationship itself: the fact that one thing produced the other. Investigators name a cause; they establish causation.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    coincidence: two things happening together purely by chance, with no producing link

    happenstance: an event that occurs by chance rather than being caused deliberately

    Where it sits · how much one event controls the next

    correlation → causation → determinism

    correlation vs. causation: Correlation only shows that two things tend to appear together, while causation means one of them actually produces the other.

    determinism vs. causation: Causation says one event brings about another, but determinism goes much further, claiming every event is completely fixed in advance by prior causes.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      formal, academic/scientific register

      Causation appears constantly in science, law, and statistics, most famously in the warning that correlation is not causation: two things rising and falling together never proves that one produces the other. Reserve causation for a demonstrated cause-and-effect link, and say correlation when things merely move together.

The analysts urged caution: a pattern is not proof, and plenty of things travel together without one producing the other. Maybe the breakfast program helped, they said, or maybe the same families who managed early breakfasts also managed homework, tutors, and sleep. To test causation, the district ran a lottery: some schools got the program a year early, chosen by draw, and the others waited. When the early schools' scores rose while the waiting schools' stayed flat, the team finally said it plainly: the breakfasts were not just riding alongside success -- they were producing some of it.
