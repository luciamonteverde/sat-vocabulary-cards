Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Ennui means a feeling of weary boredom and listlessness.

See the word in context

Retirement's first thrill wore off fast, and by spring an ennui had set in that no hobby kit could touch.

Nothing in the palace amused the young prince any longer: the jugglers were tiresome, the feasts identical, the falcons a bore. The court physician, finding no fever, wrote one word in his ledger — ennui — and prescribed a month of mucking out his own stables.

Ennui is boredom with the air let out of everything — weary, Frenchified, and hard to cure. Ordinary boredom ends when something interesting walks in; ennui yawns at the interesting thing too. No single thing is the matter, and that is precisely the matter.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    tedium: Tedium is the dullness of a task or of the hours it fills — it belongs to the work, and it ends when the work does. Ennui belongs to the person: a settled weariness carried on the clock and off it, yawning at the weekend as flatly as at the job.

    listlessness: Listlessness is the flat, limp state of having no appetite for anything — a condition of the person, never a property of a task. Ennui is listlessness with a settled, worldly flavor: the appetite has not just drained for an afternoon but gone stale altogether.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    zest: hungry enjoyment of things — the appetite ennui has lost

    eagerness: leaning forward into whatever comes next

    Where it sits · how deeply the flat mood settles

    monotony → boredom → ennui

    monotony vs. ennui: Monotony belongs to the surroundings — the same gray days on repeat; it is the soil, not the feeling, and it stops well short of ennui.

    boredom vs. ennui: Boredom goes further than monotony but stops short of ennui: boredom lifts when something interesting arrives; ennui yawns even at that.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    literary / wry

    Ennui is boredom in evening dress: use it for the deep, stylish weariness of people who have run out of appetite, and let plain boredom cover rainy Tuesdays. It pairs naturally with settle, creep, and descend — ennui arrives like weather. Among this unit's words it is the odd one out: a mood borrowed whole, with no working prefix at all.

    By his tenth year on the restaurant beat, Anton had eaten at every table worth envying in the city. Colleagues assumed the job still thrilled him; instead, a gray ennui had settled over his evenings, indoors and out. It was not the work — deadlines still came easily — and it was not fatigue, since he slept long and well. New menus, new chefs, even a free week in Lisbon stirred nothing; he yawned through the tasting that had made his younger self tremble. The appetite had not merely dipped for a season, his editor realized; it had gone stale at the root.
