ephemeral

/ih-FEM-er-uhl/ - the stress falls on FEM

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Ephemeral means short-lived; lasting only a very brief time.

See the word in context

The alpenglow was ephemeral, gilding the peaks for five minutes before the gray crept back.

A total solar eclipse is the most chased sight in nature, and the most ephemeral: at any spot on the ground, totality lasts a few minutes at the very most. People cross oceans, climb volcanoes, and charter boats for it, then stand in the moon's shadow watching the one clock that cannot be argued with. Four minutes, and the sun is back, and it is over.

Ephemeral is the exact word because it names what is short-lived, lasting only a very brief time — the Greek root means 'for a day.' It suits things whose whole existence is brief, and it carries a note of preciousness: the shortness is part of the beauty. What is here and gone cannot be stored, only witnessed.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    vanishing: Vanishing describes something in the act of disappearing — fading from sight or from existence as you watch, like steam off a kettle or a species in decline. Ephemeral makes a different claim: the whole life was always going to be brief. A vanishing thing is going; an ephemeral thing never had long.

    fleeting: A fleeting thing passes almost before you notice — a glance, a smile, a scent in a doorway. The word names the swift slip of a single moment through your attention. Ephemeral instead measures a lifespan: the thing exists fully and is gone within a day or an hour, its brevity part of its nature.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    enduring: lasting on and on through time

    everlasting: never ending at all

    Where it sits · how little time it gets to exist

    temporary → short-lived → ephemeral

    temporary vs. ephemeral: Temporary says only that an end is scheduled — a temporary bridge can stand for ten years — while ephemeral squeezes a whole lifespan into a day or an hour.

    short-lived vs. ephemeral: Short-lived tightens the claim: the whole life is brief. Ephemeral goes past even that, shrinking existence to a day, an hour, a handful of minutes.

    Word Choice

    literary

    Ephemeral is the poet's word for things whose whole life is brief — a day, an hour — and whose shortness is part of their point. This unit's momentary is briefer still, a single instant; transitory is cooler and roomier, for phases that pass without hurry. Reach for ephemeral when the brevity itself is precious.
