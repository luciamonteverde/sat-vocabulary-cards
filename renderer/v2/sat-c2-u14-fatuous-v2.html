Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Fatuous means smugly foolish; silly and empty of sense.

See the word in context

The talk show fills its last segment with fatuous predictions delivered in sober confidence — last week a guest guaranteed weather control by June.

Milo watched one nature documentary and emerged an expert with a fatuous theory: sharks, he announced at dinner, never bother swimmers who "project calm energy," and he planned to prove it in open water come August. His sister asked what the sharks were supposed to detect the calm with. Milo, beaming, said they simply know. The family let the subject drift out with the tide.

Fatuous is the exact word for Milo's theory because it is smugly foolish — silly, empty of sense, and pleased with itself on top. Plain foolishness can be humble; the fatuous remark never is. The self-satisfaction is the fingerprint: this unit's inane describes chatter that's merely empty, but fatuous empties the head and then takes a bow.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    witless: Witless means empty of intelligence — no thought at work anywhere in the remark or the plan — and it passes no judgment on the speaker's mood. Fatuous needs one thing more: the glow of self-congratulation. Witless names the vacancy; fatuous is the vacancy taking a bow.

    asinine: Asinine is foolishness at full volume — so laughably dumb that the room winces the moment it lands. Fatuous is quieter and vainer: an empty remark that sounds almost reasonable while its speaker beams with pride. Asinine earns its laugh at once; fatuous needs a second look.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    sensible: grounded in sound judgment

    perceptive: sees what is actually there

    Where it sits · how deep the foolishness runs

    silly → fatuous → idiotic

    silly vs. fatuous: A silly remark is lightweight and knows it; a fatuous one is just as empty and expects applause.

    idiotic vs. fatuous: Idiotic goes past fatuous — foolishness so complete that even self-satisfaction can't dress it up.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / cutting

    Fatuous is the reviewer's scalpel for remarks that are silly, empty, and pleased with themselves — the smugness is what earns the word. This unit's inane covers content-free chatter with no self-congratulation attached; simplistic, meanwhile, is a charge about oversimplifying something real. If the fool in question is beaming while he says it, fatuous.
