Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Candid means honest and open — saying what you actually think, uncomfortable parts included, without rehearsal or disguise.

See the word in context

Her application essay worked because it was candid: instead of polishing her record, she wrote about the semester she nearly quit, and why.

The recipe site thrived on candid reviews: beneath the glossy photographs, home cooks reported the sauce that curdled, the timing that ran long, and the cheap substitution that worked better.

Candid speech volunteers the inconvenient part. A polite review could have praised the photography and moved on; a candid one says what the cook actually found, failures included — openness offered freely, not extracted under questioning.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    frank: Frank and candid both hand over the unvarnished truth, but frank leans on the delivery of one plain statement — a frank assessment, a frank refusal. Candid describes the whole open posture: nothing rehearsed, nothing held in reserve, the speaker plain in every word.

    outspoken: Outspoken is candor with the volume up — opinions offered freely, invited or not, sometimes to the room's discomfort. Candid answers openly when the truth is called for; it doesn't need a soapbox, only a real question.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    guarded: keeps the honest part back, measuring every word

    evasive: slides away from the question instead of meeting it

    Where it sits · how unvarnished the telling gets

    honest → candid → blunt

    honest vs. candid: Honest is the milder rung: an honest answer avoids falsehood but may stop right there, while a candid one volunteers the uncomfortable parts nobody asked about.

    blunt vs. candid: Blunt is stronger than candid: candid opens the whole ledger but still minds the listener, while blunt drops it on the table, careless of what it bruises.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    warm / plain-spoken

    Candid is honesty with the door open — unrehearsed, complete, and offered rather than extracted. It is warmer than blunt, which tells the truth without caring where it lands, and straighter than diplomatic speech, which re-tailors the truth to please its hearer. Photographers use it the same way: a candid shot is one nobody staged. This unit already gave you the noun — candor is the quality, candid the person or the remark — and forthright, its close unit-mate, leans harder on directness where candid leans on openness.
