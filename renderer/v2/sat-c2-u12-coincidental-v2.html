/koh-in-sih-DEN-tuhl/ - the stress falls on DEN

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Coincidental means happening together by chance rather than through any connection or plan.

See the word in context

The fire drill landing in the middle of the surprise inspection was entirely coincidental — the two schedules were drawn up months apart by offices that never speak.

The week the village fountain failed, a new plumber happened to move into the house beside it, and the joke wrote itself: he'd broken it for the business. The parish register put the joke away — the pump had been wheezing for a year before he ever saw the place, and his transfer papers were dated well before the water stopped. The two arrivals were coincidental, nothing more.

Coincidental is the exact word because it names things happening by chance rather than through any connection or plan — two events landing side by side with no thread between them. It does the specific work of denying the link people itch to draw. Unlikely pairings happen all the time; coincidental says this one means nothing.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    accidental: Accidental describes a single mishap nobody intended — an elbow catches a glass, a wrong file gets deleted. One thing goes wrong by mistake. Coincidental needs two separate things, each fine on its own, lining up by pure chance; nothing has to go wrong at all.

    chance: A chance event is one that luck produced — a chance meeting, a chance discovery — and the word carries no argument with it. Coincidental works harder: it insists that two things landed together and then denies any plan or connection behind the pairing.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    orchestrated: arranged step by step for effect

    prearranged: settled between the parties in advance

    Where it sits · how improbable the accidental pairing is

    unplanned → coincidental → freakish

    unplanned vs. coincidental: Leaving your umbrella at home is merely unplanned; coincidental needs two separate things landing together by chance.

    freakish vs. coincidental: Freakish goes past coincidental — the alignment is so wildly unlikely that people start reaching for words like impossible.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral

    Reach for coincidental when two things land together by chance and you want to deny any link between them. Keep it apart from this unit's contemporaneous, which says only that two things share the same period — contemporaneous events may be deeply connected, while coincidental ones, by definition, are not. For a single unintended mishap, accidental is the plainer word.
