Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Defunct means no longer used or existing.

See the word in context

The city's pneumatic-tube mail system, defunct since the 1950s, still threads beneath the streets like the fossil of a faster idea.

The band played its last show in 2009; the singer teaches now, the drummer sells insurance, and the rights to the name sit in a lawyer's drawer. Yet every winter the fan club prints a newsletter and swears a reunion is close. The club is devoted; the band is defunct — it doesn't exist to reunite.

Defunct is the exact word because it certifies an ending: no longer used or existing, full stop. Bankrupt would say only that the money failed — bankrupt firms keep operating every day. And a paused project can restart. Defunct closes the file: whatever this was, it isn't anymore.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    extinct: Extinct is the ending biology reserves for species and natural kinds — the last member has died, and the world holds no more. Defunct does the same certifying work for human creations: leagues, airlines, journals, brands. The passenger pigeon is extinct; the railway that carried its hunters is defunct.

    discontinued: Discontinued means a maker chose to stop producing something — a model, a flavor, a product line — while the company itself sails on. Defunct reaches further: the enterprise has ceased to exist. A discontinued sedan still has a manufacturer; a defunct automaker has nothing left to discontinue.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    thriving: alive and doing well

    operating: currently up and running

    Where it sits · how far gone from use and memory

    fading → defunct → forgotten

    fading vs. defunct: A fading business still opens its doors, though fewer people walk through them; defunct means the doors have closed for good.

    forgotten vs. defunct: Forgotten goes even further than defunct: a defunct railroad can still be mourned and mapped, but a forgotten one has vanished from memory as well as from the timetable.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / journalistic

      Defunct is for institutions, products, and systems that have ceased to exist — airlines, journals, leagues, brands. It certifies the ending without emotion. For a building rotting where it stands, this unit's derelict is the word; a company can be defunct while its headquarters, derelict, still looms over Main Street.

      Chasing a 1979 route over the Alps, the historian needed an article from Freewheel, a cycling weekly that once outsold the national dailies every July. The magazine is defunct: its publisher liquidated in 1983, the presses were sold, and the title has belonged to no one for forty years. This was no quiet trimming of a catalog — the company itself ceased to exist, owing money and holding one last unprinted issue. Nor did it linger half-alive; there was no shrinking subscriber list, only a Tuesday when the office simply did not open. What survives is paper: bound volumes in three libraries, which is where the historian finally found her route.
