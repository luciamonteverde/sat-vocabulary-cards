Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Judicious means having or showing careful, sound judgment.

See the word in context

A judicious pause before answering has spared the ambassador more headlines than any speech.

Offered triple money to clear-cut the old-growth stand, the timber cooperative's manager chose a more judicious course. She sold the storm-downed trunks first, thinned the crowded east slope where the foresters wanted light, and left the seed trees alone. The mill got most of its order; the hillside kept its future; and the members who had shouted "take the money" were quietly glad, ten years on, that she hadn't.

Judicious is the exact word because the manager shows careful, sound judgment — every interest weighed, the near term set against the long, nothing decided in heat. The word doesn't mean timid or slow; it means the thinking was done properly before the acting. Judges gave the word its name, and it still carries their obligation: decide as if the future will read the decision.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    prudent: Prudent is careful to steer around future harm — the prudent driver slows before the icy bridge. Judicious is wider: it weighs every side of a decision, benefits as well as risks, the near term against the far. A choice can be prudent without being judicious if caution was its only virtue.

    levelheaded: Levelheaded describes temperament — staying calm and clear while others panic, so nothing rash gets decided. Judicious describes the judgment itself: every interest weighed, the long term set beside the short. A levelheaded person keeps their footing in the storm; a judicious one also picks the right road afterward.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    reckless: acts with no thought for what follows

    hasty: decides before the weighing is done

    Where it sits · how deep the wisdom runs

    sensible → judicious → sagacious

    sensible vs. judicious: A sensible choice avoids the obvious mistakes; a judicious one has weighed even the interests that weren't in the room.

    sagacious vs. judicious: Sagacious goes past judicious — beyond one well-weighed decision to a lifetime's proven depth of judgment.

      formal / level

This unit's canny also judges well, but for its own side of the table; myopic names the failure judicious avoids, judgment that can't see past this quarter. Prudent guards against harm; judicious balances the whole ledger.
