Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Vigilant means keeping careful, sustained watch for danger or trouble that has not yet shown itself.

See the word in context

The border collies stay vigilant long after the flock has settled for the night.

All through fire season, the lookout on Copper Ridge stayed vigilant from first light to last — sweeping her binoculars along the same tree line every quarter hour and logging every gray wisp that might be dust, mist, or the first smoke of a fire that hadn't yet announced itself.

Vigilant fits because the watching is sustained, purposeful, and aimed at trouble that may never come: she isn't reacting to a fire, she is watching for one. Alert would cover a single sharp moment; watchful wouldn't carry the discipline of the quarter-hour sweeps. And unlike this unit's cognizant, which is holding a fact in mind, vigilant is standing guard.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    watchful: Watchful is the everyday word for keeping an eye on things — a watchful parent at the playground. Vigilant is watchful with training and stakes: sustained, unblinking attention to a specific trouble that is expected to try something.

    alert: Alert is quick to notice what happens around you, right now, in the moment. Vigilant stretches that alertness across the whole watch — staying keyed to danger for hours or seasons, because letting up is exactly when it comes.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    oblivious: not noticing what's happening around you

    negligent: failing to keep the watch a duty demands

    Where it sits · how braced for trouble the watching is

    attentive → vigilant → paranoid

    attentive vs. vigilant: Attentive is milder than vigilant: an attentive student follows what is in front of her, while a vigilant one is braced for the thing that hasn't happened yet.

    paranoid vs. vigilant: Paranoid goes further than vigilant — the watchfulness tears loose from any real threat and starts finding danger everywhere, even where there is none.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral

    Vigilant is the guard-duty adjective: it wants a threat to watch for and a watcher on duty — vigilant lifeguards, vigilant moderators, vigilant immune cells. Where nothing is at stake, attentive or observant is the better fit. And keep it clear of its rowdy cousin vigilante, the noun for someone who quits watching and takes the law into their own hands.

    By August, guarding the quarry lake looked like the easiest job in town: sun, water, and long afternoons in a tall chair. Rosa knew better. The lake had not had a drowning in nine years precisely because its guards stayed vigilant when nothing was happening. Her eyes swept the same arc every ten seconds, resting nowhere, even when the swimmers were only floating and laughing. Trouble at a lake gives little warning, she told the trainees; the swimmer who slips under does it quietly, between one glance and the next. So the watch could never relax with the crowd, and the dull afternoons were the ones she trusted least.
