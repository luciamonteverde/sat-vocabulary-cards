What It Means

Staid means sedate, serious, self-restrained.

See the word in context

Their staid holiday card—cream stock, engraved names, no photo—hasn't changed since 1989.

The quiz show's new host inherited a staid format—two podiums, a bell, polite applause—and changed nothing but his tie, trusting the show's quiet dignity to keep its loyal audience.

Staid describes a settled, buttoned-down seriousness of style or manner—reliable, a little formal, allergic to flash. It often carries a faint sigh: staid institutions are trusted, but nobody calls them exciting.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sedate: Sedate is about pace: a sedate walk, a sedate tempo, nothing hurried. Staid is about style and manner—settled, buttoned-down, allergic to flash. A parade can move at a sedate pace and still be gaudy; a staid firm may work frantically and never show a flourish.

    sober: Sober here means serious and plain, with playfulness deliberately set aside—a sober speech, a sober report, dressed for the occasion. Staid goes further in time: seriousness settled into permanent habit, a style that stopped surprising anyone years ago. Sober can be chosen for a day; staid is simply what the place is.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    flashy: designed to grab the eye

    playful: delights in fun and surprises

    Where it sits · how much flair gets stripped away

    sensible → staid → severe

    sensible vs. staid: Sensible is milder than staid—sensible shoes are practical but might still come in red, while a staid wardrobe quietly gave up on red years ago.

    severe vs. staid: Severe goes further than sensible—a severe gray suit strips away every ornament on purpose, while staid merely declines to chase fashion.

      Word Choice

      dry / slightly wry

      Staid is for styles, institutions, and manners that have settled and stopped surprising anyone—usually with a wry edge, half compliment and half yawn. Stolid, its unit-mate, is about the face rather than the style: a stolid juror shows no feeling; a staid firm shows no flair. For pace rather than style, sedate (a chip above) is the closer fit.
