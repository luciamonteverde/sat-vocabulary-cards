arbiter

/AR-bih-ter/ - the stress falls on AR

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Arbiter means one who settles a dispute brought before them with a final, binding decision.

See the word in context

Both bands accepted the festival's stage manager as arbiter of set-length disputes, and her stopwatch ruling was final.

When the twins could not divide their grandmother's library, their aunt agreed to serve as arbiter: each twin listed the shelves she wanted, the aunt made the split, and both had promised in advance to live with it.

An arbiter is stronger than an adviser and more final than a go-between: both sides agree beforehand that this one person's word will bind them. That advance agreement — and the binding decision — are what the word names.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    umpire: An umpire rules on play-by-play calls inside a game, applying a fixed rulebook in real time. An arbiter is chosen by the two sides themselves to settle their whole dispute, and the decision binds because both agreed in advance to accept it. Umpires enforce rules; an arbiter ends arguments.

    judge: A judge holds state office and presides in a courtroom, with authority that comes from the government, not from the people in the case. An arbiter can be anyone — an aunt, a stage manager — so long as both sides agree that this person's word will settle the matter.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    contender: fights as one of the sides instead of standing above them

    bystander: watches with no power to decide anything

    Where it sits · how much say over the outcome

    adviser → moderator → arbiter

    adviser vs. arbiter: An adviser is the mildest voice here — advice can be thanked and ignored, while an arbiter's word ends the argument.

    moderator vs. arbiter: A moderator stops short of an arbiter: moderators keep the discussion orderly and fair but decide nothing, while the arbiter's decision binds both sides.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal

      Use arbiter for the person both sides agree will settle the matter — the decision binds. A judge holds office; an umpire works the game; an arbiter can be anyone the parties choose. English also extends it: an 'arbiter of taste' is whoever gets the final word on what is stylish.

      The two food-truck owners had argued about the corner spot all summer, and the farmers' market had no rule that settled it. Neither wanted lawyers. So they asked Mrs. Okafor, who had managed the market's books for twenty years, to act as arbiter. Both signed a one-page pledge first: whatever she decided, they would live with, no appeals and no grumbling. She heard each side out on a Saturday, walked the lot twice, and split the season -- mornings to one truck, festivals to the other. The ruling stuck not because she held any office, but because both had bound themselves to it in advance.
