corroborate

/kuh-ROB-uh-rayt/ - the stress falls on ROB

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Corroborate means to strengthen a claim or account with independent evidence — a second source that tells the same story.

See the word in context

Flight-recorder data corroborated the pilot's account of a sudden downdraft over the ridge.

A lone seismograph in a Nevada schoolhouse recorded a strange double tremor, and for a week its keepers were accused of an instrument glitch — until stations in Utah and Idaho released their tapes and corroborated the reading, each showing the same two pulses at the same instant.

Corroborate fits because the Utah and Idaho tapes are independent sources agreeing with the first: nobody repaired the Nevada machine or argued on its behalf — separate witnesses told the same story, and that is the word's whole job. Verify would mean re-checking the Nevada tape itself; prove would claim the question was closed. And unlike this unit's substantiate, which says how solid the backing is, corroborate points at where it comes from — another voice.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    verify: To verify something is to check its truth yourself — against the records, the math, the original. Corroborate works from outside: you don't re-check the claim, you add a second, independent piece of evidence that tells the same story.

    bolster: To bolster is to prop something up — any support will do, from money to morale. Corroborate is one specific way of bolstering a claim: an independent source that agrees. Every corroboration bolsters; very little bolstering corroborates.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    contradict: to assert the opposite of what a claim says

    undercut: to weaken the support a claim rests on

    Where it sits · how much independent weight the support carries

    suggest → corroborate → prove

    suggest vs. corroborate: Suggest is weaker than corroborate: evidence that merely suggests something leans a claim's way, while corroborating evidence arrives from an independent source and squarely backs it.

    prove vs. corroborate: Prove goes further than corroborate — corroborating testimony strengthens an account that could still be wrong, but proof is meant to close the question for good.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal

    Corroborate lives where claims are contested — courtrooms, labs, newsrooms — and it always implies a second, independent voice. Keep it apart from two of its neighbors in this unit: substantiate says the backing is solid, and validate says a check was passed, but corroborate says that someone else, separately, tells the same story. The everyday alternative is back up.

    For decades the town's favorite story was pure hearsay: that the 1911 regatta had been cancelled when a freak June snowstorm buried the harbor. Old-timers swore by it. Historians rolled their eyes. One diary mentioned the storm, but the diarist was eleven years old at the time and writing forty years later. Then the historical society digitized the lighthouse keeper's daily log, and there it was, in fading ink: 'June 14 -- snow to the knees, no boats out.' A keeper's log could not corroborate every detail of the legend -- nothing in it mentioned the regatta itself -- but on the storm, an independent record now told the same story as the diary. The society's newsletter put it carefully: one witness makes a tale; two, writing decades apart and never having met, start to make a fact.
