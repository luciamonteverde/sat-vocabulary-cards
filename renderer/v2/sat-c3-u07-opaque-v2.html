What It Means

Opaque means hard to see through or understand.

See the word in context

Decades of smoke had turned the workshop's windows opaque, so the glassblower worked in a permanent amber dusk.

The poet's late notebooks are famously opaque: private symbols, dates that match no calendar, and marginal notes in a shorthand she taught to no one.

Opaque works on two levels — light cannot pass through an opaque pane, and meaning cannot pass through an opaque rule or report; either way, you are left staring at a surface.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    inscrutable: Inscrutable belongs to faces, expressions, and people — the poker player's inscrutable stare gives nothing away. Opaque covers things: glass, prose, budgets, rules. Both refuse entry, but you meet an inscrutable person, while you stare at an opaque surface.

    cloudy: Cloudy means hazed over, swirled with particles or doubt, yet often thin enough to see through — cloudy water still shows the coins on the bottom. Opaque is the full stop: no light, no meaning, nothing gets past the surface at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    clear: letting light and meaning straight through

    see-through: so easy to look through you forget it's there

    Where it sits · how little light or meaning gets through

    hazy → murky → opaque

    hazy vs. opaque: Hazy is the mildest rung — detail softens but the shapes still come through; it stops far short of opaque.

    murky vs. opaque: Murky stops short of opaque: you still catch dim outlines through murky water, while opaque means nothing passes at all.

      Word Choice

      neutral / formal

      Opaque works literally (glass, water) and figuratively (rules, prose, finances) — in both, nothing gets through. Its unit-mate abstruse concedes there is depth to reach; opaque suggests the surface itself refuses you.
