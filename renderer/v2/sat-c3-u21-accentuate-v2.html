Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Accentuate means to stress or highlight something — to make a real feature more noticeable or prominent.

See the word in context

The gallery hung one small lamp above each canvas, angled to accentuate the brushwork rather than flood it with light.

A good stage-makeup artist doesn't redraw an actor's face; she accentuates what the back row would otherwise miss — a brow line here, a shadow there — until the same features carry to the last seat.

Accentuate names emphasis that stays honest: the artist strengthens features the actor already has, and stops where the truth stops. Redrawing the face would be invention; accentuating only turns up what is really there.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    highlight: To highlight is to pick something out for attention — a marker stripe, a pointed mention. Highlighting aims eyes at a feature from outside; accentuating works on the feature itself, strengthening it until it speaks up on its own.

    underscore: To underscore is to insist on a point's importance, the spoken equivalent of underlining. Underscore belongs to arguments and warnings; accentuate belongs to features and contrasts — you underscore a danger, you accentuate a silhouette.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    downplay: treats something as smaller or less important than it is

    mask: hides a feature so it cannot be noticed at all

    Where it sits · how hard the emphasis presses

    note → accentuate → exaggerate

    note vs. accentuate: Note is milder than accentuate: to note a detail is simply to mention it once and move on, while accentuating it shapes the whole presentation so the detail stands out.

    exaggerate vs. accentuate: Exaggerate goes further than accentuate — accentuating strengthens what is truly there, but exaggerating inflates it past the truth into something the facts no longer support.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / descriptive

    Accentuate stays inside the truth: it strengthens a feature that is really there, and stops where the facts stop — push past them and you are exaggerating instead. It also works on the thing itself; to aim attention from outside — a mention, a marker — is to highlight. A belt accentuates a waist; a footnote highlights a source.
