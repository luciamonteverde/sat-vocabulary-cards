What It Means

Cogent means intellectually convincing; clearly reasoned.

See the word in context

The registrar's memo was so cogent that the dean approved the new major before her coffee had cooled.

The clinic's appeal letter ran a single page: the scan dated before the deadline, the policy clause quoted beside it, the billing code matched line for line. The reviewer who had denied the claim twice called it the most cogent appeal she had seen and reversed the denial that afternoon.

Cogent is the right adjective because it means intellectually convincing and clearly reasoned — the letter wins not by pleading but by leaving no gap between evidence and conclusion. Eloquent would praise beautiful wording; cogent measures the logic's grip. An argument can be cogent in a monotone.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sound: A sound argument simply contains no errors — the logic holds and the facts check out — but sound is a pass mark, not a wow. A cogent argument is sound and forceful at once: it does not just survive inspection, it compels agreement. Many sound arguments persuade no one; a cogent one wins the room.

    airtight: Airtight is the informal word for a case with no gap for an objection to slip through — it stresses the absence of holes. Cogent stresses the positive push: reasoning so clear and well-ordered that it moves you to agree. An airtight case cannot be beaten; a cogent one wins you over.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    flimsy: collapses at the first push

    muddled: reasoning too tangled to follow

    Where it sits · how much reasoned force the argument has

    convincing → cogent → irrefutable

    convincing vs. cogent: Convincing is a broader, weaker rung than cogent — a slogan or a single statistic can be convincing without much rigor, while a cogent case compels agreement through the reasoning itself.

    irrefutable vs. cogent: Irrefutable goes further than cogent — a cogent case compels agreement, but an irrefutable one cannot even be argued against.

    Word Choice

    formal / academic

    Cogent judges the logic, not the delivery: tight steps, evidence in place, conclusion earned. This unit's eloquent praises the language and lucid praises its clarity; cogent says the reasoning itself compels. A speech can be eloquent, lucid, and still not cogent.
