Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Docile means easily taught, led, or managed — a word for people and animals, not for things.

See the word in context

The visiting teacher braced for a rowdy afternoon, but the class proved so docile that even the fire drill ran in a straight, silent line.

The new board was famously docile. The chairman slid the budget across the table; it came back signed. He floated selling the archive; heads nodded. Consultants joked that the boardroom's only sound was the shuffle of papers moving in one direction — until the year all that nodding cost the firm its charter.

Docile is the exact word for that board, because docile means easily taught, led, or managed — the leading meets no resistance, and that is precisely the worry. Meek is different: meek people are timid by nature, while docile says nothing about fear — only that the teaching and the leading go through unopposed.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    tame: Tame means no longer wild — an animal word for creatures at ease around people, however that ease arrived. Docile adds the teaching: docile means easily taught, led, and managed. A stray cat can grow tame with no lessons at all, while a docile horse takes the saddle because teaching goes through smoothly.

    biddable: Biddable means ready to do as one is bidden, seldom asking why — point, and it goes; ask, and it fetches. Docile is the temperament underneath: a docile animal takes teaching and leading calmly, while biddable describes the moment orders are actually followed.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    headstrong: determined to have its own way

    unruly: resisting all handling and direction

    Where it sits · how much self-assertion remains

    agreeable → docile → meek

    agreeable vs. docile: Agreeable is milder than docile — an agreeable person is simply pleasant company and still pushes back when something matters, while docile means the teaching and leading go through unopposed.

    meek vs. docile: Meek goes further than docile — the docile student still needs a teacher to follow, while the meek one no longer speaks up for itself at all.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral, cooling toward critical

    Docile is warm for animals and children — easily taught, calm under handling — and cooler for adults and institutions, where it hints the leading has gone too unquestioned. Unit-mates: compliant meets written rules, obedience follows spoken orders; docile describes the temperament underneath, and servile is that temperament curdled into self-abasement.
