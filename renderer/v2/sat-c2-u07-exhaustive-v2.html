exhaustive

/ig-ZAW-stiv/ - the stress falls on ZAW

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Exhaustive means covering every possible detail; thoroughly complete.

See the word in context

The detectives' sweep of the block was exhaustive — all fifty-one doors, including the three everyone swore stood empty.

The used-bookshop's new owner spent her first March on an exhaustive count of the stock: every shelf, every cellar box, every paperback wedged behind the radiator. Forty-one thousand titles later, she could say with a straight face that nothing in the building had escaped the ledger.

Exhaustive is the claim that nothing was left out — coverage complete down to the last item. Its near-miss is exhausting, which describes fatigue. March was surely both, but the count earns exhaustive because it reached everything, not because it wore anyone out.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    comprehensive: Comprehensive work takes in every major area of its subject — the whole scope, wall to wall. Exhaustive makes a harder claim: within that scope, every single item was reached. A comprehensive review covers every part of the curriculum; an exhaustive one accounts for every last worksheet.

    complete: Complete says the finished whole has nothing missing — no piece absent, no part unaccounted for. Exhaustive is that idea with a method attached: a sweep down the whole list, item by item, until nothing could be called skipped. A jigsaw is complete; a census is exhaustive.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    partial: covering only some of the ground

    hurried: done at a pace that guarantees things get skipped

    Where it sits · how close to every item it gets

    careful → thorough → exhaustive

    careful vs. exhaustive: A careful reader watches for errors; exhaustive promises far more — that every line, without exception, was checked.

    thorough vs. exhaustive: Thorough work digs deep, but exhaustive goes to the end of the list: not most items, not nearly all — every one.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / precise

    Exhaustive makes a bold, checkable claim: everything was covered, without exception. Say thorough when the work is deep but you can't vouch for every item, and keep exhausting for fatigue — the two get mixed up daily. An exhaustive list is the one from which nothing could be called missing.
