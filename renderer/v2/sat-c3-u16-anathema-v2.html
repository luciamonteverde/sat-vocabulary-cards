anathema

/uh-NATH-uh-muh/ - the stress falls on NATH

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Anathema means something or someone utterly detested and rejected.

See the word in context

Instant coffee is anathema to my grandfather, who roasts his own beans and grinds them by hand before dawn.

Synthetic dyes were anathema to the weavers' cooperative: one chemical batch, the elders ruled, and the offending loom sat empty for a year.

Anathema is not strong dislike but total rejection — the word began as a formal curse of banishment, and it keeps that force. Use it as a predicate ('X is anathema to Y') for what a person or group casts out on principle, not merely what they would rather skip.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    curse: A curse is a thing formally damned, with ritual or a church behind the damning — anathema's church-flavored cousin. Anathema began the same way but now lives in everyday prose: whatever a person or group utterly rejects on principle is anathema to them, no ceremony required.

    taboo: A taboo is forbidden by rule or custom — the community's shared law does the rejecting, and breaking it invites punishment. Anathema is personal: the rejection comes from a person or group's own principles, and it holds even when no rule forbids the thing. Instant coffee breaks no law, yet it is anathema to some.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    delight: a thing that gives open pleasure

    darling: a cherished favorite of a person or group

    Where it sits · how completely the thing is cast out

    irritant → abomination → anathema

    irritant vs. anathema: An irritant merely chafes and can be lived with — nowhere near anathema, which names a thing utterly rejected.

    abomination vs. anathema: An abomination disgusts, but it stops short of anathema, which adds banishment: the thing is not only loathed but cast out entirely.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / literary

      Anathema is for absolute rejection, usually as a predicate: 'X is anathema to Y.' Use hated or detested for ordinary strong dislike; save anathema for what a group casts out on principle — and note it names the thing, where abhor (this unit) names the feeling toward it.
