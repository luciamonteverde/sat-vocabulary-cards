Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impetuous means acting quickly and rashly, without thinking things through.

See the word in context

One impetuous bid — doubled on a whim in the auction's final minute — cost Serena the entire restoration budget.

The climbing school's first lesson targets impetuous beginners: the ones who see a handhold and lunge for it, halfway up the wall before anyone has checked a rope. Strength they have, the instructor says; what they skip is the thinking.

Impetuous means acting on the surge of the moment — quickly, forcefully, and without weighing what follows. It is about the missing pause: the impetuous move may even succeed, but nobody, including the mover, thought it through first.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    headlong: Headlong pictures the motion itself — plunging straight in, momentum doing the deciding, like a dive or a downhill charge. Impetuous judges the mind behind the act: the pause for thought was available and got skipped. A rockslide is headlong; only a person can be impetuous.

    impulsive: Impulsive points at the inner urge — a sudden want or whim obeyed on the spot, like blurting out a secret. Impetuous stresses the headlong force of the act itself, the lunge with no pause for thought. The urge is impulsive; the charging, unweighed act is impetuous.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    careful: weighing steps before taking them

    cool-headed: keeping judgment clear when the moment surges

    Where it sits · how deep the disregard for consequences runs

    hasty → impetuous → reckless

    hasty vs. impetuous: Hasty is milder than impetuous — a hasty choice is rushed by the clock, while an impetuous one skips the thinking even with time to spare.

    reckless vs. impetuous: Reckless goes further than impetuous — the impetuous swimmer dives before thinking, while the reckless one dives knowing the rocks are there.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / critical

    Impetuous faults the missing pause before an act — sudden force, no forethought. Its everyday twin impulsive (in this unit's reference list) stresses the inner urge; impetuous stresses the headlong energy of the act itself. Both stop short of reckless, which knows the danger and charges anyway.
