clamor

/KLAM-er/ - rhymes with hammer

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Clamor means a loud, continuous outcry or noise of many voices.

See the word in context

By the second week of the bread shortage, the clamor outside the bakery started before the ovens did.

When the reunion tour added a single hometown date, a clamor filled the box-office plaza by dawn — fans chanting for more shows, phones held up on speaker, somebody's air horn punctuating the demand — and it did not let up until a second night was posted.

Clamor is the exact noun because it means a loud, continuous outcry of many voices — and this noise wants something; it is demand you can hear. Racket is loudness with no message in it, and an uproar can be pure confusion; a clamor keeps asking until it is answered.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    uproar: An uproar is a sudden burst of loud commotion — shock, laughter, or anger boiling over — with no particular demand in it. A clamor is loud and wants something: many voices insisting until they are answered. A courtroom erupts into an uproar; a crowd keeps up a clamor for lower fares.

    outcry: An outcry is the public's protest at something felt to be wrong, and it can live in headlines and petitions as easily as in raised voices. A clamor is always heard — continuous, many-voiced, and demanding. An outcry says this was wrong; a clamor keeps shouting for what it wants.

    hubbub: A hubbub is the mixed, busy noise of a crowd going about its own business — market stalls, a hotel lobby — asking for nothing. A clamor has a spine of demand in it: the voices are loud together because they want something and mean to be answered.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    hush: the sudden quiet that swallows a room

    murmur: many voices kept low and soft

    Where it sits · how loud and unruly the noise grows

    murmur → clamor → bedlam

    murmur vs. clamor: A murmur sits far below a clamor — voices kept so low that no single word survives the trip across the room, where a clamor is many voices raised and insisting.

    bedlam vs. clamor: Bedlam goes further than clamor — a clamor is loud voices demanding something; bedlam is the room gone past demands into pure chaos.
