circulate

/SUR-kyuh-layt/ - the stress falls on SUR

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Circulate means to move around through a group or from person to person — the way news spreads, a host works a room, or blood makes its rounds.

See the word in context

Within an hour of the announcement, three rival versions of the story were circulating through the dorm, each more dramatic than the last.

The petition to save the corner mural circulated through the neighborhood for two weeks — passed across fences, left on café counters, forwarded to cousins — until nearly every block had signed it.

Nothing was announced from a stage and no office mailed it out: the page moved hand to hand, each signer passing it along to the next. That person-to-person travel through a group is what circulate describes — and it is why the same verb names what blood does, moving continuously along its circuit.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    spread: Spread is the broadest word for reaching more people or covering more ground, by any means at all. Circulate specifies the route: the thing travels through a group, passed from person to person or making its rounds along a loop.

    disseminate: Disseminate is deliberate, top-down scattering — an office disseminates guidelines outward to a list of recipients. Circulate moves sideways and often on its own: the members themselves pass the memo, the rumor, the petition around the circle.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    suppress: to stop something from getting out or moving at all

    hoard: to keep a thing to oneself instead of letting it travel

    Where it sits · how far and freely the message travels

    seep → circulate → saturate

    seep vs. circulate: Seep is weaker than circulate: news that seeps escapes slowly at the edges, a detail here and there, while news that circulates is actively passed along, making the full rounds of the group.

    saturate vs. circulate: Saturate is stronger than circulate: a story that circulates is making its rounds, but a story that saturates has soaked every channel until no one in town could possibly have missed it.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / journalistic

    Circulate is the neutral verb for information on the move: memos, rumors, petitions, and hosts at their own parties all circulate. Broadcast fires one message at everyone simultaneously; disseminate pushes material outward on purpose; circulate lets it travel the loop, passed along by the group itself — which is why the word so often carries a hint of the unofficial.
