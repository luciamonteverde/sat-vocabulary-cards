oligarchic

/ol-ih-GAR-kik/ - the stress falls on GAR

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Oligarchic means ruled or controlled by a small group of people.

See the word in context

The debate league is quietly oligarchic: three coaches write the rules, pick the judges, and decide which schools may join.

On paper the fishing cooperative has ninety members; in practice it is oligarchic, run by the four families who own the boats, set the prices, and fill every seat on the disputes committee.

Oligarchic is more precise than 'unfair' or 'corrupt' because it names a shape of power: a small group holds it. The group can be honest or crooked, rich or merely entrenched — what makes rule oligarchic is how few hands are on the wheel.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    clannish: Clannish means run like a closed family circle, cool to outsiders — loyalty flows to kin and long-timers, and newcomers stay strangers for years. Oligarchic is about power rather than warmth: a small group actually rules. A clannish shop can be friendly inside and powerless; an oligarchic one holds the wheel.

    clubby: Clubby describes coziness among insiders — nicknames, standing favors, the same faces at the same table. Oligarchic adds the part an analyst cares about: the insiders hold actual power and write the rules for everyone else. A clubby office is merely irritating; an oligarchic one is governed by its clique.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    democratic: spreads power across everyone's vote

    broad-based: rests on many hands and many voices

    Where it sits · how narrowly power is concentrated

    elitist → oligarchic → autocratic

    elitist vs. oligarchic: Elitist attitudes merely favor a polished few — milder than oligarchic, where the few have stopped being favorites and started being the government.

    autocratic vs. oligarchic: Autocratic goes further than oligarchic along the narrowing road of power: the small circle shrinks to a single ruler holding everything.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / political

      Oligarchic is analysis, not just insult: it claims power sits with a small circle, whatever the letterhead says. Its unit-mates split hairs that essays love — plutocratic tells you the few are the rich, theocratic that the rulers are religious authorities, meritocratic that rank was earned. When the only fact you have is 'a handful rules,' oligarchic is the honest choice.

No one remembered voting on it, because no vote had happened.
