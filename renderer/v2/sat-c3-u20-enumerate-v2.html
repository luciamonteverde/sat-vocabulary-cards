enumerate

/ih-NOO-muh-rayt/ - the stress falls on NOO

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Enumerate means to list items one by one.

See the word in context

Before naming her price, the realtor enumerated the cottage's failings: the well, the wiring, the chimney, and the stairs, in that order.

At the lost-and-found window, the clerk asked the caller to enumerate the wallet's contents before releasing it; only when she had named the ferry stub, the two-dollar bill, and the pressed clover did he slide it across.

Enumerate is listing with intent: item by item, first to last, nothing bundled and nothing skipped. To mention is to touch; to list is to set down; to enumerate is to count the items out loud so everyone can watch the total being built.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    itemize: To itemize is to break a total into its billed parts — the line items on a receipt, an invoice, a tax return. Enumerate is not about money at all: to enumerate is to tell things one by one, first to last, so the whole accounting is out loud and complete.

    tally: To tally is to count items toward a total — tick marks on a clipboard where the final number matters more than the naming. Enumerate points the other way: the number is incidental; what counts is naming every item, one by one, no item merged and none left out.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    lump: to sweep separate things into one undifferentiated pile

    blur: to run items together until none can be told apart

    Where it sits · how complete the accounting is

    mention → list → enumerate

    mention vs. enumerate: Mention touches a thing once in passing — the opposite of an accounting; it stops far short of enumerate.

    list vs. enumerate: List goes further than mention but stops short of enumerate, which insists on one-by-one completeness, first item to last.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / procedural

    Enumerate when the one-by-one telling is the point — grievances, safety checks, the terms of a deal. Itemize for money and receipts, tally for counts, plain list for everything casual. Its unit-mate encapsulate pulls the opposite way: one seals the whole into a phrase; the other unrolls it item by item.

    Coach Adeyemi never raised her voice after a loss; she reached for the whiteboard instead. On Monday she enumerated the breakdowns from Friday's semifinal, one by one and in game order: the missed switch in the second quarter, the rushed inbound pass, the two late fouls, the silent bench. Nothing was skipped and nothing was lumped together; each mistake got its own line and its own minute of film. She wrote no scores and kept no running count — the point, she said, was the naming, not the arithmetic. By the end the players had stopped dreading the board; a list that complete felt less like blame and more like a map.
