generative

/JEN-er-uh-tiv/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Generative means having the power to originate, produce, or create something new.

See the word in context

The residency proved unexpectedly generative: in ten weeks she drafted two plays and invented a new staging technique.

The city's 'broken block' had been studied for decades with nothing to show. Then a librarian began hosting open repair nights in the empty storefront. Within a year, the repair nights had spawned a tool library, the tool library a bicycle co-op, and the co-op a youth apprenticeship that redesigned the block's own lighting. Planners started calling the storefront the district's most generative square meter — not because of what it contained, but because of what it kept bringing into being.

One repair night keeps originating new things — a tool library, a co-op, an apprenticeship — and that power to bring new things into being is what generative means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    creative: Creative means bringing new things into existence, and it usually credits a person's imagination — a creative director, a creative solution. Generative shifts the praise to the source itself: a generative idea, place, or practice keeps producing new things beyond its maker's plans.

    fertile: Fertile means rich in the power to produce new growth, a soil metaphor: a fertile mind or field is full of latent possibility. Generative is that power caught in the act — actually originating new things, one after another, rather than merely being capable of them.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    sterile: producing nothing new

    unproductive: yielding no results

    Where it sits · how genuinely new the results are

    productive → generative → seminal

    productive vs. generative: Productive is weaker than generative: a productive process turns out plenty of output, while a generative one originates genuinely new things.

    seminal vs. generative: Seminal is stronger than generative — a seminal work seeds whole fields that grow from it for generations, while generative describes the ongoing power to produce new things at any scale.
