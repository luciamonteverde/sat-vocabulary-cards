Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Individualism means the belief that each person should think, choose, and act independently — that the single person, not the group, is what matters most.

See the word in context

The studio's founder prized individualism, hiring animators whose portfolios matched no house style.

At the conservatory's juried recital, the examiner counted forty nearly identical Chopin programs, then gave her yearly speech on individualism — not a repertoire choice, she said, but the refusal to let the room decide for you.

Individualism is a stance, not a mood: the conviction that each person's own judgment and self-direction come before the group's demand to match. And here the unit's prefix finally means what it says. In- is 'not,' and an in-dividual is literally 'the undividable': cut society into ever smaller pieces, and the single person is where the knife stops. The -ism turns that unsplittable unit into a creed.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    self-reliance: Self-reliance is the practical habit of counting on your own hands and resources. Individualism raises that habit into a creed: not just managing alone, but believing the single person is what society exists for.

    nonconformity: Nonconformity is defined by what it refuses — the customs and expectations everyone else follows. Individualism is the positive doctrine underneath: the person comes first, whether or not any custom is being defied.

    autonomy: Autonomy is the condition of governing yourself — a question of who holds authority, asked of nations, regions, and patients. Individualism is the belief that such self-rule matters most; autonomy is the arrangement, individualism the creed.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    conformity: bending to match what the group expects

    collectivism: the belief that the group's needs outrank any single member's

    Where it sits · how far the single self is set above the group

    independence → individualism → egoism

    independence vs. individualism: Independence is weaker than individualism: an independent person simply manages without help, while individualism turns that independence into a creed a whole life is built on.

    egoism vs. individualism: Egoism goes further than individualism: individualism asks that every person be free to choose, while egoism dismisses everyone else's claims entirely.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral

    Individualism is the creed word — use it for the belief, not the personality. A capable loner shows self-reliance; a rule-flouter shows nonconformity; a self-governing region has autonomy. Reach for individualism when independence itself is being argued for as a value — and expect to meet its opposite number, collectivism, since writers wield both as compliment and accusation.

    When the city offered the Pell Prize to a design team, the jury chair, Ruth Okada, sent it back with a letter that made the papers. The award, she wrote, honored teamwork so automatically that it had forgotten what she called the plain truth of individualism: buildings, like poems, begin as one person's conviction. Her critics answered that architecture is the most collective of arts. Okada agreed — and held her ground anyway. Cooperation, she wrote, is how a vision gets built. It is not where a vision comes from. The single mind, she insisted, is what the whole collaboration exists to serve. The city never changed the prize. But for years afterward, every jury felt obliged to say which one person's idea it was rewarding.
