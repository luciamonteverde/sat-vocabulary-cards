Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Audible means able to be heard.

See the word in context

Bats hunt by cries pitched too high to be audible to us; the night forest is full of shouting we cannot hear.

In the hearing-test booth, the audiologist's tones start below threshold and climb: nothing, nothing — then a thin beep the boy can finally raise his hand for. That is the line the word draws. The tone was sounding the whole time; only at that step did it become audible to him.

Audible claims exactly one thing — the sound can be heard. Not loud, not clear, not pleasant: a whisper at the threshold and a foghorn are both audible. Loud would overstate the whisper; faint would sell the foghorn short. Audible is the humble gate that both of them pass through.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    perceptible: Perceptible means noticeable by any sense at all — a perceptible chill on the skin, a perceptible flicker at the corner of the eye. Audible makes the narrower claim: this thing can be heard, specifically, by an ear. Everything audible is perceptible, but only through one particular door.

    detectable: Detectable says something can be picked up by whatever is doing the detecting — an instrument, a dog's nose, a lab test — with no ear required. Audible stakes its whole claim on human hearing: a sound an ear can catch. A gas leak may be detectable by sensors while staying perfectly silent.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    inaudible: beyond hearing — the same word with the door shut

    silent: making no sound at all, so there is nothing to hear

    Where it sits · how easily the sound reaches the ear

    faint → audible → blaring

    faint vs. audible: A faint sound sits at the very edge of hearing — you can't swear it's there; audible crosses the line: it can actually be heard.

    blaring vs. audible: Blaring is far past audible — not merely hearable but flooding the whole street, impossible not to hear.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / technical

    Audible draws science's line — above the threshold of hearing — without judging loudness or charm. Reach for it in hearings, acoustics, and safety rules, where 'could it be heard?' is the whole question. For how a sound feels, English has loud, faint, piercing; audible only opens the gate.
