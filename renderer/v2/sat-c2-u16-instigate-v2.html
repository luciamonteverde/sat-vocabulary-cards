Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Instigate means to set something in motion by urging others on.

See the word in context

Historians still argue over which minister instigated the border war, and which merely failed to prevent it.

The duel that emptied two of the county's great houses began, everyone later agreed, with neither duelist. A cousin who stood to inherit from both had spent the winter instigating it, carrying remarks between them — each quote real, each trimmed by a word or two — until the challenge finally crossed a card table on its own. He attended both funerals in deep mourning, and inherited on schedule.

Instigate is the exact word for the cousin's winter of work, because to instigate is to set something in motion by urging others on — the instigator picks the quarrel he wants and gets other people to have it. Incite, this unit's louder verb, works a crowd out in the open; instigating can be done at a whisper, one poisoned quote at a time.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    foment: To foment is to keep unrest warm over time — feeding grievances like heat under a pot until the town simmers. To instigate is to strike the starting spark of one particular quarrel. Fomenting tends the mood; instigating starts the event.

    abet: To abet is to help along a wrong that someone else has already chosen — the lookout at the door, the loaned car. To instigate is to plant the choice itself, urging others into a quarrel that would not exist without you.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    smooth over: pats the quarrel back down before it can stand up

    make peace: ends outright the trouble an instigator wanted started

    Where it sits · how deeply the person drives events

    propose → instigate → engineer

    propose vs. instigate: Propose is the smallest part a person can play, and far milder than instigate — the idea is laid on the table once, openly, and the room does what it likes with it, while the instigator keeps working on people until the quarrel he wants is underway.

    engineer vs. instigate: Engineer goes further than instigate — the instigator strikes the spark and steps back; the engineer designs the whole scheme and stays to run it.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / accusatory

      Instigate pins the blame for a beginning: the fight, the walkout, the lawsuit traces back to its instigator, however clean his hands look. Incite is its public twin — crowds, noise, unlawful aims — while instigating can happen over tea. When no people are being worked on at all, the neutral verb is this unit's induce.
