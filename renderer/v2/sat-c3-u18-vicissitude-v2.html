vicissitude

/vih-SIS-ih-tood/ - the stress falls on SIS

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Vicissitude means an unpredictable change in circumstances, usually an unwelcome one.

See the word in context

Ten seasons in the league taught her every vicissitude of the sport: two trades, a lockout, a torn tendon, and a coach who never learned her name.

Nothing in the guidebook prepared them for the trail's vicissitudes: a bridge out on day two, a heat wave in the pass, and a bus strike at the far end that stranded them for a week.

A vicissitude is one of fortune's turns — unplanned, usually unwelcome, and plural at heart: troubles that arrive in no particular order and belong to no plan. The word suits lives and institutions long enough to have weathered several, which is why it so often travels with 'the vicissitudes of life.'

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    upheaval: An upheaval is a violent, large-scale disruption — a revolution, a market collapse — that overturns the whole order of things at once. A vicissitude is smaller and more patient: one of fortune's ordinary unwelcome turns, the kind a long life absorbs several of and continues past.

    reversal: A reversal is one specific shape of bad luck: fortune that was running your way flips against you — the verdict overturned, the lead lost. A vicissitude needs no prior success to spoil; it is any unpredictable turn of circumstances, arriving from any direction, usually unwelcome.

    twist of fate: A twist of fate is the storyteller's phrase for a single dramatic turn, and the twist can be a jackpot as happily as a downfall. Vicissitude drops the wonder and keeps the weariness: fortune's unwelcome turns taken together, the standard weather of any long life.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    steadiness: an even, unchanging course of events

    smooth sailing: the informal phrase for a stretch with no trouble at all

    Where it sits · how hard the setback hits

    hiccup → vicissitude → catastrophe

    hiccup vs. vicissitude: A hiccup is far milder than a vicissitude — a small snag smoothed over by lunchtime, not a real turn of fortune.

    catastrophe vs. vicissitude: A catastrophe is stronger than a vicissitude — vicissitudes are the turns a life absorbs and continues past, while a catastrophe can end the story outright.

    Apply

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Vicissitude is for the long view — memoirs, histories, obituaries — where a life's turns are surveyed together, and it is most at home in the plural. For a single stroke of bad luck, setback or reversal is plainer; vicissitude implies the wheel will keep turning.
