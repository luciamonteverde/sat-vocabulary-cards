pretext

/PREE-tekst/

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Pretext means a reason given to justify an action that is not the real reason — a cover story or invented excuse.

See the word in context

The spy entered the ballroom under the pretext of delivering flowers, when his true goal was to photograph the ambassador's guest list.

The principal dropped by the classroom under the pretext of checking the radiator, though she really wanted to see how the new teacher handled the class.

The radiator story is the stated reason, but it is not the true one. A cover reason offered to hide a real purpose is a pretext.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    cover: A cover is whatever hides a real purpose — a front business, a fake job title, an assumed role. It can be a thing, an identity, or a story. A pretext is narrower: specifically the stated reason someone gives for acting. The spy's florist van is his cover; 'delivering flowers' is his pretext.

    alibi: An alibi is a defensive claim that you were somewhere else when something happened, offered after the fact to clear yourself of blame. A pretext works before the act: it is the false reason given in advance to justify doing something. An alibi answers an accusation; a pretext opens a door.

    smokescreen: A smokescreen is anything thrown up to obscure what is really going on — a flurry of statistics, a manufactured controversy. It hides by confusing and distracting. A pretext hides by explaining: it hands people a tidy false reason so that they stop looking for the real one.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    truth: the actual fact or real reason

    honesty: truthfulness; freedom from deceit

    Where it sits · how much deception lies behind it

    excuse → pretext → ruse

    excuse vs. pretext: Excuse is weaker: an excuse may be honest, just feeble, while a pretext is a stated reason deliberately hiding the real one.

    ruse vs. pretext: Ruse is stronger: a ruse is a full trick or scheme in action, while a pretext is only the false reason offered at the start.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Formal, usually negative

    Calling a reason a pretext implies dishonesty — the stated reason is a screen for the real one. The most common pattern is 'under the pretext of doing something.'
