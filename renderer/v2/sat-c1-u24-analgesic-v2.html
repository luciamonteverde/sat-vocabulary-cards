Meaning

What It Means

Analgesic means a substance that relieves pain.

See the word in context

After the dental surgery, the oral surgeon recommended an over-the-counter analgesic to manage the soreness.

By mile twenty, Priya's knees throbbed with every stride. At the finish-line medical tent, a volunteer offered her ice and a mild analgesic. Within an hour the sharp ache had faded to a dull background hum. Nothing about the pill fixed her knees — it simply quieted the pain they were sending.

The pill relieves Priya's pain without curing or treating anything else — doing exactly and only what an analgesic does.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    painkiller: A painkiller is any drug that relieves pain, and it is the everyday word — the one you use at home or hear across a drugstore counter. Analgesic names exactly the same kind of drug in clinical language: the term printed on medicine labels and written on charts. The difference is register, not meaning.

    pain reliever: Pain reliever is the gentle phrase printed on over-the-counter boxes for a medicine that eases aches — plain words chosen to reassure shoppers. An analgesic is the same medicine under its technical name, the word a pharmacist or a research paper would choose. Say pain reliever in the aisle; write analgesic on the chart.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    irritant: a substance that causes soreness or discomfort

    toxin: a substance that harms the body

    Where it sits · how much feeling it takes away

    balm → analgesic → anesthetic

    balm vs. analgesic: A balm merely soothes minor discomfort, while an analgesic actively relieves real pain.

    anesthetic vs. analgesic: An anesthetic goes further than an analgesic — it blocks sensation entirely rather than just easing pain.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      medical, pharmaceutical

      You'll meet analgesic on medicine labels and in doctors' offices. Keep it apart from antiseptic (another word in this unit): an analgesic relieves pain, while antiseptic describes something that prevents infection by keeping germs away — a well-stocked first-aid kit needs both, because neither does the other's job.

      Putting it all together

      Read the passage, then answer Part A. Part B gives you a new passage.

      Long before pharmacies, people chewed willow bark when their joints ached. They could not have named the chemistry, but they had found a genuine analgesic. The bark's bitter compound, salicin, dulls pain without putting the patient to sleep — relief, not unconsciousness. In the nineteenth century, chemists learned first to isolate the compound and then to tame it, softening a molecule that had been hard on the stomach. The result, sold as aspirin, became the most widely taken drug on earth. Its job description never changed: quiet the pain and otherwise leave the body alone. Modern successors crowd the shelves, but they are judged by the old bark's standard — how much hurt they remove, and how little else they disturb.
