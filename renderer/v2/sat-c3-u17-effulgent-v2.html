effulgent

/ih-FUL-jent/ - the stress falls on FUL

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Effulgent means radiant, splendorous.

See the word in context

By ten each morning the whitewashed courtyard is effulgent, and the café retreats into the arcade's shade.

For the opera's final scene the designers rigged three hundred bulbs behind a gauze scrim, and every reviewer reached for the same word: effulgent — the stage did not seem lit so much as made of light.

Effulgent beats 'bright' or 'shiny' here because it names light that pours out in a steady, glorious flood. A desk lamp is bright; a stage that seems to be light itself is effulgent — the word carries splendor, not just wattage.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    radiant: Radiant is the everyday cousin — a warm glow from a face, a heater, a winter sun. Effulgent goes grander: light pouring out in a steady, glorious flood, the word for skies and candlelit halls. A smile is radiant; a sunrise that fills the sky is effulgent.

    dazzling: Dazzling light is so bright it overwhelms the eye for a moment — you squint and look away. Effulgent light never hurts: it pours out steadily and gloriously, splendor you can gaze into. Dazzling is about the jolt to your eyes; effulgent is about the splendor.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    dim: giving barely any light

    lightless: giving off no light at all

    Where it sits · how intense the light is

    bright → effulgent → blinding

    bright vs. effulgent: Bright is everyday light — a good reading lamp; it stops far short of effulgent.

    blinding vs. effulgent: Blinding goes further than effulgent: the radiance stops being splendid and starts to hurt.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    literary / elevated

    Effulgent is a special-occasion word for light that pours out steadily and gloriously — a sunrise sky, a hall of candles. Keep it apart from this unit's scintillating, which is light broken into dancing sparks: a full harvest sky is effulgent, sun on rippled water is scintillating. For ordinary light, bright does the job.

    Every November, the river town of San Marcos floats ten thousand paper lanterns on the water for the Feast of Remembrance. Tourists expect fireworks and are quietly corrected. Nothing here bursts or flashes; the lanterns are set loose at dusk, one household at a time, until the whole river runs gold. By full dark the water is effulgent — a broad, steady flood of light that spectators can gaze into for an hour without once needing to look away. Photographers complain, cheerfully, that there is no single source to shoot: the glory comes from everywhere at once, poured out rather than aimed. Children fall asleep on the banks facing it, which, as the mayor likes to say, is something you could never do facing a searchlight.
