rehabilitate

/ree-huh-BIL-i-tayt/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Rehabilitate means to bring a person or thing back to health, working condition, normal life, or a good reputation, especially after illness, injury, addiction, imprisonment, or disrepair.

See the word in context

Staff at the wildlife center worked for months to rehabilitate the injured hawk before releasing it back into the wild.

The carpentry workshop was designed to rehabilitate former inmates, and after a year of training, many graduates left with certifications and steady job offers waiting for them.

The program takes people whose lives were disrupted by imprisonment and restores them to productive, normal life through new skills and real jobs. That patient restoration of a person to good standing is precisely what rehabilitate describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    restore: Restore means to bring something back to its original condition — furniture, faded photographs, historic houses. It centers on how the thing looked or was. Rehabilitate centers on function and health: rehabilitating a patient, an offender, or a wetland returns it to working order, not to an original appearance.

    recondition: Recondition means to repair something mechanical so it works well again — engines and appliances get reconditioned. Rehabilitate is for living things and lives: patients, injured knees, former inmates, damaged habitats. You recondition what runs; you rehabilitate what lives, heals, and rejoins.

    reintegrate: Reintegrate means to bring someone back into a community after time away — one step, the social one. Rehabilitate names the whole restoration: rebuilding health, skills, standing, or working condition after illness, injury, imprisonment, or disrepair. Reintegration is often the last stage of rehabilitating someone.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    damage: to harm something so it no longer works well

    abandon: to give up on entirely

    Where it sits · how sweeping the repair work is

    mend → rehabilitate → remake

    mend vs. rehabilitate: Mend is weaker than rehabilitate; you can mend a small tear in an afternoon, but rehabilitating something takes sustained effort to bring it back to full health.

    remake vs. rehabilitate: Remake is stronger than rehabilitate; rehabilitating restores what was there before, while remaking tears everything down and builds something entirely new in its place.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      Formal / medical and legal

      Used for people, reputations, habitats, and injuries: rehabilitate a patient, an offender, a knee, or a damaged wetland. Keep it separate from renovate, which is reserved for buildings and structures rather than people.

      Mill Creek had been written off for decades: straightened into a concrete chute in the 1950s, it ran gray and lifeless through the middle of town. The rehabilitation crews did not try to make it pretty. They tore out the concrete, re-bent the channel into its old curves, and planted the banks with willow. The measure of success was blunt: could the creek do a creek's work again? Three springs later, the answer arrived on its own -- mayflies first, then minnows, then a heron that stood in the shallows all of June. A creek that had been a drain was back on duty, filtering runoff and holding its banks, rehabilitated in the only sense the crews cared about: it worked.
