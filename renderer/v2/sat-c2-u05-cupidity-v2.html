cupidity

/kyoo-PID-ih-tee/ - the stress falls on PID

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Cupidity means an overpowering greed for wealth.

See the word in context

The board meeting turned tense when the founder proposed cutting safety inspections to widen profits - cupidity dressed up as efficiency, one engineer muttered.

The lottery winner's cousin appeared within a week, full of investment ideas that all happened to route the money through his own accounts. When the winner hesitated, the cousin dropped the pretense and simply demanded a share. It was cupidity in its plainest form: not need, not a plan to build anything, just hunger for someone else's money.

Cupidity is narrower and harsher than greed: it is greed aimed specifically at wealth, and it carries the writer's judgment that the craving has taken the person over. A dragon coiled on a pile of gold is the picture cupidity paints.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    covetousness: Covetousness is fixated wanting aimed at what already belongs to someone else - the neighbor's car, a rival's title. Cupidity needs no owner to rob: it is an overpowering hunger for wealth itself, whoever holds it, and it would be just as ravenous over unclaimed treasure.

    acquisitiveness: Acquisitiveness is the collector's steady itch to obtain and add - more stamps, more sneakers, more of anything - and it can be harmless, even cheerful. Cupidity is neither mild nor neutral: a hunger for money so overpowering it has taken the person over, and the word itself accuses.

    avarice: Avarice is miserly greed that clutches wealth and cannot let it go - the hoard counted nightly, the purse never opened. Cupidity points at the craving side: the overpowering hunger to seize wealth in the first place. Avarice grips what it has; cupidity reaches for more.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    generosity: open-handed giving

    contentment: satisfaction with what one already has

    Where it sits · how fiercely the wanting fixes on money

    longing → greed → cupidity

    longing vs. cupidity: Longing is any strong wish and can be perfectly innocent; there is nothing innocent about cupidity.

    greed vs. cupidity: Greed is the broad vice of wanting too much of anything; cupidity sharpens it into an overpowering hunger for wealth in particular.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Cupidity is a courtroom-and-editorial word: it accuses. Everyday speech says greed. Reach for cupidity when the greed is specifically for money and you want the sentence to pass judgment - and note that despite the shared god, it has nothing to do with modern valentine romance.
