obdurate

/OB-duh-rit/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Obdurate means unyielding to persuasion, pity, or moral appeals.

See the word in context

The warden stayed obdurate even after hundreds of letters begged him to reopen the old case.

For ten years, descendants of the sculptor asked the collector to return the bronze figure to their village square. Historians wrote open letters, and schoolchildren mailed drawings of the empty pedestal. The collector read every appeal, admitted the family's grief was real, and refused each request without exception. Friends said no appeal to conscience had ever moved him on any matter of property. His heart, one curator sighed, was simply obdurate.

The collector hears every emotional and moral appeal, admits they are moving, and still refuses every single time — that hardened resistance to pity is exactly what obdurate describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unyielding: Unyielding means refusing to give ground under any kind of pressure — force, argument, demands, time. Obdurate is narrower and darker: hardened specifically against appeals to pity, conscience, and persuasion. A cliff face is unyielding; only a heart that hears the plea and still refuses is obdurate.

    hardhearted: Hardhearted names a general lack of pity or warmth toward others' suffering — an everyday word for a cold nature. Obdurate is more specific and more literary: appeals have actually been made — tears, arguments, conscience — and every one has bounced off. A hardhearted person may never be tested; an obdurate one refuses appeal after appeal.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    relenting: softening and giving in

    softhearted: easily moved to sympathy

    Where it sits · how impossible to soften

    firm → obdurate → implacable

    firm vs. obdurate: Firm describes someone who holds a position steadily but can still be reasoned with; obdurate goes further, suggesting a heart hardened against every plea.

    implacable vs. obdurate: Implacable is even stronger than obdurate — an implacable person cannot be appeased at all, ever, while an obdurate one is 'merely' deaf to persuasion and pity.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      formal, literary; usually disapproving

      Obdurate is the harshest of this unit's 'stubborn' words: it suggests a heart hardened against pity or moral appeals specifically. Unlike obstinate (a general refusal to change one's mind or course) or adamant (loud, emphatic insistence on a particular point), obdurate implies that even tears and conscience cannot get through.
