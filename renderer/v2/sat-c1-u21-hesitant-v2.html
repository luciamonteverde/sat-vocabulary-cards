hesitant

/HEZ-ih-tent/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Hesitant means slow to act because of uncertainty.

See the word in context

The new lifeguard was hesitant on his first whistle, unsure whether the swimmers were really too far out.

The scholarship application sat open on Jun's laptop for eleven days. He reread the essay prompt each night, typed a sentence, and deleted it. His teachers had told him he was exactly the kind of candidate the program wanted, but he kept wondering if his grades from freshman year would sink him. The night before the deadline, the form was still blank. Jun wasn't lazy or uninterested — he was hesitant, and his uncertainty had nearly cost him the chance.

Jun's slowness to act — eleven days of typing and deleting — flows directly from his uncertainty about his own chances, which is exactly what hesitant means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    reluctant: Reluctant means unwilling — you drag your feet because you do not want to do the thing at all. Hesitant is about doubt, not desire: a hesitant person usually wants to act and is slowed by uncertainty. A reluctant witness resents being called at all; a hesitant one wants to speak but keeps stalling.

    tentative: Tentative describes the cautious, unconfident manner of an action, or a plan made only provisionally — a tentative knock, a tentative agreement. Hesitant is about the delay itself: uncertainty slowing you down before you act at all. A tentative step is soft and careful; a hesitant one is late in coming.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    confident: sure of oneself

    eager: keen to act at once

    Where it sits · how much doubt blocks action

    unsure → hesitant → paralyzed

    unsure vs. hesitant: Unsure is the milder state — you can be unsure and still act quickly; hesitant means the uncertainty is visibly slowing you down.

    paralyzed vs. hesitant: Paralyzed is far stronger than hesitant: a paralyzed decision-maker cannot act at all, while a hesitant one acts late and haltingly.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral, everyday

      Hesitant is the adjective for a person or manner ('a hesitant knock'); its verb cousin hesitate names the pause itself. Being hesitant is about uncertainty, not refusal — a hesitant person usually wants to act and can't quite start.
