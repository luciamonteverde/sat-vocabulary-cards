Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A statistic is a single numerical fact drawn from collected data — one measured number standing in for a much larger pattern.

See the word in context

One statistic anchored the entire transit hearing: ridership had doubled in five years while the fleet had grown by exactly one bus.

The plant's safety officer opened every training with the same statistic — four of every five ladder injuries on site happened on the bottom three rungs — because one measured fact ended more arguments than an hour of warnings.

A statistic is more exact than a 'fact' or a 'figure' because it makes a double claim: the number was actually measured, and it stands for something bigger than itself. Four-in-five is a statistic because someone counted the injuries — the count is what earns the number its authority, and losing sight of the people inside the count is why 'becoming a statistic' sounds so cold.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    figure: A figure is any written number — a price, a guess, a rounded total scribbled on a napkin. A statistic is a figure with a pedigree: gathered from data, backed by a count, and offered as evidence.

    data point: A data point is one raw observation — a single reading in the pile. A statistic usually stands a step above the pile: a number distilled from many data points to say what they add up to.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    anecdote: a single told story, offered as loose evidence

    guesswork: conclusions reached with no data behind them

    Where it sits · how much hard measurement stands behind the number

    hunch → estimate → statistic

    hunch vs. statistic: A hunch is the bottom rung, far below a statistic — a feeling about how things stand, with no data behind it at all.

    estimate vs. statistic: An estimate climbs closer to a statistic — it does put a number on things — but the number is approximated; a statistic's number has been measured and counted.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      analytic / journalistic

      Statistic is singular and specific: one measured number — 'a startling statistic' — while statistics, with the s, is the whole discipline. Keep it apart from anecdote, its natural sparring partner in every argument about evidence; and notice the chill in 'don't become a statistic,' where a person shrinks to a data entry. If the number was ballparked rather than counted, call it an estimate and stay honest.

      When the county proposed cutting library hours, the head librarian answered with a single statistic. Circulation records from all nine branches, tallied over three years, showed that four in ten checkouts happened after five in the evening. She was careful about what the number was not. It was not a guess, and it was not one busy Tuesday standing in for the rest. Commissioners tried to wave it away as a fluke, so she brought the tally sheets — thirty-six months of them, branch by branch. The evening hours survived the vote. One measured number, she said later, had done what a year of pleading letters could not.
