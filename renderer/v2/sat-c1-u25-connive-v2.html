connive

/kuh-NYVE/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Connive means to secretly cooperate or plot with others in a scheme, often a dishonest one.

See the word in context

Prosecutors alleged that the referee had connived with gamblers to shave points off the final score.

For months, the harbor's night dispatcher logged certain fishing boats as 'in for repairs' whenever they slipped out after dark. The boats returned before dawn, their holds heavy, and the dispatcher's logs never showed they had left. When customs agents finally compared satellite records with the logbook, the pattern was unmistakable. The dispatcher had not merely looked the other way — he had connived with the crews, coordinating the false entries that kept their runs invisible.

The dispatcher secretly cooperates with the crews — coordinating false log entries to keep their scheme hidden — which is exactly the joint, secret plotting that connive describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    collude: Collude means to cooperate secretly in wrongdoing, and it is the closest partner to connive — both involve a hidden, dishonest joint effort. Connive keeps a shade of passivity: people who connive may simply coordinate quietly to let the wrong thing happen, where colluding partners work the scheme as equals.

    plot: Plot means to plan something harmful in secret, and a plot can be one person's work alone. Connive requires company: to connive is to cooperate secretly with others, helping a scheme along rather than merely designing it. A lone saboteur plots; the guard who quietly assists him connives.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    expose: to bring hidden wrongdoing into the open

    thwart: to block a plan rather than help it along

    Where it sits · how actively the scheme is joined

    condone → connive → conspire

    condone vs. connive: Condone is weaker than connive: someone who condones wrongdoing merely accepts it without protest, while someone who connives actively helps the scheme along in secret.

    conspire vs. connive: Conspire is stronger than connive — conspirators build an organized plot with defined roles, while people who connive may simply cooperate quietly to let the wrong thing happen.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal; legal and journalistic, strongly disapproving

      Connive usually takes 'with' ('connived with the vendors') and implies joint, secret wrongdoing. Keep it apart from this unit's furtive, which describes a sneaky manner rather than a scheme, and reclusive, which means avoiding company altogether with no wrongdoing implied at all.

      In 1911, the harbor commission noticed that the Elmport ferry was somehow selling more discount tickets than existed. The scheme needed no mastermind on board. A dockside printer ran off extra ticket books, and two veteran conductors agreed to connive with him, punching the counterfeits as if they were genuine. The conductors never touched the press and took only a small cut. Their contribution was quieter: they simply declined to see what was in front of them. An auditor finally caught the fraud by weighing the punch confetti — too much paper for the tickets officially sold. At the hearing, the ferry line's lawyer argued the conductors were bystanders; the judge disagreed, noting that a scheme this smooth requires helpful eyes willing to close.
