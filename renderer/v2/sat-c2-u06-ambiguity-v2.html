What It Means

Ambiguity means the quality of having more than one possible meaning or interpretation.

See the word in context

The coach's text - 'practice moved to 5' - carried an ambiguity that scattered the team between the 5 a.m. and 5 p.m. buses.

The will left 'my china to Lee' - and the family split over whether Grandmother meant the rose-pattern dishes or the walnut china cabinet that held them. Each reading was sharp, sensible, and fully supported by the sentence; that was the ambiguity, and it took a retired judge to settle what one extra word would have prevented.

Ambiguity is exact because the sentence has two clean interpretations, not zero: everyone understood both meanings perfectly. If the will had said something no one could parse at all, that would be vagueness. Ambiguity's trouble - and its poetry - is the choice between clear readings.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    murkiness: Murkiness is general unclearness - meaning dimmed all over, like silt stirred into water. Ambiguity is a sharper failure: the wording is perfectly clear twice over, and the trouble is choosing between two clean readings. A murky sentence gives too little meaning; an ambiguity hands you one meaning too many.

    equivocation: Equivocation is ambiguity put to work on purpose - wording engineered to carry two readings so the speaker can slide between them and dodge blame. Ambiguity itself is neutral about intent: a will or a headline can carry it by accident. Only the deliberate dodge earns the harsher name.

    uncertainty: Uncertainty lives in the reader - the unsettled state of not knowing how things stand. Ambiguity lives in the wording itself: one sentence supporting two sharp readings at once. An ambiguity in a clause usually produces uncertainty in its readers, but only wording, never the feeling, can be ambiguous.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    clarity: one meaning, plainly delivered

    exactness: wording pinned down to a single reading

    Where it sits · how much readable meaning survives

    ambiguity → vagueness → gibberish

    vagueness vs. ambiguity: Vagueness goes further than ambiguity: an ambiguous clause offers two sharp readings to choose between, while a vague one offers no reading you could write down.

    gibberish vs. ambiguity: Gibberish is the far end of the ladder: ambiguity gives you two meanings, vagueness gives you a blur, and gibberish gives you no meaning at all.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / analytic

    Reach for ambiguity only when there are two or more sharp readings to choose between - a will, a border clause, a pun. If the problem is that no clear reading exists at all, that is vagueness; and a plan that never takes shape is nebulous, its cousin in this unit. Ambiguity can even be praise: poets protect it, and diplomats bank on it.

    The trouble with the yearbook's caption was not that it said too little. 'Coach Reyes thanked the seniors who carried the team' — every word plain, every reading clean. The ambiguity sat in one small clause. Did the coach thank all the seniors, who happened to carry the team? Or only those particular seniors who carried it? Read the first way, the caption was a warm farewell. Read the second, it quietly accused half the roster of dead weight. Nobody thought the editor had planned the insult; she was mortified. The wording had simply been struck twice with meaning, once for each reading. The retraction, everyone agreed, should say exactly one thing. It took the staff four drafts to be sure that it did.
