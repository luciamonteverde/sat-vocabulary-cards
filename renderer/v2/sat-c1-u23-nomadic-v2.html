nomadic

/noh-MAD-ik/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Nomadic means wandering from place to place rather than settling permanently.

See the word in context

The young male wolf led a nomadic life for two years, crossing four mountain ranges without ever holding a territory.

Most songbirds of the boreal forest keep to the same few hectares all their lives. Crossbills do not. A flock ringed in Finland one autumn turned up 1,400 kilometres east the next year, then in Norway the year after, always where the spruce cones hung heaviest and never twice in the same wood. Because cone crops fail unpredictably, the birds can hold neither a fixed route nor a fixed home, and their nomadic habit is the only way they keep eating.

Birds that travel 1,400 kilometres to wherever the cones are heaviest, never return to the same wood, and hold no home range at all are wandering rather than settling, which is the heart of nomadic.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    wandering: Wandering means moving about with no fixed home or destination, and it can describe one lost afternoon as easily as a lifetime. Nomadic makes the larger claim: the wandering is the whole way of life, permanent and organized around movement.

    drifting: Drifting means moving along with no aim at all, carried wherever chance takes you. Nomadic life also lacks a fixed home, but its movement has purpose -- herders track pasture, crossbill flocks track cone crops -- so a nomadic people is not simply drifting.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    settled: living permanently in one place

    sedentary: staying in one place rather than moving

    Where it sits · how fully travel defines the life

    mobile → itinerant → nomadic

    mobile vs. nomadic: Mobile means only that something is able to move, while nomadic says the moving never stops long enough to make a permanent home.

    itinerant vs. nomadic: Itinerant is nomadic's close cousin, but it usually describes people travelling for work, such as an itinerant repairman, rather than a whole way of life.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral and descriptive

    Nomadic describes a pattern of living rather than wildness or automatic behaviour, so keep it clear of feral (a domesticated animal now living wild) and instinctive (a single automatic behaviour) elsewhere in this unit. Note too that nomadic movement is unpredictable and homeless by definition, which is what separates it from a seasonal journey along a fixed route.

    For centuries the Tuareg of the central Sahara built their entire year around movement. Families followed the grazing with their camel herds, and twice a year great salt caravans crossed the desert to trade. Their nomadic life was not aimless roaming. Every route was set by wells that could not be missed and by pastures that lasted only a few weeks. Households carried their architecture with them: tents of hide and woven mats that two people could raise in an hour. To settle in one place, the elders said, was to watch your herds strip the land bare around you; the desert fed only those who kept moving.
