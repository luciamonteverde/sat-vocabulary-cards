Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Fatalistic means convinced that events are decided by fate and cannot be changed.

See the word in context

Dario was fatalistic about auditions: 'They cast the part before you walk in,' he would shrug, 'so you might as well walk in relaxed.'

His fatalistic grandmother refused to check the lottery numbers or the forecast, on identical grounds: 'What's coming is already on its way, and it doesn't need my permission.'

Fatalistic is more precise than 'gloomy' or 'negative' because it names a belief, not a mood: that events are decided in advance and effort cannot bend them. A fatalistic person can even be cheerful — they've simply stopped believing that trying changes anything.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    defeatist: A defeatist is braced to lose this particular fight — the match, the election, the audit — and behaves as if the loss has already happened. Fatalistic runs wider and deeper: it says every outcome, wins included, was scripted in advance. A defeatist expects to lose; a fatalistic person expects nothing anyone does to matter.

    hopeless: Hopeless means without hope of any good outcome — the future looks dark from every angle. Fatalistic is not about darkness but about authorship: a fatalistic person may cheerfully expect good things, believing only that fate, never effort, will deliver them. Hopeless despairs of the ending; fatalistic just hands over the steering wheel.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    determined: keeps pushing, certain that effort moves the result

    hopeful: expects things can turn out better — and that trying helps

    Where it sits · how deep the giving up goes

    passive → fatalistic → nihilistic

    passive vs. fatalistic: Passive people simply don't act — a milder condition than fatalistic, which supplies the reason no one should bother: the outcome is already written.

    nihilistic vs. fatalistic: Nihilistic goes further than fatalistic: fatalism says the ending is fixed, nihilism says there was never any meaning in the story at all.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / analytical

      Fatalistic describes a stance toward the future — miners, gamblers, and stormwatchers who have decided outcomes are prewritten. It is not sadness; for that use 'gloomy.' Its unit-mate phlegmatic can look similar from outside, but the phlegmatic person stays calm while still acting; the fatalistic one has concluded acting is beside the point. And quixotic is its perfect opposite — charging at windmills fatalism would walk past.

The outlook is fatalistic down to its bones — 'the sea decides,' the saying goes — and it covers the good years exactly as it covers the drownings. When the co-op proposed a forecasting subscription, the older captains shrugged and paid for it, then went out on the same days they would have gone anyway. It is not gloom.
