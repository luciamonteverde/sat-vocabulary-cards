What It Means

Incipient means just beginning to exist or develop.

See the word in context

An incipient smile tugged at the witness's mouth before she caught it and looked down.

Walking the rows at dawn, the vineyard manager found incipient mildew on two leaves — a gray dust you'd miss from a meter away — and had the sprayer out before the dew burned off.

Incipient flags the very first stage of something that is developing — often something you'd rather stop. Where inchoate (this unit) describes the shapelessness of what has begun, incipient simply marks how early it is: the thing has just crossed the line into existing.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    budding: Budding means just beginning to grow, and it keeps the garden's hopefulness — budding careers, budding friendships. Incipient is the clinical cousin, neutral to wary: incipient rust, incipient panic. Both mark the earliest stage; budding cheers it on, incipient watches it warily.

    emerging: Emerging means coming into view and gathering notice — an emerging trend is a stage past the very start, already showing its direction. Incipient sits earlier: the thing has only just crossed the line into existing, easy to miss altogether.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    full-blown: arrived at full force, nothing early about it

    advanced: far along in its development

    Where it sits · how far along it has grown

    incipient → developing → full-blown

    developing vs. incipient: Developing goes further than incipient: an incipient project has barely appeared, while a developing one is visibly under way.

    full-blown vs. incipient: Full-blown goes far beyond incipient — the storm has arrived in full force, with nothing 'just beginning' about it.

      formal
