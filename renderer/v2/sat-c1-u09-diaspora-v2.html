diaspora

/dy-AS-pur-uh/ - the stress falls on AS

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A diaspora is a people scattered far from their homeland — and the name for the scattered community itself, settled in many countries yet still one people.

See the word in context

Letters, remittances, and recipes stitched the island's diaspora to villages its children had never seen.

After the famine, the valley's weavers scattered to mill towns on three continents; a century later their diaspora still holds a reunion every summer, and the old pattern books pass between cousins by mail.

No single move made this a diaspora — one family relocating is just a move. The word asks for a whole people dispersed from a shared homeland, and for the threads that keep the scattered community one thing: the reunions, the pattern books, the sense that 'home' still names the valley.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    dispersion: Dispersion is the physics-neutral word for things spread out from a center — seeds, droplets, data points. Diaspora is dispersion with a people and a homeland in it: the scattered still belong to somewhere, and to each other.

    exodus: An exodus is the mass departure itself — one dramatic leaving, out the door together. A diaspora is what exists afterward and for generations: the same people, now settled in many places, still tied to the home they left.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    homecoming: the return of the scattered to the place they came from

    ingathering: the drawing of a dispersed people back together in one land

    Where it sits · how far, and how lastingly, the people are scattered

    migration → diaspora → dissolution

    migration vs. diaspora: Migration is milder than diaspora: a migrating people may move once and settle together in a single new home, while a diaspora is flung across many countries at once, with the homeland left behind.

    dissolution vs. diaspora: Dissolution goes further than diaspora — a diaspora is scattered yet still one people, writing home and marrying in, while dissolution means the scattering wins and the community quietly stops being a community at all.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / historical and cultural

    Diaspora began as a proper noun — the Diaspora, the Jewish communities living outside Judea — and the capital-D sense is still current. Lowercase, it now serves any people scattered from a homeland: writers speak of the Irish, Armenian, or West African diaspora. It is a collective word; one emigrant belongs to a diaspora but cannot be one. And mind the timeline it implies: an exodus is the departure itself, the event — a diaspora is the long afterward, measured in generations.
