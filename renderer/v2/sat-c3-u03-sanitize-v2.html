Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Sanitize means to make something thoroughly clean and free of germs.

See the word in context

Wipe-down stations let members sanitize each bench and bar before the next lifter steps up.

On the research ship, the water samplers must be sanitized between stations, or bacteria from the harbor would ride along and show up in the Arctic data. The chemist runs each bottle through peroxide, then a sterile rinse, and seals it in a numbered bag. One skipped bottle in 2019 cost the team a whole transect of readings.

Sanitize is the exact verb because the goal is not visible cleanliness — the bottles already look spotless — but freedom from germs. Wash and scrub describe effort; sanitize names the standard the effort must reach: microbes knocked down far enough that they cannot contaminate what comes next.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    disinfect: To disinfect is to kill germs with a chemical agent — the sprays and wipes of the cleaning aisle name the weapon. Sanitize names the standard instead: a surface is sanitized once its germs are down to safe levels, however you got there. You disinfect with something; you sanitize to a level.

    cleanse: To cleanse is to clean something deeply and thoroughly — skin, a wound, even a reputation — with no particular promise about germs. Sanitize makes exactly that promise: not just clean, but germ-safe. A cleansed counter looks and feels spotless; a sanitized one has been made safe to eat from.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    contaminate: to introduce the germs or dirt you were trying to remove

    pollute: to foul something on a larger scale — air, water, whole rivers

    Where it sits · how far past visible dirt it goes

    tidy up → clean → sanitize → sterilize

    tidy up vs. sanitize: Tidying up is far weaker than sanitizing: it only puts things where they belong and leaves the germs untouched, while sanitizing scrubs a surface until the germs themselves are gone.

    clean vs. sanitize: Clean handles what the eye can see — crumbs and dust are gone — but it stops short of sanitizing, which goes after the germs you cannot see.

    sterilize vs. sanitize: Sterilize goes further than sanitize — sanitizing knocks germs down to safe levels, while sterilizing kills every last organism, lab-style.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / technical

    Sanitize is a standard, not a chore: it means cleaned to the point of being germ-safe. Below it sit clean and tidy; above it, sterilize, the operating-room word. It also has a second career — a sanitized report has had the embarrassing parts scrubbed out — and that use always carries a raised eyebrow.

    The city pool's water is tested at noon and again at six, and the numbers are posted by the diving board for anyone to read. State law does not tell the staff how to sanitize the water -- chlorine, salt systems, and UV lamps are all acceptable. What the law fixes is the outcome: the bacterial count must sit below a set line, every test, every day. One July the count crept upward, and the pool closed for an afternoon while the staff adjusted the system. By the evening test the water was back under the line, and the gates reopened -- clean enough to swim in, by the only measure the law counts.
