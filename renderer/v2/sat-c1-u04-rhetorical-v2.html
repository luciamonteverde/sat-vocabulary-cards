Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Rhetorical means relating to the art of persuasive speaking or writing; (of a question) asked for effect, not expecting an answer.

See the word in context

At the debate-team workshop, students marked every rhetorical device in the famous speech, from its unanswered questions to its drumbeat of repeated phrases.

When Mr. Okada asked the class, 'Do we really want to be the group that gives up before the experiment even starts?', the question was rhetorical—he was already handing out the lab goggles.

No answer is expected; the question exists purely for effect, to rally the class. That is the classic rhetorical question. The word can also describe anything relating to the craft of persuasive speaking or writing itself.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    oratorical: Oratorical relates to formal public speaking as a whole — the delivery, the podium manner, the grand occasion. Rhetorical points at the persuasive craft inside the speaking: the devices and strategies designed to move an audience. A booming voice is an oratorical gift; a well-placed repeated phrase is a rhetorical one.

    stylistic: Stylistic covers any feature of how something is written or said — sentence length, tone, word choice — with no particular aim attached. Rhetorical names the features aimed specifically at persuading an audience. A fondness for semicolons is stylistic; a drumbeat of repeated phrases built to sway listeners is rhetorical.

    unanswerable: Unanswerable describes a question no one can answer — too hard, or without any answer to give. A rhetorical question could be answered easily; it simply is not meant to be, because it exists for effect. The unanswerable question defeats replies; the rhetorical question never wanted one.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    genuine: A genuine question truly seeks information; a rhetorical one expects no answer at all.

    plainspoken: Plainspoken delivery skips crafted devices entirely and just says things straight.

    Where it sits · how heavily persuasion tools are used

    polished → rhetorical → bombastic

    polished vs. rhetorical: Polished speech is simply well-finished; rhetorical speech deliberately deploys the tools of persuasion.

    bombastic vs. rhetorical: Bombastic language inflates rhetorical technique into overblown, self-important noise.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    academic; everyday in the fixed phrase 'rhetorical question'

    In essays and criticism, rhetorical describes the craft of persuasion—rhetorical devices, rhetorical strategies. In everyday speech it lives almost entirely in the phrase 'rhetorical question,' one asked for effect with no answer expected. Note that calling something rhetorical says nothing about whether it actually convinces anyone—that would be persuasive.
