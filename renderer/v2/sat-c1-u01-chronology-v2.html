chronology

/kruh-NOL-uh-jee/ - the stress falls on NOL

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Chronology means the order in which events actually happened in time, or an account that sets events out in that order.

See the word in context

No two biographers agree on the chronology of the poet's lost years — the letters are undated, and the landlords kept no books.

After the derailment, investigators refused to discuss blame until they had the chronology pinned down: which alarm sounded first, when the brakes engaged, and how many seconds passed before the signal changed.

Chronology is the exact word because the investigators are not yet judging causes or motives; they are fixing the order in which the events actually happened, second by second. Establish that time-order first, and every later argument has something solid to stand on.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    timeline: A timeline is the graphic form: events plotted along a drawn line, often for a project still ahead. Chronology is the underlying time-order itself, and it belongs to events that have actually happened, whether or not anyone draws them.

    chronicle: A chronicle is a written record kept as events unfold — a monastery's year-by-year annals. Chronology is the time-order itself, whether or not anyone wrote it down; you can reconstruct a chronology centuries later, but a chronicle is set down as things happen.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    jumble: a heap of items in no order at all

    hodgepodge: a confused mixture thrown together without any arrangement

    Where it sits · how tightly events are pinned to the clock

    sequence → chronology → timetable

    sequence vs. chronology: A sequence is a weaker claim than a chronology: it promises only that one item follows another, on any basis at all, while a chronology commits every event to its real place in time.

    timetable vs. chronology: A timetable is stricter than a chronology, nailing each entry to an exact clock reading; a chronology fixes the true order of events without demanding the minute of every one.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral - at home in history, law, and lab reports

    Use chronology when what matters is the order in which events actually happened, or the account that fixes that order — investigators establish a chronology, historians defend one. For events still ahead, the honest words are schedule and itinerary; for a record written down as events unfold, chronicle. And keep it apart from this unit's sequential, which orders steps on any basis at all: a recipe is sequential, but only events in time have a chronology.
