analogue

/AN-uh-log/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Analogue means a thing that corresponds to another — a counterpart that plays the same role and works in the same way in a different setting, form, or time.

See the word in context

Historians call the Roman bath house an ancient analogue of the modern gym: a place people visited daily to exercise, wash, and trade gossip.

Studying how leafcutter ants farm fungus in their underground chambers, the ecologist described the colony as a natural analogue of human agriculture: the ants plant their crop, weed it, fertilize it, and harvest it — a food supply they tend because they cannot survive without it.

The ant colony is not agriculture, but it plays the same role and works the same way in a different form — and a counterpart like that is exactly what an analogue is.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    counterpart: Counterpart means the matching item that holds the same position in another system — your counterpart abroad does your job in another country. Analogue stresses likeness of form and working, not just of position: an analogue corresponds in how it operates.

    parallel: Parallel means a case that runs alongside another with striking points of likeness — historians draw parallels between eras. A parallel is a comparison you draw; an analogue is the corresponding thing itself.

    equivalent: Equivalent means something interchangeable in value or function — equal enough to swap one for the other. An analogue corresponds without being swappable: the octopus eye mirrors a camera, but neither can do the other's job.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    antithesis: the direct opposite — the case that reverses rather than mirrors

    one-off: a thing with no counterpart anywhere; nothing else maps onto it

    Where it sits · how fully the counterpart matches

    likeness → analogue → twin

    likeness vs. analogue: A likeness is a fainter claim than an analogue: two things may share a likeness in one respect only, while an analogue corresponds in role and working across the board.

    twin vs. analogue: Twin goes further than analogue: an analogue mirrors another thing's role in a different form, but twins are the same thing twice over, matching down to the details.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral, academic

    Analogue is the noun in a family this unit teaches twice: analogous is the adjective — the eye is analogous to a camera — while an analogue is the corresponding thing itself: the eye is an analogue of the camera. Science and history writing lean on it constantly. You will also meet the other analogue, the adjective meaning non-digital, as in analogue clocks and analogue film; it grew from the same idea of one thing standing in proportion to another.

    Historians hunting for the roots of social media keep landing in an unexpected place: the London coffeehouse of the early 1700s. For the price of a penny, anyone could enter, read the papers left on the tables, and argue with strangers about politics, science, or trade. News traveled through these rooms faster than through any official channel, and rumors traveled faster still. That is why many historians call the coffeehouse an early analogue of the online feed. It played the same role and worked much the same way: a public space where information, opinion, and gossip mixed and spread. The correspondence is not an equivalence — no one could carry a coffeehouse in a pocket — but role for role, the mapping holds.
