Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Anecdotal means based on personal stories rather than systematic evidence.

See the word in context

The professor granted that cold-plunge fans swear by the habit, but she called the benefits anecdotal until someone counts them properly.

At the town meeting, eleven neighbors described smoother commutes since the roundabout opened, and the council nearly voted on the spot. The traffic engineer held up a hand: the improvement was still anecdotal — eleven happy drivers is a start, not a measurement — and the sensor study would report within the month.

Anecdotal does not mean false; every story may be true. It means the support is personal experience rather than systematic evidence, and that is the critical force the SAT tests: anecdotal evidence is the charming kind that proves the least.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    story-based: Story-based support rests on individual accounts rather than measurements — what particular people say happened to them. Anecdotal means the same resting-on-stories, but adds a quiet verdict: in serious writing it concedes the stories are real while denying them the weight of evidence.

    hearsay: Hearsay is what circulates at second hand — you repeat what someone told you someone else saw. Anecdotal accounts are usually firsthand: the teller lived it. Both fall short of systematic evidence, but hearsay adds distance from the event, while anecdotal keeps the witness and loses only the rigor.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    data-driven: resting on measured numbers gathered on purpose

    controlled: tested under conditions built to rule out luck

    Where it sits · how thin the support beneath it

    untested → anecdotal → baseless

    untested vs. anecdotal: Untested is milder than anecdotal — it says no one has checked the claim yet, not that the only support is stories.

    baseless vs. anecdotal: Baseless goes further than anecdotal: anecdotal evidence is at least somebody's real experience, while a baseless claim has nothing under it at all.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      academic / critical

      In test passages and serious writing, anecdotal is quietly damning: it concedes the stories are real while denying them the weight of evidence. Use hearsay for rumor at second hand; use anecdotal when true-enough personal accounts are offered where data belongs.
