Meaning

What It Means

Assuage means to ease, pacify.

See the word in context

The counselor's steady evening routine helped assuage the recruits' homesickness during the first weeks of training.

When the bakery's oven died the week before the festival, the owner's panic eased only after a rival across town offered her ovens overnight; the gesture assuaged a dread that no amount of pep talk had touched.

Assuage is more precise than 'comfort' because it acts on the feeling itself—you assuage hunger, guilt, grief, or dread, easing its grip without pretending to erase it. Comfort soothes the person; assuage shrinks the ache.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    ease: Ease is the plainest, most everyday word for making something less painful, and it works on almost anything — pain, traffic, an awkward transition. Assuage is more literary and more exact: it takes a named feeling — grief, guilt, dread, hunger — and loosens its grip. To assuage is to ease with the ache named.

    alleviate: Alleviate means to lessen a burden or symptom — medicine alleviates pain, aid alleviates a shortage. It is practical, aimed at measurable problems. Assuage stays with the inner feeling itself: you assuage guilt, fear, or grief, quieting an emotion rather than shrinking a workload.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    worsen: makes the feeling stronger instead

    embitter: turns pain into lasting resentment

    Where it sits · how completely the distress is removed

    soothe → assuage → banish

    soothe vs. assuage: Soothe is a gentler touch than assuage — a warm drink soothes you for an hour without changing what you're facing, while to assuage a dread is to genuinely loosen its hold.

    banish vs. assuage: Banish goes further than soothe or assuage—when a fear is banished it is gone entirely, not merely quieted.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / literary

      Assuage aims at the feeling, not the person: you assuage grief, fears, hunger, guilt. Its unit-mate placate aims at people—you placate an angry crowd—and appease, in this unit's reference list, goes further than placate, buying peace by granting demands. Use plain 'ease' in casual speech; assuage suits writing that names the exact ache being quieted.
