Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Correlate means to vary together — when two things correlate, changes in one are matched by changes in the other across the record.

See the word in context

The trainer's logbook shows resting heart rate and race times correlate: as one falls through the season, so does the other.

Since the shop opened, the owner of Spoke & Chain has penciled two numbers into her day book every Friday: the week's average gas price and the number of bikes sold. Laid side by side, the columns correlate — when gas climbs a dime, sales tick upward within a week or two, and the long flat stretches match almost line for line. She has stopped guessing at spring inventory; she reads the fuel report instead, letting one column tell her where the other is headed.

Correlate is the exact word for those two columns, because correlate means to vary together — changes in one matched by changes in the other across the whole record. Notice what the word does not claim: it never says expensive gas causes bike sales, only that the two move in step. That restraint is the point. A researcher who reports that two things correlate is describing a pattern and stopping there, one careful step short of naming a cause.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    correspond: Correspond says two things match up, point for point — a map corresponds to the terrain even though neither ever changes. Correlate adds movement: correlated things vary together, each shift in one answered by a shift in the other.

    go hand in hand: Go hand in hand is the everyday phrase for two things regularly found together — effort and success go hand in hand. Correlate is its measured cousin: it lives in charts and studies, claiming the pairing shows up in the data, not just in proverbs.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    diverge: split apart; one climbs while the other wanders off on its own

    vary independently: each moves to its own rhythm, deaf to the other

    Where it sits · how tightly the two movements are bound together

    coincide → correlate → synchronize

    coincide vs. correlate: Coincide is weaker than correlate: two schedules can coincide once, at a single shared moment, while correlating takes a whole record of rises and falls that keep answering each other.

    synchronize vs. correlate: Synchronize goes further than correlate — correlated things move together loosely, lag and all, while synchronized things are locked to the same beat, matched moment for moment.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / academic

      Correlate is the cautious verb of charts and studies: it reports that two things move together and claims nothing more. Ascribe, its unit-mate, goes a step further — to ascribe is to assign a cause outright, which is exactly the step correlate refuses to take. Correspond leans static, a point-for-point match; link is the loose everyday verb. When a researcher is not yet ready to say 'because,' correlate is the word that holds the line.
