Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Precarious means dangerously insecure or unstable; likely to fall or fail.

See the word in context

The truce between the two dockworkers' unions was precarious, held together by one aging negotiator and a handshake nobody had written down.

Every April the ospreys rebuild atop the channel marker, and every April the harbormaster photographs the result: a meter-wide crown of sticks on a pole the width of a dinner plate, swaying with each wake, shedding twigs in every gust. The nest is precarious in the plainest sense — one strong northeaster from ending up in the bay.

Precarious beats shaky or insecure here because it adds the drop: not just unsteady, but unsteady with a fall waiting. The word's oldest sense — held only by someone else's favor — survives in every borrowed use: a precarious job, a precarious lead, and a precarious peace all hang on something that owes them nothing.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    insecure: Insecure means not firmly fastened or assured — a loose railing, a wobbly job offer, a doubtful lead — but the word stays quiet about what happens next. Precarious is louder about consequences: it insists a real fall or failure waits below, one nudge from arriving.

    unstable: Unstable means liable to shift, tip, or swing without warning — the balance inside the thing is off, whether it is a chemical, a currency, or a mood. It stops at the wobble itself. Precarious adds what waits underneath: a drop or a collapse, so the instability carries real stakes.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    secure: firmly fixed; safe from falling or failing

    solid: sound and dependable through and through

    Where it sits · how close to disaster it stands

    shaky → precarious → doomed

    shaky vs. precarious: A shaky ladder wobbles and warns you; precarious raises the stakes — the wobble stands over a drop.

    doomed vs. precarious: Doomed passes precarious on the way down: the precarious scheme might still be rescued, but the doomed one is already lost, rescue or not.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / vivid

    Precarious is for what could fall or fail at a touch — footing, finances, partnerships — and it always implies the drop below. Its unit-mate unsustainable runs on a longer clock: a precarious load may crash this afternoon, while an unsustainable pace will fail eventually even if nothing ever wobbles. Pick by the clock.

    The weather hut on the col has never missed a season, which surprises everyone who learns how it runs. Its whole supply line is one mule train, led by one man, over one pass that closes at the first heavy snow. Meteorologists call the arrangement precarious and mean it precisely: the hut functions beautifully, but a single turned ankle — man's or mule's — would end the record by November. Nothing about the hut itself is fragile. The walls are a meter of stone; the instruments are new. What tilts is everything beneath the arrangement: no second driver, no second pass, no margin. The station thrives one misstep from silence.
