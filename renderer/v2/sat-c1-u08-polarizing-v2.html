polarizing

/POH-luh-rye-zing/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Polarizing means causing people to split into sharply opposed camps, with almost no one left in the middle.

See the word in context

The proposal to build a new stadium downtown proved so polarizing that neighbors who had been friendly for years stopped speaking over it.

The director's new film proved so polarizing that reviews split between raves and angry walkouts, with almost nothing in between.

The film pushed viewers to two opposite extremes — love or fury — and emptied out the middle ground, which is precisely what a polarizing work does.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    divisive: Divisive means driving people into opposing sides, and it stresses the damage: a divisive figure fractures the team, the family, the party. Polarizing stresses the shape of opinion instead — two committed camps and an empty middle — and a polarizing work can be a masterpiece; the word passes no verdict on quality.

    contentious: Contentious means provoking heated argument — a contentious issue gets fought over, loudly and at length. Polarizing describes the positions rather than the fighting: people split into two far-apart camps, and they may do it quietly. A question can be contentious without splitting cleanly in two, and polarizing without a single shouted word.

    controversial: Controversial is the broad umbrella for anything sparking strong public disagreement — and something can be controversial while most people land on the same side of it. Polarizing is stricter: opinion must split into two opposed camps with almost no one left lukewarm in the middle.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unifying: bringing people together

    uncontroversial: provoking no disagreement at all

    Where it sits · how much conflict it stirs up

    unpopular → polarizing → inflammatory

    unpopular vs. polarizing: An unpopular proposal is one most people dislike together; a polarizing one splits them into fierce, opposing camps.

    inflammatory vs. polarizing: An inflammatory remark goes beyond polarizing: it is designed not merely to divide an audience but to enrage it.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      Journalistic

      Polarizing almost always appears as an adjective in front of words like figure, issue, decision, and candidate. It describes the shape of the reactions, not the quality of the thing: a polarizing film is not necessarily a bad film — it simply leaves no one lukewarm.
