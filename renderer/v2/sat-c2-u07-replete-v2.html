Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Replete means full, abundant.

See the word in context

At low tide the pools at Cutler Point are replete with hermit crabs — three to a stone, brawling over shells.

Great-aunt Lil's attic turned out to be replete with her unfinished projects: quilts folded into cedar chests, coffee cans of buttons sorted by shade, four dresses still pinned to their tissue paper. We went up for one lamp and came down two hours later, carrying inheritance in armfuls.

Replete is the right word because the attic wasn't merely occupied — it was richly, thoroughly stocked, supplied past any need. Full reports a level, the way a gas gauge does; replete reports wealth of contents, and it usually travels with with: replete with quilts, with buttons, with the makings of a hundred afternoons.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    brimming: Brimming means filled to the very lip of its container, about to spill over — a mug, a stadium, eyes brimming with tears. Replete never sits at a brink; it says richly, thoroughly supplied, often far past need, and it almost always travels with 'with': replete with quilts, with trophies, with proof.

    teeming: Teeming means swarming with living, moving plenty — a pond teems with tadpoles, a station teems with commuters. Replete cares nothing for motion: a silent archive can be replete with letters. If the plenty crawls and jostles, the scene is teeming; if it is simply richly stocked, it is replete.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    devoid: holding none at all

    lacking: short of what is needed

    Where it sits · how far past need the supply goes

    stocked → replete → bursting

    stocked vs. replete: A stocked pantry has what the week requires; a replete one is supplied past any counting of the week's needs.

    bursting vs. replete: Bursting outdoes replete — the seams themselves complain; nothing more will go in.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Replete is the connoisseur's 'full': richly stocked, and nearly always followed by with. Copious, its unit-mate, counts what pours forth (copious tears, copious notes); replete describes the vessel those things fill — a memoir replete with escapes, a shelf replete with trophies. If your sentence has a container and a 'with,' replete is likely your word.
