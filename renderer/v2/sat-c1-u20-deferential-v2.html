deferential

/def-uh-REN-shul/

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Deferential means showing respect for another person's authority, rank, or greater experience.

See the word in context

The young lawyer was deferential toward the retired judge, standing when she entered and weighing her every suggestion with care.

On his first day in the restaurant kitchen, the culinary school graduate arrived with plenty of his own ideas about the menu. Still, when the head chef of thirty years demonstrated her way of preparing the sauces, he watched closely, asked permission before suggesting changes, and addressed her as 'Chef' at every turn. His deferential manner made clear that he recognized her decades of experience outranked his fresh diploma.

The graduate showed respect specifically for the head chef's authority and greater experience, which is exactly what deferential describes.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    respectful: Respectful means showing due regard for another person - any person, in any direction: adults can be respectful to children, rivals to each other. Deferential is respect with a direction built in: it flows upward, acknowledging that the other person's rank or experience outranks yours. You can be respectful to an equal; you defer to a superior.

    reverent: Reverent means showing deep, awed respect - the hushed manner reserved for sacred places, solemn ceremonies, and heroes. Deferential is more workaday: it yields to a person's higher rank or seniority in ordinary dealings, standing when the judge enters or letting the veteran speak first. Reverence borders on worship; deference is a professional bow.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    insolent: rudely disrespectful toward authority

    defiant: openly resisting authority

    Where it sits · how deeply one bows to rank

    polite → deferential → servile

    polite vs. deferential: Polite behavior shows ordinary good manners to anyone at all, while deferential behavior specifically acknowledges another person's higher rank or greater experience.

    servile vs. deferential: A deferential junior still keeps her own judgment while honoring a superior's, but a servile one surrenders it entirely, obeying every instruction without question.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal register

      Deferential usually appears in the patterns 'deferential to' or 'deferential toward' plus the person being honored (deferential to the committee chair). It is stronger and more specific than polite: deferential behavior acknowledges that the other person holds higher standing, rank, or experience, not merely that they deserve courtesy.
