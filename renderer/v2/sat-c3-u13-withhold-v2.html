withhold

/with-HOHLD/ - ends like 'gold'

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Withhold means to hold back something that is yours to give — payment, permission, information — and decline, for now, to hand it over.

See the word in context

The magazine withheld the winner's name until the ceremony, though three editors had known it for weeks.

The inspector withheld her signature on the elevator permit — one bolt grade didn't match the drawings — and the building sat finished but unopenable until the contractor produced the paperwork.

Withholding is refusal with a hand still on the door: the signature exists, the inspector may yet give it, and everyone knows what would release it. That expectation is the word's engine — you can only withhold what someone is waiting to receive.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    retain: to keep what you already have — neutral, and no one need be waiting for it. Withhold sharpens the scene: someone expects or is owed the thing, and you are declining to hand it over.

    suppress: to hold something down so it never surfaces at all, often by force. Withhold is quieter and more provisional — the thing exists, someone waits for it, and it simply has not been given yet.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    surrender: to hand something over, willingly or not

    disclose: to bring held-back information into the open

    Where it sits · how final the refusal to hand something over is

    delay → withhold → deny

    delay vs. withhold: Delay is milder than withhold: a delayed payment is merely late and still on its way, while a withheld one is being deliberately kept back until the holder chooses to release it.

    deny vs. withhold: Deny is stronger than withhold: withholding keeps the thing back for now, with the door still ajar, while denying shuts it — the request is refused outright.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / transactional

    Withhold wants three things in the frame: a holder, a thing that is theirs to give, and someone waiting for it. Taxes are withheld from paychecks, consent is withheld, names are withheld from reports. It is not a verb of muscle — guards restrain a crowd, walls withstand storms — and it is not mere possession: a museum keeps its meteorite; nobody says it withholds it unless someone is asking.

    The community center's new wing is nearly done, but the builder and the bank are locked in a quiet standoff. Citing a leaky roof seam, the bank has chosen to withhold the final payment -- the money is approved, set aside, and sitting in an account. The builder calls the sum his by right and points at the finished walls; the bank agrees he will get it, just not yet. Nothing about the standoff is hidden: both sides state their positions in polite letters, copied to the center's board. The day the seam passes inspection, the transfer goes through -- which is exactly what makes the holdback a lever rather than a punishment.
