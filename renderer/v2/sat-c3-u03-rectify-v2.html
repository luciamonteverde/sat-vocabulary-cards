Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Rectify means to correct something that is wrong — to act on an error, a fault, or an unfair situation until it is genuinely set right.

See the word in context

When the utility admitted it had overbilled four hundred customers, it promised to rectify the mistake before the next statement went out — refunds first, apologies second.

The registrar discovered that a scanning glitch had shaved a decimal point off dozens of GPA records, and she spent the week rectifying every affected transcript before colleges began requesting them.

Rectify is weightier than fix because it treats the mistake as a wrong that must be genuinely set right — the registrar is not tinkering with a machine but restoring each record to what it should have said all along.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    correct: To correct is the everyday, all-purpose word for fixing anything from a typo to a bad habit. Rectify is its formal cousin: it is what officials, banks, and agencies do once a wrong has been identified and must be put right on the record.

    remedy: To remedy is to supply a cure for a bad situation — the word looks toward relief, easing the harm the problem causes. Rectify acts on the fault itself: you rectify the billing error; you remedy the hardship it created.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    worsen: to make a fault or a bad situation more serious instead of setting it right

    neglect: to leave a known problem sitting exactly as it is, unaddressed

    Where it sits · how completely the fault is put right

    patch → rectify → overhaul

    patch vs. rectify: Patching is weaker than rectifying: a patch covers the fault so things limp along, while rectifying digs the fault out and sets the thing fully right.

    overhaul vs. rectify: An overhaul goes further than rectifying: rectifying fixes the specific wrong that was found, while an overhaul tears the whole system down and rebuilds it, sound parts included.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral

      Rectify is the formal word for putting a wrong right — an error, an oversight, an injustice — and it promises the fix is real, not cosmetic. For everyday slips, fix or correct is plainer. Two look-alikes to keep straight: "rectitude", moral uprightness, is a cousin from the same Latin rectus, but it names a quality a person has, while rectify names an action you take; and ratify, to formally approve an agreement, involves no fixing at all.
