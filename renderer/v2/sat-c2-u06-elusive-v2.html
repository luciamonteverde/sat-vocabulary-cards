Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Elusive means hard to find, catch, or pin down.

See the word in context

A perfect pizza crust stayed elusive all winter, no matter how Ben timed the rise or juggled the oven racks.

The whine started the moment the lights went off. For an hour Jo stalked the bedroom with a rolled magazine - lamp on, silence; lamp off, the whine at her ear. The mosquito was maddeningly elusive: unmistakably there, and never once where she looked.

Elusive is exact because the mosquito really exists and actively escapes capture - that combination is the word's core. It is not imaginary and not merely uncommon; it is present, close, and forever slipping the grasp. Anything that behaves that way - a name, a melody, an agreement - earns the word.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    slippery: Slippery is the plain-talk version: the thing wriggles out of your grip just as you close it - an eel, an excuse, a definition. Elusive covers the same escape in more formal dress and stretches further, to anything that stays beyond reach: a cure, a culprit, a full night's sleep.

    shadowy: Shadowy means glimpsed only at the edges and never seen plainly - a shadowy figure, a shadowy backer. Elusive is about escape, not dimness: an elusive thing can be vivid and unmistakable yet never where you reach. Shadowy blurs the view; elusive defeats the grab.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    obvious: sitting in plain sight, impossible to miss

    attainable: reachable with everyday effort

    Where it sits · how completely it escapes pursuit

    shy → elusive → untraceable

    shy vs. elusive: A shy cat merely hangs back and can be coaxed out; elusive goes further - the thing actively slips every attempt to catch it.

    untraceable vs. elusive: Untraceable is the extreme: an elusive signal keeps slipping your instruments, but an untraceable one leaves nothing behind to follow at all.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / literary

    Elusive is for things with real existence that keep escaping - an animal, a word, an agreement, sleep. Nebulous, its neighbor in this unit, means the thing never had an outline to begin with. If you could catch it if only it held still, it is elusive; if there is nothing solid to catch, it is nebulous.
