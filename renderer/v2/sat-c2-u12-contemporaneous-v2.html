What It Means

Contemporaneous means existing or occurring during the same period of time.

See the word in context

The two grandmothers were contemporaneous, born the same spring on opposite sides of the world, though their paths never crossed.

Pub-quiz history runs on the shock of the contemporaneous: the last woolly mammoths were still grazing an Arctic island while the pyramids were rising in Egypt, and Oxford dons were lecturing two hundred years before the Aztec capital was founded. Nothing connects these pairs except the calendar — which is precisely the surprise.

Contemporaneous is the exact word because it names things existing or occurring during the same period of time — the same slice of the calendar, whether or not they ever touched. It makes no claim of connection, influence, or cause: two contemporaneous events may be tightly linked or utterly unrelated. The word just sets two clocks side by side and notes that they agree.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    coexisting: Coexisting things share the world at the same time and usually stand side by side — two species in one pond, rival shops on one street. Contemporaneous is the cooler, historian's word: it says only that two things belong to the same period, whether or not they ever meet.

    concurrent: Concurrent means running at the same moment — two meetings held at once, prison sentences served side by side. It is tighter than contemporaneous, which asks only that two things share the same period, even a loose stretch of years.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    successive: coming one after the other

    staggered: pushed apart on purpose so they do not share the same time

    Where it sits · how tightly matched in time

    overlapping → contemporaneous → synchronized

    overlapping vs. contemporaneous: Overlapping claims only that two spans share some stretch; contemporaneous sets both things inside the same period outright.

    synchronized vs. contemporaneous: Synchronized goes past contemporaneous — not merely sharing an era, but matched beat for beat to the very same instant.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / scholarly

      Contemporaneous is the historian's and lawyer's word for things that share the same period. This unit's coincidental answers a different question — not when two things happened, but whether chance explains their pairing: contemporaneous events may be deeply linked; coincidental ones are not. For things striking at the very same instant, plainer phrases like 'at once' do the job.
