depravity

/dih-PRAV-ih-tee/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Depravity means a state of deep moral corruption or wickedness in a person's underlying character.

See the word in context

The novelist reveals her villain's depravity slowly, one cold-blooded choice at a time, until readers grasp how rotten his character truly is.

In her lecture, the historian traced how court records portrayed the deposed king's depravity — his cruelty, his greed, and his utter indifference to his starving subjects.

The records describe the ruler's whole moral character as corrupt — a lasting inner state shown across many behaviors, not one isolated bad act — and that underlying corrupt quality is what depravity names.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    wickedness: Wickedness is deep moral badness wherever it shows — a wicked act, a wicked scheme, a wicked heart. Depravity pushes the idea inward and downward: it names a character so thoroughly corrupt that the badness is the person's settled state, not a quality of this or that deed.

    immorality: Immorality is the broad failure to follow moral principles, and it covers everything from cheating at cards to far worse. Depravity is immorality at the bottom of the scale and rooted in character — not a lapse or a bad patch but a nature corrupted through and through.

    degeneracy: Degeneracy tells a story of decline: something or someone that was better has slid downward into corruption. Depravity needs no such fall — it names the corrupt state itself, however it arose. A degenerate court was once noble; a depraved one may never have been anything else.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    virtue: moral goodness of character

    decency: honest, proper, considerate behavior

    Where it sits · how deep the badness runs in character

    misconduct → depravity → evil

    misconduct vs. depravity: Misconduct describes bad behavior in one setting — a broken rule, an abused position; depravity describes a character that is corrupt through and through.

    evil vs. depravity: Depravity names deep moral corruption inside a person; evil goes even further, suggesting active, deliberate malice toward others.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      Formal / literary & moral

      Depravity is a noun for a person's underlying corrupt moral nature — a lasting state, not a single act. Two adjectives taught in other lessons of this unit cover nearby ground: heinous describes how shockingly evil one particular act is, and nefarious describes a scheming, villainous person or plot. Reserve depravity for the rotten character underneath: a single terrible act might reveal a lifetime of depravity.
