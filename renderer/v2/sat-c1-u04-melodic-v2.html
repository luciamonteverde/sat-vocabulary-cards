melodic

/muh-LOD-ik/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Melodic means having a pleasant, tuneful quality, like music; characterized by a flowing melody.

See the word in context

Even the pigeons seemed to settle when the violinist began, her melodic phrases drifting across the plaza like a half-remembered song.

Kneading dough at dawn, Grandma Rosa hummed a low, melodic tune that rose and fell so smoothly the whole kitchen seemed to sway along.

The sentence describes an actual sound—a hummed tune with a smooth rise and fall—which is exactly what melodic means: pleasant and tuneful, like music. Notice that melodic describes sound quality itself, not writing or ideas.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    tuneful: Tuneful is the plainer, everyday word for a sound that carries a pleasing tune — a tuneful whistle, a tuneful jingle. Melodic is the slightly more formal term and points at the shape of the line, the smooth rise and fall of the melody. Reviews say melodic; a kid's happy humming is tuneful.

    musical: Musical is the broad word for anything involving or resembling music at all — musical talent, a musical family, a musical laugh. Melodic makes a narrower claim about the sound itself: it carries a flowing, pleasing tune. A child can be musical without singing a note; a sound is melodic only when it carries a melody.

    harmonious: Harmonious praises how separate notes or voices blend together at the same moment — a choir's chords, instruments in agreement. Melodic praises a single line's shape: the tune's own rise and fall. One voice alone can be melodic but never harmonious; a chord can be harmonious with no melody in it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    discordant: Discordant sounds clash unpleasantly instead of flowing like a tune.

    grating: A grating sound scrapes at the ears—the opposite of a smooth, pleasing melody.

    Where it sits · how tuneful and smooth the sound is

    pleasant → melodic → mellifluous

    pleasant vs. melodic: A sound can be merely pleasant without forming any real tune; melodic goes further, flowing like actual music.

    mellifluous vs. melodic: Mellifluous—literally 'flowing with honey'—describes sound so sweetly smooth it goes beyond ordinary melodic quality.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral, everyday

    Use melodic for actual sound: voices, birdsong, instruments, ringtones. It fits reviews, everyday talk, and descriptive writing alike. Be careful not to borrow it for writing style—prose that flows beautifully is lyrical, not melodic.
