nullified

/NUL-uh-fyd/ - the stress falls on NUL

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Nullified means made officially void; deprived of legal force.

See the word in context

The waiver Marcus signed at the trampoline park was nullified the day a judge confirmed he had been fifteen when he signed it.

The parking amnesty lasted nine days: the state's high court ruled the mayor had no power to forgive state fines, and every ticket he had torn up was reinstated, his order nullified from the moment of signing.

Nullified is force switched off: the paper survives, the signatures remain, but in law the thing no longer counts. Rulings, contracts, patents, results — anything whose power comes from law can be nullified.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    voided: Voided is the plain, everyday legal stamp: no-longer-counts. Nullified means the same deadness with a more formal, weightier ring — a court nullifies a patent; a cashier voids a receipt. Reach for nullified when real legal force is at stake, voided for routine paperwork.

    overturned: Overturned means reversed by a higher authority on review — and usually something new stands in the old ruling's place. Nullified merely switches the legal force off: nothing need replace the dead ruling, which often simply stops counting from the moment it was signed.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    upheld: confirmed as valid and still binding

    enforced: given effect — made to actually happen

    Where it sits · how much of the ruling still stands

    challenged → nullified → erased

    challenged vs. nullified: Challenged is milder than nullified — a challenged ruling wobbles but still binds until someone with authority acts, while a nullified one has already lost its force.

    erased vs. nullified: Erased goes a step beyond nullified: a nullified ruling still sits in the file, stripped of force, while an erased one vanishes from the record entirely.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / legal

      Say nullified when something's legal force has been switched off — a ruling, patent, waiver, or result that no longer counts. Annul (this unit) is the act of erasing a thing as if it never was; abrogate (this unit) is authority killing a law or pact. Nullified reports the resulting deadness.
