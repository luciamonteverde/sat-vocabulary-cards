dissemination

/dih-sem-uh-NAY-shun/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Dissemination means the act of spreading information or ideas widely to many people.

See the word in context

After spotting the comet, the observatory turned at once to the dissemination of its findings: within hours, telegrams carried the coordinates to astronomers on three continents.

After the flood maps were redrawn, the county turned to the dissemination of the new evacuation routes, printing them in three languages and walking through them at every neighborhood meeting.

Dissemination is the organized, wide spreading of information to many people. The county is not moving objects around — it is pushing the new routes, as information, out through multiple channels until the whole community has them.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    circulation: Circulation is information moving from person to person — a rumor circulates, a memo makes the rounds. Dissemination is more organized and deliberate: someone actively pushes information out through multiple channels to reach many people at once. Circulation can just happen; dissemination is a planned effort.

    propagation: Propagation is the spreading of ideas or beliefs to an ever-wider audience, and it highlights the growth itself — the idea catching on. Dissemination centers on the sender's organized act of pushing information out widely. Ideas propagate almost on their own; findings are disseminated on purpose, through chosen channels.

    broadcasting: Broadcasting sends one message out to many receivers at once, in a single transmission — radio and television are the model. Dissemination is broader and more sustained: many channels, many audiences, over time. One broadcast can be a piece of a dissemination effort, but dissemination names the whole campaign.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    suppression: deliberately keeping information from spreading

    concealment: hiding information away from view

    Where it sits · how widely the message travels

    announcement → dissemination → saturation

    announcement vs. dissemination: An announcement shares news once, with whoever happens to be present; dissemination is a sustained effort to reach many audiences through many channels.

    saturation vs. dissemination: Saturation goes further than dissemination — it floods every available channel until no member of the audience could possibly miss the message.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Formal; at home in academic, governmental, and journalistic writing.

    Use dissemination for the organized, wide spreading of information or ideas — research findings, safety guidelines, news. It is a formal word: committees write 'dissemination plans,' and journals discuss the dissemination of results. Keep it for information; seeds gave the word its root, but objects, goods, and people are distributed or transported, not disseminated.
