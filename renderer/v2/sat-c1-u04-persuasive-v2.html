What It Means

Persuasive means able to win others over to believe or do something, as a well-crafted argument, speech, or appeal does.

See the word in context

Maya's persuasive presentation on cafeteria composting won over even the skeptical head custodian by the final slide.

The documentary about the town's overflowing landfill was so persuasive that, within a year, weekend recycling drop-offs had tripled.

The film actually changed what people did—tripling recycling—which is the test for persuasive: it convinces others to believe or act. A speech can be full of clever technique, but it only earns the word persuasive if it genuinely moves its audience.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    convincing: Convincing is what evidence and proof are when they remove doubt — a convincing alibi, convincing data. Persuasive belongs to appeals crafted to move people: speeches, ads, essays. The security footage is convincing though it argues nothing; the closing speech is persuasive because it was built to win you over.

    influential: Influential describes broad power to shape opinion over time — an influential critic, an influential study whose ideas spread for years. Persuasive works close up: one argument moving one audience toward one belief or decision. An influential thinker shapes a field; a persuasive presentation wins Tuesday's vote.

    effective: Effective says only that something achieves its purpose, whatever the purpose is — an effective medicine, an effective filing system. Persuasive names one specific effect: changing what people believe or do. A door lock can be effective; only something that moves minds is persuasive.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unconvincing: An unconvincing case leaves its audience exactly where it found them.

    feeble: too weak to sway anyone

    Where it sits · how strongly it moves the audience

    plausible → persuasive → compelling

    plausible vs. persuasive: A plausible claim merely seems believable; a persuasive one actually moves people to agree or act.

    compelling vs. persuasive: A compelling argument is stronger than a persuasive one — it grips its audience so firmly that resisting it feels nearly impossible.

      Word Choice

      neutral, everyday

      Persuasive is judged by results: a persuasive essay, ad, or friend actually moves people to agree or act. It pairs naturally with the arguments you write in school. Unlike rhetorical, which points at technique, persuasive points at effect—did any minds actually change?
