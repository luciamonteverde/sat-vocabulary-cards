Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Insinuate means to suggest something indirectly or subtly.

See the word in context

When Grandpa asked about the scratched fender, my sister glanced at my keys and said, 'Some people do take corners fast'—managing to insinuate everything while claiming nothing.

The neighbor never said Rita's prize pie was store-bought. He preferred to insinuate it: every time the trophy came up he wondered aloud 'how she finds the time for such professional crimping,' then let the sentence trail off while the listeners did his arithmetic.

Insinuate goes beyond 'imply' when the idea is slipped in slyly and points somewhere unflattering. Something implied can be innocent and open; an insinuation works its way in sideways, like the winding root the word is built on.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    suggest: Suggest is the plain, neutral verb for putting an idea forward without stating it outright, and it carries no verdict on the speaker. Insinuate is suggestion gone sly: the idea slips in sideways, it is usually unflattering, and the speaker keeps deniability. You suggest a restaurant; you insinuate a motive.

    allude: To allude is to touch on something indirectly, trusting the listener to recognize what is meant — a toast that alludes to a famous blunder without naming it. Alluding points at a thing both parties already know; you allude to it, never 'allude that' something is true. Insinuate plants a fresh and usually unflattering idea the listener never held before.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    declare: to state openly and plainly

    announce: to make known publicly

    Where it sits · how deliberately a harmful point is planted

    hint → imply → insinuate

    hint vs. insinuate: A hint merely gestures toward an idea and lets you notice it or not; insinuate plants a specific suggestion and makes sure it takes root.

    imply vs. insinuate: Imply is stronger than a hint but still neutral: a speaker implies a conclusion and trusts you to draw it, while insinuate goes further, slipping in a damaging one and keeping deniability.

      Word Choice

      neutral, often disapproving

      Insinuate is the verb for planting a suggestion slyly—usually an unflattering one—and innuendo, its partner in this unit, is the noun for the hint that gets planted. If the indirect meaning is innocent, imply is the fairer word; insinuate almost always accuses the speaker of a tactic.

      In Shakespeare's Othello, the villain Iago never once tells Othello that his wife is unfaithful. He does not need to. A pause here, a murmured 'I like not that' as another man walks away, a question asked and then withdrawn as too dangerous to finish. Each move lets Iago insinuate the betrayal without asserting it, so the jealous conclusion seems to be Othello's own discovery. Challenged, Iago can retreat instantly: he said nothing, he claimed nothing, he only wondered. Scholars have counted the scene's direct accusations. There are none -- and by its end, Othello is convinced.
