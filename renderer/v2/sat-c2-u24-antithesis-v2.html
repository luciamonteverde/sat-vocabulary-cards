Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

The antithesis of something is its absolute opposite — the thing that contradicts it point for point.

See the word in context

Everything about the new principal — soft-spoken, door open, first name on her badge — made her the antithesis of the man she replaced.

The architect's own cabin is the antithesis of her downtown towers: one room instead of sixty floors, hand tools instead of cranes, a wood stove instead of a climate system — as if every choice had been made by holding up the blueprints and doing the reverse.

Antithesis is the exact word because the opposition runs point for point: scale, method, and machinery are each inverted, not merely different. A cabin that was simply smaller would contrast with the towers; the antithesis is built by contradicting them item by item.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    polar opposite: Polar opposite is the everyday phrase for two things at opposite ends of one axis — hot and cold, north and south. Antithesis is its formal cousin and claims more: opposition in essence, point for point, the kind a writer builds an argument around.

    converse: The converse is a logical reversal of one particular statement or relation — flip the terms and read it back. Antithesis opposes something whole — a person, a style, an idea — not just a single proposition turned around.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    twin: an exact double, alike in every point

    equivalent: a different thing that amounts to the same

    Where it sits · how completely the two things oppose each other

    contrast → opposite → antithesis

    contrast vs. antithesis: A contrast is far weaker than an antithesis: two things merely need to differ noticeably to contrast, while an antithesis contradicts the other thing point for point.

    opposite vs. antithesis: Opposite comes close but stops short of antithesis: opposites disagree on the main axis, while calling something the antithesis declares total, emphatic opposition — nothing shared but the axis itself.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / emphatic

      Antithesis is the strongest opposition English offers — save it for point-for-point contradiction, and let mere difference stay 'contrast.' In rhetoric, the same word names the device of setting opposites side by side ('ask not what your country can do for you...'), a sense worth recognizing in passages about speeches. It pairs naturally with 'very': the very antithesis of glamour.

      Chess writers of the 1970s never tired of pairing Elena Roth and Mikhail Sarnov, and not merely because they were rivals for the same title. It was that each seemed built to refute the other. Roth played fast and attacked from the first move; Sarnov thought for ten minutes and defended. She trusted instinct and skipped study; he memorized whole libraries of openings. Even away from the board the pattern held — she gave interviews in three languages, he issued one written statement a year. One columnist finally reached for the strongest word available: Sarnov was not merely Roth's rival or her foil, but her antithesis — her contradiction point for point, at the board and away from it. When the two finally met in Vienna, the game was a draw. Some differences, the columnist wrote, are too complete for either side to win.
