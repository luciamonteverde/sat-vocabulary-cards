deprecate

/DEP-rih-kayt/ - the stress falls on DEP

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Deprecate means to belittle something or speak of it as being of little worth.

See the word in context

Collectors deprecate the factory's late-period vases so reliably at auction that bargains are still to be had.

Ask Grandma Lo about the quilt on the guest bed and she will deprecate it before you finish the question: scraps, she says, sewn crooked in front of the television, a way of using up her sister's old aprons. The county museum has asked to borrow that "way of using up aprons" for six years running. She always says no, and always adds that they must be short of real quilts over there.

Deprecate is the exact word for her habit, because it means to belittle something or speak of it as being of little worth — and the judgment lands on worth, often gently, even fondly. Denigrate needs a victim and a smear; deprecate can be aimed at your own handiwork over coffee.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    talk down: To talk something down is to run its worth down out loud, remark by remark, as you go. Deprecate is the settled judgment behind such talk — speaking of a thing as being of little worth, often mildly, even fondly, and famously about one's own work.

    downplay: To downplay something is to make it sound less important or less serious than it is — often to calm nerves or dodge attention. Deprecate passes judgment on worth itself, filing a thing's value down a notch. You downplay a risk; you deprecate a gift.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    talk up: pump its worth up aloud

    glorify: dress it in more praise than it earned

    Where it sits · how much worth is still granted

    undersell → deprecate → write off

    undersell vs. deprecate: Undersell merely trims — the thing sounds one size smaller than it is — while deprecate passes real judgment, speaking of the thing as worth little.

    write off vs. deprecate: Writing something off goes further than deprecating it — deprecation grants a little worth grudgingly; a write-off grants none and stops looking.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / often self-directed, mild

    Deprecate is the mildest slighter in this unit, and the only one you can politely aim at yourself — self-deprecating is its most common outfit. Denigrate is unfair and personal, deride laughs out loud, demean lowers a person's standing; deprecate just files a thing's worth down a notch. (Accountants mean depreciate, a different word, when value falls on a schedule.)

    Regulars at the bistro knew the ritual: praise the trout, and chef Amara would wave it off. 'A fisherman's leftovers,' she would say, 'a Tuesday dish.' She liked to deprecate the plate that had made her name, and she did it with a smile. Food writers took the modesty at face value until one noticed the truth in the kitchen: the trout was the only dish she never let anyone else cook. The verdict she spoke and the verdict she practiced were opposites. Her staff understood. Calling your best work small is sometimes how you keep it yours.
