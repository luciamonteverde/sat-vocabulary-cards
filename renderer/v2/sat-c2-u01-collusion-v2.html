collusion

/kuh-LOO-zhuhn/ - rhymes with confusion

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Collusion means a secret agreement to act together dishonestly.

See the word in context

Skaters began whispering about collusion when two judges' scores matched to the decimal, event after event, all season long.

At the regional esports final, two supposedly rival teams played out a semifinal they had privately scripted weeks earlier, having agreed to split the prize money no matter who advanced. When the chat logs surfaced, the league voided the match and named the offense: collusion.

Collusion is sharper than 'cheating' because it names a secret, dishonest partnership—usually between parties who are supposed to be competing—so the deceit lies in the cooperation itself.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    connivance: Connivance is quietly letting wrongdoing happen — the guard who sees the theft and looks the other way. Collusion is more active: the parties secretly agree and work the dishonest scheme together. A conniver permits what others do; collusion means doing it as partners.

    complicity: Complicity is being involved in someone else's wrongdoing — by helping, by knowing, or by staying silent — and it requires no agreement. Collusion needs more: a secret pact, usually between supposed rivals, to act together dishonestly. One person can be complicit alone; collusion takes coordinated partners.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    competition: genuine rivalry, with no secret coordination

    openness: conducting dealings in public view

    Where it sits · how dishonest the shared effort is

    teamwork → collusion → conspiracy

    teamwork vs. collusion: Teamwork is open, honest coordination; collusion borrows the cooperation but hides it and points it at something dishonest.

    conspiracy vs. collusion: Conspiracy goes beyond collusion: colluding firms secretly rig a market they still operate in, while conspirators plot an entire scheme—often a crime—from start to finish.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / legal

      Collusion belongs to news reports, courtrooms, and rulebooks: it accuses supposed rivals of secretly working as one. For an open joint effort, say cooperation or teamwork; for a full secret plot, conspiracy. Collusion pairs naturally with words like 'evidence,' 'investigation,' and 'between.'
