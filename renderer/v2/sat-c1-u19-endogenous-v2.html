endogenous

/en-DOJ-uh-nus/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Endogenous means caused by factors from within a system rather than from outside it.

See the word in context

Biologists concluded the fishery's crash was endogenous, driven by the population's own overcrowding rather than by anything in the water flowing in.

A once-beloved restaurant began losing customers, and the owners first blamed a new food hall across town. An outside consultant found something different: the kitchen had quietly cut portions, veteran servers had left after scheduling disputes, and reviews had complained about cold food long before the food hall ever opened. The decline was endogenous, growing from problems inside the restaurant itself rather than from any rival outside it.

The restaurant's troubles arose from within its own operation, not from an outside competitor, which is what endogenous means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    self-generated: Self-generated means produced by the thing itself — self-generated momentum, self-generated funding. Endogenous is the scholarly cousin that analysts use for causes arising inside the system under study, especially when weighing them against outside explanations. A team's energy is self-generated; an economic slowdown is endogenous.

    homegrown: Homegrown means developed from within, and it is informal with a warm, local flavor — homegrown talent, a homegrown solution. Endogenous is the technical term for the same inward origin, used when scientists or economists must classify a cause precisely. A festival celebrates homegrown music; a report labels a crisis endogenous.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    exogenous: caused by factors from outside a system

    external: coming from the outside

    Where it sits · how deeply it springs from within

    internal → endogenous → innate

    internal vs. endogenous: Internal simply locates something inside, while endogenous makes a stronger claim: that a change was actually generated by the system's own workings.

    innate vs. endogenous: An endogenous problem develops from within a system over time, but an innate trait is stronger still, built into a thing's very nature from the start.

    Apply

    Word Choice

    formal, academic/scientific register

    Endogenous labels causes that originate inside the system under study (an endogenous hormone, an endogenous slowdown). Its direct antonym is exogenous, caused from outside; when analysts debate whether a problem is endogenous or exogenous, they are asking whether it was homegrown or imported.

The distinction mattered, the report argued, since a wound from outside might heal on its own, but a system that manufactures its own deficit will run one in good times and bad alike.
