coincide

/koh-in-SIDE/ - the stress falls on SIDE

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Coincide means to happen at the same time, or to occupy exactly the same place or position — two separate things falling together.

See the word in context

Her flight home happened to coincide with the lantern festival, so the taxi crawled the last mile through drummers and drifting confetti.

The two research teams had argued for years about the eruption's date until the evidence was laid side by side: the dead ring in the oak cores coincided exactly with the ash layer in the lakebed mud — two records that had never met, pointing at the same terrible summer.

Neither record causes or copies the other; they simply land on the same point in time, and that falling-together of independent things is what coincide names. Dates, boundary lines, and opinions can all coincide — the word claims only that they match, not that anyone arranged the match.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    correspond: Correspond means to match point for point in some systematic pairing — answers correspond to questions, ribs to vertebrae. Coincide adds the fall of chance or timing: two separate things landing on the same spot or moment at once.

    converge: Converge means to move toward the same point from different directions — the roads, the search parties. Coincide says nothing about approach: the two things simply turn out to be at the same place or time.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    diverge: to split apart in different directions

    clash: to conflict so that both cannot hold at once

    Where it sits · how completely the two line up

    overlap → coincide → fuse

    overlap vs. coincide: Overlap is weaker than coincide: overlapping schedules share only a stretch of common ground, while schedules that coincide fall together completely, point for point.

    fuse vs. coincide: Fuse goes further than coincide: interests that coincide still belong to two separate parties who happen to line up, while interests that fuse have melted into a single cause that cannot be pulled apart.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / precise

      Coincide states a match and stays scrupulously silent about cause: when two openings coincide, nobody claims either was planned — for deliberate joint effort, say cooperate. And keep it clear of its sound-alike collide: trains whose schedules coincide share a timetable; trains that collide share a headline.

      In June 1858, the secretary of a London scientific society opened two envelopes in one morning's post. One held Charles Darwin's sketch of natural selection; the other, mailed from an island half a world away, held Alfred Russel Wallace's -- the same theory, worked out with no contact between the two men. The arrivals were said to coincide, and the word earned its keep: two entirely separate journeys of thought landing on the same desk at the same moment, unplanned. Neither man had raced the other; neither even knew a race existed. No committee had paired the papers, and no plan had steered them together. The society read both aloud on one evening that July, and the minutes record the astonishment better than any speech: they noted, twice, the date.
