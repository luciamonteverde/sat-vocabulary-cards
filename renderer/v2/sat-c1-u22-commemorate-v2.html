commemorate

/kuh-MEM-uh-rayt/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Commemorate means to honor and keep alive the memory of a past event or person — usually with a ceremony, an anniversary, or a lasting marker.

See the word in context

The library commemorates its founder each October by displaying her battered reading journal under glass.

Fifty years after the mine closed, Ash Hollow still commemorates the twelve men of the 1974 rescue. Every March the fire siren sounds once at 4:10 in the afternoon — the minute the last stretcher reached daylight — and school lets out early so children can lay pine boughs at the sealed entrance to the shaft.

Notice what the town is doing: a fixed date, a repeated ritual, a marked place. Those are acts built to keep a past event's memory alive, and that — not throwing a party — is what commemorate means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    honor: Honor means to show public, formal respect, and you can honor someone living, present, and sitting in the room. Commemorate is honor aimed at memory: it keeps a past event or person from being forgotten, usually on an anniversary or with a lasting marker.

    memorialize: Memorialize means to preserve a memory in permanent form — a statue, a plaque, a renamed bridge. Commemorate is broader: a single ceremony, a moment of silence, or an annual reading can commemorate what no permanent object marks.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    forget: to let a memory slip away entirely

    disregard: to treat as not worth attention

    Where it sits · how lasting and formal the tribute is

    acknowledge → commemorate → enshrine

    acknowledge vs. commemorate: Acknowledge is weaker than commemorate: acknowledging a past event just notes that it happened, while commemorating it builds ceremony and care around its memory.

    enshrine vs. commemorate: Enshrine goes further than commemorate: a commemorated event is remembered with ceremonies, but an enshrined one is fixed permanently in place — set apart as almost sacred.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    solemn, formal — it looks backward

    Commemorate points at the past: something is being remembered, usually on a schedule and often in public. That separates it from this unit's celebratory, which marks a joyful occasion as it happens — a team celebrates the championship on the night it is won and commemorates it at the ten-year reunion. Reach for memorialize only when the tribute takes permanent, physical form, and remember that what you commemorate need not be happy: wars, floods, and losses are commemorated as often as victories.
