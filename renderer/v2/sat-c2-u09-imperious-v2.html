Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Imperious means commanding and domineering; expecting to be obeyed.

See the word in context

Grandmother Petra ran Sunday dinner in an imperious style, seating guests with a pointed finger and waving off every offer of help.

The new head of the tasting panel never asked for the salt spoon; she extended a hand, palm up, and waited without looking to see who would fill it. Judges twice her age learned to fall silent when her pen lifted. Nothing she said was rude, exactly — it was the assumption underneath, that obedience would simply happen, that made the whole room call her imperious.

Imperious is the exact word because it names command worn as a birthright: domineering, and expecting to be obeyed before any order is even spoken. A strict judge enforces rules that exist; an imperious one behaves as if her preferences were the rules. The word carries an emperor inside it, and the manner does too.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    domineering: Domineering rule bullies its way to control — orders barked, objections flattened, the grip visibly maintained. Imperious rule skips the effort: it assumes control is already its due, and expects obedience before a single order has been spoken.

    overbearing: An overbearing presence presses its weight down on everyone nearby — interrupting, insisting, filling the room. Imperious adds a crown to the weight: not pressure but calm entitlement, the settled certainty that others will comply because compliance is owed.

    high-handed: High-handed describes what power does — deciding without asking, overriding whoever it inconveniences. Imperious describes the manner the power is worn with: command carried like a birthright, so certain of obedience that nothing needs announcing at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    humble: claims no special place

    meek: yields quietly rather than commands

    Where it sits · how heavily authority is pressed on others

    bossy → imperious → tyrannical

    bossy vs. imperious: A bossy cousin tells everyone where to stand; an imperious one expects the standing to happen unasked — command carried like a birthright.

    tyrannical vs. imperious: Tyrannical goes past imperious: the imperious chairman expects obedience, while the tyrannical one has the locks changed on whoever withholds it — manner hardened into rule by force.
