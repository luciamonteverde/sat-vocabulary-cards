Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Inviolable means never to be broken or dishonored.

See the word in context

In the lifeboat drills one rule was inviolable: crew boards last.

On the glacier, the rope team's law is inviolable: no one unclips, ever — not for a photograph, not for a dropped mitten, not for the last easy hundred meters. Guides who shrug at most regulations go quiet about this one; in eighty years of the hut's logbook, not one entry marks an exception.

Inviolable stacks a claim on top of strict. Breaking a strict rule costs you something; breaking an inviolable one is unthinkable — the rule carries a kind of holiness. Violare, 'to break, to profane,' hides in the middle: what is inviolable must never be profaned, whether it is a treaty, a confidence, or the rope.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sacred: Sacred means held holy — set apart for reverence, so that mistreating it feels like sacrilege. Inviolable borrows that holiness and lends it to rules, promises, and rights: the thing itself may be plain paper, but breaking it is desecration. Sacred names the reverence; inviolable names the ban on breaking.

    unbreakable: Unbreakable is the plain physical twin — the word for locks, vaults, and codes that force cannot crack. Inviolable does its work in the moral world of treaties, confidences, and oaths, where breaking is perfectly possible but utterly unthinkable. Call the padlock unbreakable; call the promise inviolable.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    negotiable: open to revision by agreement

    optional: yours to skip without a second thought

    Where it sits · how untouchable the rule stands

    respected → binding → inviolable

    respected vs. inviolable: A respected rule is honored by most people most of the time, and that is all it asks; inviolable stands far above it — a rule no one would even think to test.

    binding vs. inviolable: A binding rule must be obeyed while it stands, yet binding deals are renegotiated every season; inviolable is the far rung — breaking it is not renegotiation, it is desecration.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      solemn / formal

      Inviolable wraps a rule, promise, or right in something like holiness: breaking it is desecration, not infraction. Its unit-mate impregnable protects places from capture; inviolable protects covenants from betrayal. If exceptions get granted on busy days, the word is overclaiming.
