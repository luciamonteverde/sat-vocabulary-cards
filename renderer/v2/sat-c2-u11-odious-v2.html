odious

/OH-dee-uhs/ - the stress falls on OH

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Odious means instilling hatred or intense displeasure.

See the word in context

The bridge company doubled the toll again at harvest, making itself odious to every farmer in the valley.

The clause was buried on page nine: tenants who fell behind would owe, on top of everything else, the cost of their own eviction paperwork. Even the other landlords at the association meeting went quiet when it was read aloud. By spring, the odious 'paperwork clause' had become shorthand across the county for everything hateful about the housing market.

Odious is the exact word because it describes the thing that makes people hate it — the quality lives in the object. A clause, a tax, a practice, a villain: whatever earns loathing at first description is odious. Note the direction: malicious (this unit's cousin) judges the doer's intent; odious judges how the deed strikes everyone else.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    hateful: Hateful is the plain twin of odious — both name a thing that stirs up genuine hatred in the people who meet it. Odious carries a bookish, formal edge and attaches to laws, clauses, villains, and practices; hateful does the same work in everyday clothes.

    loathsome: Loathsome adds a shudder of physical disgust to the hatred — the loathsome thing repels the stomach as well as the judgment. Odious earns hatred without needing disgust: an odious clause or toll is despised, not gagged at. Both are strong; only loathsome is revolting.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    delightful: stirring pleasure instead

    endearing: winning affection at first meeting

    Where it sits · how violently it repels people

    disagreeable → odious → abominable

    disagreeable vs. odious: Disagreeable earns a frown and a complaint; odious earns real hatred.

    abominable vs. odious: Abominable goes past odious — the thing is beyond bearing, fit only to be stamped out.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Odious is strong: not merely unpleasant but hatred-earning, and a touch bookish — laws, clauses, villains, practices. For a person's harmful intent, this unit's malicious is the word; odious describes how the result strikes the rest of us. If the offense is only mildly off-putting, say that instead — calling a squeaky chair odious spends the word where it isn't owed.

    The Hartwell mill paid its weavers not in dollars but in scrip, paper good only at the company's own store. The store priced flour at twice what the grocer in town charged, and the ledger clerks knew it. Within a year the arrangement had become odious to every family on the mill rows; people said the word 'scrip' the way they said the name of a swindler. No one claimed the paper made them ill. They hated it on the merits: it took a fifth of every wage and called the taking a convenience. When the state finally outlawed the practice, the weavers burned their leftover scrip in the mill yard, and nobody mourned it.
