Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Frugal means careful and sparing with money — avoiding waste in one's spending and habits.

See the word in context

Two frugal habits — mending his own gear and buying seed in bulk — let the young farmer clear his loan a full year early.

The lighthouse keeper's logbooks read like a course in frugal living: wicks trimmed to burn longer, packing crates reborn as furniture, rainwater saved for wash days, and a coin jar that eventually paid for his daughter's telescope.

Frugal is praise, or at least neutral: it names care with money and a hatred of waste, not a refusal to spend. A frugal manager still buys good boots — once. When thrift curdles into hoarding, English switches words: that is parsimonious, this unit's harsher cousin.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    thrifty: Thrifty is the homey, everyday cousin of frugal: it praises a person who manages money cleverly — coupons clipped, bargains found, savings growing. Frugal runs deeper and wider, naming a whole sparing way of living that hates waste itself. A thrifty shopper hunts deals; a frugal farmer wastes nothing at all.

    sparing: Sparing means using little of something — money, salt, or praise — and it stops at the smallness of the amount. Frugal adds the judgment behind the smallness: care with money and a hatred of waste, spending readily where value lasts. Sparing measures quantity; frugal names the sensible habit.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    extravagant: spending freely and showily, beyond what sense allows

    wasteful: letting money or resources drain away for nothing

    Where it sits · how tightly the purse strings are held

    economical → frugal → miserly

    economical vs. frugal: Economical is milder than frugal — it praises efficient use of anything, fuel or words or funds, while frugal names a person's whole sparing way with money.

    miserly vs. frugal: Miserly goes far beyond frugal — the frugal farmer spends carefully and wastes nothing, while the miserly one hoards even what he needs.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / approving

    Frugal admires care with money — waste avoided, value kept. Parsimonious (also in this unit) is the insult on the same scale: spending withheld even where it's needed. Call the treasurer who ends the season under budget frugal; save parsimonious for the one who wouldn't buy pencils.

    My great-aunt Rosa ran her orchard house on habits the family still argues about. She was frugal in a way that had nothing to do with being poor: the harvest paid well, yet no jar, board, or bootlace left her service before its time. Bruised apples went to sauce, worn shirts went to quilts, and the woodstove burned prunings the neighbors would have hauled to the dump. When the roof needed slate, though, she paid for the best without blinking — waste offended her; sound spending never did. Visitors sometimes mistook the bare shelves for hardship. Rosa called it arithmetic: everything used fully, nothing bought twice.
