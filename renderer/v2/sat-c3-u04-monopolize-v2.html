monopolize

/muh-NOP-uh-lyz/ - the stress falls on NOP

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Monopolize means to take exclusive control of something and shut others out.

See the word in context

By buying every ice machine in the beach town, the vendor monopolized the cold-drink trade for the whole summer.

For a full semester, the robotics club monopolized the school's only 3-D printer, booking it under six different member names until the art class finally rebelled.

Monopolize goes beyond 'dominate' or 'lead': a dominant player still has rivals in the game, while monopolizing means the door is closed — the resource, the market, or the conversation belongs to one holder, and everyone else is shut out.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    corner: To corner a market is to grab a hard grip on one supply — often to move its price — while rivals still scramble at the edges. Monopolize goes further: exclusive control, the door closed, every rival shut out. Cornering squeezes a market; monopolizing owns it outright.

    hog: To hog is the informal jab — grabbing far more than your share of something, with no real control over it. Monopolize is the formal accusation of exclusivity: one holder controls the whole resource, and everyone else is shut out. A hog takes the most; a monopolist leaves none.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    share: to let others have part of a thing

    release: to loosen your grip and let a thing go

    Where it sits · how completely rivals are shut out

    lead → dominate → monopolize

    lead vs. monopolize: Leading a market is far milder than monopolizing it — you are out in front, but the race is still full of runners.

    dominate vs. monopolize: Dominating is weaker than monopolizing — a dominant firm towers over its rivals, while a monopolist has no rivals left to tower over.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / economic

      Monopolize is at home in economics — markets, supplies, licenses — but it travels well to dinner tables and group chats, anywhere one holder crowds everyone else out. Use 'hog' when you want the informal jab and 'dominate' when rivals still exist. The accusation inside monopolize is exclusivity: not just taking the most, but leaving none.

By spring, every slot on the sign-up sheet carried the same three initials. Students needing a single afternoon were told to try a rival university an hour away. Colleagues grumbled that he was not even the heaviest user in the building -- the imaging core downstairs ran more samples -- but volume was never the point. The point was the door he had quietly closed: no one else got in at all, and the dean finally wiped the sheet clean in May.
