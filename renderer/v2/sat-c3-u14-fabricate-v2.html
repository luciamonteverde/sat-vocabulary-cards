Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Fabricate means to invent something and present it as real.

See the word in context

The salesman fabricated a waiting list, quoting names and dates to buyers who never suspected the ledger page was empty.

To win the lighthouse posting, the applicant fabricated a seafaring past — a captain's license with a convincing number, and letters of praise from a shipping line that had never employed him.

Fabricate comes from the workshop: it means to build. A fabricated alibi, résumé, or record is manufactured — constructed piece by piece and presented as real. Concocting is quicker and looser; fabricating implies deliberate construction, often with props to show.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    invent: To invent is to create something new — a machine or a lie with equal ease, and the word itself passes no judgment. Fabricate narrows invention to deception: a fabricated record is built piece by piece and presented as real, and the accusation is always deliberate.

    forge: To forge is to fake a specific document or signature, producing a counterfeit passed off as genuine. Fabricate is wider — a whole alibi, career, or past can be fabricated — and it points at the deliberate construction, not the imitation of someone else's paperwork.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    come clean: admit what really happened

    attest: formally vouch that a thing is true

    Where it sits · how much of it never happened

    misstate → doctor → fabricate

    misstate vs. fabricate: Misstate is the mildest rung, far below fabricate — a misstated figure gets one thing wrong, sometimes not even on purpose.

    doctor vs. fabricate: Doctor stops short of fabricate: to doctor a report is to tamper with something real, while to fabricate is to build the entire thing from nothing.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      neutral / forensic

      Fabricate is the investigator's verb — evidence, records, and résumés get fabricated, and the word implies deliberate construction. Concoct, in this same unit, is its quicker, flimsier cousin, thrown together on the spot. For paperwork faked in someone else's name, forge is more exact.
