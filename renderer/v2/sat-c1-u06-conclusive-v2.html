What It Means

Conclusive means putting an end to doubt; proving something so firmly that no real dispute remains.

See the word in context

Fingerprint evidence from the window latch proved conclusive, and the suspect's lawyer quietly abandoned the alibi defense.

The laboratory's pigment analysis was conclusive: the blue paint contained a dye first manufactured in 1901, so the 'seventeenth-century' portrait had to be a forgery.

One hard fact — a dye that didn't exist until 1901 — settles the painting's age beyond argument. Evidence that ends the doubt like that is conclusive.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    decisive: Decisive means settling something quickly and firmly — a decisive victory, the deciding vote. It highlights the act or moment that settles an outcome. Conclusive belongs to evidence and proof: conclusive results end doubt about what is true. A general wins decisively; a lab report is conclusive.

    irrefutable: Irrefutable means impossible to argue against — no counterargument can even stand. It runs a shade stronger than conclusive: conclusive evidence ends the real dispute in practice, while irrefutable claims that no reply is possible at all. Call proof conclusive when it settles the case, irrefutable when nothing could answer it.

    undeniable: Undeniable means too plain to be denied — obvious on its face, like undeniable talent or undeniable charm. Conclusive is about proof doing work: evidence strong enough to end a dispute. Something can be undeniable without any evidence at all; only tested proof earns conclusive.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    inconclusive: leaving the matter unsettled

    ambiguous: open to more than one interpretation

    Where it sits · how completely doubt is shut down

    suggestive → conclusive → incontrovertible

    suggestive vs. conclusive: Suggestive evidence merely points in a direction; conclusive evidence settles the matter.

    incontrovertible vs. conclusive: Incontrovertible is even more absolute than conclusive — not just ending the dispute, but making disagreement logically impossible.

      Word Choice

      Formal / evidentiary

      Conclusive pairs naturally with evidence, proof, results, and tests. It's related to the noun conclusion (an ending, or a judgment you reach) — but the two aren't interchangeable: conclusive is an adjective describing something strong enough to end all doubt, while a conclusion is the thing you arrive at. Don't swap one for the other.
