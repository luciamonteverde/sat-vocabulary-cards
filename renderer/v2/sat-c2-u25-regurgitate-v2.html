What It Means

Regurgitate means to repeat information mechanically without real understanding.

See the word in context

When visitors asked follow-up questions, the new docent could only regurgitate the wall plaque, word for anxious word.

The sommelier course failed him anyway. He could regurgitate every region's rainfall, every vintage chart, every tasting note the manual printed — and one sip of an unlabeled red left him guessing wildly. The examiners had heard the manual before. They wanted to know what he could do when the page ran out.

Regurgitate is the exact word for his performance, because it means to repeat information mechanically without real understanding — swallowed whole, brought back up unchanged. Recite is the neutral cousin for from-memory delivery; regurgitate adds the sneer, the strong hint that nothing was digested on the way through.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    parrot: Parrot means to repeat by rote imitation, the way the bird gives back sounds it cannot mean — the everyday, slightly teasing image. Regurgitate is harsher and more clinical: information swallowed whole and brought back up unchanged, with the accusation that nothing was ever understood.

    spit back: Spit back is the blunt classroom phrase for handing an answer over exactly as it was given, no thought added. Regurgitate says the same thing in a formal, biting register — the word a critic or examiner uses when memorized material comes back with no understanding behind it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    digest: take ideas in until they become genuinely your own

    rethink: work the material through fresh

    Where it sits · how little understanding lies behind it

    quote → recite → regurgitate

    quote vs. regurgitate: Quote shows far more understanding than regurgitate ever implies — a line repeated on purpose, with credit, because the speaker knows exactly why it fits — while regurgitate hands words back with none.

    recite vs. regurgitate: Recite goes further than quote — whole passages delivered from memory — but it stops short of regurgitate, which adds the fatal ingredient: no understanding anywhere behind the words.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    informal / dismissive

    In classrooms and test prep, regurgitate is always an accusation: words given back exactly as swallowed, understanding nowhere in sight. Parrot is the gentler everyday image; recite is neutral from-memory delivery. Its respectable unit-mate recapitulate names a deliberate, organized walk back through the points — regurgitation is what recapitulation becomes when the mind checks out.
