impinge

/im-PINJ/ - rhymes with hinge

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impinge means to encroach or intrude on something.

See the word in context

Late-night messages from the office had begun to impinge on the one hour Vera kept for her cello.

Privacy researchers warned that the new toll scanners, which log every trip a driver makes, impinge on the freedom to move about unwatched — a boundary crossed quietly, one record at a time.

Impinge is not a synonym for annoy: it names pressure across a boundary — into someone's time, rights, or territory — and it keeps its preposition, impinging on whatever it crowds. Next to it, intrude is what a person does bodily; encroach creeps forward by degrees.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    encroach: To encroach is to advance gradually into territory that is not yours — a hedge creeping over a lot line, meetings inching into lunch. Impinge needs no slow creep: a pressure impinges the moment it crosses the boundary, and it always takes on.

    intrude: To intrude is to push in where you are not welcome, and a person can do it bodily — stepping into the room, cutting into a line. Impinge stays abstract: forces and pressures impinge on time, rights, or quiet, while the walking, breathing visitor intrudes.

    trespass: Trespass is the physical, legal cousin: to cross onto land or property unlawfully, a matter for signs and courts. Impinge crosses subtler lines — noise impinges on quiet, rules impinge on rights — where nothing is deeded and no law is broken.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    withdraw: to pull back out of the space instead of pressing into it

    respect: to honor a boundary and stay on your own side of it

    Where it sits · how far into another's space it goes

    brush → impinge → invade

    brush vs. impinge: To brush against something is the lightest passing contact — it stops well short of impinge, which presses into space that isn't yours.

    invade vs. impinge: Invade goes further than impinge: impinging crowds the edge of someone's territory, while invading storms in and claims it.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal

    Impinge is the formal word for pressure across a boundary, and it is locked to on: noise impinges on quiet, rules impinge on rights. A person barging in intrudes; a slow advance encroaches. If nothing is being crowded, you may just mean bother.
