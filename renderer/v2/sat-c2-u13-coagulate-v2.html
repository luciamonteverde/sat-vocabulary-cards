Meaning

What It Means

Coagulate means to thicken and set into a semi-solid mass.

See the word in context

Left open overnight, the pot of hide glue had coagulated into a rubbery mass no amount of stirring could loosen.

The food-science teacher does the whole lesson with one egg and a thermometer. At sixty-three degrees, she says, the white begins to coagulate — its proteins unfold, tangle, and set — while the yolk holds out until seventy. That seven-degree gap is the entire secret of the six-minute egg: firm white, molten center, and a cook who trusts the clock more than the spoon.

Coagulate is the exact word because it means to thicken and set into a semi-solid mass. It names a one-way change of state, liquid crossing over into something that holds its shape. Cooling is not required and neither is spoiling; what matters is the setting, the moment the liquid stops pouring and starts sitting.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    clot: Clot is the word blood itself takes — blood clots at a cut, and surgeons watch for clots. Coagulate covers the same setting in any liquid at all: egg white, latex, hide glue. Every clot has set this way, but a pot of glue coagulates and no one calls it a clot.

    set: Set is the kitchen's gentle word for the same change — custard sets, jelly sets, cement sets — and it sounds friendly at the table. Coagulate is the laboratory's and the clinic's term, precise about the mechanism and often carrying an unappetizing edge the cook would rather avoid.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    liquefy: to turn back into liquid

    thin: to loosen a liquid the other way — more pourable, not less

    Where it sits · how firm the mass becomes

    thicken → coagulate → solidify

    thicken vs. coagulate: To thicken is the first, mildest step on this scale — a sauce thickens and still pours — while to coagulate is to cross the line into a semi-solid mass that holds its shape.

    solidify vs. coagulate: Solidify goes past coagulate — coagulated blood is a soft, quivering mass; solidified wax is hard all the way through.

      Word Choice

      technical / scientific

      Coagulate belongs to labs, kitchens, and clinics — blood, proteins, latex, glue. For blood alone, clot is the plainer twin; for desserts, set sounds friendlier at the table. People and plans do not coagulate: when a crowd or a committee draws together, the word is coalesce, a different Latin verb about growing, not driving.
