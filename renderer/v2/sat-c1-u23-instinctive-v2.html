instinctive

/in-STINK-tiv/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Instinctive means arising automatically from instinct rather than from learning, reasoning, or experience.

See the word in context

The spiderling spun a flawless orb web on its first night alive, though it had never seen another spider's work.

A garden spider's egg sac hatches in a laboratory where no adult web has ever been built. The young are separated at hatching and raised alone in glass boxes with nothing to imitate. On its first night each one lays a bridge line, then a frame, then a spiral, then a hub, in the same order and the same proportions as wild adults. With no model to copy and no practice runs, the behaviour can only be instinctive.

Flawless webs on the first attempt, built by spiders raised in isolation with nothing to imitate, rules out learning entirely and leaves instinct, which is what instinctive means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    innate: Innate names a capacity carried in the makeup from birth -- an innate sense of direction -- and it may sit unused for years. Instinctive describes the behaviour itself, running off automatically without ever being learned. The capacity is innate; the performed act is instinctive.

    automatic: Automatic describes any action that runs without conscious thought, whether built in at birth or drilled in by years of practice -- a pianist's trained arpeggio is automatic. Instinctive is narrower: automatic because it was never learned at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    learned: acquired through teaching or experience

    deliberate: done after conscious thought and choice

    Where it sits · how little learning or choice is involved

    intuitive → instinctive → reflexive

    intuitive vs. instinctive: Intuitive knowledge feels immediate but can still be built out of long experience, while instinctive behaviour needs no experience at all.

    reflexive vs. instinctive: Reflexive is the most automatic word of the three, since a reflex fires in a fraction of a second with no possibility of choice, while an instinctive behaviour can be a long sequence of actions.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral; used in science and in everyday speech

    Instinctive describes one automatic behaviour pattern, not a way of life and not a physical condition. Keep it clear of two cousins in this unit: nomadic names a lifestyle built on moving rather than settling, and feral describes a domesticated animal now living wild, so an animal can be nomadic or feral without any particular behaviour being instinctive.

    Each autumn, monarch butterflies leave Canada and fly up to four thousand kilometres to a handful of mountain valleys in central Mexico. No monarch makes the trip twice. The butterflies that fly south are three or four generations removed from the ones that flew the year before. They cannot be following elders, and they cannot be remembering a route they have never seen. The navigation is instinctive: a program for reading the sun's angle that every monarch carries from the moment it leaves the chrysalis. Raised indoors away from any other butterfly, a monarch released in October still turns south-west, as surely as if it had made the journey a hundred times.
