pittance

/PIT-unss/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Pittance means a very small amount, especially of money — a sum so tiny it feels unfair or insulting.

See the word in context

Pittance means a very small amount, especially of money — an intern working full weeks for a pittance may find the monthly stipend gone the moment rent is due.

The magazine offered Lena a pittance for her two-thousand-word article — fifteen dollars, less than she had spent on lunch while researching it.

Fifteen dollars for weeks of research and writing is an insultingly small payment. Comparing the fee to the cost of a single lunch makes the tininess of the sum concrete — that is a pittance.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    peanuts: Peanuts is the informal, scornful word for an insultingly small payment — 'they work for peanuts.' It belongs to casual speech and always sounds dismissive. Pittance means the same unfairly small sum but fits formal writing: reporters and historians write that laborers earned a pittance, not peanuts.

    trifle: A trifle is a thing or matter of no importance — a knickknack, a small worry you wave away. Pittance is angrier and specifically about money: pay so small it shortchanges real work. You shrug off a trifle; you resent a pittance.

    song: A song, in the fixed phrase 'for a song,' means a laughably small price — a house bought or sold for a song. It is about bargain prices, not wages. A pittance is what someone is paid for work, and the word accuses the payer of unfairness.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    fortune: a very large amount of money

    windfall: a large, unexpected gain

    Where it sits · how severely the pay falls short

    stipend → pittance → nothing

    stipend vs. pittance: A stipend is small but fair — a fixed, modest payment; a pittance is smaller still, and calling pay a pittance means it insults the work done.

    nothing vs. pittance: A pittance at least exists; being paid nothing at all is the only step further down the scale.

    Apply

    Word Choice

    Disapproving

    Pittance almost always carries indignation about money: workers are 'paid a pittance,' heirlooms are 'sold for a pittance.' It implies the amount is unfairly small, not just small — you wouldn't call a reasonable discount a pittance.

For years the pay was a pittance — a few cents a bushel, so little that a full week of climbing barely covered a picker's bus fare and boots. Then a picker taped her pay slip to the co-op notice board, next to the price of a single apple downtown, and people did the math themselves. The next season, the orchard doubled the rate. The fruit cost pennies more; the ladders felt the same, but the paychecks finally answered the work.
