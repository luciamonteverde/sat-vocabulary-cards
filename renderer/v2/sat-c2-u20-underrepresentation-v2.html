Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Underrepresentation means the state of having fewer members or less presence than a fair share.

See the word in context

The atlas's underrepresentation of the southern hemisphere was literal: forty pages north of the equator, nine below it.

When the transit authority mailed its rider survey, it heard back mostly from people with desks: nine-to-fivers, downtown commuters, laptop bags. The underrepresentation was stark: night cleaners, hospital aides, and kitchen crews — a third of actual riders by fare data — filled out four percent of the forms. So the board redesigned the routes around the answers it had, cut the 2 a.m. loop, and spent a year wondering why ridership fell.

Underrepresentation is the exact word for that gap, because underrepresentation means having fewer members or less presence than a fair share — the group exists at full strength out in the world and shows up at quarter strength in the room, the record, or the count. Exclusion bars the door outright; underrepresentation leaves the door open and somehow still ends up with an empty chair.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    shortfall: A shortfall is any gap between what should be there and what is — budgets, harvests, and donations all have shortfalls. Underrepresentation is a shortfall specifically of presence: a group holding fewer seats, pages, or voices than its real share. Every underrepresentation is a shortfall of one particular kind.

    undercount: An undercount is a tally that misses part of the group — a counting error, a survey that never reached them. Underrepresentation can exist with a perfect count: everyone tallied accurately, and the group still holding less presence than its share. An undercount hides people; underrepresentation seats too few of them.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    overrepresentation: the same unfairness pointed the other way

    equal footing: presence matching the group's real share

    Where it sits · how fully the group is shut out

    imbalance → underrepresentation → exclusion

    imbalance vs. underrepresentation: An imbalance is milder than underrepresentation — any tilt in the numbers, often accidental and small; underrepresentation is a tilt deep enough to cost a group its fair share of presence.

    exclusion vs. underrepresentation: Exclusion goes further than underrepresentation — underrepresentation leaves a group thin in the room; exclusion leaves the group outside the door entirely.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / policy

      Underrepresentation is committee-report language for a measurable unfairness of presence. A shortfall is any gap; exclusion is the door shut outright. Use underrepresentation when the group is present, counted — and visibly short of its share.
