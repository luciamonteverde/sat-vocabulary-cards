impassive

/im-PASS-iv/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impassive means showing no emotion; calm and unmoved, especially under stress or provocation.

See the word in context

The bomb-disposal technician stayed impassive as the timer display flickered, her face betraying nothing.

As the inquiry's findings were read, cameras zoomed in on the accused engineer. Reporters watched for a flinch, a swallow, anything at all. Through forty minutes of findings — some devastating, some clearing her completely — her expression never changed once. Colleagues later admitted they could not tell whether she had won or lost until her lawyer smiled. Her face, one observer recalled, was completely impassive.

Her expression stays unreadable through both devastating and favorable findings — that unmoved, emotionless exterior under intense pressure is precisely what impassive means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    expressionless: Expressionless means showing no feeling on the face at a given moment, whatever the cause — boredom, fatigue, or a daydream can all blank a face. Impassive is blankness that means something: staying calm and unmoved under stress or provocation that would crack most faces.

    unmoved: Unmoved reports the inner state: not stirred emotionally by what is happening. Impassive reports the outer one — the face and manner show no emotion, whatever storms inside. A judge may be deeply moved and still look impassive; someone truly unmoved has nothing to hide.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    expressive: openly showing feeling

    agitated: visibly upset and unsettled

    Where it sits · how completely emotion is hidden

    composed → impassive → stony

    composed vs. impassive: Composed is weaker than impassive: a composed person stays in control but may still show a tight smile or a worried glance, while an impassive one shows nothing at all.

    stony vs. impassive: Stony is stronger than impassive — a stony face suggests cold, almost hostile blankness, while impassive simply means unreadable and unmoved.

    Word Choice

    neutral to formal; often admiring under pressure

    Impassive describes an unreadable, unemotional exterior, especially under provocation — which sets it apart from this unit's pensive (wistfully thoughtful) and contemplative (calmly, deeply reflective): the pensive and contemplative person is visibly lost in thought, while the impassive one shows you nothing whatsoever.
