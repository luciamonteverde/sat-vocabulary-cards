revoke

/rih-VOHK/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Revoke means to officially cancel a right, privilege, or official document, often as a consequence or punishment.

See the word in context

After the third safety violation in one month, the city moved to revoke the factory's operating permit.

The state medical board gave Dr. Hale two written warnings about falsifying patient records. When an audit uncovered a third violation, the board finally acted: it voted to revoke his medical license. The decision was a direct consequence of his misconduct, and it took away the one specific privilege his career depended on. From that day forward, he could no longer legally practice medicine anywhere in the state.

The board canceled a specific professional privilege as a punishment for wrongdoing, which is precisely what revoke means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    withdraw: Withdraw means to take back something that was granted or put forward — a company withdraws a job offer, a diplomat withdraws a proposal — and it implies no wrongdoing. Revoke is punitive and official: an authority cancels a specific right, privilege, or document as a consequence of misconduct. Anyone can withdraw for any reason; revoking answers a violation.

    cancel: Cancel is the all-purpose word for officially calling off or ending something — a meeting, a subscription, an order. Revoke is narrower and harsher: an authority takes back a granted right, privilege, or official document, usually as punishment. Anything can be canceled; only something granted can be revoked, and usually for cause.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    reinstate: to restore something that was taken away

    grant: to officially give a right or privilege

    Where it sits · how much is permanently taken away

    suspend → revoke → strip

    suspend vs. revoke: Suspend is weaker than revoke: a suspended license is only paused and can come back, while a revoked license is canceled outright.

    strip vs. revoke: Strip is stronger than revoke: revoking takes back one specific privilege, while stripping someone of their rights takes away nearly everything at once.

    Word Choice

    formal, official register

    Revoke is punitive and specific: an authority takes back a particular right, privilege, or document, usually as a consequence of wrongdoing, as in revoking a license or a visa. Compare rescind, which is a deliberate, formal cancellation of a law, order, or agreement by a governing body rather than a punishment aimed at one holder's privilege.
