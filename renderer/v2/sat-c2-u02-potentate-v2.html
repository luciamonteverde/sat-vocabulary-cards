potentate

/POH-tuhn-tayt/ - the stress falls on POH

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Potentate means one who has great power; a ruler.

See the word in context

No festival in the port began until the potentate rose from his carved chair and clapped twice.

The opera's second act opens in the court of a potentate: petitioners kneel with their cases, ministers wait upon his nod, and when he lifts one finger, a treaty that two kingdoms spent a year drafting becomes law by dinner.

Potentate outranks 'leader' and even 'ruler' in flavor: it names one who holds great personal power — power gathered into a single pair of hands, usually a throne's — and it carries a whiff of the absolute, even the theatrical.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    monarch: A monarch is anyone who wears a crown — king, queen, emperor — whether they rule absolutely or only cut ribbons while a parliament governs. Potentate insists on the power itself: real command gathered into one pair of hands. A ceremonial king is still a monarch, but never a potentate.

    strongman: A strongman rules through force — the army behind him, rivals jailed — and the word is modern: his power is usually seized, not inherited. Potentate is older-flavored and grander, naming great personal power worn with a throne's theatrical authority. A strongman grabs power; a potentate sits enthroned in it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    subject: one who lives under a ruler's power

    commoner: a person of no rank at all

    Where it sits · how far the ruler's command reaches

    chieftain → potentate → emperor

    chieftain vs. potentate: A chieftain commands a single clan or valley; a potentate rules a whole realm, with the machinery of a court behind his word.

    emperor vs. potentate: Emperor goes beyond potentate in reach: a potentate is supreme within his realm, while an emperor stacks realm upon realm — kings themselves may kneel in his hall.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / historical

      Potentate suits rulers of great personal power, especially in historical or faraway courts — and, half-jokingly, any executive who behaves like one. Its unit-mate sovereign is the adjective for the authority itself; its opposite number figurehead names a throne emptied of power. For a crowned ruler generally, monarch is the plainer word.

      The trade delegation had been told the Khan of Aral was a minor official who could be hurried. They understood their error at the first audience. The Khan was a potentate in the full sense: every caravan tax, every garrison, and every judge's verdict in the desert kingdom moved at his word. The envoys watched a line of petitioners kneel on the carpeted steps while scribes recorded each decree; nothing in the realm was too small for his seal. No treaty clause could be agreed, the delegation wrote home, until the man on the dais nodded -- and no minister dared guess when that would be. The trade agreement took three years. It was signed the week the Khan chose, on terms the Khan named, in ink the Khan approved.
