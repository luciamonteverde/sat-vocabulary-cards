What It Means

Aggrieved means distressed, wronged, injured.

See the word in context

Aggrieved season-ticket holders demanded refunds when the club moved its biggest home game to a stadium four hundred miles away.

The inventor left the awards banquet aggrieved: the machine on the stage was her design, patented under her employer's name while she was told the project had been scrapped. She did not shout; she hired a lawyer.

Aggrieved is more precise than 'upset' or 'angry' because it always contains a claim of injustice — someone did you wrong, and the hurt comes wrapped around that fact. Courts still call the injured party 'the aggrieved.'

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    wronged: Wronged states the plain fact that an injustice was done to you — cheated, robbed, betrayed. Aggrieved names what that fact leaves behind: the wounded, brooding sense of unfair treatment a person carries afterward. You are wronged the day it happens; you stay aggrieved long after.

    offended: Offended means your dignity has been hurt by an insult or a slight, with no real injury required. Aggrieved is heavier: it claims a genuine wrong was actually done to you. A snub at the door leaves you offended; a stolen patent leaves you aggrieved.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    content: at peace with how life is treating you

    satisfied: feeling fairly dealt with, nothing owed

    Where it sits · how long the hurt lasts and hardens

    miffed → aggrieved → embittered

    miffed vs. aggrieved: Miffed is much milder than aggrieved — a miffed guest is briefly annoyed at some small slight and forgets it by dessert.

    embittered vs. aggrieved: Embittered goes further than aggrieved — the sense of being wronged has hardened over years into permanent bitterness.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal, often legal

    Aggrieved is the word for someone nursing a justified sense of injustice — contracts and courtrooms even speak of 'the aggrieved party.' Plain 'upset' or 'annoyed' misses the claim of unfairness at its core. And unlike its unit-mate anguish, which is crushing inner sorrow, aggrieved points outward, at the wrong that was done to you.
