Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Vociferous means loud with voices; noisily outspoken.

See the word in context

Trivia night turned vociferous at the final question, both teams shouting rulebook passages over the quizmaster's bell.

The dawn fish market runs on lungs. Every stall calls its prices over the next stall's prices; a vociferous dispute about scale weights is refought at full volume each Tuesday; and when the mackerel boat is late, three hundred voices tell the harbor about it together. First-time visitors stand at the gate and brace, as if against weather.

Vociferous is the exact word because it is loudness made of voices — the Latin roots literally mean 'voice-carrying.' A jackhammer can rattle windows but is never vociferous; the market is, because its noise is people saying things at volume: prices, gripes, opinions. Loud, boisterous, and above all voiced.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    clamorous: Clamorous is a din with voices in it — shouts, calls, and noise all tangled together, the sound of a crowd wanting things at once. Vociferous is narrower and more pointed: its voices mean what they shout. A clamorous room is loud with people; a vociferous one is loud with opinions.

    noisy: Noisy is the broadest loudness word there is: it covers any loud sound at all, voiced or not — engines, machinery, weather, waves. Vociferous is loudness made specifically of human voices saying things. Every vociferous crowd is noisy; a noisy machine can never be vociferous.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    soft-spoken: quiet by habit

    hushed: quieted to near silence

    Where it sits · how overwhelming the volume becomes

    outspoken → vociferous → thunderous

    outspoken vs. vociferous: Outspoken says its piece plainly, at any volume; vociferous says it at the top of its lungs.

    thunderous vs. vociferous: Thunderous goes past vociferous — the voices have merged into weather, and no single word survives the roar.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal

    Vociferous is loudness with language in it — picket lines, fans, gripes, anything shouted and meant. Its unit-mate vehemently measures the feeling behind an expression, which can stay quiet; vociferous measures the decibels of actual voices. For noise without voices — engines, horns, surf — reach for plain loud or earsplitting instead.
