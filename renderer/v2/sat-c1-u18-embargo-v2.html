embargo

/em-BAR-goh/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Embargo is an official government ban on trade or the movement of goods with a particular country or in a particular product.

See the word in context

After the missile tests, several countries placed an embargo on electronics exports to the regime.

When Valdoria's neighbor began testing long-range missiles, Valdoria's parliament voted for an immediate embargo on all weapons and aircraft parts. Customs agents received new orders that same night, and by morning three cargo ships had been turned away from the harbor. Companies with signed contracts scrambled to cancel shipments rather than break the law. Officials said the ban would stay in place until international inspectors were allowed back in.

The government's official, legally binding ban on trade in specific goods with a specific country is exactly what an embargo is.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    ban: A ban is an official rule forbidding something, and any authority can issue one — a school bans phones. An embargo is a particular kind of ban: a government's official halt to trade or the movement of goods, aimed at a country or a product. Every embargo is a ban; only banned commerce is an embargo.

    prohibition: A prohibition is a formal order forbidding an activity, whatever the activity is — parking, hunting, burning. An embargo is the trade-specific version: an official government ban on commerce or the movement of goods, usually aimed at another country. Call any forbidding a prohibition; reserve embargo for trade stopped by government order.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    authorization: official permission for something to happen

    permission: approval allowing something to be done

    Where it sits · how thoroughly trade is cut off

    restriction → embargo → blockade

    restriction vs. embargo: A restriction limits some trade, while an embargo bans it outright by official government order.

    blockade vs. embargo: An embargo cuts off trade on paper, while a blockade physically stops the ships and trucks from getting through at all.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal, news and policy register

      Embargo appears constantly in news and policy writing, almost always with the verbs impose, enforce, or lift, and with the preposition on: a nation imposes an embargo on oil exports. It names an official act by a government, never a personal refusal to buy something.

      When inspectors documented illegal logging across the border, the coastal republic answered with economics rather than soldiers. Its government declared an embargo on all timber from its inland neighbor: no logs, no lumber, no paper pulp could enter the country's ports, effective at midnight. Customs officers turned back seventeen barges in the first week. Contracts collapsed, and warehouse districts on both sides of the river went quiet. Diplomats stressed that ordinary trade in grain and machinery continued untouched; the order was aimed at one industry, in one country, for one stated reason. Within a season, the inland government agreed to independent forest monitors, and the ports reopened to timber the day the agreement was signed.
