biodiversity

/BY-oh-dih-VUR-sih-tee/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Biodiversity means the variety of plant and animal life found in a habitat or across the world.

See the word in context

A single hectare of that rainforest holds more tree biodiversity than the whole of northern Europe.

Two ponds sit on either side of one road. Netting the first turns up 4,000 individual animals, all belonging to two species of midge and nothing else. The second yields only 600 individuals, but they belong to 71 species, including snails, water beetles, three dragonflies, and two newts. The ecologist's report calls the second pond far richer in biodiversity, even though it holds a fraction as many animals.

The second pond wins on biodiversity with 71 species against two, despite holding far fewer animals, because biodiversity counts the variety of life rather than the quantity of it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    assortment: An assortment is an informal mix of noticeably different things, with no tally kept and no comparison made -- a tin of assorted buttons. Biodiversity is a counted, scientific quantity: how varied a place's living things are, right down to their genes and homes.

    multiplicity: Multiplicity means a great number and variety of something, as in a multiplicity of opinions or uses. Biodiversity applies that idea to living things alone and turns it into a measurement: the variety of life a place holds, counted across species, genes, and habitats.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    monoculture: a habitat or crop made up of a single species

    uniformity: sameness, with no variety of kinds

    Where it sits · how completely life's differences are counted

    variety → richness → biodiversity

    variety vs. biodiversity: Variety is the everyday word, while biodiversity is the scientific measure of it, counted across species, habitats, and genes.

    richness vs. biodiversity: Richness is the technical term for one part of biodiversity, the raw number of species, whereas biodiversity also takes in genetic and habitat variety.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    scientific and environmental; common in policy writing

    Biodiversity measures how many different kinds of life a place holds rather than how much life it holds, so a field of a million identical wheat plants has enormous abundance and almost no biodiversity. It is an uncountable noun, so write 'high biodiversity' and never 'many biodiversities.'

    When the county paid farmers to rip out hedgerows in the 1970s, the goal was simple: straighter fields and bigger harvests. The bill came due slowly. Within twenty years, surveys found fewer songbirds, fewer wild bees, and fewer of the beetles that eat crop pests. Ecologists measured the losses as a collapse in biodiversity: the fields still grew wheat, but the web of living things around the wheat had thinned to a few hardy species. Replanting began in the 1990s. Each mile of restored hedge brought back nesting sites, berries, and shelter, and the counts began to climb. Today the same agency pays farmers to keep hedgerows in, having learned that a field's output depends on the life at its edges.
