Meaning

What It Means

Pertinacious means holding firmly, even stubbornly, to a purpose.

See the word in context

His pertinacious refusal to sell the corner house outlasted three developers and ten years of rising offers.

Mrs. Abara's crosswalk campaign was pertinacious in the purest sense: she returned to the council podium at every session for six years, refiling the same petition with fresh signatures, until the paint truck finally came.

Pertinacious sits a shade past 'persistent': it praises—or gently teases—a grip on purpose that will not loosen for fatigue, refusal, or embarrassment. Persistence keeps trying; pertinacity cannot be pried off.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    dogged: Dogged means grinding steadily on through difficulty — head down, effort renewed, stamina admired. Pertinacious adds a different note: the grip itself cannot be pried loose, even after everyone advises letting go, and the word often carries a raised eyebrow. Dogged praises the effort; pertinacious marks the refusal to release.

    persistent: Persistent is the plain, everyday word for continuing despite difficulty — a persistent cough, a persistent salesman. Pertinacious sits a shade past it: persistence keeps trying, while the pertinacious cannot be pried off the purpose at all, by fatigue, refusal, or embarrassment. Choose persistent for effort, pertinacious for the unshakable grip.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    yielding: giving way when pressed

    irresolute: unable to settle on a purpose and hold it

    Where it sits · how tightly the goal is gripped

    determined → pertinacious → obsessive

    determined vs. pertinacious: Determined stops short of pertinacious — determination pursues a goal, while pertinacity refuses to release it after everyone else has.

    obsessive vs. pertinacious: Obsessive goes further than pertinacious — the obsessive could no longer let go even if they chose to.

      Word Choice

      formal / admiring or teasing

      Pertinacious can tip either way — admiration for a grip that will not quit, or a tease for stubbornness past all sense; context decides. Its close root-cousin tenacious (met in the root check above) is the warmer, more common word; pertinacious adds the raised eyebrow.
