perpetuate

/per-PECH-oo-ayt/ - the stress falls on PECH

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Perpetuate means to cause something to continue — to keep a practice, belief, or situation going long after it would otherwise have faded.

See the word in context

Forty years after the chemistry teacher retired, an alumni scholarship still perpetuates her name, sending one new graduate a year into the field she loved.

Nineteenth-century mapmakers copied one another's charts wholesale, so a nonexistent island sketched by a single careless surveyor was perpetuated for sixty years — outliving the man who drew it and the ships that failed to find it.

Perpetuate points at the handing-forward that keeps a thing going. The island had no reality to hold it on the charts; only the copying did, and the error would have died the moment the copying stopped. That agency is what separates perpetuate from merely continue: something, or someone, is actively passing the belief along.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sustain: To sustain is to supply what something needs to keep running — fuel, funding, attention — and it only operates while the thing is already underway. Perpetuate reaches further: it hands the thing on so it keeps going indefinitely, often outliving the people who started it.

    keep alive: Keeping something alive is the everyday phrase for stopping it from dying out — a campfire, a friendship, a debate. Perpetuate is the formal word and adds reach: what is perpetuated gets passed forward, year after year, whether or not anyone still remembers why.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    discontinue: to stop a practice deliberately and let it end

    abolish: to end something officially and for good

    Where it sits · how far past its natural end it is kept going

    maintain → prolong → perpetuate

    maintain vs. perpetuate: Maintain is weaker than perpetuate: a caretaker maintains the clock tower week to week, keeping it as it is, while the bell-ringers' guild perpetuates the ringing itself, training each new generation to carry it on.

    prolong vs. perpetuate: Prolong is weaker than perpetuate — a devoted editor can prolong a struggling magazine's run by a few more issues while it is still underway, but only its readers can perpetuate it, keeping it in print past anyone's lifetime.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral

    Perpetuate takes an object that is still alive — a custom still practiced, a belief still held, a cycle still turning. Three neighbors divide the timeline: you prolong what is still underway, you perpetuate what would otherwise fade but has not yet stopped, and you revive what has already lapsed. Perpetuate also prefers intangibles: you preserve a building, but you perpetuate the tradition of caring for it, or the memory attached to it. The word often carries a critical edge — stereotypes, myths, and outdated fears get perpetuated — though a scholarship can perpetuate a name with nothing but honor. Its relatives divide the labor too: the adjective perpetual describes what results, and self-perpetuating, met in Unit 1 of this course, names a thing that continues itself; perpetuate is the act, with someone or something doing the continuing. And keep it clear of the near-twin perpetrate, which means to commit a crime or hoax.
