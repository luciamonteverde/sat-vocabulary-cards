morass

/muh-RASS/

Section 1

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Morass means a wet, swampy bog — or, far more often in reading, a confusing, entangled situation that swallows time and effort.

See the word in context

What began as a simple porch addition sank into a morass of permit forms, inspection backlogs, and conflicting instructions from the city office.

Preparing the food truck's first tax return meant wading through a morass of overlapping city, county, and state rules.

The rules overlap and pile on top of one another with no clear path through, which is exactly the entangling, bewildering mess that morass names — notice the owner is wading, not walking.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    tangle: A tangle is a knotted, confused mess — cords, schedules, regulations twisted together — that you can set about unpicking strand by strand from the outside. A morass is a bog: you do not unpick it, you sink into it, and working harder only pulls you deeper.

    labyrinth: A labyrinth is a bewildering maze with no clear way through — but you stay on your feet, wandering and lost. A morass takes you downward: it is the swamp that swallows time and effort. You wander into a labyrinth; you sink into a morass.

    snarl: A snarl is a knot that jams one particular spot — a traffic snarl, a snarl in the supply chain. It is local, and traffic eventually crawls past it. A morass is the whole terrain gone soft: an entire undertaking swallowed by confusion, with no firm ground anywhere.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    order: a tidy, well-organized state

    clarity: clearness that is easy to understand

    Where it sits · how long the problem traps you

    muddle → morass → nightmare

    muddle vs. morass: A muddle is a small mix-up — a double-booked meeting — that an afternoon can straighten out; a morass entangles you for weeks.

    nightmare vs. morass: A nightmare goes beyond a morass: people reach for that word when a situation stops feeling merely tangled and starts feeling unbearable.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      Formal; literal and figurative

      Morass began as a literal word for a swamp and still works that way, but in modern reading it almost always means a situation that entangles and confuses — a morass of regulations, paperwork, or data. Its cousins in this unit split the territory: a maelstrom batters you with churning violence, a quagmire holds you stuck, and a morass buries you in confusion.
