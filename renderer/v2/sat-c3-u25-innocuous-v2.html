Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Innocuous means harmless and inoffensive — unable to injure anyone or to ruffle anyone's feelings.

See the word in context

The question sounded innocuous — 'Where did you grow up?' — but the diplomat had learned that no question at that table ever was.

The 'suspicious device' that emptied the terminal for an hour proved entirely innocuous: a child's science-fair project, a shoebox of wires and glitter built to measure humidity.

Notice where the word does its work: harm was expected, searched for, and found absent. Innocuous covers both fronts at once — the shoebox can neither injure anyone nor offend anyone — and it appears most often exactly here, at the moment a feared thing is cleared.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    harmless: Harmless is the plain, physical word: it does no damage — a harmless spider, a harmless dose. Innocuous reaches further, promising that the thing will neither injure anyone nor even ruffle anyone; it covers feelings as well as bodies.

    inoffensive: Inoffensive means giving no offense — nobody's feelings are bruised. Innocuous makes the larger claim: no offense and no injury of any kind, which is why lab reports and security sweeps reach for it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    harmful: causing real damage or injury

    provocative: designed to needle, offend, or inflame

    Where it sits · how completely the sting is absent

    mild → innocuous → anodyne

    mild vs. innocuous: Mild stops short of innocuous: a mild remark still carries a trace of sting, while an innocuous one has none to carry at all.

    anodyne vs. innocuous: Anodyne goes further than innocuous — the innocuous merely does no harm, while the anodyne has been scrubbed so clean of edge that it no longer says much of anything.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / faintly dismissive

    Innocuous most often appears where harm was suspected — a 'seemingly innocuous' email, a package that proves innocuous — so the word carries a small sigh of relief. Applied to art or conversation it turns faintly dismissive: an innocuous little comedy is safe and forgettable. Its dark twin noxious (met in the root check above) is the same root with the harm left in.
