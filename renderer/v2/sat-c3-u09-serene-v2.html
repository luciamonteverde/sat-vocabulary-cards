Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Serene means at peace from deep within — an effortless peace that holds even when things go wrong.

See the word in context

The abbot moved through the crowded market with a serene half-smile, as though the shouting merchants were birdsong.

Down two sets and serving into the wind, Amaia looked serene—loose shoulders, easy breaths, the same easy bounce of the ball before every serve.

Deeper than 'calm' and steadier than 'relaxed', serene names an effortless peace that seems to come from inside and doesn't flinch when circumstances turn. Calm can be a surface; serene goes all the way down.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    untroubled: Untroubled means free of worry right now, for whatever reason—the test went fine, so you are untroubled. Serene is bigger: an inner peace that seems to come from within and would hold even if the news turned bad. Untroubled describes this moment; serene describes the spirit.

    unruffled: Unruffled means nothing disturbed your surface—the manner stayed smooth through the bump. It describes the outside. Serene reaches deeper: the peace glows from within, in face and spirit, not just an undisturbed exterior. You can look unruffled while churning inside; a serene person genuinely is not churning.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    agitated: stirred up and unable to settle

    frantic: driven to wild, hurried action

    Where it sits · how little effort the peace takes

    calm → composed → serene

    calm vs. serene: Calm is the mildest word here and claims far less than serene—calm means nothing stirs at the moment, with no promise about the next, while serene names a deep peace that comes from inside and holds.

    composed vs. serene: Composed stops short of serene—composure is calm held by effort, knuckles quietly gripping the wheel, while serenity needs no grip at all.

      Word Choice

      elevated / literary

      Serene is the glowing word in this unit: use it when the peace seems to come from within and survives bad news. Placid is resting temperament; tranquil is the quiet of places and hours; composed (a rung on this card's ladder) is calm maintained by effort. Faces, smiles, and spirits are serene; harbors and evenings are usually tranquil.
