substantiate

/suhb-STAN-shee-ayt/ - the stress falls on STAN

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Substantiate means to support a claim with solid evidence.

See the word in context

A single receipt substantiated her version of the evening better than an hour of argument could.

The tribe's oral history put the fishing village at the river bend for six centuries, and the archaeologists' report substantiated it layer by layer: hearth charcoal dated to the 1400s, netting weights in every stratum, and pollen from the very crops the stories named.

Substantiate is exact because a claim — the oral history — is being given solid evidence to stand on; the word literally means giving a claim substance. Authenticate would test whether an object is genuine; corroborate, which you met in this unit's readings, adds a second independent voice. Substantiate is evidence shoring up the claim itself.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    back: To back a claim is to stand behind it — adding your name, your agreement, or your reputation to it, often informally. Substantiate deals in evidence instead of endorsement: a substantiated claim rests on documents and facts, while a backed one may still rest on somebody's word.

    bolster: Bolster strengthens a claim that already has some footing — fresh evidence shoring up a case half-built. Substantiate does the whole job from nothing: it gives a bare claim the solid support it needs to stand at all. You bolster what stands shakily; you substantiate what has not yet stood.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    undercut: to knock the support out from under a claim

    disprove: to show the claim is false outright

    Where it sits · how solidly the claim is backed

    support → substantiate → prove

    support vs. substantiate: Support is the milder word — any evidence that leans a claim's way supports it, while substantiate demands enough solid evidence to hold the claim up on its own.

    prove vs. substantiate: Prove goes further than substantiate: a substantiated claim stands firmly supported, while a proven one is settled beyond argument.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal

    Substantiate lives in law, journalism, and grant offices: claims get substantiated with documents, figures, and dated records. Corroborate — met in this unit's readings — is its cousin for confirmation by a second independent source. And keep the split with authenticate: things are authenticated, claims are substantiated.

When a rival town challenged the boast last spring, the historical society realized no one had ever substantiated it.
