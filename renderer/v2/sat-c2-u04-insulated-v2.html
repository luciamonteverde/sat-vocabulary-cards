insulated

/IN-suh-lay-tid/ - the stress falls on IN

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Insulated means shielded or protected from outside influences or pressures.

See the word in context

Palace advisers kept the young king insulated from every piece of bad news, so the famine caught him entirely by surprise.

The star quarterback has never read his own press. His coaches screen every interview request, an assistant filters his mail, and teammates have standing orders not to repeat what the radio shows say. Insulated from a decade of criticism, he plays with a rookie's easy confidence — and, his critics grumble, a rookie's blind spots.

Insulated fits better than sheltered because it names a barrier kept up on purpose against a specific pressure — criticism — rather than a general softness of upbringing. And it is not insular: the quarterback's team studies every rival's film hungrily; only the noise is kept out.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    shielded: Shielded means actively defended — something stands between you and a blow, like a bodyguard or a legal team, usually against a visible attack. Insulated is quieter and more constant: a layer keeps the pressure from ever being felt, the way thick walls keep out cold.

    immune: Immune means the pressure can land on you and still do no harm — the body itself shrugs it off. Insulated means the pressure never reaches you at all: a barrier holds it away, and if the barrier comes down you can be hurt again.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unprotected: with nothing standing between you and the blow

    vulnerable: able to be hurt by what arrives

    Where it sits · how thoroughly outside pressure is kept away

    cushioned → insulated → cocooned

    cushioned vs. insulated: A cushioned fall still lands — the impact is only softened; insulated goes further, keeping the pressure from being felt at all.

    cocooned vs. insulated: Cocooned outdoes insulated: an insulated firm still trades in the open market with one pressure blocked, while a cocooned heir floats sealed away from the world entirely.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral

      Insulated is for protection from pressures: markets, criticism, bad news, cold. It shares its island root with this unit's insular, but the two judge differently — insulated describes a shield someone or something provides, and can be lucky or dangerous, while insular accuses a group of sealing its own mind. A senator can be insulated from donors yet never insular in her reading.
