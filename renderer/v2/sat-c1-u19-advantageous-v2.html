advantageous

/ad-van-TAY-jus/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Advantageous means providing a benefit or an edge; favorable to one's interests.

See the word in context

Arriving a day early proved advantageous: the debate team got the quiet practice room and two full rehearsals before the judges ever appeared.

A small furniture startup had to choose between two warehouses that cost the same to rent. One sat beside the freight rail line; the other was twenty miles from any major route. The founders chose the rail-side site, and within a year their shipping costs ran far below their competitors' and orders arrived days sooner. The location proved advantageous in exactly the way the founders had hoped, giving the young company a genuine edge in a crowded market.

The rail-side warehouse gave the company a real benefit and an edge over its rivals, which is precisely what advantageous describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    favorable: Favorable means leaning your way — favorable conditions, favorable reviews, a favorable wind. Advantageous is stronger and more concrete: something actually gives you a benefit or an edge over others. A favorable forecast merely helps your plans along; an advantageous contract measurably improves your position against rivals.

    beneficial: Beneficial means producing good or helpful effects, the way exercise is beneficial to health or rain is beneficial to crops. Advantageous adds a competitive angle: it gives one party an edge or a better position. Something can be beneficial to everyone at once, but advantageous implies someone gains ground on someone else.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    detrimental: causing harm or damage

    unfavorable: working against one's interests

    Where it sits · how big the payoff is

    convenient → advantageous → invaluable

    convenient vs. advantageous: Convenient describes something that merely saves you a little effort, while advantageous means it actually improves your position or prospects.

    invaluable vs. advantageous: Advantageous describes a real edge, but invaluable describes a benefit so great that success would be hard to imagine without it.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral-to-formal, analytic register

    Advantageous often pairs with 'to' or 'for' plus whoever gains the edge (advantageous to the buyer, advantageous for early applicants). It is about benefit, not ease: a merger can be advantageous even if carrying it out is exhausting, and an easy task can be advantageous to no one.
