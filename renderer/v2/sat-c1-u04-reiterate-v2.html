reiterate

/ree-IT-uh-rayt/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Reiterate means to say or state something again, usually for emphasis or clarity.

See the word in context

The guide had set the turnaround time at breakfast; on the ridge she paused to reiterate it: whatever the summit looked like, the group would turn back at noon.

The chemistry teacher stated the goggles rule on the first day of class; before the first real experiment, she chose to reiterate it, reminding students that no one approaches the burners with bare eyes.

To reiterate is to state again something already said, usually for emphasis. Notice the two moments: the rule was announced on day one, and before the experiment the teacher says it a second time to give it weight.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    restate: Restate means to say something again in clearer or different words, so that it lands better or is finally understood — restating aims at clarity. Reiterate aims at force: the same point, stated again to give it weight or remove doubt. You restate a rule a student misheard; you reiterate a rule people keep testing.

    repeat: Repeat is the all-purpose word for saying or doing something again, for any reason at all — an echo, a chorus, a rerun. Reiterate is deliberate and rhetorical: a speaker returns to a point already made, specifically to press it. Recordings and parrots repeat; only someone driving a point home reiterates.

    underscore: Underscore means to stress a point so it stands out — and you can underscore something the very first time you say it, with a pause, a statistic, or a raised voice. Reiterate requires a second telling: its emphasis comes from saying the thing again. Underscoring highlights; reiterating repeats.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    retract: to take back something previously said

    recant: to formally withdraw an earlier statement

    Where it sits · how insistent the repetition is

    mention → reiterate → hammer

    mention vs. reiterate: To mention a point is to raise it once in passing; to reiterate is to return to it deliberately, a second time, for emphasis.

    hammer vs. reiterate: To hammer a point home is to reiterate it so relentlessly that no listener can escape it.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Neutral to formal; frequent in meetings, official statements, and academic writing.

    Use reiterate when someone states again what has already been said, usually to add emphasis or remove doubt: a spokesperson will reiterate a denial, a memo will reiterate a deadline. If the point is being made for the first time, there is nothing yet to reiterate.
