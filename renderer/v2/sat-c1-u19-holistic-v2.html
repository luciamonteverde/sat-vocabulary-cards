holistic

/hoh-LISS-tik/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Holistic means considering something as a whole interconnected system rather than analyzing or treating its parts separately.

See the word in context

The clinic took a holistic view of health, asking about the patient's sleep, stress, diet, and relationships instead of examining the sore knee alone.

Frustrated by downtown gridlock, a city first tried simply widening its busiest road, and traffic got worse as more drivers poured in. A new planning team then took a holistic approach: they studied bus routes, bike lanes, school start times, parking prices, and housing locations together, because each one affected all the others. By staggering school schedules and improving two bus lines, they eased the congestion without pouring a single new lane of concrete.

The planners treated traffic as one interconnected system of schedules, transit, and housing rather than fixing one road in isolation, which is exactly what a holistic approach means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    integrated: Integrated means combined so the parts work as one connected whole — an integrated transit system where trains and buses share one schedule. Holistic describes a way of looking rather than a way of building: it considers a thing as one interconnected system instead of analyzing the parts separately. Engineers integrate parts; a holistic review treats them as inseparable.

    big-picture: Big-picture means focused on the whole situation rather than the details — a big-picture thinker skips the fine print. Holistic keeps the details in: it insists the parts be studied together as one connected system, not skipped. A big-picture summary may ignore the parts; a holistic assessment connects every one of them.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    piecemeal: dealing with parts one at a time, with no plan for the whole

    fragmented: broken into disconnected parts

    Where it sits · how much it connects and takes in

    broad → holistic → all-encompassing

    broad vs. holistic: A broad review touches many separate areas, while a holistic one treats those areas as parts of a single connected system.

    all-encompassing vs. holistic: Holistic thinking connects the parts of one system, but an all-encompassing plan tries to fold in everything around that system as well.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral-to-formal, academic/wellness register

      Holistic pairs most often with approach, view, care, and assessment (a holistic approach to city planning). It is not just a fancy word for comprehensive: comprehensive means nothing was left out, while holistic means the parts were treated as one connected system. A report can be thorough without being holistic if it studies each part in isolation.
