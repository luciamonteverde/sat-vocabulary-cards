disparate

/DIS-puh-rit/

What It Means

Disparate means fundamentally different, or made up of sharply contrasting and unrelated parts.

See the word in context

The planning committee united people with disparate backgrounds, from dairy farmers to software engineers.

Dev's road-trip playlist jumps from opera to country to electronic dance music. His friends tease him that the songs are so disparate that no two of them sound like they belong on the same playlist.

The songs are not just slightly different; they come from sharply contrasting, unrelated genres, which is the extreme kind of difference disparate describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    dissimilar: Dissimilar is the measured, neutral way to say two things are not alike, and it carries no drama - two dissimilar designs can still sit comfortably side by side. Disparate is stronger: the elements are so fundamentally unlike that they hardly seem to belong in the same collection at all.

    contrasting: Contrasting things are strikingly different in a way that plays them off each other - contrasting colors are often paired on purpose because the difference works. Disparate elements do not set each other off; they simply share no common ground, so putting them together feels arbitrary rather than artful.

    unrelated: Unrelated is about connection: two unrelated events simply have no link between them, and the word passes no judgment on how they look. Disparate is about character: the pieces are jarringly unalike in kind. Two unrelated songs might happen to sound similar; disparate songs could never be mistaken for one family.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    similar: alike in significant ways

    matching: corresponding or going together

    Where it sits · how far apart the elements stand

    different → disparate → irreconcilable

    different vs. disparate: Different can describe any two things that are not the same, while disparate suggests elements so unlike that they barely seem to belong together.

    irreconcilable vs. disparate: Disparate is milder than irreconcilable, which means so deeply opposed that the elements can never be brought into agreement.

      Word Choice

      Formal, academic

      Disparate is stronger than merely different: it signals that the elements are so unlike that they barely seem to belong together. It appears constantly in academic and journalistic writing about combining disparate sources, fields, communities, or data sets.
