Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Regimentation means strict, uniform organization and control.

See the word in context

Camp Alder's regimentation starts at the flagpole: whistle at seven, bunks squared by eight, canoes assigned by number.

The cherry orchard runs its six picking days on pure regimentation: crews assigned by a numbered board at dawn, buckets weighed on the hour, breaks called by whistle, every ladder chalked to its row. Nobody calls it charming. But cherries spoil in a careless hand and ripen all at once, and the owner will trade charm for six perfect days.

Regimentation is the exact word for those six days, because regimentation means strict, uniform organization and control — one pattern, imposed from above, the same for everyone. A schedule organizes your time and leaves you the rest; regimentation decides the rest as well.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    regimen: A regimen is one prescribed routine for one purpose — a diet, a training plan, a course of pills — and it governs only the corner of life it was written for. Regimentation runs the whole day for everyone: every hour drilled into the same pattern from above.

    drill: A drill is a practiced routine imposed from above for a single activity — fire drill, rifle drill — repeated until it runs automatically. Regimentation stretches that same top-down discipline past any one exercise until it covers meals, bunks, and minutes. A drill ends when the whistle blows.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    laxity: rules loose or unenforced

    free-for-all: no order at all

    Where it sits · how far order is imposed from outside

    habit → schedule → regimentation

    habit vs. regimentation: A habit falls far short of regimentation — one person's repeated act, chosen and private, while regimentation imposes one pattern on everyone from above.

    schedule vs. regimentation: A schedule goes further than a habit — it fixes a whole day's shape — but it stops short of regimentation, which fixes everyone's day the same way, from above, down to the minute.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      formal / faintly disapproving

      Regimentation usually arrives with a curled lip: control past the point of usefulness. Discipline earns respect; a regimen is one prescribed routine; regimentation is a whole life drilled into columns. Praise a team's order as discipline; complain about it as regimentation.

Regimentation is the school's open secret: every boarder, from the eleven-year-olds to the graduating class, wakes to the same bell, wears the same gray practice clothes, and eats the same three meals at the same three times. Even hair is regulated — one approved style, pinned identically. Defenders say the sameness frees the mind: with every small decision made in advance, all that remains is the dancing. Critics answer that a school which schedules every minute of every child has confused training the body with running it.
