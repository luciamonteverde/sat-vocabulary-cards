What It Means

Punctual means arriving or doing things exactly on time.

See the word in context

Punctual to a fault, he arrives at parties exactly on the hour, while the hosts are still setting out chairs.

The stage manager's curtain rises at 7:30, and 7:30 is what she means: the house lights dim as the lobby clock's minute hand touches six. Actors mid-yawn have been caught by it, and a mayor once wrapped up her welcome speech to a rising curtain. The theater is punctual as an institution, not just a person, and its audiences have learned to be the same.

Punctual is the exact word because it names arriving or doing things exactly on time — honoring the appointed minute, neither late nor loosely early. Its root is the Latin for 'point': the punctual hit the point of time they promised. Speed is beside the point; a slow train that meets its timetable is punctual, and a fast one that misses it is not.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    on-time: On-time is the plain everyday phrase for meeting a set time — the bus was on-time today. It reports one arrival. Punctual turns that single fact into a standing virtue of a person or an institution: the punctual honor the appointed minute every time, as a matter of character.

    dependable: Dependable means worth relying on across every duty — the work done, the promise kept, the secret held — not only about the clock. Punctual is dependability's narrow, time-keeping slice: it says nothing about the rest of your character, only that you hit the minute you promised.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    tardy: late — the report-card word

    dawdling: losing time along the way

    Where it sits · how precise the arrival is

    timely → punctual → clockwork

    timely vs. punctual: Timely means within the useful window — help that comes soon enough; punctual tightens the claim to the exact appointed minute.

    clockwork vs. punctual: Clockwork goes past punctual — so exact, arrival after arrival, that other people set their plans by it.

      Word Choice

      neutral

      Punctual is for keeping set times — appointments, curtains, timetables. Its close cousin prompt is about answering a summons without delay, no appointment needed: a punctual doctor starts at 8:00; a prompt one answers the page at once. No cluster-mates crowd it in this unit; just don't let the sound pull you toward punctuation, the points on a page.

Listeners joked that they set their kettles by her voice, and new hires at the station were warned never to schedule anything against her opening. Being punctual, she liked to say, was the cheapest promise a person could keep: it cost nothing but attention, and it paid out every single day. When a flood closed the studio one January, she broadcast from her kitchen rather than start four minutes late. The station manager kept her farewell plaque simple.
