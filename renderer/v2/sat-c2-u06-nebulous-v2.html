Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Nebulous means vaguely defined, cloudy.

See the word in context

The band's plan to 'reinvent our sound' stayed nebulous through three rehearsals that sounded exactly like the old sound.

Since fifth grade Amir has carried a nebulous dream to 'work with the ocean' - some years it is dolphins, some years ships, one memorable year 'waves in general.' The wish gleams, drifts, and never gains enough outline to choose a single class by.

Nebulous is exact because the dream never takes definable form: no job title, no course list, no first step. It is not ambiguous - there are not two clear versions to pick between - and it is not a precise idea awaiting examples. It is a cloud that shines and will not condense.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    vague: Vague is the everyday, all-purpose word for anything not pinned down — a vague answer, a vague promise, a vague sense of direction. Nebulous is stronger and more pointed: it says a plan or idea is all cloud, with no outline at all for anyone to grab.

    indistinct: Indistinct belongs to perception: something real is there, but the eye or ear cannot quite make out its edges — a coastline in fog, a voice through a wall. Nebulous points at the thing itself, saying the idea or plan has never taken a settled form.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    definite: settled into one clear, stated form

    concrete: specific enough to touch, date, or measure

    Where it sits · how completely the outline dissolves

    fuzzy → hazy → nebulous

    fuzzy vs. nebulous: A fuzzy plan has real edges that blur here and there; nebulous goes further - the plan is all cloud and no outline.

    hazy vs. nebulous: A hazy memory keeps its outline half in view behind the mist; nebulous means even the outline is gone - nothing but vapor remains.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral, mildly critical

      Nebulous is the cloud-word for ideas, plans, and promises that never take shape. Amorphous is its cluster-mate for physical things and organizations; ambiguity means two sharp readings, not none; and an abstraction can be perfectly precise. Calling a plan nebulous is a criticism: it says there is nothing yet to grab.

      For forty years, director Mara Voss kept announcing the same project: a film she called simply 'The Crossing.' What the film actually was remained nebulous for her entire career. In interviews she offered moods -- 'a story about thresholds,' 'water, but not the sea' -- while declining to name a plot, a period, or a single character. Studios that asked for a page of outline received, one executive recalled, 'weather instead.' When her notebooks were opened after her death, archivists found hundreds of pages of images and not one scene: the project had never taken a form even she could state.
