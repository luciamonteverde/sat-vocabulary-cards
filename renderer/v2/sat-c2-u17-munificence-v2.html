Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Munificence means extraordinary generosity in giving.

See the word in context

Whole wings of the aquarium exist only because of one trawler magnate's munificence.

The stonecutters' guild still reads aloud, each founding day, the munificence of the quarry's first owner: in a single decade he gave it new derricks, a bridge for its wagons, a pension for every widow of the craft, and the seam of white marble it works to this day.

Munificence is the exact word for that decade of giving, because munificence means extraordinary generosity in giving — the scale is the point. Generosity covers a borrowed ladder and a covered lunch; munificence builds bridges and endows pensions, giving at a size that changes what a town can be.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    lavishness: Lavishness is spending on a grand scale, giving or not — a lavish yacht spends the fortune on its owner. Munificence is grand scale pointed outward: extraordinary generosity in giving, where the fortune leaves the giver's hands and builds something that outlasts the giver.

    bountifulness: Bountifulness is plentiful supply — the harvest word for plenty flowing steadily from a rich source: orchards, rains, full nets. Munificence is about a giver's choice rather than nature's plenty: one person deciding to give at a size that changes what a town can be.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    penny-pinching: counting every coin twice before parting with it

    hoarding: keeping it all, giving none

    Where it sits · the scale of what is given

    kindness → generosity → munificence

    kindness vs. munificence: Kindness is much weaker than munificence on this scale — a held door or a patient answer already counts as kindness and can cost nothing at all, while munificence gives at a size that builds bridges and endows pensions.

    generosity vs. munificence: Generosity goes further than kindness — something real is given away — but ordinary generosity is within anyone's reach, while munificence funds bridges and endowments.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / grand

    Munificence is the formal, marble-lobby word for giving of extraordinary scale. Largess, its unit-mate, shines differently — a shower of lavish gifts, often with an audience; munificence weighs more and shows off less. For everyday givers, generosity is the honest size, and the benefactor is the person behind any of the three.

    The conservatory had survived on bake sales and borrowed pianos for thirty years. Then a retired soprano, who had trained there as a scholarship girl, sold her villa abroad and gave nearly all of it back. Her munificence rebuilt the concert hall, endowed twelve full scholarships, and paid the faculty a living wage for the first time. She asked for one thing only: that her name appear nowhere on the building. The director tried to argue. 'A plaque costs so little,' he said. 'That,' she answered, 'is why it isn't enough.' The school's annual report simply recorded a gift at a scale that changes what an institution can be — and every student who auditions now walks through a door her fortune quietly opened.
