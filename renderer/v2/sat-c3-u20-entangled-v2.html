Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Entangled means caught in a tangle — twisted together with something, or so deeply involved in a complicated situation that pulling free is difficult.

See the word in context

By 1914 the great powers of Europe had grown so entangled in rival alliances that a single assassination pulled every one of them into war.

Halfway up the climbing gym's tallest wall, Noor's rope became entangled with a line the previous climber had left clipped through the anchors, and she lost ten minutes to the resulting knot before she could climb on.

The rope is not merely touching the old line — it has wrapped and knotted with it, and every hard pull makes the mess worse. That twisted-together, tightens-when-you-tug quality is what entangled asserts, and it is what the word carries into figurative use: a firm entangled in a lawsuit finds that every attempt to pull away knots it in deeper.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    ensnared: Ensnared means caught in a trap that someone or something set — a snare has an owner and a purpose. Entangled needs no trapper: two garden hoses, two families, or two companies can knot themselves together with nobody hunting anybody, and the word points at the twisting, not the intent.

    embroiled: Embroiled means dragged into a conflict — an argument, a scandal, a feud. Entangled is wider and quieter: the knot can be made of money, promises, or plain rope, with no quarrel anywhere in it, and it stresses how hard the pulling-free will be.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    freed: worked loose, with nothing holding on any longer

    unattached: tied to nothing and involved with no one

    Where it sits · how hard it is to pull free

    snagged → entangled → trapped

    snagged vs. entangled: Snagged is milder than entangled: a snagged sleeve comes free with one careful tug, while an entangled line has wrapped and knotted until pulling only tightens it.

    trapped vs. entangled: Trapped goes further than entangled: whatever is entangled still has a knot to work at, but whatever is trapped has no knot, no slack, and no way out at all.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral - news, history, and science writing

    Entangled takes in for the situation (entangled in a lawsuit) and with for the other party (entangled with a partner's debts). Its unit-mate encumber weighs you down — an encumbered hiker moves slowly but can still walk away — while entangled ties you to something, so that pulling away is exactly what tightens the knot. Physicists borrowed the word for particles whose fates stay knotted at any distance, a tribute to how vivid the image is. And for a light, one-point catch, plain snagged is the honest word; save entangled for a real snarl.
