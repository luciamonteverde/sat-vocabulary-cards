Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Sporadic means occurring at irregular, scattered intervals.

See the word in context

Sales of the board game are sporadic: the shop can go a month without selling one, then wrap three before lunch.

The pickup's knock was sporadic in the way that drives mechanics to poetry. It spoke twice on one spring drive, once in a parking lot a season later, then went quiet for half a year. The shop kept the truck a full week and heard it never; the owner heard it again the day she drove it home — at a red light, once, and that was all till fall.

Sporadic is the exact word because it names what occurs at irregular, scattered intervals — no schedule, no rhythm, no promise about the next time. The Greek root means scattered like seed, and the picture holds: events flung across the calendar, thick here, bare there. What is sporadic cannot be predicted, only waited for.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    scattered: Scattered is the root picture behind sporadic: things strewn thinly about, like seed flung across a field. It can cover space or time, but it reports only the thin spread. Sporadic sharpens the claim about timing — events at irregular intervals, in clusters and droughts, with no telling when the next comes.

    patchy: Patchy describes uneven coverage — fog patchy over the valley, a lawn gone patchy after a dry August. The gaps sit in fixed places you could point to. Sporadic is about time instead: events arriving at irregular, scattered intervals that no clock will pin down.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    steady: holding one even pace

    unbroken: without a single gap

    Where it sits · how unpredictable the timing is

    occasional → sporadic → patternless

    occasional vs. sporadic: Occasional says only that a thing happens now and then; sporadic adds the jolt of irregularity — clusters, droughts, no telling which.

    patternless vs. sporadic: Patternless goes past sporadic — not merely scattered in time, but with no logic left for even the patientest observer to find.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / clinical

    Sporadic is the observer's word for events scattered at irregular intervals — outbreaks, sightings, sales. Its mirror in this unit is incessant, which allows no gaps at all, and cyclical returns on a rhythm sporadic never keeps. Where the gaps are regular — every ten seconds, every third day — the precise word is periodic.

    Rain in the basin is sporadic, and everything alive there is built around that fact. A storm may soak one arroyo three times in a week, then skip the whole valley for fourteen months. The wildflowers cannot afford a calendar. Their seeds wait in the gravel — a year, a decade — for the one wet night that tells them to go. Ranchers keep rain logs going back to their grandparents, hoping to find a rhythm. The logs show streaks and silences, and no rhythm at all. Visitors from wetter places always ask when the rainy season starts. The oldest rancher likes to answer: it doesn't start; it ambushes.
