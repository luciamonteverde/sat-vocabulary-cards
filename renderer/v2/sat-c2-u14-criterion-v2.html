criterion

/kry-TEER-ee-uhn/ - the stress falls on TEER

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A criterion is a standard that something must meet in order to be judged, chosen, or approved.

See the word in context

Price was never the panel's criterion; every bid was judged on its safety record alone.

Mr. Abara let the class pick the criterion for the paper-airplane title before a single plane flew: distance, airtime, or surviving three throws intact. They argued for a full period and chose airtime. From that moment the winner was decided in principle — the slow, wide gliders would beat the sleek darts every time — and the dart builders knew it the instant the vote closed.

Criterion is the exact word because the class wasn't choosing a winner; it was choosing the standard that would choose the winner. That's the power the word names: set the test and you've half-decided the results. One more thing worth keeping: criterion is the singular — one standard is a criterion, several are criteria, and careful writers never let "a criteria" slip past.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    benchmark: A benchmark is usually a number — a fixed reference point that performance is measured against, like last quarter's sales. A criterion can be any quality or condition that must be met, no arithmetic required.

    yardstick: Yardstick is the informal cousin: a rough measure you hold things against for comparison. A criterion is more official — a stated standard with pass and fail on either side of it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    whim: a choice made on impulse, with no standard behind it

    hunch: a feeling followed instead of a test

    Where it sits · how binding the standard is

    guideline → criterion → prerequisite

    guideline vs. criterion: A guideline is softer than a criterion: guidelines advise and can bend for a good reason, while a criterion is a standard that must actually be met before something passes.

    prerequisite vs. criterion: Prerequisite is more binding than criterion — a criterion is one standard among several in the judging, but a prerequisite is the locked door before judging even begins: fail it and you are never considered at all.

    Apply

    Word Choice

    precise / evaluative

    Criterion is the word for a stated, defensible standard — and it is strictly singular: one criterion, several criteria, and "a criteria" marks a writer faster than any misspelling. This unit's judicious describes the person doing the weighing well; a criterion is what the weighing rests on. Where the standard is just a number to beat, benchmark is the honest word.
