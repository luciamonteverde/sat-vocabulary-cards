virulent

/VEER-yuh-luhnt/ - the stress falls on VEER

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Virulent means extremely severe and destructive in its effects.

See the word in context

The senator's lead evaporated under a virulent whisper campaign that no denial seemed able to slow.

Australia loosed the myxoma virus on its rabbit plague in 1950, and the first summer's numbers stunned even the planners: better than ninety-nine of every hundred infected rabbits died. Nothing in field medicine had seen so virulent an agent. Within a decade, though, the virus itself had mellowed — versions that killed too fast ran out of hosts to carry them — and virologists still teach the story as virulence negotiating with survival.

Virulent is the exact word for that first summer's virus, because it means extremely severe and destructive in its effects — it grades the force of the blow, not the reach. A contagious germ spreads easily; an epidemic one has already spread everywhere; a virulent one hits hard, killing or crippling what it touches. The word travels well, too: criticism and rumors turn virulent when they stop wounding and start destroying.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    deadly: Deadly makes one claim and stops: it kills. Virulent grades the whole force of the harm, fatal or not — how hard a disease, a criticism, or a rumor strikes whatever it touches, and how much is destroyed when it does.

    savage: Savage describes the manner of an attack — brutal, wild, unrestrained — however much or little damage finally lands. Virulent measures the damage itself: a virulent strain or a virulent rumor is defined by what it destroys, even when it works quietly.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    harmless: doing no damage at all

    gentle: soft in its effects

    Where it sits · how dangerous the disease is

    mild → severe → virulent

    mild vs. virulent: A mild strain passes through in a day or two and is soon forgotten — the opposite end of the scale from virulent, which kills or cripples what it touches.

    severe vs. virulent: Severe goes further than mild — beds fill and recoveries drag — but virulent claims the most: a strain so destructive it kills or cripples swiftly.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    grave / evaluative

    Virulent grades severity — of pathogens literally, of attacks and rumors by extension — and it always means destructive force, not reach. Contagious describes spread; deadly counts only the kills. In this unit it is the adjective among the nouns: a blight or a bane names the destroyer, while virulent tells you how hard it strikes.
