privation

/pry-VAY-shun/ - the stress falls on VAY

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Privation means a lack of basic necessities.

See the word in context

The lighthouse posting meant a winter of privation: tinned food, brackish cistern water, and oil worth more than firewood.

The flood took the bridge in March, and the valley's privation began in April: the store emptied a category a week — first flour, then batteries, then boots — and the school lunch shrank to what the county helicopter could spare. When the road reopened in June, the first truck up the grade carried nothing but salt, soap, and yeast.

Privation is the exact word for that spring, because privation means a lack of basic necessities — not discomfort, not inconvenience, but the absence of things no household does without. Abnegation is going bare by choice; privation is the valley's version, where the choosing was done by the river.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    want: Want is the old, plain word for going without — 'families in want' — and it can stay general. Privation is sharper: what is missing are the basic necessities themselves, food, fuel, and light. Want says someone lacks; privation says they lack the essentials.

    hardship: Hardship is difficulty of any kind — a brutal commute, a lawsuit, and a failed harvest all qualify. Privation is one particular hardship: life with the basic necessities gone. Every privation is a hardship, but a hardship only becomes privation when the pantry and the woodpile stand empty.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    plenty: enough and more of everything

    luxury: comforts far past necessity

    Where it sits · how severe the shortage has become

    pinch → privation → starvation

    pinch vs. privation: A pinch is far milder than privation — a tight month where the budget groans but nothing essential is missing yet.

    starvation vs. privation: Starvation goes further than privation — privation is necessities running short; starvation is the body itself running out.

      Word Choice

      formal / grave

      Privation is for necessities gone: sieges, failed harvests, ruined pensions. Hardship is broader and softer. And mind the border with this unit's abnegation — privation is imposed, abnegation chosen. The fast is abnegation; the famine is privation.
