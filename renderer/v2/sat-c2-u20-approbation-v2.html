What It Means

Approbation means praise or official approval.

See the word in context

The old professor's approbation arrived as a single word — 'sound' — penciled at the top of the thesis.

The guide-dog school graduates a handful of new trainers a year, and its examiners are famously stingy with kind words. So the single line at the bottom of Imani's evaluation — 'exemplary in every respect' — counted as thunderous approbation, and she photographed it before the ink was a day old.

Approbation is the exact word for that line, because approbation means praise or official approval — praise with weight behind it, pronounced by someone entitled to judge. Applause is any crowd's noise; approbation is the examiner's considered yes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    applause: Applause is a crowd's noisy, immediate yes — hands together in the moment, nobody weighing anything. Approbation is the considered, often official approval pronounced by someone entitled to judge, after the merits have been weighed. The crowd claps on the spot; a board deliberates first and only then grants its approbation.

    esteem: Esteem is steady high regard held over time — a standing opinion of someone's worth that nobody needs to announce. Approbation is the moment approval is actually pronounced: a verdict delivered, a stamp given. You can hold a colleague in esteem for years without once putting the approbation in writing.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    disapproval: the plain opposite verdict

    scorn: contempt that does not bother to weigh merit

    Where it sits · how formal and unquestioning the admiration is

    praise → approbation → worship

    praise vs. approbation: Praise is far milder than approbation — any warm word, from anyone, about anything; it needs no title and no letterhead, while approbation is a verdict from someone entitled to judge.

    worship vs. approbation: Worship goes further than approbation — approbation approves with judgment still switched on; worship has stopped judging altogether.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / institutional

    Approbation suits verdicts from people entitled to judge: boards, examiners, juries of one's peers. For a friend's kind words, praise is enough; for a crowd, applause. Reach for approbation when the approval carries a stamp.

    In 1761, a young clockmaker named Ester Vane submitted her marine timekeeper to the Board of Longitude, the panel with sole power to certify instruments for the navy. The Board did not clap and did not hurry. Its examiners ran the clock through a six-week sea trial, logged its drift each noon, and questioned her about every spring and screw. Sailors who saw the device loved it at once, and their letters praised its steadiness in rough water. But affection moved nothing. Only the Board's approbation could put her clock aboard a king's ship, and the Board granted it slowly: a signed certificate, read into the minutes, eleven months after she first knocked. Ester framed the certificate. The letters from sailors she kept in a drawer.
