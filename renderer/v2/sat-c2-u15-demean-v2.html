Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Demean means to lower the dignity or status of someone.

See the word in context

The propeller cap and floppy shoes the theme park issues its PhD tour guides demean them a little more each summer.

The geriatric ward's training day begins with a clip: a nurse calling an eighty-year-old retired judge "sweetie pie" and asking whether "we" want "our" pudding. Half the trainees laugh; the instructor waits them out. The judge in the clip spent forty years being addressed as "Your Honor," she says, and baby talk strips it from him at the exact hour he cannot object. Nothing in the voice is cruel. All of it is demeaning.

Demean is the exact word for that baby talk, because it means to lower the dignity or status of someone — the harm lands on standing, not on feelings alone. An insult stings and passes; demeaning treatment rearranges where a person ranks, and it can do so with a smile on.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    degrade: Degrade is the blunt twin of demean: it drags a person down hard and openly — shouted orders, public dressing-downs. To demean can be quiet, even polite-faced; the dignity is lowered by how someone is treated, with never a harsh word spoken.

    insult: An insult is one open blow — a slur, a sneer — that stings and passes. Demeaning treatment can smile the whole time: no single remark to point to, only an everyday arrangement that treats a person as smaller than they are.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    respect: treat as fully ranked

    dignify: raise the standing of

    Where it sits · how much dignity gets stripped away

    slight → demean → humiliate

    slight vs. demean: A slight is far milder than demeaning treatment — one small, single omission, a skipped introduction or a name not remembered; to demean someone is to lower their standing steadily, day after day.

    humiliate vs. demean: Humiliate goes further than demean — demeaning treatment wears dignity down; humiliation strips it off in front of an audience, all at once.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / serious

    Demean takes a person as its object; that is what separates it inside this crowded unit. Debase lowers a thing's worth, denigrate blackens a name behind its back, deride laughs in its face — demean simply treats someone as smaller than they are, and can manage it politely. Degrade is its blunter twin for harsher treatment.
