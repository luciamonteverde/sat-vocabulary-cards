painstaking

/PAYNZ-tay-king/

Meaning

What It Means

Painstaking means taking great pains or care; extremely thorough and careful.

See the word in context

Reassembling the shattered vase was painstaking work that took the conservator an entire month.

The forger had been clever, but the document examiner was more patient. She photographed the letter under six different wavelengths of light. She measured the spacing of every character and compared the ink's chemistry against samples from the claimed decade. She even counted the fibers along the torn edge. After two weeks of this painstaking analysis, one microscopic inconsistency in the ink finally exposed the fake.

The examiner's slow, exhaustive attention to every tiny detail — wavelengths, spacing, chemistry, even fiber counts — is what makes her analysis painstaking.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    meticulous: Meticulous describes extreme, exacting care over small details — a standard the finished work meets, however easily the care comes. Painstaking praises the effort itself: the pains taken, the hours spent getting details right. Work can be meticulous without struggle, but nothing is painstaking unless real labor went in.

    thorough: Thorough means complete in coverage — nothing skipped, every part dealt with. Painstaking is about the intensity of care and effort poured into the details, not mere coverage. A checklist can make you thorough; only sustained, effortful attention makes a job painstaking.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    careless: not paying enough attention

    slapdash: done hastily and sloppily

    Where it sits · how far the attention to detail goes

    careful → painstaking → obsessive

    careful vs. painstaking: Careful is the everyday baseline — you can be careful in a minute; painstaking implies sustained, effortful care over every detail of a demanding task.

    obsessive vs. painstaking: Obsessive goes beyond painstaking into unhealthy territory, where the attention to detail no longer serves the work at all.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral, admiring; describes work and workers

    Painstaking is literally 'taking pains' — spending real effort on the details of one task. It is close to diligent, but the two point at different things: diligent praises steady, ongoing effort over time, while painstaking praises the intense care lavished on the fine details of a particular piece of work.
