Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Bard means a poet, often a singer as well.

See the word in context

Carved over the concert hall's door is a bard with his harp, mid-verse, mouth open in stone song.

When the mine closed, a retired welder became the town's bard: he set the shutdown, the picket winter, and the last cage-lift down in rhyming stanzas and sang them at the union hall until everyone under thirty knew the story by heart.

Bard fits because a bard is a poet, often a singer as well — he did not just report what happened; he made it memory, composed in verse and carried aloud. A narrator only tells a story; a bard composes it, keeps it, and sings a community's own life back to it.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    minstrel: A minstrel is a working entertainer who sings wherever there is supper and a crowd to please. A bard does more than entertain: a bard composes verse that keeps a community's memory. The minstrel plays for tonight's coins; the bard sings a people's story meant to last.

    troubadour: A troubadour is a courtly singer-poet devoted above all to polished songs of love. A bard's range is wider and weightier — history, grief, a town's whole life set into verse. A compliment sung beneath a castle window is troubadour work; a people's memory in song belongs to the bard.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    scribe: writes down what others say and sings nothing

    mime: performs without a single word

    Where it sits · how much cultural weight the craft carries

    rhymer → storyteller → bard

    rhymer vs. bard: A rhymer sits far below a bard — birthday couplets and refrigerator verse, nothing meant to outlive the party, while a bard's verses are built to carry a community's memory.

    storyteller vs. bard: A storyteller stops short of a bard — the tales may hold a room, but a bard shapes them into verse and song and carries a whole people's memory in them.

      Word Choice

      literary / historical

      Bard carries honor and age: use it for poets who sing a community's memory, or, capital-B, for Shakespeare. This unit's narrator tells a story aloud without composing it; a minstrel entertains for pay. Calling a modern songwriter a bard is pointed praise, not plain description.

      The night the Greenbrier broke its banks in 1937, half of Marrow's Bend washed away. What the town still holds of that night it owes to Ellis Vance, its bard: within a year he had set the flood into thirty verses, naming the lost families one by one. He was not paid, and he refused to perform for touring strangers. He taught the song to the schoolchildren, and they taught it to theirs. Folklorists who arrived decades later expecting a quaint jingle found something else: a sung record, accurate enough to check against the county's own death rolls. Ask anyone in Marrow's Bend who was lost in the flood, and they will not reach for a book. They will sing.
