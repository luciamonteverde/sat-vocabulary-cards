salient

/SAY-lee-uhnt/ - the stress falls on SAY

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Salient means most noticeable or most important — standing out from everything around it.

See the word in context

Forty slides into the safety briefing, only one salient fact followed everyone to the parking lot: the west stairwell floods.

The home inspector's report ran nineteen pages, from gutter pitch to attic vents, but its salient finding sat in the first line: the south foundation is sinking. The buyers forgot the gutters by dinner. They repeated the foundation sentence to each other for a week, and it alone decided the price.

Salient is the right word because that finding does two things at once: it leaps off the page, and everything else turns on it. Plenty of details are merely noticeable; the salient one is noticeable because it carries the most weight — the point a reader would be wrong to miss.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    prominent: standing out so it is easily seen — a prominent tower, a prominent family. Prominence is about position and visibility; salient adds weight: the salient point is the one the whole matter turns on.

    striking: seizing attention through sheer force or vividness — a striking image can stop you cold and still decide nothing. Salient marks what stands out because it matters, not merely because it dazzles.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    inconspicuous: blending into the background, drawing no eye

    negligible: too small to matter or to notice

    Where it sits · how insistently it claims your attention

    noticeable → salient → glaring

    noticeable vs. salient: Noticeable is weaker than salient: a noticeable detail catches the eye if you happen to look, while the salient one leaps out and organizes everything else around it.

    glaring vs. salient: Glaring is stronger than salient: a salient flaw stands out to a careful reader, but a glaring one is so blatant it cannot be missed — and usually embarrasses someone.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / analytical — essays, reports, test questions

    Salient is the analyst's word: the salient features of a proposal, the salient facts of a case. Keep it apart from merely relevant — a relevant fact bears on the question but can hide in a footnote, while the salient fact is the one that leaps off the page. And it is not a synonym for loud: a quiet number can be the most salient thing in a report if everything else depends on it.

The reports offered plenty to look at -- times, addresses, witness lists, the makes of cars. Most trainees fixated on the vivid details. Find that fact, she said, and the story writes itself; miss it, and no amount of colorful detail will save you.
