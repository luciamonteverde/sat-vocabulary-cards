impudent

/IM-pyuh-duhnt/ - the stress falls on IM

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impudent means boldly rude and disrespectful, without a trace of shame.

See the word in context

No one expected the intern to grade the director's own slides and hand them back marked 'B-minus' — an impudent stunt that somehow earned a laugh.

When the quiz-show host mispronounced 'quinoa,' the impudent contestant corrected her on air — then did it again in the lightning round, grinning wider each time.

Impudent goes past cheeky: the cheeky remark keeps a wink of affection, but the impudent one is shameless — im- ('not') + pudere ('to feel shame'), disrespect with the blush removed. Its cousin impertinent (this unit) stresses the overstep; impudent stresses the missing shame.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    brash: Brash means loudly overconfident — barging ahead, talking big, certain the room wants your opinion. It is pushy rather than disrespectful. Impudent aims at a person and cuts deeper: bold rudeness toward someone in particular, delivered without a flicker of shame.

    insolent: Insolent means openly contemptuous toward authority — the eye-roll at the officer, the drawled answer to the teacher. It overlaps with impudent, but insolence stresses the defiance of rank, while impudent stresses the missing shame: rudeness with the blush removed.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    respectful: minding rank and feeling — shame intact

    meek: so mild it wouldn't dream of talking back

    Where it sits · how little shame the boldness keeps

    cheeky → saucy → impudent

    cheeky vs. impudent: Cheeky is playful boldness that still means well — it stops far short of impudent, which has stopped caring how it lands.

    saucy vs. impudent: Saucy talk is bolder than cheeky but still winks at its own naughtiness; it stops short of impudent, which drops the shame altogether.

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Impudent is the word for disrespect delivered without a flicker of shame — bolder than cheeky, and blunter than impertinent, which marks the polite line being crossed (both are in this unit). Saucy and sassy are its playground cousins; impudent belongs in the headmaster's report.

    The food critic Adele Marsh had reviewed a thousand dinners, but the waiter at Table Nine was something new to her. He read her order back, paused, and told her — to her face, without a flicker of apology — that her palate was stuck in the last decade and her usual dish was beneath the kitchen. The room froze. Regulars knew Marsh could close a restaurant with a paragraph, and the man had just insulted her over the bread basket. Her column called the remark impudent: bold rudeness aimed at one particular person, delivered without a trace of shame. She spent only two sentences on the food. The owner apologized by phone the next morning. The waiter, unrepentant to the end, reportedly asked whether Marsh planned to tip.
