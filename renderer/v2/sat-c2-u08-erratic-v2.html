Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Erratic means following no regular pattern — uneven and unpredictable in behavior or movement.

See the word in context

Doctors ordered a monitor after her pulse turned erratic, sprinting and slowing with no exertion to explain it.

At the spring kite festival the wind turned erratic by noon: a long dead calm, then a gust out of the east that stood every kite on its tail, a shove from the north, calm again, two quick slaps from opposite quarters. Fliers who had handled strong wind all their lives came down shaking their heads — strength they could work with; this had no rhythm to learn.

Erratic is the precise word because the wind's behavior held no pattern even while it blew — not weak, not rare, just lawless from minute to minute. Sporadic wind would be something else entirely: a mostly still day broken by an occasional breeze, irregular only in when it shows up. The erratic is patternless in how it acts; the sporadic is only scattered in when it appears.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sporadic: Sporadic is about timing: sporadic events surface only now and then, with long gaps between — a few sightings a year, a cough that comes and goes. Erratic is about the behavior itself, uneven and lurching even while it is happening.

    haphazard: Haphazard points at the missing plan — a haphazard stack of boxes was thrown together with no care or system. Erratic describes the missing pattern in how something moves or performs; an erratic driver may be trying hard and swerving anyway.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    steady: holding one even, reliable rhythm

    consistent: the same in behavior and quality, time after time

    Where it sits · how far the behavior strays from any rhythm

    uneven → erratic → chaotic

    uneven vs. erratic: Uneven is milder than erratic: an uneven pace merely varies, a little fast here and a little slow there, while an erratic one lurches, stalls, and races with no rhythm anyone could learn.

    chaotic vs. erratic: Chaotic goes further than erratic: erratic behavior still comes one surprise at a time from a single source, but chaos is every part of a system misfiring at once, with no thread left to follow.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    negative / clinical

    Erratic is a chart-reader's word: it describes any motion, signal, or performance that follows no pattern — a heartbeat, a serve, a delivery schedule — and it passes no judgment on motive. Its unit-mate capricious reads a mind into the swings, blaming whim and fancy; a faulty sensor is erratic, never capricious. When the pattern is broken and no one chose the breaking, erratic is the exact word.
