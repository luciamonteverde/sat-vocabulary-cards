What It Means

Compunction means a pang of guilt about doing something wrong.

See the word in context

He returned the overdue books with real compunction, having loudly blamed the library's records all semester.

The intern shipped the update at midnight, knowing the release notes credited her alone. The code ran; nobody objected; her manager even forwarded congratulations. But the compunction arrived anyway, a small stone in her shoe: half those functions were Priya's, written the week before she left. By Friday she had asked the team lead to fix the credits.

Compunction is the exact word for that stone, because compunction means a pang of guilt about doing something wrong — a needle, not a wave. Remorse aches for years after real harm; compunction is the prick sharp enough to make you repair the thing while it is still small.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    qualm: A qualm is a flicker of conscience that arrives before the act — the uneasy pause with your hand already on the door. Compunction usually arrives during or after: the pang of guilt over a wrong already done, sharp enough to push you back to repair it.

    scruple: A scruple is the standing principle that balks — the fixed rule inside you that refuses certain acts, whether or not you feel anything. Compunction is the felt sting itself: the actual pang pricking you over one particular wrong. Scruples stop the act beforehand; compunction needles you afterward.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    shamelessness: no sting at all, ever

    indifference: not caring enough to feel one

    Where it sits · how deep and lasting the guilt runs

    twinge → compunction → remorse

    twinge vs. compunction: A twinge of conscience is far slighter than compunction — the faintest flicker, noticed, then gone by lunch, while compunction keeps pricking until the wrong is put right.

    remorse vs. compunction: Remorse goes further than compunction — compunction pricks over a wrong still small enough to fix; remorse aches for years over harm already done.

      Word Choice

      neutral / slightly formal

      Compunction is the small, useful guilt, and it loves the negative: without compunction, no compunction about. Remorse is the heavyweight, after real harm. Keep it apart from this unit's trepidation — compunction looks back at a wrong; trepidation looks ahead at a fear.
