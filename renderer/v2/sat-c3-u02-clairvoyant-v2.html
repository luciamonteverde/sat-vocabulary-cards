What It Means

Clairvoyant means able to perceive things that ordinary senses cannot reach.

See the word in context

In the radio drama, the innkeeper is clairvoyant, greeting each traveler by the hometown he has not yet mentioned.

In the novel's third chapter, the orchard keeper proves clairvoyant: she describes the hailstorm flattening her sister's farm ninety miles east as it happens, down to the broken weathervane, though no call or letter could have told her. When the sister arrives days later, every detail matches.

Clairvoyant is French for 'clear-seeing,' and it is more precise than 'psychic' because it names one specific power — perceiving what ordinary senses cannot reach — rather than supernatural ability in general. It is also not a compliment for good guessing: a forecaster who predicts rain from the radar is well informed, not clairvoyant.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    psychic: Psychic is the broad, everyday word for supernatural powers of any kind — reading minds, moving objects, sensing spirits. Clairvoyant is narrower and more precise: it names the one specific power of perceiving what lies beyond the reach of the ordinary senses. Every clairvoyant counts as psychic, but psychic promises no clear-seeing.

    prophetic: Prophetic looks forward: a prophetic vision or warning foretells what has not yet happened. Clairvoyant is about reach rather than time — perceiving what already exists but sits beyond the ordinary senses, like the contents of a room never entered. A dream can be prophetic without any clear-seeing of the present at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unobservant: missing even what is in plain view

    shortsighted: seeing no further than the moment in front of you

    Where it sits · how far perception reaches past the senses

    alert → observant → clairvoyant

    alert vs. clairvoyant: Alert means awake to what is happening in front of you; it stops far short of clairvoyant.

    observant vs. clairvoyant: Observant goes further than alert — catching details others miss — but everything it catches still reaches the ordinary senses, which is exactly where clairvoyant begins.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    literary / neutral

    Clairvoyant makes a strong, specific claim: perception that reaches past the senses entirely. Keep it for fiction, folklore, and claims of second sight; for someone who merely reads people or situations shrewdly, say sharp or well informed. Psychic is the broader, more everyday word for supernatural powers of any kind.

    In 1894 the Halloway Society invited the stage performer Vera Lin to prove her act was more than clever trickery. She refused, as always, to predict anyone's future, insisting her gift reached across distance, not ahead in time. Behind locked doors two floors above, the committee arranged seven objects on a table; Lin, seated in the cellar, listed all seven, ending with the cracked pocket watch. The newspapers announced a genuine clairvoyant; the Society recorded only that no explanation had yet been found. Either way, what Lin claimed was not prophecy but perception — a sight that traveled past walls to things already present.
