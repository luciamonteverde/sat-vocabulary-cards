satirical

/suh-TIR-ih-kuhl/

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Satirical means using humor, irony, or exaggeration to criticize or mock foolishness.

See the word in context

The late-night show's satirical sketch "thanked" smartphone makers for finally freeing families from the burden of dinner conversation.

The school paper's satirical review raved that the cafeteria's 'five-star tasting menu' offered 'a bold interplay of ketchup and mystery,' and every student understood the joke.

The over-the-top praise is not sincere—it uses humor and absurd overstatement to poke fun at the cafeteria food. Mockery delivered through comedy is what makes something satirical rather than plain criticism.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    mocking: Mocking covers any ridicule at all — a sneer, a cruel imitation, a scornful laugh — with no method required. Satirical ridicule works through humor and clever overstatement, and it aims at a real flaw worth exposing. All satire mocks; most mocking has none of satire's craft or purpose.

    tongue-in-cheek: Tongue-in-cheek describes playful insincerity — saying what you don't quite mean, with a wink, purely for fun. Satirical work borrows that wink and points it at a target: the humor exists to criticize a real foolishness. A tongue-in-cheek toast teases; a satirical sketch indicts.

    sardonic: Sardonic names a tone — scornful, biting, darkly amused — that can color a single remark and needs no purpose beyond itself. Satirical names a project: using humor, irony, or exaggeration to expose a real flaw. A sardonic aside mocks the moment; satirical work is built around its target.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    earnest: Earnest expression means every word sincerely, with no hidden mockery.

    reverent: Reverent treatment honors its subject rather than poking fun at it.

    Where it sits · how sharply the humor bites

    playful → satirical → scathing

    playful vs. satirical: Playful teasing amuses without any real target; satirical humor aims its jokes at a genuine flaw.

    scathing vs. satirical: Scathing criticism burns without needing jokes at all—harsher than satirical wit.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      critical, often playful

      Reach for satirical when humor carries a critique: cartoons, sketch shows, mock news stories. If a piece is funny but attacks nothing, it is merely comic; if it attacks without humor, it is plain criticism. Satire needs both the joke and the target.
