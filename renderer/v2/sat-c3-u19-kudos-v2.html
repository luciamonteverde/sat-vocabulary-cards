kudos

/KOO-dohss/ - the stress falls on KOO

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Kudos means praise and public credit given for a specific achievement.

See the word in context

The kudos for rescuing the payroll system landed on Tessa all week — sticky notes from accounting, a shout-out in the all-staff email, and a cake with a spreadsheet iced on top.

After the block's power came back in record time, kudos went where it was due: to the retired lineman who had walked the crews straight to the one burned splice everyone else had missed.

Kudos is credit made public — praise pinned to a particular achievement and a particular name. Notice it goes to the person who did the thing, for the thing: the splice was found, so the lineman gets the kudos. Vague good vibes aimed at everyone are not kudos; the word wants a name and a feat.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    recognition: Recognition is the acknowledgment itself — your work was seen — and it can be as dry as a line on a certificate. Kudos is recognition with warmth on it: praise offered openly, usually by name and with a little applause in the voice.

    props: Props is street-level credit — quick, casual respect ('props to the drummer'). Kudos does the same job one register up: still informal enough for a group email, but equally at home in print.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    flak: sharp criticism fired at someone

    derision: mocking, contemptuous ridicule

    Where it sits · how far the recognition travels

    compliment → kudos → renown

    compliment vs. kudos: A compliment is milder and smaller than kudos: one person's kind remark, offered once. Kudos is bigger and more public — credit announced to the room, pinned to something actually pulled off.

    renown vs. kudos: Renown is stronger and further-reaching than kudos: kudos is applause for one achievement and fades with the week, while renown is recognition that has spread far and settled in to stay.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    warm / conversational

    Kudos sits in the friendly middle of the praise register: warmer than recognition, lighter than its unit-mates plaudits and accolade, and perfectly at home in an email subject line. Two cautions earn their keep. Kudos is singular — kudos is due, never 'a kudo' — and it needs a feat: the word attaches to something done. For applause in print and ceremony, reach for plaudits or accolade; when the credit is quick, warm, and by name, kudos is the word.
