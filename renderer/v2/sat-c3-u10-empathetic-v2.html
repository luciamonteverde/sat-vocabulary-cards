Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Empathetic means understanding another person's feelings from the inside — sharing what they feel, not just noticing it.

See the word in context

The clinic's most empathetic nurse never said 'I know how you feel'; she asked what the pain was interrupting, and then she listened to the whole answer.

When the rookie's fumble lost the final, the veteran keeper's response was empathetic: he sat down beside him in the emptying stadium and talked, quietly, about the championship he himself had thrown away at nineteen.

Empathetic is more precise than 'kind' because kindness is about what you do, and empathy is about where you stand. The empathetic listener crosses over — feeling the loss from inside the other person's experience, often because they can remember standing there themselves. You can be kind from a distance; you cannot be empathetic from one.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    compassionate: Compassionate pairs concern with the urge to relieve suffering — it reaches for the bandage. Empathetic is about position rather than action: you are standing inside what the other person feels, whether or not there is anything you can do about it.

    understanding: Understanding grasps someone's situation and makes allowances for it — a fair-minded reading from the outside. Empathetic crosses the distance: not just seeing why she is overwhelmed, but feeling the overwhelm alongside her.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    callous: hardened to other people's feelings, unmoved by them

    detached: observing from a careful emotional distance

    Where it sits · how far inside another person's feelings you go

    considerate → sympathetic → empathetic

    considerate vs. empathetic: Considerate is the mildest rung, well below empathetic — it minds other people's comfort without ever needing to enter their feelings.

    sympathetic vs. empathetic: Sympathetic goes further than considerate — you genuinely feel for someone's trouble — yet it still watches from outside; empathetic crosses over and feels the trouble with them, from within.

      Word Choice

      warm / psychological

      Empathetic is the precise word for feeling with someone; reach for sympathetic when you feel for them from the outside, and compassionate when the feeling reaches for a remedy. This unit's solipsistic card names empathetic as its opposite for good reason — the empathetic mind is the one furthest from treating other people as scenery.

      Ms. Ochoa noticed that Danny had stopped raising his hand in October, around the time his family moved to the shelter across town. She did not begin with solutions. She remembered her own seventh-grade year of borrowed couches, and for a moment the boy's careful stillness was not a puzzle to solve but a weather she had lived in herself. Being empathetic did not mean fixing anything that afternoon; it meant knowing from the inside why he kept his backpack packed beside his desk. When she finally spoke to him, she didn't ask what was wrong. She said the moving year was the hardest one she ever had, and let that sit. Danny's hand came back up in December — slowly, the way anything does when it has been allowed its own time.
