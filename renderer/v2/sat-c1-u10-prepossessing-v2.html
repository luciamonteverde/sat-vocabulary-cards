prepossessing

/pree-puh-ZESS-ing/

Meaning

What It Means

Prepossessing means creating a favorable impression — attractive or appealing in appearance or manner, especially at first sight.

See the word in context

The bakery's prepossessing window display, with its rows of glossy fruit tarts, drew customers inside before they had read a single review.

The new intern had such a prepossessing manner — a warm smile, easy confidence, and genuine attention to everyone she met — that the whole office liked her by lunchtime.

Her smile, confidence, and attentiveness create an immediately favorable impression, which is exactly what prepossessing describes: winning people over at first encounter.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    appealing: Appealing means attractive and pleasing — an appealing menu, an appealing offer. It is the broad, all-purpose word and says nothing about when the attraction strikes. Prepossessing is specifically about the first encounter: a face, manner, or display that creates a favorable impression on sight, before you know anything more.

    charming: Charming means delighting people through grace and winning manner — a charming host keeps guests smiling all evening. Charm can work slowly and keep working. Prepossessing does its work up front: it names the quality that disposes people in your favor the moment they meet you, whatever comes after.

    engaging: Engaging means holding your attention and goodwill once you are involved — an engaging lecture keeps you listening. It describes the grip something has on you during the experience. Prepossessing acts earlier, winning favor at first sight, before any real involvement begins.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    off-putting: creating an unpleasant impression

    unappealing: not attractive or inviting

    Where it sits · how strongly it wins people over

    pleasant → prepossessing → captivating

    pleasant vs. prepossessing: Pleasant describes anything mildly agreeable; prepossessing is stronger, suggesting looks or manner that actively win people over the moment they meet you.

    captivating vs. prepossessing: Captivating goes further than prepossessing — it means holding someone completely spellbound, not just making a good first impression.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      Formal / slightly old-fashioned

      Prepossessing most often describes a person's appearance or manner, frequently in the phrases 'a prepossessing manner' or, in the negative, 'not very prepossessing.' It is about first impressions — use it when someone or something wins favor on sight, not after long acquaintance.
