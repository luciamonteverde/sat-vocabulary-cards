eclectic

/ih-KLEK-tik/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Eclectic means made up of a diverse variety of elements or styles drawn from many different sources.

See the word in context

Professor Nguyen's reading list was famously eclectic, pairing ancient poetry with detective stories, science essays, and graphic memoirs from around the world.

The new cafe's decor was proudly eclectic: mid-century chairs sat beside a carved Moroccan bench, vintage travel posters hung over a wall of modern photography, and no two lamps matched. Regulars said the mix of styles somehow felt exactly right.

The cafe deliberately draws its furnishings from many different styles, eras, and sources, which is what makes the decor eclectic.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    wide-ranging: Wide-ranging measures breadth: a wide-ranging survey or conversation covers many areas and topics. Eclectic is about where the pieces come from — an eclectic collection is deliberately drawn from many separate sources, styles, or traditions, chosen because the mix itself is the point.

    multifaceted: Multifaceted describes a single thing with many different sides or aspects, like a multifaceted problem or career. Eclectic describes a collection assembled from many separate sources and styles; the variety lies in where its pieces were drawn from, not in the facets of one thing.

    diverse: Diverse says a group contains many different kinds, and it describes the result no matter how it came about. Eclectic emphasizes the choosing: an eclectic mix was deliberately selected from many separate sources or traditions by someone's own taste.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    narrow: limited in range or scope

    uniform: all of the same kind throughout

    Where it sits · how widely its sources range

    varied → eclectic → encyclopedic

    varied vs. eclectic: A varied collection simply includes different kinds of things, while an eclectic one deliberately gathers its pieces from many separate sources and styles.

    encyclopedic vs. eclectic: An eclectic collector chooses favorites from many traditions, but an encyclopedic one tries to cover every tradition there is.

      Word Choice

      neutral, often admiring

      Eclectic praises a mix deliberately drawn from many sources or styles, as in 'eclectic taste' or 'an eclectic playlist.' It emphasizes the choosing, not just the mixing; a random pile of objects is merely a mixture, but a collection chosen thoughtfully from many traditions is eclectic.
