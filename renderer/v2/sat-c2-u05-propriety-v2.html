Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Propriety means conformity to accepted standards of behavior.

See the word in context

The judge reminded both lawyers that propriety in her courtroom included standing whenever they addressed the bench.

Uncle Ravi ran the funeral reception like a quiet conductor: phones silenced, plates never refilled before the elders were served, laughter kept low until the widow herself laughed first. It was not stiffness - it was propriety, behavior tuned exactly to what the occasion asked.

Propriety is not the same as politeness: politeness is warmth in the moment, while propriety is conformity to the occasion's standards - knowing what this setting calls for and doing precisely that. A joke can be perfectly polite at a picnic and a breach of propriety at a funeral.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    decorum: Decorum is dignified good form, especially in public ceremony — the hush and measured order of a state funeral. Propriety is wider and quieter: conformity to whatever standards this occasion sets, formal or not. A backyard barbecue has its propriety too; decorum would be overdressed there.

    etiquette: Etiquette is the codified rulebook of manners — named rules you could look up, from which fork to use to how one addresses a judge. Propriety is conduct that honors an occasion's standards even where no rule was ever written. You can memorize etiquette; propriety has to be sensed.

    seemliness: Seemliness is the quality of looking right for the occasion — an appearance that offends nobody's sense of fitness. Propriety goes past looks to conduct: actually behaving as the occasion's standards ask, not merely presenting a surface that seems correct.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    rudeness: open bad manners

    indecency: behavior that offends basic standards

    Where it sits · how strictly the rules of conduct bind

    politeness → propriety → prudishness

    politeness vs. propriety: Politeness is friendly good manners moment to moment; propriety goes further, meeting the occasion's whole standard of conduct.

    prudishness vs. propriety: Prudishness overshoots propriety: propriety honors the occasion's standards, while prudishness is scandalized by harmless things no standard forbids.

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Propriety is the word for conduct measured against an occasion's standards - weddings, courtrooms, state dinners. Say manners or politeness in daily life. Its neighbor in this unit, probity, judges inner honesty; propriety judges outward form - and watch the near-twin property, which shares the root but means what you own.

    Nothing prepared Lena for the harvest supper in her host village — no guidebook mentioned it, and her phrasebook was useless. There were standards, she sensed at once, though none were posted. The oldest ate first, but only after pretending twice to refuse. Praise for the food was expected; praise for the house was not. Her host sister coached her in whispers: bring nothing the first year, bring bread the second. Never leave before the singing, never sing the first verse. By the third autumn Lena moved through the evening without coaching, and a neighbor paid her the village's quiet compliment: she has a feel for propriety — she honors standards nobody ever wrote down. Asked later to list the rules, Lena laughed and said the list was the one thing the supper would never allow.
