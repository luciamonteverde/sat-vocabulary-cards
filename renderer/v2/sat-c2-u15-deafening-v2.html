Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Deafening means so overwhelmingly loud that, for the moment, nothing else can be heard.

See the word in context

The jets passed low over the parade route, and for three deafening seconds every conversation along the avenue simply stopped.

The mill's stamping press ran day and night, and new hires learned its lesson within an hour: on the floor, the noise was deafening. Foremen gave orders in hand signals; the lunch whistle was a light bulb, not a whistle, because no whistle could climb over the machines. Workers who wanted to talk stepped into the stairwell and pulled the door shut — and even there, they shouted.

Deafening is the exact word for the press floor because the noise does not merely annoy — it swallows hearing itself, so completely that whistles, warnings, and voices all disappear into it. Loud describes any strong sound; deafening claims the total kind, the noise that leaves your ears nothing else.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    ear-splitting: Ear-splitting is about pain — a shriek or alarm so sharp it feels like a blade in the ear. Deafening is about totality: the sound may not even hurt, but it swallows every other sound whole.

    roaring: Roaring describes the texture of big noise — the continuous, engulfing rush of a crowd or a waterfall. Deafening states the effect: whatever the texture, nothing else can be heard through it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    faint: so soft it can barely be heard

    hushed: deliberately quieted, kept low

    Where it sits · how completely the noise takes over

    noisy → thunderous → deafening

    noisy vs. deafening: Noisy is far milder than deafening: a noisy cafe makes you raise your voice, while a deafening one would erase your voice altogether.

    thunderous vs. deafening: Thunderous goes further than noisy — a deep boom that fills the whole air; deafening claims the most: noise so total that hearing shuts down for everything else.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      vivid, journalistic — reaches easily into figurative use

      Deafening is the loudness ladder's ceiling, and writers borrow it for one famous figure: the deafening silence, a quiet so pointed it drowns out everything the way noise would — the word still means 'nothing else gets through.' Keep it off small annoyances and out of the other senses: a glaring color or an overpowering flavor is loud only by metaphor, and neither can deafen. And keep it aimed at the noise, not the hearer — a person losing hearing is going deaf, not growing deafening.
