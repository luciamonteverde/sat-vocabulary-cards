Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Signify means to be a sign of something — to stand for a meaning or make it known, often through a signal people have agreed to read the same way.

See the word in context

At the racetrack, a waved yellow flag signifies caution: hold your position, no passing, until the wreck ahead is cleared.

The linguistics professor opened the term with a dollar bill and a maple leaf: neither object resembles what it means, she said, yet one signifies value and the other signifies Canada, because communities have agreed to read them that way. By the end of the hour the class was listing everything on campus that signified something — the bells, the robes, the unlocked quad gate.

To signify is to carry meaning for those who know the code. The sign can be arbitrary — nothing about the color green means 'go' — but once the convention holds, the signal speaks by itself. Carrying a standing, agreed meaning is what separates signifying from merely causing something or resembling it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    indicate: Indicate is the everyday, instrumental verb for pointing something out or showing it — a gauge indicates speed and is done. Signify runs deeper: the sign carries a standing meaning, often by convention, the way a handshake signifies agreement.

    denote: Denote is the technical twin: a symbol denotes exactly what it is defined to mean and nothing more — the skull on the label denotes poison. Signify is broader and warmer, covering everything a gesture, mark, or event can mean to the people who read it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    conceal: to keep a meaning or fact from being seen at all

    mask: to cover a signal so that it reads as something else

    Where it sits · how strongly the sign announces its meaning

    hint → signify → proclaim

    hint vs. signify: To hint is weaker than to signify: a hint gestures sideways at a meaning and can be missed or denied, while a sign that signifies carries its meaning plainly to everyone who knows the code.

    proclaim vs. signify: Proclaim is stronger than signify: what a symbol quietly signifies to readers of the code, a proclamation announces outright — loudly, publicly, and to everyone at once.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / formal

      Signify is the workhorse for meaning-carrying: gestures, symbols, and events all signify. Indicate is lighter and instrumental; denote is the logician's exact term; symbolize implies resemblance or association doing the work; portend and foreshadow, this unit's forward-looker, point specifically at what is to come, while signify marks a present meaning. An older sense — 'it signifies little,' meaning it hardly matters — survives mostly in period fiction. On the SAT you will often meet the word as 'signifies': same verb, third person.
