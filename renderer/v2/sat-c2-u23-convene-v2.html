convene

/kuhn-VEEN/ - rhymes with between

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Convene means to call a group together for a meeting.

See the word in context

When frost took half the seedlings, the co-op president convened an emergency growers' meeting in the seed barn.

The comet finding was eleven hours old when the observatory director convened a working group: four astronomers, one software engineer, and a pot of coffee that never left the table.

Convene is the officeholder's verb: someone with standing calls a body into session, and the gathering exists because of that call. To invite is to ask; to host is to hold the room; to convene is to bring the meeting itself into being.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    gather: Gather is the plain, all-purpose verb for coming or bringing together — friends, firewood, a crowd — with no authority implied on either side. Convene is official: someone with standing calls a body into session, and the meeting exists because that call was made.

    assemble: Assemble means to bring people or parts together into one working whole — a cast, a crew, volunteers on a Saturday. It needs no office behind it; anyone can assemble a team. To convene is narrower: officially calling a deliberative body into session in the first place.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    adjourn: to close the session and send everyone away

    disband: to break the group up for good

    Where it sits · how binding the call is

    invite → convene → summon

    invite vs. convene: Invite is gentler than convene: an invitation asks and waits while every guest is free to say no, but when a chair convenes a committee, the meeting is officially called and members are expected.

    summon vs. convene: Summon goes further than convene — a convened panel is called into session; a summoned witness must appear, ready or not, or answer for the failure.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / procedural

    Convene is for official calls to order — boards, panels, courts, task forces — and it points at the caller's authority. Gather is neutral; summon compels a person rather than a body. Its unit-mate caucus is what like-minded members do privately before the body you convene ever votes.
