Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Accretion means slow growth in which each new layer bonds onto the whole.

See the word in context

Core samples show the glacier grew by accretion, each winter's snowfall pressed into one more thin band of ice atop the last.

The cave guide aims her lamp at a stalactite the size of a man: water seeps, each drop leaves a mineral film thinner than paper, and a meter of stone takes ten thousand years. Nothing about it ever looks like growth while you watch — the accretion shows only when you compare centuries.

Accretion is the exact word because it names slow growth in size or amount, addition by addition — not a spurt and not a purchase. Each new layer fuses onto the whole, which is what separates accretion from a mere pile: the stone doesn't collect the drips, it becomes them.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    buildup: A buildup is any gathering over time — traffic, dust, tension — and the word says nothing about the pieces joining. Accretion makes the stronger claim: each new layer bonds onto the whole, so the thing itself grows. Every accretion is a buildup, but most buildups stay a loose crowd of parts.

    expansion: Expansion is growth outward in size, scope, or reach, and nothing new has to be added for it — a gas expands, an empire expands. Accretion is the opposite mechanism: the thing grows only because new material keeps arriving and bonding on, addition by addition.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    erosion: slow wearing away, grain by grain — accretion run backward

    shrinkage: loss of size from any cause, fast or slow

    Where it sits · how firmly each addition joins the whole

    increase → accumulation → accretion

    increase vs. accretion: An increase is any gain at all — a jump, a spike, a windfall; accretion is the patient kind, growth so slow you only see it by looking back.

    accumulation vs. accretion: An accumulation piles things up but leaves them a pile; accretion goes further — each addition bonds to the body of the thing, the way drip after drip becomes solid stone.

      formal / technical

      Reach for accretion when growth is slow, layered, and additive — reefs, glaciers, case law, a collection nobody planned. For everyday gains, growth or increase is plainer. And keep it apart from this unit's stagnation: stagnation is no growth at all, while accretion is growth too patient to watch.
