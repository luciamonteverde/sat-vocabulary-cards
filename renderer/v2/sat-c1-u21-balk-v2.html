balk

/BAWK/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Balk means to stop abruptly and refuse to continue, especially at an obstacle.

See the word in context

The colt balked at the trailer ramp no matter how gently the trainer coaxed.

The moving crew worked quickly through the whole apartment. Boxes, couch, bookshelves — everything went down the stairs without complaint. Then they reached the antique piano and saw the narrow, twisting stairwell. The crew chief balked, setting down his straps and refusing to attempt the turn without a hoist and two more workers. No amount of pleading from the owner would get him to take another step with the piano until the equipment arrived.

The crew chief stops abruptly at a specific obstacle — the twisting stairwell — and refuses to continue until conditions change, which is precisely what it means to balk.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    recoil: Recoil means to spring or jerk back from something in a sharp flinch of alarm or disgust - you recoil from a snake or from a gruesome photograph. Balk is a refusal to proceed: at the obstacle, you stop and will not take the next step. Recoiling moves you backward; balking roots you where you stand.

    refuse: Refuse means to say no to a request or command - the general word for declining anything, anywhere, for any reason. Balk is a specific way of refusing: stopping short at an obstacle, cost, or demand, usually at the very moment you meet it. Every balk is a refusal; most refusals are not balks.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    proceed: to go forward

    comply: to go along with a request

    Where it sits · how forcefully the person resists

    waver → balk → rebel

    waver vs. balk: Waver is weaker — a wavering person merely goes back and forth, while someone who balks plants their feet and flatly refuses to go on.

    rebel vs. balk: Rebel is stronger than balk: balking is refusing to take the next step, but rebelling is actively fighting back against the whole plan.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral; common in journalism ('balked at the price')

      Balk almost always takes 'at': people balk at a cost, a risk, or a demand. It describes the moment of stopping short, not a long campaign of resistance — a committee balks at a price tag in the meeting itself.

      The trail guide had warned them: the gorge crossing turns hikers around more often than the summit does. Marcus had crossed streams, scree, and one rockslide without a word of complaint. At the rope bridge he balked. It was not a flinch, and it was not fear of heights, exactly. He simply stopped at the first plank and would not take the step, while the group waited behind him. The guide did not argue. She had seen it before: a specific obstacle, met at a specific moment, closing a person's legs down like a switch. Ten minutes later Marcus crossed — on his own schedule, after the stop had run its course — and finished the hike without another pause.
