impartial

/im-PAR-shul/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impartial means not favoring one side over another.

See the word in context

The dispute was settled by an impartial mediator who had no ties to either family.

When two star players filed complaints against each other, the league brought in an impartial arbiter from another city who had never met either one. She declined dinner invitations from both agents, reviewed the same footage the same number of times for each side, and released a decision that quoted only the rulebook. Even the losing player's coach admitted he could not point to a single sign of favoritism.

The arbiter is impartial because she favors neither side and bases her judgment only on the evidence and the rules.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unbiased: Unbiased means free from personal preference or slant, and it describes someone actually weighing or reporting something — a juror, a referee, a news story. Impartial is its close partner for decision-makers who treat both sides with equal fairness. You can only be unbiased about a judgment you are actually making; impartial likewise presumes you are judging.

    objective: Objective means based on facts rather than feelings — an objective review lets the evidence decide. Impartial is about sides rather than sources: an impartial judge favors neither party, whatever the evidence says. A critic can be objective about a lone film with no contest in sight, while impartiality matters exactly when two sides face off.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    biased: unfairly favoring one side

    prejudiced: judging beforehand without fair grounds

    Where it sits · how strongly fairness resists being swayed

    neutral → impartial → incorruptible

    neutral vs. impartial: A neutral observer simply takes no side, while an impartial judge actively weighs both sides with equal fairness.

    incorruptible vs. impartial: An impartial referee favors no one today, while an incorruptible one could never be swayed by any bribe or pressure.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral to formal register

    Use impartial for the people and processes that must decide something fairly: judges, referees, juries, and investigations. Impartial describes an unbiased decision-maker, unlike equitable, which describes a genuinely fair outcome or distribution, or parity, which measures whether two things stand at the same level.
