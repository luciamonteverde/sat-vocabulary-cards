fickle

/FIK-uhl/ - rhymes with pickle

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Fickle means changing often and without warning.

See the word in context

He was a fickle collector, obsessed with stamps in June, coins by August, and telescopes before the first frost.

Their manager was fickle about the schedule: the Tuesday shift she demanded all winter was suddenly wrong in April, and the plan that replaced it lasted eleven days.

Fickle is for changeability you cannot bank on, and it always carries a hint of blame — a fickle person or thing lets you down. A sky can be changeable without offense; call it fickle and you are filing a complaint.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    flighty: Flighty describes attention so scattered and impulsive that nothing gets settled on at all — flitting off before any choice is made. Fickle is about choices that do get made and then abandoned: a fickle diner had a favorite dish and dropped it; a flighty one never ordered.

    inconstant: Inconstant is the formal, older word for failing to stay faithful, at home in poetry and vows. Fickle is its everyday, sharper-tongued cousin: both describe loyalty that will not hold, but fickle carries the modern sting of a complaint you might actually say aloud.

    capricious: Capricious behavior is governed by whim — sudden impulses that arrive from nowhere and rule the moment, as a capricious judge sentences by mood. Fickle points more narrowly at loyalty and preference that keep switching; capricious covers any decision, rule, or act driven by impulse.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    steadfast: holding firmly to the same loyalty or course

    unwavering: never once bending or flickering

    Where it sits · how deeply the trust is broken

    changeable → fickle → treacherous

    changeable vs. fickle: Changeable is milder than fickle — changeable simply reports that something varies, while fickle adds the sting of broken trust.

    treacherous vs. fickle: Treacherous is stronger than fickle — a fickle ally merely drifts away, but a treacherous one turns and does you harm.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      informal / mildly reproachful

      Fickle suits everyday complaints about loyalty that will not hold — fans, appetites, luck, spring skies. It always points a finger; if you want the fact of variation without the accusation, say changeable, and if the change is a measured rise and fall or a swing between two poles, this unit's fluctuate or oscillate is the better tool.
