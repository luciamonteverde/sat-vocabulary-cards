perilous

/PAIR-uh-luss/

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Perilous means full of danger; exposing people to serious risk of harm.

See the word in context

Early fur traders made a perilous crossing over the half-frozen river, testing each step as the ice groaned beneath them.

The window-washing crew worked the perilous fiftieth-floor scaffold in gusting winds, re-clipping their harnesses one buckle at a time as the platform swayed beneath them.

Every detail, the extreme height, the gusting wind, the swaying platform, points to a serious risk of injury or death. A task surrounded by that much danger is perilous.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    hazardous: Hazardous means posing risks to health or safety, and it is the technical, warning-label word: hazardous materials, hazardous road conditions. It flags risk coolly and factually. Perilous is dramatic, describing danger that is serious and close, the kind a sailor feels rounding a storm cape.

    treacherous: Treacherous danger hides and betrays: footing that looks firm and gives way, a calm current that pulls you under. Deception is the heart of the word. Perilous danger can be perfectly open: a perilous climb announces its threat, while a treacherous one disguises it.

    unsafe: Unsafe means likely to cause harm, a flat, factual label for anything below the safety bar, from a loose railing to a recalled toy; it carries no drama and no scale. Perilous is reserved for serious danger, real risk to life and limb, vividly felt.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    safe: free from danger or harm

    secure: protected against threat or loss

    Where it sits · how severe the threatened harm is

    risky → perilous → deadly

    risky vs. perilous: A risky choice carries some chance of harm, but a perilous one places you in serious, immediate danger.

    deadly vs. perilous: A perilous route threatens your safety, but a deadly one has already proven that it kills.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      Slightly formal / literary

      Often paired with journeys and undertakings: 'a perilous journey,' 'a perilous crossing,' 'a perilous climb.' Perilous is about danger and risk, not about failure; a perilous plan might still succeed. Distinguish it from 'precarious,' which emphasizes instability and uncertainty rather than outright danger.
