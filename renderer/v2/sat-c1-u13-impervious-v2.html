impervious

/im-PUR-vee-us/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impervious means impossible to penetrate, damage, or affect, usually by one specific thing, and it is most often followed by the word to.

See the word in context

The bank's vault door is impervious to drills and cutting torches, which is why its basic design has not changed in forty years.

During the championship debate, hecklers in the back row shouted through Amara's entire speech, but she was impervious to the noise and never once lost her place.

The shouting was meant to rattle Amara, yet it had no effect on her at all. Being unable to be affected or penetrated by something, in this case the heckling, is the core meaning of impervious.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    impenetrable: Impenetrable describes the barrier itself — a wall, a vault, a defense that nothing can find a way through. Impervious describes how something stands toward one specific force, and usually takes to: impervious to water, impervious to criticism. Armor is impenetrable; the knight inside is impervious to arrows.

    unaffected: Unaffected simply reports an outcome — something happened and left no mark this time. Impervious makes a stronger, standing claim: the thing cannot be penetrated or affected by that force at all. A speaker might be unaffected by one heckler yet not truly impervious to heckling.

    immune: Immune means protected by one's own makeup, the way a vaccinated body is immune to a virus, and it also takes to, as in immune to flattery. Immune stresses built-in protection; impervious stresses that the force simply cannot penetrate or touch the thing at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    vulnerable: open to harm or attack

    permeable: allowing liquids or gases to pass through

    Where it sits · how completely harm is kept out

    resistant → impervious → invulnerable

    resistant vs. impervious: Resistant is weaker than impervious; a resistant surface holds up against damage for a while, but an impervious one is not affected at all.

    invulnerable vs. impervious: Invulnerable is stronger than impervious; impervious usually means unaffected by one specific thing, while invulnerable means impossible to harm in any way at all.
