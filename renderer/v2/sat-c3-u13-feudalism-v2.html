Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Feudalism means a medieval system in which land was held from a lord in exchange for service and loyalty.

See the word in context

The seminar's first slide reduced feudalism to one line: land flows down, loyalty and service flow up.

Feudalism in the shogun's Japan ran on the same engine as Europe's: a daimyo held his province from the shogun, samurai held stipends from the daimyo, and every rank owed the one above it soldiers in war and rice in peace.

Feudalism names a whole system, not a single custom — land tenure, sworn loyalty, and inherited duty bundled together. Used of a modern office ('pure feudalism in accounting'), it is a jab: favors flowing up, patronage trickling down, and no way out by merit.

    Section 2

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    vassalage: Vassalage is the bond of service itself — one man kneeling to one lord, owing loyalty in exchange for land or protection. Feudalism is the whole system built from thousands of such bonds, stacked rank on rank from crown to plowman, with land tenure and inherited duty bundled in.

    manorialism: Manorialism is the economic half — the manor, its fields, and the peasants who work them for their lord. Feudalism covers far more: the entire chain of loyalty and landholding running from the crown down through every rank, of which each manor is only one small link.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    meritocracy: rank earned by proven ability, not birth or land

    mobility: the freedom to rise, fall, or simply leave

    Where it sits · how tightly the system binds its people

    hierarchy → feudalism → serfdom

    hierarchy vs. feudalism: A hierarchy is the milder arrangement — ranks above and below, as any office has; feudalism binds far tighter, bundling land, loyalty, and livelihood into one inherited package.

    serfdom vs. feudalism: Serfdom is harsher than feudalism as a whole: the serf at the system's floor could not lawfully leave the land, while a knight within feudalism held real rights beside his duties.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    historical / academic

    Feudalism names the specific medieval bundle — land from a lord, service and loyalty back, generation after generation. Historians use it precisely; modern speakers borrow it as a jab at any workplace run on favors and fealty. Both uses work, but know which one you are making.
