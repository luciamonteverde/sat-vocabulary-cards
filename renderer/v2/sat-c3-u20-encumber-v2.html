encumber

/en-KUM-ber/ - rhymes with lumber

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Encumber means to weigh down with a load that hampers movement or freedom.

See the word in context

Decades of borrowing encumbered the railway with debts that no fare increase could outrun.

The costume shop's prize peacock gown encumbers whoever wears it: a wired ten-foot tail, forty pounds of sequined skirt, and wings that snag on every doorframe, so the actress needs two dressers just to reach the stage.

Encumber is heavier than 'carry': whatever encumbers you actively gets in your way — a load, a debt, a duty. Lawyers even speak of an encumbered estate, property so tied up in claims that its owner cannot freely sell it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    burden: A burden is the plain, often emotional word for a heavy load someone must carry — worries, costs, responsibilities. Encumber is more active in feel: whatever encumbers you tangles your movement or freedom, hampering each step rather than simply weighing on you.

    saddle: To saddle someone is to dump a duty, debt, or chore on them in one stroke — you are saddled with the paperwork the moment it lands. Encumber describes the ongoing drag of the load itself, hampering movement or progress long after it arrives.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unburden: to lift the load off — shoulders or conscience alike

    relieve: to take away what was weighing or worrying

    Where it sits · how much movement it takes away

    inconvenience → encumber → immobilize

    inconvenience vs. encumber: An inconvenience is a pebble in the shoe — mildly bothersome; it stops far short of encumber.

    immobilize vs. encumber: Immobilize goes further than encumber — an encumbered mover still inches forward; an immobilized one cannot move at all.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / formal

      Encumber when the load — physical, legal, or financial — actively hampers movement or freedom: encumbered by gear, by debt, by regulations. Burden is the plainer, more emotional cousin; weigh down is the everyday phrasing. Only encumber carries the lawyer's sense of property tied up in claims.
