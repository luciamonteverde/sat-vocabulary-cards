maverick

/MAV-er-ik/ - the stress falls on MAV

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Maverick means an independent person who refuses to go along with the group.

See the word in context

The orchard co-op set one spraying schedule for all its growers, but its lone maverick kept his own calendar and sold his apples from a hand-painted truck.

Every buyer at the shoe company works from the same trend forecast — except Imani, who ignores it. She stocks what she likes, argues with the committee in every meeting, and has twice been nearly fired for it. Twice, too, her oddball picks have carried the fall season. The chairman introduces her, with a sigh, as 'our maverick.'

Maverick is more particular than 'individualist': an individualist quietly goes her own way, while a maverick refuses, out loud, to run with the group — the herd is right there, and she can see it, and she will not join it. The word admires and warns in the same breath.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    freethinker: A freethinker forms opinions independently of doctrine, especially in matters of belief — the independence lives in the thinking. A maverick's independence is public conduct: refusing, out loud, to run with the group while the group watches. A freethinker may reach unusual conclusions quietly; a maverick makes the refusal visible.

    contrarian: A contrarian takes the opposite side almost on principle — if the group says yes, the contrarian says no. A maverick does not need the group to be wrong: she keeps her own course whether or not anyone argues. Opposition is the contrarian's habit; independence is the maverick's.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    follower: goes wherever the group is already going

    joiner: signs up for every club and committee going

    Where it sits · how open the break with the group

    individualist → maverick → renegade

    individualist vs. maverick: An individualist quietly goes her own way without defying anyone, stopping short of a maverick's open refusal to run with the group.

    renegade vs. maverick: A renegade goes further than a maverick — not just refusing to follow the group, but deserting it and turning against it.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      informal to neutral / admiring with an edge

      Maverick praises independence with a raised eyebrow — newsrooms love it for politicians and coaches who buck their own side. In this unit, mind its neighbors: an iconoclast attacks what the group reveres, while a maverick simply will not run with the herd, and heterodox describes the beliefs rather than the person. For quiet self-direction without any defiance, individualist is the fairer label.
