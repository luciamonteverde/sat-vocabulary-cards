Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Corporatism means control of policy or society by organized interest groups, especially large corporations.

See the word in context

Historians describe the old guild republic as early corporatism: the weavers' and bankers' associations cast votes in the senate itself.

Look at who signs the fishing quotas and you are looking at corporatism: on the nine-member board that sets each season's limits, the trawler owners' association holds three seats by statute, and its staff writes the first draft of every rule the ministry later announces. The small day-boat crews, who own no association, read the finished quotas in the newspaper like everyone else.

Corporatism is the exact word for that arrangement, because corporatism means control of policy or society by organized interest groups, especially large corporations — the mark is the seat at the table. A lobbyist waits in the hallway and hopes; under corporatism the industry sits on the board, pen in hand, an organ of the very government it once had to persuade.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    cronyism: Cronyism steers favors to friends informally — a contract slipped to an old classmate, nothing written down. Corporatism is formal: under corporatism, organized groups hold actual seats and votes inside government, their power printed in the procedure itself. Cronyism works off the books; corporatism is the book.

    insider rule: Insider rule is the blunt everyday phrase for governance captured by the very interests it is supposed to referee. Corporatism is the analyst's precise term for one version of it: organized industry groups seated formally inside government, drafting and voting on the rules that bind everyone else.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    fairness: treating every group the same, favoring none

    independence: government kept free of the industries it referees

    Where it sits · how much power private interests hold

    lobbying → corporatism → oligarchy

    lobbying vs. corporatism: Lobbying is a much weaker grip than corporatism — lobbyists argue their case from the hallway, and the government can still shut the door; corporatist groups sit inside with a pen and a vote.

    oligarchy vs. corporatism: Oligarchy goes further than corporatism — corporatist groups share the table with the state; an oligarchy's few interests own the table outright.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / political

    Corporatism is analysis-and-editorial vocabulary, and the test is formal standing: if organized industries hold actual seats, pens, or votes inside government, corporatism is the word. Cronyism is the informal cousin — favors without the furniture. Its unit-mates map the rest of the field: mercantilism is the state running trade, laissez-faire the state stepping back, consumerism the households buying.
