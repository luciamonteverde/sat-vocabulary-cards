engender

/en-JEN-der/ - rhymes with blender

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Engender means to give rise to a feeling, bond, or condition.

See the word in context

Nothing engenders loyalty in a kitchen crew like a chef who scrubs pots beside them on the worst nights.

When the false rumor of a bank failure reached the market town, it engendered a panic no official denial could catch: by dawn a silent queue wrapped the square, and by noon the tellers' drawers were empty.

Engender is for what grows in people — trust, resentment, hope, panic — brought into being by something that happened. You cause a delay and create a product, but you engender a feeling or a climate; the word remembers its root: to beget, to bring to birth.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    spark: To spark something is to set it off in an instant — one match dropped in dry grass, and the blaze is suddenly alive. Engender works at the speed of a season: what a policy engenders — trust, resentment, hope — gathers gradually in people rather than detonating.

    breed: To breed a feeling is to produce it steadily over long stretches of time, the way neglect breeds mistrust in a workplace. Engender is quicker and more bookish, but both are growth words: something engendered still takes root in people rather than appearing in an instant.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    squelch: to stamp a growing feeling out before it spreads

    extinguish: to put it out entirely, like a doused flame

    Where it sits · how actively it produces the result

    invite → engender → ignite

    invite vs. engender: Invite merely leaves the door open — conditions that invite gossip stop short of engendering it.

    ignite vs. engender: Ignite goes further than engender — what engender raises gradually, ignite sets blazing all at once.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / analytical

      Engender belongs in analysis — editorials, histories, board reports — wherever one thing gives rise to a mood or condition: reforms engender trust, shortages engender resentment. For concrete outcomes use cause or produce; for a sudden flare, spark. If nothing is growing in anyone, engender is the wrong tool.
