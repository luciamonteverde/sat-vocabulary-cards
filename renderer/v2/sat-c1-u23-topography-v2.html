topography

/tuh-POG-ruh-fee/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Topography means the arrangement of an area's physical features, especially its elevation and shape.

See the word in context

The valley's topography funnels every storm into one narrow gap, which is why the town at the mouth floods.

Two neighbouring farms receive the same rainfall and grow the same wheat, yet one loses topsoil every spring and the other does not. A surveyor walking both fields explains the difference in an afternoon: the first farm's ground tilts eight degrees toward a single gully, while the second's lies almost flat behind a low ridge. Nothing about the soil or the seed differs between them. The whole difference is topography.

With the same soil, the same seed, and the same rain, the only difference between the farms is the slope and shape of the ground, which is exactly what topography describes.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    terrain: Terrain is the ground considered as something to move across -- soldiers and hikers judge terrain rough or easy going. Topography is the ground considered as something to measure and map: the arrangement of an area's elevations and shapes, whether or not anyone crosses it.

    lie of the land: The lie of the land is the plain-spoken phrase for how ground rises, falls, and drains -- what a farmer reads on an afternoon's walk. Topography is the surveyor's term for the same thing set down precisely: an area's shape and elevation, measured and mapped.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    flatness: an absence of variation in elevation

    uniformity: sameness, with no distinguishing surface features

    Where it sits · how fully it maps a landscape's shape

    layout → relief → topography

    layout vs. topography: Layout is a loose word for how anything is arranged, while topography is specifically the arrangement of a landscape's physical surface.

    relief vs. topography: Relief is narrower and more technical than topography, naming elevation differences alone, while topography takes in ridges, valleys, rivers, and slopes together.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      technical and geographic

      Topography names the ground itself and normally takes 'the ... of': the topography of the basin, rather than 'a topography.' It covers shape and elevation only, since climate, soil, and plant life are separate matters, and mountainous country is one kind of topography rather than a synonym for the word.

      When Linville hired its first bicycle planner, she did not begin with traffic counts. She began with a contour map, because the city's topography would decide everything else. Linville sits in a bowl. Three steep ridges wall in the eastern neighborhoods, and a single low saddle carries every road that leaves town to the west. On paper, the shortest route from the mill district to downtown ran straight over the highest ridge. Legs, unlike lines on paper, must climb. So the planner traced the valley floors instead, letting her routes follow the flat ground beside the creeks, adding distance but sparing riders the steepest grades. Her final network looked nothing like the road grid above it. It looked like the land itself: a map of where the hills allowed people to go.
