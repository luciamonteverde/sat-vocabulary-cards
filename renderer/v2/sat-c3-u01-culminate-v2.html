culminate

/KUHL-muh-nayt/ - the stress falls on KUHL

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Culminate means to reach a final and highest point at the end of a process.

See the word in context

Years of quiet fundraising culminated in a single afternoon when the library paid off its mortgage and rang the bell on the desk twice.

A stonemason's apprenticeship at the cathedral workshop runs seven years and culminates in the carving of a masterpiece: one block, chosen by the guild, worked without help while the masters watch in silence. Nothing comes after it but the verdict. Every earlier year — the sharpening, the lettering, the endless practice moldings — exists to point at that block.

Culminate is exact here because the process reaches its final and highest point at once. A process that merely stops has ended, not culminated; a process whose best moment came in year three peaked early. Culminate promises the summit and the finish line are the same place.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    climax: To climax is to hit the moment of greatest intensity, and the word belongs to drama and feeling — a thriller, a symphony, a shouting match. Culminate makes a cooler, structural claim: a process reaches its highest point exactly where it ends. Intensity is climax's business; shape is culminate's.

    crest: To crest is to reach the top and begin easing off — a wave crests, a fever crests, and things keep moving afterward. To culminate is stricter: the height must arrive exactly where the process ends. Cresting can happen anywhere along the way; culminating happens only at the finish.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    begin: to start the process off

    fizzle: to end weakly, with no height at all

    Where it sits · how final the high point is

    build → peak → culminate

    build vs. culminate: Build is milder than culminate — excitement or pressure builds long before anything arrives, and building promises nothing about where the rise ends; to culminate is to reach the top at the very end.

    peak vs. culminate: Peak stops short of culminate: a season can peak on its middle weekend and keep running, while to culminate is to arrive at the height exactly where the whole run ends.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / neutral

    Culminate belongs to processes with a shape — campaigns, seasons, apprenticeships — and insists the ending is also the top. Use peak when the high point may come mid-run, and plain end when there is no height to claim. Its mirror in this unit is originate: originate names where a line first comes into being; culminate names where it arrives.

The expedition culminated on the ridge at dawn on May 9th, when she stood on the summit with nothing above her and nothing left undone. She distrusted the word at first -- it sounded grand -- until a climbing partner defended it as simple geometry: the highest point, reached last. Descent began twenty minutes later, but the descent belonged to a different story, the one about getting home.
