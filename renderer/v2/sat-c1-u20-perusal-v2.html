perusal

/puh-ROO-zul/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Perusal means a careful, thorough reading or examination of something.

See the word in context

A slow perusal of the treaty's footnotes turned up a clause the negotiators had overlooked for months.

An archivist received a water-stained diary donated by a farming family. She spent four afternoons on it, comparing handwriting across entries, checking dates against county records, and reading every cramped margin note twice. Only on the last page did she notice a name that tied the diary to a famous 1848 land dispute. That discovery came out of a genuine perusal, not a quick flip through the pages.

The archivist's slow, line-by-line study, deliberately contrasted with a quick flip through the pages, is exactly what a perusal is.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    examination: Examination is a close look at anything at all - an engine, a patient, an insurance claim. Perusal is specifically a careful, complete read of a text, moving through the pages line by line. A mechanic performs an examination; only a reader performs a perusal.

    scrutiny: Scrutiny is close and searching attention to detail - the narrowed eye a customs officer turns on a passport. It names the intense looking itself, and it stops at looking. Perusal is that same care applied to reading a text through. A guard gives a badge scrutiny in five seconds; a lawyer gives a contract a perusal over an afternoon.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    glance: a quick, brief look

    skimming: reading rapidly to catch only the main points

    Where it sits · how thoroughly something is checked

    reading → perusal → audit

    reading vs. perusal: An ordinary reading may move briskly from start to finish, while a perusal means working through the text closely and thoroughly.

    audit vs. perusal: A perusal is a thorough reading, but an audit goes further, checking a record item by item against official evidence.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal, legal and scholarly register

    Perusal usually appears in the patterns 'a perusal of' something or 'for your perusal.' Despite a very common misconception, it does not mean skimming: a perusal is slow and complete, so pair it with words like careful, close, or line by line rather than quick or hurried.

    When a collector offered the library a previously unknown letter by Charles Dickens, the head archivist did not celebrate; she scheduled three quiet days. Authentication, she likes to say, is not glancing — it is perusal: every line of the letter gone through slowly, each phrase checked against the novelist's habits. On the second day she found it: 'week-end,' hyphenated in a fashion Dickens never used, tucked in the middle of an otherwise flawless page. A forger, she explained later, can copy handwriting beautifully; what he cannot survive is a patient enemy with time. The collector withdrew the letter, and the library bought nothing but three days of her salary — a bargain, the trustees agreed.
