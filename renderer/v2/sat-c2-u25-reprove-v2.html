What It Means

Reprove means to scold or correct, usually gently.

See the word in context

With one raised eyebrow and two soft words, the conductor reproved the trombones for rushing.

The ranger caught the family mid-toss, peanuts arcing toward the squirrels, and reproved them without raising her voice or reaching for the citation pad. She named what the food does to a wild animal's winter instincts, thanked them for hearing her out, and walked on. The peanuts went back in the pack, and the family waved to her later from the trailhead.

Reprove is the exact word for what she did, because it means to scold or correct, usually gently — disapproval made unmistakable while everyone's dignity stays intact. Scold runs hotter and louder and often just vents; a reproof aims to fix the behavior, and it usually does.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    scold: To scold is the everyday word for telling someone off — often louder, hotter, and aimed more at venting than at fixing anything. To reprove is cooler and more deliberate: disapproval made plain while everyone's dignity stays intact, so the behavior changes and the relationship survives.

    correct: To correct is to fix the behavior with no feeling in it at all — a coach adjusting a grip, an editor repairing a comma. To reprove adds the moral note: the person is told, gently but unmistakably, that they earned some disapproval.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    praise: name what was done well

    commend: praise formally, on the record

    Where it sits · how much heat the criticism carries

    remind → reprove → chew out

    remind vs. reprove: Remind is gentler than reprove — it carries no criticism at all, just a nudge that the rule exists, while a reproof makes real disapproval plain.

    chew out vs. reprove: Chew out goes further than reprove — the volume rises and the anger shows, while a reproof stays gentle enough to thank someone for afterward.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / gentle

      Reprove is correction with the volume down — disapproval made plain, dignity intact. Scold runs hotter; correct has no feelings in it at all. In this unit's disapproval family, each word takes a different object: reprove a person, gently, for an act; repudiate a claim or belief, formally and entirely; repulse a physical advance; and save reprobate for the character past correcting. If the correction could plausibly end with a thank-you, it was a reproof.
