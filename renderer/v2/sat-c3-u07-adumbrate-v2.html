Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Adumbrate means to sketch out in a vague way.

See the word in context

In the trailer's final seconds, the studio adumbrates the sequel's plot: a face in shadow, a burned map, one line of warning.

Before the orchestra played a note, the composer adumbrated the evening's new symphony for the audience — a storm, a truce, a slow homecoming — and then let the music fill in everything else.

Adumbrate is more precise than 'describe' or 'summarize' because it means giving only the dim outline of a thing — from Latin umbra, 'shadow,' it traces the shape and deliberately leaves the details dark.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sketch: To sketch is to give a rough version of anything — a plan, a face, a chapter — with no hint of shadow or secrecy about it. Adumbrate is narrower and more deliberate: it traces only the dimmest outline and keeps the details dark on purpose.

    prefigure: To prefigure is to be an early sign of something that comes later, without anyone intending the signal — a rough drawing can prefigure a finished masterpiece. Adumbrate is deliberate: whoever gives the shadowy outline chooses to give it, and chooses what stays dark.

    foreshadow: To foreshadow is to plant an early hint of what is coming, usually inside a story, so the later event feels prepared when it arrives. Adumbrate points at the thing itself: it sketches a plan's or design's own outline now, dimly and on purpose, rather than hinting at a separate later event.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    detail: to spell a thing out feature by feature

    itemize: to list every part, one line at a time

    Where it sits · how much detail the account gives

    mention → adumbrate → itemize

    mention vs. adumbrate: To mention a plan is barely to touch it — a name and nothing more; it stops short of adumbrate, which at least traces the plan's broad shape.

    itemize vs. adumbrate: Itemize goes further than adumbrate: an adumbrated plan is a silhouette, while an itemized one lists every part in full light.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Adumbrate is a scholar's word for sketching something in its dimmest outline — use it for plans, themes, or futures traced but not filled in. In everyday speech, 'sketch out' or 'hint at' does the job; adumbrate adds the deliberate shadow.

    For the premiere of her Fifth Symphony, the composer Aleta Varga wrote a program note that critics still tease her about. It promised a finale built on 'a theme you have already half-heard,' and would say no more. Varga liked to adumbrate her endings this way: a program note would trace the finale's shape in the dimmest possible line — a rhythm hinted, a key withheld — while every detail stayed locked in the score. She defended the habit as a courtesy. Announce the theme outright, she argued, and the audience spends the evening waiting; give them a shadowed outline and they spend it listening. The Fifth's finale, when it came, landed exactly as designed — recognized in the instant, impossible to have predicted.
