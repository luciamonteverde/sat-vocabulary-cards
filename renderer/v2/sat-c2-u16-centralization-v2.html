/sen-truh-lih-ZAY-shun/ - the stress falls on ZAY

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Centralization means the gathering of power or control into a single central authority.

See the word in context

After centralization, the six regional academies answered to one national director, down to the color of the training bibs.

The lighthouse service ran for ninety years as a scatter of little kingdoms — a keeper ordered oil when oil ran low, patched his own lantern glass, and answered to nobody nearer than God. The new Board's centralization ended that in a single winter. Watch rotas come down from one office; lamp parts leave one warehouse; and a keeper on the remotest rock cannot buy rope without a countersigned form.

Centralization is the exact word for that winter's work, because centralization means the gathering of power or control into a single central authority — the lights still stand where they stood; what moved was the deciding. Consolidation would only say that scattered things were merged into fewer hands; centralization says whose hands: the center's.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    concentration: Concentration is the piling up of power in fewer hands, wherever it happens to land — a committee, a rich family, a single office. Centralization is more specific: the power flows to the recognized center of the system, the top of the org chart, and rules from there.

    unification: Unification joins separate things into one whole — two countries, two leagues, two firms become a single body with a single name. Centralization can leave every part standing exactly where it was and take only the deciding: the branches survive, but their say moves to the center.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    decentralization: the same offices, with the deciding handed back out to the branches

    devolution: powers formally passed down to local bodies

    Where it sits · how tightly one point holds all control

    coordination → centralization → absolutism

    coordination vs. centralization: Coordination is far lighter than centralization — the branches agree on a common calendar and keep every decision their own; nothing about who decides ever moves to the center.

    absolutism vs. centralization: Absolutism goes further than centralization — a centralized service still runs on rules that bind the center itself; under absolutism the center answers to nothing.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / administrative

    Centralization is the word for org charts, states, and systems — power flowing to one center. It is structure, not personality: a single harsh ruler is this unit's despot, and one power lording it over its supposed equals is hegemony. When scattered offices merely merge, consolidation is the honest word — nothing about it says who ends up in charge.
