Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Jurisprudence means the philosophy and science of law — and, concretely, the accumulated body of court decisions and principles a legal system reasons from.

See the word in context

The seminar read no statutes at all; it was a course in jurisprudence, asking what makes any law worth obeying.

Nothing in the statute said who owned a drifting iceberg, so the young judge turned to two centuries of maritime jurisprudence — salvage cases, whaling cases, collision cases — and reasoned her way to an answer that later courts still cite.

Jurisprudence is law at one remove: not a statute's text or a single verdict, but the reasoned body of decisions and principles that tells a court how to think. When judges ask what earlier courts held and why, they are consulting jurisprudence, not just looking up a rule.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    case law: the rules courts have already laid down, decision by decision. Jurisprudence includes that record and reaches beyond it, into the reasoning and philosophy of why the law holds what it holds.

    legal philosophy: the abstract study of what law is and what it should be. Jurisprudence covers that ground and adds something concrete: the actual body of decisions a court system has built and still reasons from.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    lawlessness: the absence of legal order altogether

    caprice: rule by whim, with no settled principle behind it

    Where it sits · how much of the law a single word takes in

    ruling → precedent → jurisprudence

    ruling vs. jurisprudence: A ruling is far narrower than jurisprudence: it settles one dispute on one day, while jurisprudence is the whole fabric of decisions and principles a legal system reasons from.

    precedent vs. jurisprudence: Precedent reaches further than a ruling but still stops short of jurisprudence: a precedent guides the cases that resemble it, while jurisprudence gathers every precedent — and the philosophy behind them — into one body of thought.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / scholarly

    Jurisprudence lives at the level of systems: a nation's jurisprudence, First Amendment jurisprudence, two centuries of maritime jurisprudence. It is never a single statute — legislatures pass laws, not jurisprudence — and never a synonym for a person's good sense, whatever the 'prudence' inside it suggests. If you mean one court's decision, say ruling; if you mean the whole reasoned edifice, jurisprudence is the word.

Colleagues described how she could trace a question backward through eighty years of decisions, then forward through the principles those decisions had slowly built. Young lawyers remembered her chambers exams: name the holding, then explain the reasoning a court would use to extend it. The law, she liked to say, was not a rulebook but a long conversation.
