What It Means

Prudence means careful good judgment and caution about risks in practical matters.

See the word in context

Out of prudence, the archive keeps three copies of every reel — one of them in a salt mine two provinces away.

The bakery's expansion was prudence in motion: Amaka signed a five-year lease only after running one winter from a rented cart, kept her old ovens as backups when the new ones arrived, and wrote the loan's payments against her slowest month's takings, not her best's. Nothing dramatic ever happened — which, her accountant notes, was the point.

Prudence is the exact word because it means careful good judgment and caution in practical matters — foresight doing arithmetic. It is not timidity: the prudent choice can be bold, provided the risks were weighed first. The word's Latin root is literally 'seeing ahead,' and everything prudent starts there.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    foresight: Foresight is the seeing-ahead half of prudence: picturing what could go wrong or come next before it does. On its own it decides nothing — a gambler can have perfect foresight about the odds and still bet the rent. Prudence completes the job, letting the view ahead steer the choice.

    caution: Caution is the braking half of prudence — slowing down, holding back, steering wide of the risk in front of you. It reacts to danger without necessarily weighing it, and caution mostly says no. Prudence weighs the risk first and can still choose boldly once the weighing is done.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    recklessness: risk taken unweighed

    heedlessness: the risks never even seen

    Where it sits · how strongly risk holds one back

    care → prudence → timidity

    care vs. prudence: Care is the everyday habit asked of everyone — looking both ways, reading the label — and it is milder than prudence, which weighs a risk deliberately before acting on it.

    timidity vs. prudence: Timidity goes further than prudence — prudence weighs the risk and still acts; timidity sees the risk and stays home.

      Word Choice

      formal / approving

      Prudence is the planner's virtue — money, weather, health, contracts. Its unit-mates each take a slice: circumspect is prudence at the moment of speaking or acting; appraise is what prudence does to a deal before signing. Timidity is the counterfeit: refusing the risk instead of weighing it.
