Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Abdicate means to formally give up a throne or supreme power, or to abandon a solemn duty.

See the word in context

When the scandal broke, the young king chose to abdicate rather than drag the crown through the courts, and the throne passed to his cousin by nightfall.

King Edvin reigned for eleven months. When parliament refused to accept his marriage to a divorced commoner, he chose the woman over the crown and abdicated in a radio address that stunned the empire: 'I cannot carry the burden of kingship without the help of the woman I love.'

Abdicate is sharper than 'quit' or 'step down' because it names the formal surrender of sovereign power itself: a cashier quits and a coach steps down, but only someone holding a throne — or an authority nearly as supreme — can abdicate it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    relinquish: To relinquish is to let go of something you hold — a grip, a claim, control of a project — at any scale, and often reluctantly. Abdicate is narrower and grander: it names the formal surrender of a throne or supreme power. You can relinquish the TV remote; only sovereigns abdicate.

    cede: To cede is to hand something over to a specific someone else — territory to a neighbor, power to a rival — usually under pressure or by agreement. Abdicate looks at the giving-up itself: a monarch can abdicate without ceding to anyone in particular, simply laying the crown down.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    retain: to keep holding what you already have

    reclaim: to take back a position or right you had lost

    Where it sits · how much is surrendered, and how finally

    resign → abdicate → renounce

    resign vs. abdicate: Anyone can resign — a lifeguard, a treasurer, a prime minister; abdicate raises the stakes to sovereign power, the surrender of a crown rather than a job.

    renounce vs. abdicate: Renounce goes further than abdicate: an abdicating king gives up the crown he wears today, while renouncing the throne rejects even the claim to it — for himself and often for his heirs — leaving nothing to return to.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal

    Abdicate is the formal word for surrendering supreme power, above all a throne; it also stretches to duties solemn enough to feel sovereign, as in abdicating one's responsibility. For ordinary jobs and offices, resign or step down is the right register, and renounce is the word when someone gives up a claim or belief rather than a seat of power.
