perturbs

/per-TURBZ/ - rhymes with curbs

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Perturbs means disturbs or troubles; makes uneasy.

See the word in context

The silence from the usually noisy server room perturbs the night technician, who keeps inventing reasons to walk past its door.

A single misfiled key perturbs the archivist for days; order, once broken, whispers to her that anything at all could be lost.

Perturbs is quieter than alarms and deeper than annoys: it names the unease that keeps returning — something is off, even if you cannot say what. Nobody flees a room because of what perturbs them; they just stop sleeping well.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unsettles: To unsettle someone is to shake their calm without terrifying them — the ground shifts once and the composure wobbles. What perturbs a person goes on working afterward: a quieter unease that keeps returning, whispering that something is off long after the first jolt has passed.

    rattles: Rattles is the informal word for throwing someone off their usual balance — a heckler rattles a rookie pitcher for an inning. It is sharp, visible, and soon over. Perturbs works lower and longer: quiet unease that troubles sleep rather than performance, with nothing to point at.

    flusters: To fluster someone is to make them flustered and clumsy in the moment — dropped notes, tangled words, a red face. It ends when the moment does. What perturbs you follows you home: no clumsiness at all, just a worry that resurfaces and will not name itself.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    calms: settles someone's nerves

    reassures: removes someone's doubts and worry

    Where it sits · how much worry it stirs up

    distracts → perturbs → alarms

    distracts vs. perturbs: What distracts you merely pulls at your attention — milder than perturbs, which leaves real unease behind.

    alarms vs. perturbs: Alarms goes further than perturbs: alarm announces that danger is here, while perturbs only whispers that something is off.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / precise

    Perturbs names quiet, persistent unease rather than fright — the right word when something is wrong but unnamed. Alarms is louder; annoys is shallower. Note the odd one out: this is the unit's only word about worry rather than hostility, so don't let the angry neighbors rub off on it.
