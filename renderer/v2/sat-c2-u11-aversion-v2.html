Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Aversion means a particular dislike for something.

See the word in context

Even as a kitten she showed a marked aversion to the vacuum cleaner, relocating to the top shelf at the first click of its cord.

The film director's aversion to flying is part of festival legend. She has crossed to Cannes by freighter, taken four trains to Venice, and once judged a Sydney festival entirely over video link. Ask her about it and she laughs; book her a flight and her office quietly books the sleeper car instead.

Aversion is the exact word because it names a particular dislike — and one that behaves. An aversion doesn't just disapprove; it steers. The word's Latin bones mean a turning-away, and that is precisely what her itineraries do: whatever the route, it bends around the airport.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    reluctance: Reluctance is unwillingness to take a particular action — you hang back from the next step, and duty or nerves can cause it without any dislike at all. An aversion is a settled dislike of the thing itself, and it steers the whole route: detours, refusals, itineraries bent around the airport. Reluctance drags one foot; an aversion redraws the map.

    dread: Dread is fear pointed at the future — the cold weight of something coming that you cannot stop. An aversion needn't be afraid at all: the director laughs about flying; she simply never boards a plane. Dread braces for the thing; an aversion calmly arranges never to meet it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    craving: an active pull toward the thing

    liking: plain enjoyment of it

    Where it sits · how strongly the feeling turns you away

    distaste → aversion → disgust

    distaste vs. aversion: Distaste registers and moves on — you finish the meeting anyway; an aversion redraws the route.

    disgust vs. aversion: Disgust goes past aversion: the turning-away has become physical, a recoil you can see.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral

    Aversion is the dislike that steers: it shows up as detours, refusals, and empty seats near the thing avoided. Its cluster-mate in this unit, antipathy, runs deeper and argues its case; an aversion mostly just leaves the room. Keep dread for fear — an aversion can be perfectly calm about the thing it never goes near.

Her aversion is not fear and not squeamishness; she will happily walk across a lawn to reach an interesting weed. But she has never planted one, and her own yard is a mosaic of moss, clover, and wild thyme. Asked about it, she calmly lists her reasons -- thirsty, fussy, biologically empty -- the way a judge reads a verdict. Friends have learned to route garden-club tours around the turf exhibits, because her settled dislike quietly redraws every itinerary she is part of.
