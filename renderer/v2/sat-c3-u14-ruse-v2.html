What It Means

Ruse means a trick aimed at misleading a particular target for a specific purpose.

See the word in context

Her 'forgotten' scarf was a ruse — an excuse to knock a second time and count who was sitting at the table now.

The recall notice was a ruse: collectors who brought their counterfeit coins in for a 'safety exchange' were met at the counter by two detectives.

A ruse is one engineered deception with a job to do. A trick can be as small as a prank, and a hoax fools a whole public just to see if it can — a ruse is aimed, built to move a particular person into a particular mistake.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    ploy: A ploy is a calculated maneuver toward a tactical goal — sneaky, but not always a deception at all: a delaying ploy can be perfectly open about what it is. A ruse is always a deception and always aimed, built to move a particular target into a particular mistake.

    stratagem: A stratagem is a scheme with a battlefield flavor, grander and more elaborate than a ruse, and deployed against an opponent in some contest. A ruse can be as small as a 'forgotten' scarf; a stratagem implies a campaign. Both deceive, but a stratagem needs an adversary.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    fair warning: tells the target exactly what is coming

    straight dealing: no traps anywhere in the transaction

    Where it sits · how planned and far-reaching the deception is

    trick → ruse → hoax

    trick vs. ruse: Trick is milder than ruse — any small deception or prank counts as a trick, planned or not.

    hoax vs. ruse: Hoax goes further than ruse: a ruse misleads a chosen target toward a goal, while a hoax sets out to fool a whole public.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / compact

    Ruse is short, slightly Frenchified, and precise: one engineered deception with a target and a purpose. Prefer trick for anything casual, hoax when the public itself is the mark, and legerdemain — this unit's other French import — when what impresses you is the skill of the hands rather than the plan.
