stipulate

/STIP-yuh-layt/ - starts like 'stipple'

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Stipulate means to demand or specify something formally as a required condition of an agreement or other binding document.

See the word in context

The novelist's will stipulated that her letters stay sealed for fifty years, and the archive has kept the term to the day.

When the zoo agreed to host a pair of giant pandas, the lending country stipulated the terms in writing: a purpose-built bamboo grove, a veterinarian on staff around the clock, and the pandas' return within ten years. The moment the zoo's budget plan trimmed the vet position, the agreement stalled until it was restored.

That is stipulating: not hoping, urging, or merely describing, but naming a term formally so the agreement itself carries it — no grove, no vet, no pandas. And mind the sound-alike trap: speculate, its near-twin in rhythm, means to guess without solid evidence. Stipulating is the opposite of guessing — a term nailed down in writing.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    specify: To specify is to state something exactly — a measurement, a date, a model number. Anyone can specify anything, and no agreement is needed anywhere. Stipulate adds the bargaining table: the named term becomes a formal condition, and the deal itself carries it.

    require: To require is to make something necessary, from any direction — a law, a recipe, or plain circumstance can require things. Stipulate is requiring done inside an agreement: one party names the term, formally, and it binds as part of the deal.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    waive: to give up a requirement you were entitled to insist on

    leave open: to let a term stay unsettled, for the parties to work out later

    Where it sits · how much say the other side keeps

    request → stipulate → dictate

    request vs. stipulate: Request is milder than stipulate: a request can be declined and the bargain rolls on, while a stipulated term is formally required — the deal does not close without it.

    dictate vs. stipulate: Dictate goes further than stipulate: a party who stipulates demands one term and negotiates the rest, while one who dictates sets every term and leaves the other side no say at all.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / contractual

    Stipulate is contract-table language: a party stipulates a term, or the document itself does — the charter stipulates, the policy stipulates — usually with that and often a must close behind. Courtrooms give it a second, related job: lawyers stipulate to facts, formally agreeing to them so no one has to prove them. And guard the sound-alike: speculate means to guess without solid ground, and stipulating is guessing's opposite — a term fixed in writing.
