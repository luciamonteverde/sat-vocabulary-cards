affront

/uh-FRUNT/ - rhymes with front

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Affront means an insult delivered openly and on purpose — disrespect aimed where its target is meant to see it.

See the word in context

The museum misspelled its oldest donor's name on the new wing's plaque — twice — and she read the carelessness as an affront, not an accident.

When the city club offered to field its reserve side against the village team 'to keep things fair,' the villagers took the offer as an affront: they had not asked for mercy, and the condescension stung worse than any scoreline could.

An affront is sharper than a slight: a slight can be small, even accidental, but an affront is an open show of disrespect that the injured party is meant to notice — you take offense at an affront precisely because it looks intended.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    insult: An insult is the plain, everyday word for words or acts meant to wound. An affront is more formal and more pointed: an open show of disrespect aimed where the target will see it, and meant to be read as intended. Every affront insults; not every insult is staged so openly.

    indignity: An indignity is a treatment that strips away someone's dignity — being searched twice, kept waiting, talked over. An affront is aimed disrespect the injured party is meant to notice; an indignity can be careless or systemic, wounding without anyone intending the wound.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    courtesy: an act of politeness that honors the other person

    kindness: warmth offered with no edge at all

    Where it sits · how grave and widespread the injury

    slight → affront → outrage

    slight vs. affront: A slight is milder than an affront — a name dropped from a list, a greeting skipped — and may not even be meant.

    outrage vs. affront: An outrage goes further than an affront: an affront wounds one person's pride, while an outrage is a violation so gross that whole crowds take up the injury.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal

      Affront is the formal word for an insult aimed where it will be seen — you take it, suffer it, or treat something as one. For casual rudeness, insult or dig is enough. Keep it apart from its unit neighbor flagrant: flagrant describes how glaring an offense is, while an affront is the offense itself, felt by the person it targets.
