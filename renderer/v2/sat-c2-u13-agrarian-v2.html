agrarian

/uh-GRAIR-ee-uhn/ - the stress falls on GRAIR

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Agrarian means relating to farming, cultivated land, or a society built around it.

See the word in context

The saga opens in an agrarian province where the whole year turns on plowing, sowing, and the state of the wheat.

The lecture's first slide was a painting of a village, and the professor asked what governed it. Not the castle on the hill, she said. Look at the strips of cultivated ground running to the horizon: who holds them, who works them, and who takes the crop decides everything else — marriage, debt, war. A society like that is agrarian, and for most of human history, most societies were.

Agrarian is the exact word because it names what relates to farming, cultivated land, or a society built around it — not just a place with fields in it, but a world whose law, wealth, and holidays all grow out of the soil. A beach town is coastal scenery; an agrarian village is farming all the way down.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    farm-based: Farm-based is the plain description: a farm-based economy simply earns most of its money from farms. Agrarian claims far more — a whole social order built on the land, where law, wealth, and the calendar all grow out of who holds and works the soil.

    pastoral: Pastoral is the idealized countryside of poems and paintings — shepherds, meadows, and a soft golden light. Agrarian is the working reality underneath: who plows, who owns the field, who collects the rent. A scene can be pastoral; only a society ordered around farming is agrarian.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    industrial: an economy built on factories rather than fields

    commercial: built on buying and selling rather than growing

    Where it sits · how fully life rests on the land

    rural → farming → agrarian

    rural vs. agrarian: Rural is weaker than agrarian: it says only where you are — in the countryside — and a rural county can live on tourism or a mine and never plant a row, while an agrarian one rests everything on the land.

    farming vs. agrarian: A farming town goes further than a rural one — the farms matter there; agrarian claims the most, a society whose law, wealth, and seasons are all built on the land.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / historical

    Agrarian is the historian's and economist's word: agrarian reform, agrarian unrest, an agrarian economy. For scenery, rural is enough, and for the dreamy postcard version reach for pastoral. Keep it apart from this unit's agriculture, the practice of farming itself — a country can modernize its agriculture and stop being agrarian in the same generation.
