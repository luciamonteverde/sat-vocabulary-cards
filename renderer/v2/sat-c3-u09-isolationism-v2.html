Meaning

What It Means

Isolationism means a national policy of avoiding political or economic involvement with other countries.

See the word in context

Between the wars, isolationism kept the country out of foreign quarrels—and out of the alliances that might have deterred the next one.

After the embassy bombing, the senate's older bloc urged isolationism: bring the diplomats home, cancel the joint naval exercises, and let the continent settle its own disputes. The younger bloc answered with a map of everything the country imported.

Isolationism is bigger than one decision—it names a whole national posture of staying out: no alliances, no foreign bases, minimal entanglements. A country can decline one pact without embracing isolationism, the way one quiet evening doesn't make a hermit.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    withdrawal: Withdrawal is the act of pulling back — from a treaty, a market, a friendship — and it can be as narrow as a single exit. Isolationism is the whole program: a nation's standing policy of avoiding foreign entanglements altogether, not one retreat but a settled posture of staying out.

    neutrality: Neutrality means taking no side in other nations' quarrels — a neutral country can still trade everywhere, host talks, and sign agreements. Isolationism refuses the involvement itself: no alliances, minimal entanglement of any kind. Neutral states stay in the room without picking a team; isolationism leaves the room.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    internationalism: seeks cooperation and shared institutions across borders

    engagement: active involvement in the world's business

    Where it sits · how completely a nation seals itself off

    nonalignment → isolationism → autarky

    nonalignment vs. isolationism: Nonalignment is a milder stance than isolationism — a nonaligned nation joins no bloc but keeps its embassies busy and its ports open, while isolationism pulls back from the involvement itself.

    autarky vs. isolationism: Autarky goes further than nonalignment—an autarkic state aims to need nothing from anyone, sealing off even the trade that isolationism might tolerate.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    political / analytic

    Isolationism is policy vocabulary—use it for nations, parties, and eras, not for people. A hermit's retreat is solitude; the -ism turns the island into a program. Its reference-list cousins in this unit, isolate and isolated, cover the ordinary uses: labs isolate variables, and a weather station can be isolated with no politics involved.

After a ruinous colonial war, Maro's parliament did not simply leave one alliance; it legislated against joining any. Foreign bases were banned, the diplomatic corps was cut to a single consulate, and trade treaties were allowed to expire without renewal. The policy held for sixty years, through booms its neighbors enjoyed and wars they suffered. The chapter's closing question asks students to weigh the ledger: six decades of peace and thin harvests against the region's crowded, quarrelsome prosperity.
