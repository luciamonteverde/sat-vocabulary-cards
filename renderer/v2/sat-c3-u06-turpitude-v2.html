turpitude

/TUR-pi-tood/ - the stress falls on TUR

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Turpitude means shameful wickedness; moral corruption.

See the word in context

The actor's studio contract carried a turpitude clause, voiding his salary if scandal proved him guilty of real wickedness rather than mere mischief.

The medical board distinguished sloppiness from turpitude: a mislabeled chart cost one doctor a fine, but selling forged prescriptions to addicts showed the deliberate moral rot that ends a career for good.

Turpitude is a heavyweight noun reserved for wickedness that stains character, not for slip-ups. It survives mostly in formal phrases — 'moral turpitude' in statutes and contracts — and it is a mass noun: conduct shows turpitude; nobody commits 'a turpitude.'

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    wickedness: Wickedness is the plain, everyday word for morally evil conduct — anyone can call a deed wicked, in any register. Turpitude is its courtroom cousin: the same rot named in statutes, contracts, and licensing codes, reserved for conduct shameful enough to stain character. Wickedness accuses; turpitude disqualifies.

    baseness: Baseness is meanness of character — the low, self-serving streak of the cheat or the coward, contemptible but a shade less vile than real evil. Turpitude goes darker: deliberate, shameful wickedness, the deep moral corruption named in statutes. A base act earns contempt; turpitude ends a license or a career.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    virtue: active moral goodness

    decency: ordinary honest behavior

    Where it sits · how grave the moral wrong is

    misconduct → turpitude → atrocity

    misconduct vs. turpitude: Misconduct covers any breach of the rules, from padded expenses to missed filings; misconduct stops well short of turpitude, which names genuine moral rot.

    atrocity vs. turpitude: Atrocity goes further than turpitude: turpitude is deep corruption of character, while an atrocity is a single act so monstrous the world recoils from it.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / legal

      Turpitude lives in statutes, licensing codes, and contracts — 'conduct involving moral turpitude' is the fixed phrase. In conversation, wickedness or corruption carries the meaning without the starch; save turpitude for when the register is officially grave.

      In 1911 the state bar faced a celebrated problem: its most admired trial lawyer had been quietly emptying the trust accounts of the widows he represented. His defenders pointed to a spotless courtroom record and called the missing funds a bookkeeping tangle, the kind of error any busy practice might make. The disciplinary committee disagreed. Errors, it wrote, invite correction; turpitude demands removal. Stealing from clients who could not read their own statements was not a tangle but a betrayal. The committee drew a line the profession still quotes: a lawyer may be sloppy with forms and remain a lawyer, but deliberate dishonesty for personal gain corrupts the character the license certifies. He was disbarred within the month, and the following spring the state wrote conduct of that shameful kind into its licensing code as grounds for removal.
