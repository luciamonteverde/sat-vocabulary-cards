/rih-PREEV/

Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Reprieve means an official postponement of a punishment or unwanted event — the trouble is delayed for a time, not canceled.

See the word in context

The vendor won a reprieve when the hospital agreed to push back the contract's termination date while new terms were negotiated.

The century-old theater received a reprieve from demolition while the preservationists' paperwork made its way through the county office.

The wrecking crews have not been called off forever — the demolition is only postponed while the review happens. A delay with the threat still hanging in the air is exactly what a reprieve is.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    stay: A stay is a court's own pause button — a judge orders a stay, and a legal action halts, as in a stay of eviction. A reprieve can come from anyone with power over the dreaded thing — a governor, a bank, even the weather — and it always carries the flavor of rescue.

    postponement: A postponement is neutral: anything moved to a later time, welcome or not — a dentist appointment shifted to Thursday is a postponement. A reprieve is a postponement experienced as rescue: what was coming was a punishment or something dreaded, and the delay itself is a relief.

    deferral: A deferral is an official decision to put an obligation off — payments deferred, enrollment deferred, all routine paperwork. A reprieve carries drama that a deferral lacks: the thing pushed back was dreaded, hanging over someone, and the extra time arrives as relief.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    enforcement: carrying a punishment out right away

    resumption: starting something up again after a pause

    Where it sits · how lasting the relief from punishment is

    delay → reprieve → pardon

    delay vs. reprieve: A delay is any small postponement at all — your bus can be delayed without anyone facing punishment, so it is much weaker and more everyday than a reprieve.

    pardon vs. reprieve: A pardon goes much further than a reprieve: it wipes the punishment away forever, while a reprieve only holds it off for a time.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      Formal, with common figurative use

      A reprieve is strictly temporary — it postpones a punishment or dreaded event but never erases it, so don't use it where you mean a pardon or a cancellation. It also thrives figuratively in everyday writing: a cool morning can be 'a brief reprieve from the heat wave.' Don't confuse it with reprise, a repeated section of music.
