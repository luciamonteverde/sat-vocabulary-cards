callous

/KAL-uhs/ - rhymes with palace

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Callous means coldly unfeeling toward the suffering of others.

See the word in context

Mocking the rookie's stage fright in front of the whole cast was callous, the director said, and she benched the veteran actor for a week.

The expedition memoir saves its anger for the leader who proved callous toward his exhausted porters: he waved off frostbitten hands at inspection, demanded double miles from men who could barely stand, and slept warm while their shelters leaked.

Callous is stronger than 'insensitive' and colder than 'rude': it names a hardened indifference to other people's suffering. The word pictures skin worn hard — a callus — so a callous heart is one that pain no longer gets through.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    heartless: Heartless is the blunter, more damning accusation — no pity anywhere, as if the organ were missing. Callous is more specific about the mechanism: feeling worn hard, like a callus, so that another person's suffering no longer gets through. A heartless villain never had warmth; a callous one has stopped letting pain in.

    unfeeling: Unfeeling names a flat, passive absence of sympathy — a temperament with the warmth switched off. Callous is more pointed and more active: a callous person sees particular suffering in front of them and stays hard toward it. Unfeeling describes what someone lacks; callous describes how they treat pain they witnessed.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    compassionate: moved by others' suffering and eager to ease it

    tenderhearted: easily touched, quick to sympathy

    Where it sits · from ignoring pain to inflicting it

    uncaring → callous → cruel

    uncaring vs. callous: Uncaring is milder than callous — uncaring simply fails to notice, while callous notices suffering and remains unmoved by it.

    cruel vs. callous: Cruel goes further than callous — the callous boss ignores your pain, while the cruel one inflicts it.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / condemning

      Callous is an accusation: it says someone saw suffering and stayed hard. Use 'insensitive' for mere social clumsiness and save callous for indifference that wounds — a callous remark, a callous policy. It attaches naturally to acts and decisions rather than to looks or moods.
