restitution

/res-tih-TOO-shun/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Restitution means restoration of something lost, or compensation paid for a loss or injury.

See the word in context

The judge ordered the con artist to pay full restitution to the retirees whose savings he had drained.

After Theo accidentally knocked over and shattered Mrs. Bell's antique porch lamp, he mowed her lawn every week that summer as restitution.

Theo cannot un-break the lamp, so he makes up for the loss with work of equal value. Providing something to compensate for what another person lost is precisely what restitution means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    compensation: Compensation is any payment made to make up for a loss, injury, or inconvenience — insurance payouts, damages, even overtime pay. Restitution is the pointed version: restoring to a specific victim what they lost, or its full value, typically because the payer is answerable for the loss.

    repayment: Repayment means giving back what is owed — settling a loan or a debt you took on. The money was always due. Restitution answers a loss rather than a debt: something was taken or destroyed, and its value is restored to the person who suffered the loss.

    redress: Redress is a remedy that sets a wrong right, and it can take almost any form — an apology, a policy change, a payment. Restitution is one specific kind of redress: restoring what was lost, or paying its value, to the person who was harmed.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    confiscation: officially seizing someone's property

    deprivation: the taking away or withholding of something needed

    Where it sits · the scale of the loss made good

    refund → restitution → reparations

    refund vs. restitution: A refund is weaker than restitution, a refund simply returns the price you paid, while restitution makes up for the full loss a person actually suffered.

    reparations vs. restitution: Reparations are stronger than restitution, restitution repays one victim for a specific loss, while reparations attempt to compensate entire groups for large-scale harm.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      Formal / legal

      Most at home in courtrooms and formal writing, in collocations like pay restitution, make restitution, and restitution to the victims. Do not confuse it with the sound-alike retribution, which means punishment or revenge; restitution repays a loss, while retribution inflicts one in return.
