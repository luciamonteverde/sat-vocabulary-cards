enclave

/EN-klayv/ - the stress falls on EN

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Enclave means a distinct community or territory lying entirely inside a larger one.

See the word in context

Lesotho is that rarest of things, a national enclave — an entire country lying wholly inside a single neighbor.

Sail into the marina and you find an enclave afloat: forty houseboats moored rail to rail, with their own noticeboard, tool library, and unofficial mayor-of-the-docks — and not one resident who thinks of the city ashore as home.

An enclave is not merely a neighborhood: the word insists on enclosure and difference. Whatever surrounds it — another country, another language, another way of life — the enclave sits wholly inside it and stays distinct, a sealed pocket rather than a blurred edge.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    pocket: A pocket is a small, isolated patch of something spread thin — a pocket of fog, of resistance, of poverty. It is loose and informal, and it has no fixed edge. An enclave is bounded and lasting: a real community or territory with a line around it that a map can draw.

    island: Island, used figuratively, is anything wholly surrounded by what it is not — an island of calm in a loud house. It borrows the shape of an enclave but none of the substance: an enclave is an actual community or territory whose boundary and difference are really there.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    melting pot: the place where differences blend away — an enclave is where they stay intact

    expanse: open, unbroken territory with no pocket in it anywhere

    Where it sits · how sealed off the place is

    quarter → enclave → citadel

    quarter vs. enclave: A quarter — like a city's theater quarter — has a name and a flavor, but its edges stay open; it stops short of an enclave's real enclosure.

    citadel vs. enclave: Citadel goes further than enclave — an enclave is distinct and enclosed; a citadel is fortified, holding the world outside at spearpoint.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    geographic / journalistic

    Enclave is precise where neighborhood and district are loose: it says surrounded, bounded, and different from what surrounds it. Maps use it for territory — one nation's land inside another — and reporters use it for communities that keep their own language or ways. If nothing encloses it, it is just a district.
