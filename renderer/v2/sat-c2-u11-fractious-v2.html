Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Fractious means troublesome or irritable.

See the word in context

By day three in the jury room, the twelve had grown fractious, quarreling over lunch orders as sharply as over the timeline.

Three weeks into the tour, the quartet has gone fractious. The first violin and the violist are quarreling about bowings, the bowing quarrel is secretly about the thermostat in the van, and this morning the second violin threatened to quit over a parking space. Their manager has begun scheduling rest days the way paramedics schedule shifts.

Fractious is the exact word because it names the state of being troublesome and irritable — friction-prone, snappish, hard to manage. It isn't hatred and it isn't a feud; nobody in the van is an enemy. The group is simply worn to the point where everything scrapes, and every small thing breaks into quarrel.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unruly: Unruly means hard to control — a crowd that won't form lines, hair that won't lie flat — and it needs no bad temper at all. Fractious adds the irritability: a fractious group is hard to manage because it is snappish and quarrelsome, scraping against its handlers and itself. Unruly disobeys; fractious disobeys crossly.

    peevish: Peevish is whiny and easily annoyed, usually one person at a time, sulking over small discomforts. Fractious scales up and turns outward: a fractious jury or touring quartet quarrels, digs in, and makes real trouble for whoever has to manage it. Peevish grumbles in its corner; fractious fights the whole room.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    mild-mannered: gentle by disposition

    sweet-tempered: hard to ruffle at all

    Where it sits · how hard they are to manage

    cranky → fractious → uncontrollable

    cranky vs. fractious: Cranky is a mood one nap can fix; fractious has turned outward — snapping, digging in, making trouble for whoever's in charge.

    uncontrollable vs. fractious: Uncontrollable goes past fractious: nothing the handler tries works at all anymore.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral

    Fractious is for the irritable and hard-to-manage — overtired children, balky animals, committees at hour three. Its cluster-mates in this unit argue on purpose: a contentious person disputes claims and a pugnacious one wants the fight, but a fractious group has no agenda at all beyond being impossible to steer. If one adult is merely grumbling, cranky is the humbler, truer word.
