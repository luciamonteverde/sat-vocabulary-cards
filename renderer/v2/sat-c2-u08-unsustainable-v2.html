unsustainable

/uhn-suh-STAY-nuh-buhl/ - the stress falls on STAY

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Unsustainable means unable to be continued at the same rate or level.

See the word in context

Accountants ruled the club's wage bill unsustainable: player salaries swallowed every ticket, shirt, and television dollar, with nothing left for the stadium loan.

The island's mayor loved the cruise ships until the arithmetic arrived: four thousand visitors a day on lanes built for four hundred, springs pumped faster than the winter rains refilled them, and the very quiet the brochures sold now impossible to find. The council capped landings, calling the old pace what the numbers said it was: unsustainable.

Unsustainable is a judgment about trajectory, not manners: continue at this rate and the thing breaks — whether or not anyone objects, and even if today feels fine. That is the difference from excessive, which scolds the amount. An unsustainable pace can look glorious right up until the month it can't.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    untenable: Untenable is about defense: a position — an argument, a policy, a military line — that cannot be held once real pressure arrives. Unsustainable is about arithmetic: a rate that cannot be continued, whatever anyone argues about it. An untenable claim collapses under questioning; an unsustainable budget collapses on schedule.

    excessive: Excessive scolds the amount: more than is needed, wanted, or proper, judged right now. It is a verdict about size, not about time. Unsustainable is a verdict about trajectory — the amount might be exactly what is needed, and the rate still cannot be kept up.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    maintainable: able to be kept up as long as needed

    affordable: within one's means to keep paying

    Where it sits · how soon the cost forces a collapse

    punishing → unsustainable → ruinous

    punishing vs. unsustainable: A punishing schedule hurts but can be endured — people keep punishing schedules for decades; unsustainable adds the deadline: this one has an expiry.

    ruinous vs. unsustainable: Ruinous overtakes unsustainable: the unsustainable budget will fail someday, while the ruinous one is wrecking the household now, invoice by invoice.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / analytic

      Unsustainable judges a rate: keep this up and it fails, however smoothly it runs today. Reach past it when the timing differs — this unit's precarious means one nudge could topple it now, and ruinous means the wreckage has already started. Budgets are usually unsustainable long before they are ruinous.
