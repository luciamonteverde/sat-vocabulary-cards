usurp

/yoo-SURP/ - rhymes with chirp

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Usurp means to seize power or a position without the right to it.

See the word in context

While the guildmaster lay fevered, his son-in-law usurped the chair at council, sealing letters with a ring nobody had given him.

Their grandmother's will named all four cousins equal heirs to the orchard; the eldest simply moved into the farmhouse, changed the locks, and usurped the running of the harvest before the others had unpacked from the funeral.

Usurp is not merely 'take over': it means seizing a role, power, or right that lawfully belongs to someone else — usually without violence, often with paperwork and nerve. You usurp thrones, titles, and authority, never wallets.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    seize: Seize is the all-purpose grab — police seize contraband, armies seize bridges, banks seize collateral, by force or by law. Usurp is narrower and always illegitimate: it takes an office, title, or right that lawfully belongs to someone else. Anything can be seized; only another's rightful place is usurped.

    hijack: To hijack is to take something over mid-course, by force and in the moment — a plane, a meeting, a conversation. Usurp works quieter and aims higher: with paperwork and nerve it takes the office or the authority itself, not just the controls for an afternoon.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    renounce: to give up a claim of your own free will

    cede: to hand power over formally

    Where it sits · how much of the office is taken

    meddle → encroach → usurp

    meddle vs. usurp: To meddle is to poke into business that isn't yours; meddle stops far short of usurp — the meddler interferes, the usurper takes the chair.

    encroach vs. usurp: To encroach is gradual — a fence creeping a foot a year into a neighbor's field; encroach stops short of usurp, which seizes the whole office in one stroke.

      formal / literary

      Usurp belongs to chronicles, charters, and constitutional rows. Beside it, appropriate (also in this unit) pockets goods and funds; usurp takes the office, the title, the right itself. If a thing can sit in a drawer, it is probably appropriated, not usurped.

      Putting it all together

      The reed warbler builds one of the neatest nests on the river, and the common cuckoo builds none at all. Instead, the cuckoo waits for a warbler pair to finish their work, slips in while they forage, and leaves a single egg among their clutch. Naturalists of the last century wrote that the hatchling usurps the nest, and the verb is precise: within hours the young cuckoo heaves the warblers' own eggs over the rim, one by one. What it takes is not merely food or shelter but the whole rightful place of the brood that should have been — the warblers feed the intruder as though it were their own. The pair labor on for weeks, raising a chick three times their size, tenants in service to the occupier of their own home.
