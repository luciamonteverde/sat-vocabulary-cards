What It Means

Resonate means to stir a deep and lasting sense of connection.

See the word in context

The night-shift nurse never guessed a picture book about moving day would resonate so with her young patients; three families have asked to keep the copy.

The documentary's quietest scene — a father re-lacing his daughter's skates with numb fingers — resonates far beyond hockey towns: months later, it is the moment reviewers name when asked what stayed with them.

Resonate means to stir a connection that is deep and lasting, not merely to please. The test is time: what resonates keeps returning — quoted, remembered, felt again — long after the applause has faded. An image can delight for an evening; the one that resonates follows you home.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    strike a chord: To strike a chord is to connect at once with someone's feelings — the phrase names the instant of recognition, the nod in the moment. To resonate asks what happens afterward: the connection must run deep and keep lasting. Plenty of ads strike a chord on first viewing; far fewer still resonate a month later.

    ring true: To ring true is to feel accurate to your own experience — a matter of believability, the detail that sounds exactly right. To resonate goes past accuracy into attachment: the thing stirs a deep and lasting connection. A crime drama can ring true without moving anyone; what resonates keeps mattering after the credits.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    fall flat: to land with no response at all

    leave cold: to stir nothing in someone

    Where it sits · how long its grip on you lasts

    appeal → resonate → haunt

    appeal vs. resonate: To appeal is a fainter thing than to resonate — a poster can appeal to you and be forgotten by dinner.

    haunt vs. resonate: Haunt goes further than resonate: what resonates keeps meaning something to you, while what haunts you follows you into quiet moments uninvited.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / warm

      Resonate claims depth and duration — save it for what keeps mattering after the moment passes. If something merely pleases, say it appeals; if it stays in memory without meaning much, it simply sticks or lingers; what resonates does more than stay — it keeps speaking.
