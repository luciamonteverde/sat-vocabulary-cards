Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Volatility means the tendency to change rapidly, sharply, and unpredictably — swinging in any direction without warning.

See the word in context

Analysts warned that election-year volatility in fuel prices could whipsaw the airlines twice in a single quarter.

For two years the vintage-sneaker resale market ran on volatility and nothing else: a pair that traded at ninety dollars on Monday hit four hundred by Friday, fell to one-sixty over a weekend rumor about reissues, then doubled again before the rumor was even disproved. Sellers set alarms for three a.m.; buyers learned to feel seasick just reading a price chart.

Volatility is the right word because the market's defining trait was the speed and size of its swings — in both directions, over and over. It was not instability: the shops stayed open, the shoes were real, and the system never once threatened to give way. The danger was never collapse; it was whiplash.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    instability: Instability is shakiness in the footing — an unstable ladder, government, or patient is liable to give way. Volatility adds motion: rapid, sharp swings in both directions, which can ride on top of a foundation that never actually fails.

    turbulence: Turbulence is the churn of a rough passage — air, water, or a stormy stretch of history tossing everything caught in it. Volatility is measured in reversals instead: quick, steep swings from one extreme to the other and back.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    stability: the state of holding firm and unchanged

    constancy: steadiness that stays the same through time

    Where it sits · how sharp and sudden the swings run

    fluctuation → volatility → upheaval

    fluctuation vs. volatility: Fluctuation is milder than volatility: prices fluctuate a few points in any ordinary week, while volatility means swings sharp, fast, and wide enough to make traders flinch.

    upheaval vs. volatility: Upheaval goes further than volatility: a volatile market still swings inside a working system, while upheaval tears the system itself loose — exchanges close, rules change, nothing stands where it stood.

      Word Choice

      wary / analytical

      Volatility is the market page's word for sharp, fast swings in any direction — prices, polls, tempers. Note what it does not claim: a volatile stock may end the year exactly where it began, because volatility measures the ride, not the destination. When the danger is weak footing and collapse, reach for instability; when the danger is whiplash, volatility.
