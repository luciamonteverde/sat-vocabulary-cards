Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Irreverence means playful, teasing lack of respect for what is usually taken seriously.

See the word in context

The tour guide's irreverence delighted the students: she introduced each solemn portrait by its sitter's childhood nickname.

For its hundredth issue, the physics magazine reviewed gravity as if it were a gadget — 'reliable, ubiquitous, poor customer support' — an irreverence its readers still quote.

Irreverence aims at things the room treats as sacred — institutions, rituals, great names — and usually keeps a streak of affection. Mockery wants to wound; cheek and impudence (this unit) are aimed at people. Ir- is in- ('not') reshaped before r: reverence withdrawn.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    cheek: Cheek is impudent boldness delivered live -- a saucy remark to a teacher or a boss, minor, face-to-face, and over in a moment. Irreverence targets institutions and solemn things rather than people, and can run through a whole book. A cheeky kid talks back; an irreverent columnist unsalutes the monument.

    mockery: Mockery is ridicule meant to belittle -- the affection gone, the target meant to feel it. Irreverence teases what the room holds sacred and usually keeps a streak of liking for it. An irreverent biography can flatter its subject; mockery sets out to wound.

    flippancy: Flippancy is refusing to be serious when the moment asks for it -- a joke at the funeral, a shrug in the crisis. It is about mismatched tone, not about targets. Irreverence takes deliberate aim at something solemn; flippancy simply declines to rise to the occasion.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    reverence: deep, worshipful respect — the ir- removed

    solemnity: gravity of manner that keeps every ritual intact

    Where it sits · how harshly the teasing treats its target

    playfulness → irreverence → scorn

    playfulness vs. irreverence: Playfulness teases without any target at all — it stops short of irreverence, which pokes fun at something usually treated as serious.

    scorn vs. irreverence: Scorn goes further than irreverence: the irreverent joke still likes what it teases, while scorn holds its target in contempt.

    Word Choice

    neutral

    Irreverence is for solemn things gleefully unsaluted — and it often compliments: an irreverent biography can be affectionate. When the target is a person owed courtesy, this unit gives you impertinent and impudent instead; when the wit curdles into wounding, it has become mockery.

    Every October the Grover Academy Bugle publishes its Founders' Week issue, and every October the headmaster braces himself. The student editors take the school's most solemn traditions and tickle them: this year's issue ranked the oil portraits in the Great Hall by 'most likely to be judging you.' The paper's irreverence has rules the editors enforce on themselves -- no individual targets, no genuine wounds -- and underneath it runs an obvious fondness for the place. Alumni collect the Founders' Week issues; two framed ones hang, of all places, in the headmaster's own study. 'They tease what they love,' he admits, 'and they only tease what everyone else salutes.'
