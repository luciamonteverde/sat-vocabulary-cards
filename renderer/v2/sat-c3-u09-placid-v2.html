Meaning

What It Means

Placid means calm, peaceful.

See the word in context

The night nurses trade shifts to care for the placid Otero baby, who naps through blood draws and gurgles at the thermometer.

Two hours into the road-trip traffic jam, the twins were howling and the GPS had given up, but Grandpa stayed placid behind the wheel, humming along with the radio and letting the miles come when they came.

Placid describes a settled, even calm that nothing much disturbs—usually a temperament rather than a mood. A placid person isn't working to stay calm the way a composed one is; the stillness is simply their resting state.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unflappable: Unflappable means impossible to fluster even when a crisis is in full swing—it is calm proven under pressure. Placid is quieter: a resting calm that was never disturbed in the first place. Unflappable describes performance in emergencies; placid describes an everyday temperament that nothing much tests.

    even-tempered: Even-tempered means your moods stay level—no sharp highs or lows from day to day. Placid goes further: not merely level but genuinely undisturbed, a stillness that is simply the person's resting state. An even-tempered manager can still feel the stress; a placid one barely registers it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    excitable: set off by the smallest spark

    jumpy: nerves on a hair trigger

    Where it sits · how hard the calm is to shake

    easygoing → placid → imperturbable

    easygoing vs. placid: Easygoing is milder than placid—an easygoing roommate shrugs at noise and mess but can still be rattled by real trouble, while a placid person's calm runs deeper and takes far more to shake.

    imperturbable vs. placid: Imperturbable goes further than easygoing—an imperturbable surgeon cannot be shaken by anything the day brings, while a placid person simply rests calm until something truly extraordinary intrudes.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / descriptive

    Placid is resting calm—a temperament (a placid baby, a placid pony) or an undisturbed surface. Serene, its unit-mate, glows a little: an inner, almost elevated peace. Tranquil belongs to places and hours more than persons. And don't confuse placid with complacent, its cousin from the same root: placid is calm; complacent is calm about the wrong things.
