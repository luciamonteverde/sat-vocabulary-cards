Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Verdant means green with growing plants.

See the word in context

Rice terraces climb the hillside in verdant steps that glow against the grey stone above them.

From the last dune crest the caravan saw it a full hour before reaching it: a verdant smudge floating on the shimmer, date palms over barley plots over channels of led water. The drivers, who had watched rock and gravel for nine days, said nothing. The youngest rider counted shades on the way in and gave up at six kinds of green.

Verdant is the exact word for that oasis, because it means green with growing plants — living green, green that is busy photosynthesizing, not painted on. The word does not require trees, lushness, or rain; it requires growth. Wherever chlorophyll is at work in quantity, from barley plots to a well-kept lawn, verdant applies.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    green: Green is the plain everyday word, and it is only a color — a painted door and a spring hillside earn it equally. Verdant claims more: green with growing plants, chlorophyll actually at work, a green that no paint can fake.

    leafy: Leafy is green specifically with foliage — trees and shrubs, branches overhead, leaves you could gather by the armful. Verdant asks only for growth: a cropped pasture or a barley plot is verdant with not a tree in sight.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    barren: bare of growing things altogether

    sun-scorched: browned and burnt dry by heat

    Where it sits · how densely the vegetation grows

    grassy → verdant → lush

    grassy vs. verdant: Grassy states a bare fact — grass grows here, thick or thin; it claims less than verdant, which wants living green in real quantity.

    lush vs. verdant: Lush goes past verdant — beyond green and into extravagance, growth so thick it crowds and spills over itself.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      literary / descriptive

      Verdant is green upgraded for the page — travel writing, brochures, poems about spring. It claims living green and nothing more: for growth so thick it overwhelms, step up to lush, and when the green world you mean is specifically woods, this unit's sylvan carries the forest with it. A cropped pasture can be perfectly verdant; nobody would call it lush.
