harangue

/huh-RANG/ - rhymes with sang

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Harangue means a long, ranting speech.

See the word in context

What began as a note about dish duty became a ten-minute harangue, with Rafael listing every unwashed mug since the semester began.

The referee's harangue at the coin toss surprised both captains: eight uninterrupted minutes on diving, time-wasting, and the collapse of sporting honor, delivered before either team had touched the ball.

A harangue is a speech gone feral: long, loud, accusatory, and aimed at a captive audience. A lecture instructs and a scolding corrects, but a harangue mostly vents — its length and heat serve the speaker's temper more than any listener.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    rant: A rant is the everyday word for angry talk that will not stop — anyone can rant, about anything, to anyone or no one. A harangue is a full ranting speech: longer, shaped like an address, and aimed at a captive audience. Every harangue contains a rant; not every rant grows into a harangue.

    diatribe: A diatribe is a bitter, sustained attack, and it leans written — the scathing review, the furious pamphlet. A harangue is delivered live and out loud, at listeners who cannot leave. If it was composed at a desk, call it a diatribe; if it was shouted at a room, a harangue.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    aside: a brief remark dropped quietly in passing

    pleasantry: polite small talk with no heat in it at all

    Where it sits · how long and loud the complaint grows

    grumble → scolding → harangue

    grumble vs. harangue: A grumble is complaint at a mutter's volume, half meant to be overheard; it sits at the mild end, far below a harangue.

    scolding vs. harangue: A scolding goes further than a grumble — direct, pointed, aimed at your mistakes — but a harangue crowns this ladder, swelling into a ranting speech that forgets to end.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    disapproving / vivid

    Calling a speech a harangue passes judgment: too long, too loud, too angry. Rant is its casual cousin; diatribe leans written and bitter; a scolding at least means to correct you. Harangue also works as a verb — the coach harangued the referees — with the same heat either way.

    The volunteer docent at the maritime museum was a retired ferry captain, and we learned quickly never to stand near the model of the modern harbor. Any visitor who paused there risked triggering the full harangue: forty minutes on the decline of seamanship, delivered at quarterdeck volume to whoever was cornered by the glass case. It had shape, the way a speech has shape. It opened with radar, built through automated buoys, and closed, every single time, with 'and nobody can read the water anymore.' Regulars knew the escape routes. School groups, herded and ticketed, did not, and their chaperones aged visibly. The museum finally posted a sign suggesting visitors 'allow ample time' for that gallery. The captain took it as a tribute.
