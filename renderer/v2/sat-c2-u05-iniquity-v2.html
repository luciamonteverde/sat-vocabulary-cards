iniquity

/ih-NIK-wih-tee/ - the stress falls on NIK

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Iniquity means profound wickedness or injustice.

See the word in context

Union organizers in the mill towns kept diaries of each iniquity: children at the looms, wages paid in company scrip, inspectors paid to look away.

The commission's report on the prison did not use the word 'abuse' once; it chose iniquity - for the falsified deaths, the sold pardons, the men held years past their sentences - because the wrong was systemic, deliberate, and profound.

Iniquity is a word of moral weight: heavier than wrongdoing, wider than crime. A crime breaks a law; an iniquity breaks something older - the basic fairness owed between people. Reach past 'unfair' and 'illegal' to iniquity only when the injustice is deep, deliberate, and grave.

    Section 2

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    wickedness: Wickedness is plain moral evil of any kind — cruelty, malice, spite — and it makes no claim about fairness. Iniquity narrows the field: the evil it names is measured against the basic fairness owed between people. A tormentor's cruelty is wickedness; his rigged courts are iniquity.

    injustice: An injustice is unfairness of any size at all — a parking ticket you did not deserve qualifies. Iniquity reserves itself for unfairness that is profound, deliberate, and grave. Every iniquity is an injustice, but almost no everyday injustice rises to iniquity.

    villainy: Villainy is evil with a face — the schemes and deeds of a particular villain. Iniquity needs no single author: it can name the deep, systemic wrong of a whole institution or an era, with no lone villain left to blame.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    virtue: active moral goodness

    righteousness: firm, upright justice

    Where it sits · how grave the wrongdoing is

    misdeed → iniquity → atrocity

    misdeed vs. iniquity: A misdeed can be as small as a swiped cookie; iniquity is wrong on another scale entirely - profound, deliberate, and grave.

    atrocity vs. iniquity: An atrocity goes past even iniquity: iniquity names deep wickedness or injustice, while an atrocity is a single act of cruelty so extreme it shocks the world.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / literary

      Iniquity is solemn, almost scriptural - the word of tribunals, sermons, and histories. For everyday unfairness say injustice; for garden-variety badness say wrongdoing. Spend iniquity only on wrongs profound enough to bear its weight, or the sentence will sound overwrought.

Read closely, they described an engine of ruin: families taxed past what any harvest could pay, then stripped of their land for the debt, then hired back to work it at wages set by the same office. No single official had designed it, and no single one could have stopped it; the machinery simply turned, year after year, with everyone's signature and no one's name. In her book she refused softer words. What the archive preserved, she wrote, was iniquity — wrong done on the scale of a whole people, deliberate in every detail and owned by no one. Reviewers argued with her conclusions but not with her ledgers. The columns, they conceded, spoke for themselves.
