What It Means

Construe means to interpret the meaning of something.

See the word in context

The neighbors construed the half-finished fence as a property claim, though the builder had simply run out of lumber.

When the composer marked the finale only “with fire,” every conductor construed the phrase differently: one took it as speed, another as fury, a third as brightness — three performances grown from the same two words.

Construe is more exact than interpret because the puzzle is the meaning of particular words — ambiguous ones, read closely and defensibly, often as one thing or another. And mind the sound-alike trap: construe means to read meaning out of something, while construct means to build; the two share a Latin root and little else today.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    interpret: Interpret is the plain, broad word for making sense of anything — a poem, a statute, a shrug. Construe narrows it: the careful, often disputed reading of particular words or acts, usually in the frame 'construe something as something.' Every construal is an interpretation; not every interpretation is a construal.

    decode: Decode works out meaning that was deliberately hidden — a cipher, a scrambled signal, slang built to keep outsiders out. Construe untangles meaning that is open but ambiguous: the words sit in plain sight, and the work is deciding what they were meant to do.

    parse: To parse a sentence is to take it apart — identify its grammar, its parts, how the pieces connect. Construe goes past structure to intent: what the words, once parsed, were actually meant to do. You can parse a line perfectly and still not know how to construe it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    overlook: to pass by without taking any meaning at all

    garble: to scramble a message instead of drawing sense from it

    Where it sits · how much digging the meaning requires

    read → construe → decipher

    read vs. construe: To read a note is lighter than to construe it — reading takes the words in, while construing works out what they are really meant to do.

    decipher vs. construe: Decipher goes further than construe: construing untangles ambiguous meaning, but deciphering cracks meaning that is hidden or garbled on purpose.

      The sign at the park gate says only: 'No vehicles beyond this point.' For years nobody thought twice about it. Cars stayed out, and that seemed to be the whole story. Then a ten-year-old rode her electric scooter down the main path, and the parks board discovered how much work five words can refuse to do. Was a scooter a vehicle? A stroller? A delivery robot? Each camp construed the rule to fit its own picture of what the sign was for. Safety-minded neighbors read 'vehicle' as anything with wheels and a motor; parents of scooter kids read it as cars and trucks, full stop. The board finally voted to list examples on the sign itself -- an admission that words left open will be read, and read again, until someone narrows them.
