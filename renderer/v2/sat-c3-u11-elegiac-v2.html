elegiac

/el-uh-JY-uhk/ - the stress falls on JY

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Elegiac means mournfully reflective, expressing sorrow for what is past.

See the word in context

The liner's last captain gave an elegiac toast at the scrapyard gate, praising a ship that had outlived the age that built her.

The sportswriter's farewell column about the demolished ballpark was elegiac rather than angry: he named the ushers, the organ tunes, the July twilights — mourning them tenderly and asking for nothing back.

Elegiac is more than 'sad.' It names a particular tone: grief that looks backward, tender and reflective, for something already gone. Anger, panic, or fresh raw loss are sad; only the mourning backward look is elegiac.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    mournful: Mournful means full of plain grief — a mournful face, a mournful tune. Elegiac starts from that grief and adds a tender, backward-looking reflection on something already gone. A funeral procession is mournful; a memoir lingering over a childhood farm long since sold is elegiac.

    valedictory: Valedictory means spoken in farewell — the tone of a goodbye address — and a farewell can be proud or even cheerful. Elegiac is farewell weighted with mourning: sorrow for what is already past. Every elegiac work says goodbye, but many valedictory speeches shed no tears at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    exultant: openly rejoicing in a victory or an arrival

    lighthearted: carrying no weight of loss at all

    Where it sits · how heavily mourning colors the mood

    reflective → elegiac → funereal

    reflective vs. elegiac: Reflective is milder than elegiac — thoughtful about the past without any note of mourning in it.

    funereal vs. elegiac: Funereal goes further than elegiac — elegiac mourns the past tenderly, while funereal sounds like the funeral itself, heavy and dark.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    literary / critical

    Elegiac belongs to reviews and essays: it describes art or speech that mourns what is past — tenderly, reflectively, without bitterness. Plain 'sad' won't do, because elegiac requires the backward look. Use its unit-mate dirge for the funeral music itself; elegiac is for the tone that mourning leaves on words and images.

    The library's new photography exhibit covers one block of Marsh Street across forty years, ending the summer the block was cleared for the interchange. The mood is elegiac from the first frame. Nothing in the pictures is sad in itself — kids on stoops, a grocer weighing plums — but every caption is written in the past tense. Visitors move slowly. The photographs keep saying, gently, that all of this is gone: the stoops, the grocer, the summer light on brick that no longer stands. The curator resisted calling the show a protest. It mourns, she said, but it mourns tenderly, the way you remember a house you can never enter again. People leave quieter than they came in, less heartbroken than wistful, carrying the past-tense captions with them.
