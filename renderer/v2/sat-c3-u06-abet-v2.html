abet

/uh-BET/ - rhymes with regret

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Abet means to assist or encourage someone in wrongdoing.

See the word in context

The bookmaker could not have fixed the race alone; a stable groom abetted him by feeding the favorite a heavy mash an hour before post time.

The inquiry into the sold examination papers did not stop with the proctor who copied them; it also named the night clerk who abetted the scheme by passing along the cabinet's combination for a share of the fee.

Abet is sharper than 'help' because it applies only to wrongdoing — you can help a neighbor move, but you can only abet a crime — and it covers encouragement and quiet support as well as direct participation.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    assist: Assist is neutral help of any kind — you can assist a customer, a colleague, or a stranger, and no one thinks the worse of you for it. Abet lends help only to wrongdoing: use it and you are saying a crime or a wrong is being helped along, knowingly.

    enable: Enable means to make something possible — good or bad — and often without meaning to: a lax rule enables fraud its writers never imagined. Abet is narrower and always knowing: to abet is to lend deliberate help or encouragement to a wrong you understand is under way.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    thwart: to block a scheme before it succeeds

    oppose: to stand openly against it

    Where it sits · how big a part in the crime

    encourage → abet → orchestrate

    encourage vs. abet: To encourage a scheme is to cheer it on with words alone; encourage stops short of abet, which supplies real help — a key, a schedule, a lookout.

    orchestrate vs. abet: Orchestrate goes further than abet: the accomplice who abets a robbery lends it a hand, while the one who orchestrates it designs and directs the whole crime.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / legal

    Abet lives in the courtroom phrase 'aid and abet.' It names the active help a crime receives — where complicit (also in this unit) describes the partner who gives that help, and condone (also in this unit) means merely letting the wrong pass, abet is the helping itself.

    When federal agents finally broke the rum-running ring in 1926, the surprise was not the smugglers themselves but the quiet boatyard owner who had abetted them for years. He never touched a case of liquor and never sailed a single run. Instead, between crossings, he repainted their launches and swapped their nameplates, so the patrol cutter never recognized the same hull twice. At trial his lawyer argued that a man who carries no contraband commits no crime, that fresh paint is not smuggling, and that a coat of varnish can abet no one. The jury disagreed: he knew exactly what the boats were for, and disguising them made every crossing safer -- help as real as loading the cargo himself.
