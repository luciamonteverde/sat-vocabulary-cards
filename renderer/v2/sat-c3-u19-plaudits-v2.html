Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Plaudits means enthusiastic approval.

See the word in context

The bridge crew's overnight repair drew plaudits from drivers who had braced for a week of detours.

Her redesigned wheelchair ramp earned plaudits from the whole block — two thank-you pies, a hand-lettered banner, and a proclamation from the scout troop.

Plaudits are praise you can count: the individual bursts of approval — cheers, raves, congratulations — that rain on a success. The word is always plural, and it was born at the theater: Roman actors ended plays by calling plaudite! — 'now clap, everyone!'

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    applause: Applause is the literal clapping — approval delivered by hand and heard in the room where it happens. Plaudits are wider: bursts of enthusiastic approval that can arrive as applause, as cheers, or entirely in print. Applause needs an audience present; plaudits can come by mail.

    cheers: Cheers are voiced bursts of approval — praise you can hear across a stadium. Plaudits are broader: the bursts can be shouted, written, or printed, which is why a newspaper column can deliver plaudits but never a cheer. All cheering counts as plaudits; only the audible part is cheers.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    boos: the crowd's thumbs-down, out loud

    jeers: mocking shouts — disapproval with teeth

    Where it sits · how carried away the admirers get

    claps → plaudits → raves

    claps vs. plaudits: Claps are politeness — approval offered one pair of hands at a time; plaudits are that approval multiplied past counting.

    raves vs. plaudits: Raves go further than plaudits — plaudits are warm, earned approval; raves are the critics losing their composure entirely.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    journalistic / formal

    Plaudits is a headline word for praise arriving in bursts and from many hands — and it is always plural. Beside its unit-mates: acclaim is the overall climate of praise, an accolade is one formal honor, and plaudits are the individual rounds of applause — spoken, written, or literally clapped.
