ultimatum

/ul-tuh-MAY-tum/ - the stress falls on MAY

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Ultimatum means a final demand that carries a threat if it is refused.

See the word in context

The drummer's ultimatum reached the band by text: hire a real sound engineer before the tour bus leaves, or the drum kit rides home with me.

The co-owner put it plainly at last: drop the supplier whose cheese kept arriving spoiled, or she would sell her half of the cafe. An ultimatum, she admitted — but the health inspector was due in a month.

A demand asks and a warning cautions, but an ultimatum locks the door: final terms, a deadline, and a stated consequence, with nothing left to negotiate. If the other side can still haggle, it isn't an ultimatum yet.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    threat: A threat is a promised harm — do nothing, and it may still land. An ultimatum wraps a threat around a final choice: meet the stated demand by the stated deadline, or the harm follows. The threat is only the or-else; the ultimatum is the whole locked package of terms.

    warning: A warning cautions that something bad may happen, and it can stand alone — no terms attached, nothing asked of you at all. An ultimatum always asks: it bundles a demand, a deadline, and a stated consequence into one final package. Every ultimatum warns; very few warnings are ultimatums.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    overture: a friendly opening move toward agreement

    request: an ask that leaves the other side free to say no

    Where it sits · how much consequence backs the ask

    request → demand → ultimatum

    request vs. ultimatum: A request is far gentler than an ultimatum — it asks, and takes no for an answer.

    demand vs. ultimatum: A demand is milder than an ultimatum — it insists, but an ultimatum adds the clock and the consequence: do this by then, or else.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal

      Ultimatum is the word for the moment talk ends: unions, diplomats, and exhausted parents all issue them. Use 'demand' while negotiation is still alive, and 'warning' when there are no terms attached. The test is simple — an ultimatum always contains all three parts: the demand, the deadline, and the or-else.
