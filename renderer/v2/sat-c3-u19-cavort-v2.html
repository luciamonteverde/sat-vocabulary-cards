Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Cavort means to leap about, behave boisterously.

See the word in context

The spring lambs cavorted across the pasture, springing sideways at nothing at all.

Between floats, the costumed mascots cavorted down the parade route — leapfrogging traffic cones, spinning surprised bystanders, high-kicking through the marching band's gaps.

Cavort is bigger than play and bouncier than dance: it is whole-body, look-at-me friskiness — leaping, tumbling, wheeling about. Animals cavort in fields; children cavort in leaf piles; adults cavort mostly at weddings, and deny it on Monday.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    frolic: Frolic is light, happy play — children splashing in a fountain, a puppy loose in the leaves — gentle fun with no audience in mind. Cavort turns the volume up: bigger leaps, wilder wheeling, and a distinct pleasure in being watched. Frolickers amuse themselves; cavorters half expect applause.

    gambol: Gambol means to skip and leap playfully, and it is said mostly of lambs, foals, and small children — light-footed, innocent springing. Cavorting is rowdier and showier: not just leaps but tumbling, wheeling, and mugging for the crowd. A lamb gambols in a meadow; a mascot cavorts down a parade.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    trudge: to walk heavily, joy nowhere in sight

    plod: to move slowly and dully onward

    Where it sits · how wildly the body frolics

    skip → romp → cavort

    skip vs. cavort: A skip is one happy bounce in a walk — the smallest, tamest cousin of a cavort.

    romp vs. cavort: A romp is energetic, but cavorting is wilder still — add the leaping, the wheeling, and the pleasure in being watched.

      Word Choice

      informal / playful

      Cavort when the joy is physical and a little showy — bodies in motion, dignity optional. Frolic is gentler, romp is loose energetic play, and this unit's carouse is the tablebound cousin: carousers sing and pour; cavorters leap and spin.
