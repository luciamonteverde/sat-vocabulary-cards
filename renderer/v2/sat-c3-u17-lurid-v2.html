Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Lurid means ghastly and sensational — horror played up for shock.

See the word in context

The documentary skips lurid reenactments entirely and lets the court transcripts speak.

The museum's plague exhibit drew complaints for its lurid wall panels — boils rendered in wet reds, a looping soundtrack of moans — while the curators' own catalog told the same history in careful, quiet prose.

Lurid is exact because the fault lies in the telling, not the facts: the panels crank horror up for effect. The same history told plainly is merely grim; dressed in dripping red and staged shrieks, it turns lurid.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sensational: Sensational means pumped up to thrill or shock — the tabloid tone any exciting story can take on. Lurid is darker and more specific: sensationalism about the ghastly, horror cranked up for effect. A celebrity scandal can be sensational without being lurid; lurid needs gore or dread in the telling.

    gruesome: Gruesome describes the content itself — genuinely horrifying material that needs no exaggeration, like a crash scene or a battlefield. Lurid instead accuses the presentation: the same material dressed up to shock. A plain report of a disaster is gruesome; a retelling in blood-soaked headlines has turned lurid.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    restrained: held deliberately back

    matter-of-fact: plain reporting, no drama at all

    Where it sits · how far it leans into the gruesome

    vivid → lurid → ghoulish

    vivid vs. lurid: Vivid is bright and lifelike but honest — it stops well short of lurid, which cranks the horror for effect.

    ghoulish vs. lurid: Ghoulish goes further than lurid: past shock for the audience into open relish of the gruesome itself.

    Word Choice

    critical

    Lurid condemns how a thing is told or shown: details chosen to shock, horror played for thrills. Gruesome describes the content itself; lurid accuses the presentation. It pairs with this unit's garish — garish assaults the eye with color and lurid assaults taste with sensation, and both words disapprove as they describe.
