indigent

/IN-dih-junt/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Indigent means extremely poor; lacking the necessities of life.

See the word in context

The court appointed a public defender because the accused man was indigent and could not pay for a lawyer of his own.

The county launched a program that gives indigent seniors free bus passes and grocery vouchers. For residents living on almost nothing, the passes meant they could finally reach the pharmacy and the market without choosing between a ride and a meal.

The seniors in the program lack money for even basic needs like transportation and food, which is exactly what it means to be indigent.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    impoverished: Impoverished means made very poor, often by forces like drought, war, or job loss, and it can describe whole regions as easily as families. Indigent is narrower and more official: it labels a person who lacks the means to pay for basic needs, especially in legal and government writing.

    needy: Needy means lacking enough money for basic living, and it is the warm, everyday word charities favor: needy children, needy families. Indigent names the same hardship in a colder, clinical register, the term courts and agencies apply when deciding who qualifies for help.

    poverty-stricken: Poverty-stricken is a vivid, emotional description of people crushed by severe poverty. Indigent covers similar poverty without the drama: a neutral, formal label used in official paperwork and public programs. A journalist writes poverty-stricken to move readers; an agency writes indigent to sort cases.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    affluent: having plenty of money and possessions

    prosperous: financially successful and thriving

    Where it sits · how completely the money has run out

    struggling → indigent → penniless

    struggling vs. indigent: A struggling family finds money tight at the end of each month, while an indigent family lacks even the basic necessities of life.

    penniless vs. indigent: An indigent person cannot afford essentials, but a penniless person has literally nothing left at all.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal, often legal

      Indigent is common in legal and official writing, especially in the phrase 'indigent defendants,' people entitled to a court-appointed lawyer because they cannot pay for one. Do not confuse it with indignant, which means angry at something unfair.

      On her first day in the hospital billing office, Dana Okafor was handed a stack of applications and a rulebook with one word on the cover tab: indigent. The word felt harsh to her, and she said so. Her supervisor shook his head: harsh or not, it was the term the state statute used, and the statute was what made free care possible. The rule was exact. A patient qualified if income fell below a published line and no savings, property, or family support could cover the bill. Dana noticed the word appeared nowhere in the hospital's donation appeals, which spoke instead of neighbors down on their luck. By Friday she understood: the cold word was doing quiet work, deciding — case by case, without drama — who would not be billed at all.
