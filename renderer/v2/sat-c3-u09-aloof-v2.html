Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Aloof means reserved, distant.

See the word in context

Even after three seasons with the troupe, the understudy remained aloof, warming up alone in the wings while the cast traded jokes onstage.

Everyone at the reunion assumed the visiting cousin thought himself above them: he kept to the porch rail, answered warmly enough when approached, but never once crossed the lawn to join a conversation. He wasn't rude—just aloof, holding the whole gathering at arm's length.

Aloof is more precise than 'shy' or 'unfriendly' because it describes chosen distance without open hostility—an aloof person stays apart and lets others do the approaching, which can read as coolness even when no offense is meant.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    withdrawn: Withdrawn describes someone pulled inward — often by grief, illness, or exhaustion — who retreats even from people they used to welcome. Aloof is cooler and chosen: the aloof person prefers the distance and stays composed behind it. Withdrawal happens to you; aloofness is a stance you keep.

    detached: Detached means emotionally uninvolved — weighing things without taking sides, the way a fair referee stays detached from both teams. Aloof is social rather than judicial: an aloof person keeps people themselves at arm's length, cool and apart even at a friendly gathering. Detachment is neutrality; aloofness is distance.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    approachable: easy to walk up to and talk with

    sociable: actively seeks out company

    Where it sits · how hard it is to get close

    standoffish → aloof → unapproachable

    standoffish vs. aloof: Standoffish is a milder, everyday version of aloof — a standoffish clerk just skips the small talk and still serves you cheerfully, while an aloof one keeps a cool, deliberate distance the whole shift.

    unapproachable vs. aloof: Unapproachable goes further than aloof — an aloof person still answers warmly enough when you cross the room to them, but an unapproachable director has a wall around her that nobody will even try to cross.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral, slightly formal

    Aloof describes cool, self-chosen distance without open rudeness—the aloof stay composed and apart, letting others approach first. Keep it separate from two unit-mates: bashful people hang back out of blushing embarrassment, and diffident people out of self-doubt; the aloof hang back because they prefer the distance.
