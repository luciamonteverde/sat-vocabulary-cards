elaboration

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Elaboration means the development of something with fuller detail.

See the word in context

The patent office returned the filing with a one-line request for elaboration: how, exactly, does the hinge lock?

Two sentences in the witness's first statement — 'I heard a noise. I went outside.' — grew, under the detective's patient questions, into a page of elaboration: which stair creaked, what the porch light reached, how long the gate had stood open.

Elaboration develops what is already there with fuller detail. It neither corrects (that is revision) nor condenses (that is summary): the witness's account didn't change — it grew specific. When an editor asks for elaboration, the request is 'more,' not 'different.'

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fleshing-out: Fleshing-out is the working writer's word for adding the muscle and detail that turn a sketch into a full account. Elaboration is the formal name for that same growth — the word committees and editors use when they request it — and both leave the original idea standing.

    amplification: Amplification develops a point more fully, giving it room, force, and volume on the page. Elaboration grows in one particular direction — fuller detail. An amplified point may simply get louder and longer; an elaboration must get more specific about what is already there.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    summary: the same material shrunk to its main points

    bare-bones draft: the first thin version, detail still to come

    Where it sits · how far the account is filled in

    clarification → elaboration → specification

    clarification vs. elaboration: A clarification is a lighter ask than an elaboration — restate what you meant so it can't be misread; nothing new is added.

    specification vs. elaboration: Specification goes further than elaboration: elaboration fills the picture out, while specification pins every detail to exact numbers, dates, and measurements.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / academic

    Elaboration is the formal word for requested detail — committees and editors ask for it by name. Ask for clarification when the words are unclear, specification when exact figures are missing, and elaboration when the idea is sound but thin.

    The community cookbook committee loved Rosa's tamale recipe and rejected the card it came on. Her grandmother's instructions -- 'season well, steam until done' -- had fed a family for fifty years without another word. The committee's letter asked for elaboration before the next printing: how much chile in 'well,' how many minutes in 'done,' what size pot, what heat. Rosa spent a Saturday cooking with a notebook open, measuring what her hands had always known. The printed page now runs to half a column, and the committee kept one line of the original card at the top, exactly as her grandmother wrote it.
