Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Covet means to desire enviously what belongs to another.

See the word in context

My little brother covets my record player so openly that he has already cleared a shelf for it in his room.

The duke coveted his neighbor's harbor for years, mapping its docks from a hilltop he pretended to visit for the view.

Covet is not simple wanting: it is envious desire aimed at what belongs to someone else — wanting not just such a thing, but that one, the one with an owner. The tenth commandment uses exactly this word, which tells you how old the itch is.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    crave: To crave is to want something intensely — sleep, salt, adventure — with no owner anywhere in the picture. Covet fixes on a possession that already belongs to somebody else and minds who has it. A craving names a kind of thing; covet points at the one with an owner.

    envy: Envy is resenting that another person has something; you may feel it without wanting the thing itself at all. Covet adds the wanting to the resentment: envious desire aimed at taking that very possession for your own. Envy can sit and stew; covet starts making room.

    begrudge: To begrudge is to resent that someone has or enjoys something, and the feeling stops at resentment. Covet goes one step further: besides minding that they have it, you want that very thing for yourself. You can begrudge a rival's promotion without wanting the job at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    spurn: reject something with scorn

    renounce: give up all claim to something, formally

    Where it sits · how much envy enters the longing

    admire → desire → covet

    admire vs. covet: You admire the neighbor's roses without wanting to dig them up — admiration asks for nothing, unlike covet.

    desire vs. covet: Desire wants the thing itself; it stops short of covet, which wants the very one that belongs to someone else — and minds that it does.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / biblical

      Covet carries the old commandment's shadow: envious desire for what is another's. Want and crave are innocent by comparison; envy resents the owner but may not want the thing. Use covet when the wanting fixes on someone else's particular possession — the corner office, not corner offices.
