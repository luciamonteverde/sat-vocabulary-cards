Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Harrowing means deeply distressing or disturbing.

See the word in context

The lifeboat drill turned into a harrowing hour when the winch jammed with eight trainees swinging over open water.

The night the dam failed, the county's lone 911 dispatcher stayed at her console for nine harrowing hours. She talked one family onto a rooftop, lost the line twice, got it back, and kept her voice level while the building's windows shook. The relief operator who arrived at dawn found her still wearing the headset, unable to say her own name.

Harrowing is the exact word for her shift, because it means deeply distressing or disturbing — an ordeal that drags you through it and leaves marks. The word is a farm tool: a harrow is the spiked frame dragged across plowed ground, and a harrowing experience does that to the people inside it. Upsetting fades by the next day; appalling judges what you see from outside. Harrowing is what it cost to be there.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    wrenching: Wrenching twists the heart — grief or pity concentrated in one blow, like a farewell scene that leaves a room in tears. Harrowing is about the ordeal itself dragging you through it, hour after hour, and the marks it leaves. A moment wrenches; an ordeal harrows.

    nightmarish: Nightmarish says an experience had the warped, trapped logic of a bad dream — wrong turns, no exits, dread out of all proportion. Harrowing measures the toll instead: what living through the ordeal cost. A scene can look nightmarish from safety; harrowing is paid for in person.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    soothing: settles the nerves

    comforting: eases distress away

    Where it sits · how deeply the experience marks you

    upsetting → harrowing → shattering

    upsetting vs. harrowing: Upsetting spoils an afternoon and loosens its grip by dinner — nowhere near harrowing, which drags you through an ordeal and leaves marks.

    shattering vs. harrowing: Shattering goes further than harrowing — a harrowing ordeal lets you go when it ends; a shattering loss rearranges the life that has to continue.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      strong / narrative

      Harrowing belongs to ordeals and their retelling — escapes, rescues, courtroom accounts — and it weighs the toll on whoever lived or witnessed them. Its unit-mate appalling faces the other way, judging the thing itself: the report is appalling; the week it describes was harrowing. For milder turbulence, upsetting will do.
