Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Frivolous means of little importance.

See the word in context

Aunt Rosa calls crossword puzzles a frivolous use of a sharp mind — then finishes ours in pen before the kettle boils.

With the levee story breaking, the night editor spiked the piece on a water-skiing poodle. Nobody called the poodle boring — the newsroom had watched the clip nine times. But page one belonged to the flood maps, and a frivolous item, however delightful, could wait for Sunday.

Frivolous doesn't mean bad or dull; it means light — of little importance — where weight is owed. The poodle piece fails no test of fun, only the test of mattering. That mismatch between lightness and the moment's seriousness is exactly what frivolous marks.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    trifling: Trifling measures pure size: too small to deserve attention, whoever is paying it — a trifling sum, a trifling error. Frivolous measures a mismatch instead: something light claiming the time or money owed to weighty things. A trifling cost can be ignored; a frivolous purchase already spent the allowance.

    silly: Silly is the plain, everyday word for lacking sense — a silly answer just isn't reasonable. Frivolous makes a narrower charge: the thing may be perfectly sensible, only too light for the moment or the money it claims. A frivolous lawsuit can be argued with a straight face.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    weighty: heavy with real stakes

    solemn: grave in manner and purpose

    Where it sits · how empty of worth the amusement is

    lighthearted → frivolous → mindless

    lighthearted vs. frivolous: A lighthearted joke suits the moment and harms nothing; frivolous goes further, spending real attention on what doesn't merit it.

    mindless vs. frivolous: Mindless sits past frivolous: a frivolous quiz at least amuses, while a mindless one asks nothing of you at all — no thought survives contact with it.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral, mildly disapproving

      Frivolous judges a mismatch: light things given the time or money owed to weighty ones. It is gentler than gratuitous — a unit-mate — which faults what nothing called for at all. A frivolous purchase wastes an afternoon's allowance; a gratuitous insult has no excuse in the first place.

Morale, he wrote, was not a luxury, and the banjo was cheap medicine for a dark winter. Both sides, notice, agreed on the facts — the argument was over whether five kilos of music was weight stolen from survival or spent on it.
