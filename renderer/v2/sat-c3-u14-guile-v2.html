Meaning

What It Means

Guile means the use of cunning deceit to gain an advantage.

See the word in context

The campaign was won by guile: her team leaked a false itinerary, and the rival candidate spent the final week shadowing an empty bus.

What saved the border town was guile, not walls: the mayor emptied the granary into hidden cellars and invited the raiders' scouts to tour a 'starving' town not worth the trouble of sacking.

Guile is the talent itself — the standing ability to gain advantages by cunning deceit. Craftiness is its everyday name; treachery goes further and breaks faith. And note the word's peculiar power: guile outwits you without needing to tell a single checkable lie.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    craftiness: Craftiness is the everyday name for the talent guile names — getting your way by sly, tricky dealing. Guile is the more literary word, and it suggests the deceit is elegant, almost admirable in its skill; craftiness is homelier, as small as a card-table bluff.

    artifice: Artifice is clever contrivance — a trick built, not a lie told: a false itinerary, a staged tour, a prop with a job to do. Guile is the standing talent behind such devices; artifice names one constructed trick. A schemer has guile; the scheme shows artifice.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    innocence: plans nothing because it suspects nothing

    artlessness: straightforwardness with no schemes in it

    Where it sits · how far from fair play it falls

    shrewdness → guile → treachery

    shrewdness vs. guile: Shrewdness is milder than guile — shrewdness reads the game sharply but still plays it straight.

    treachery vs. guile: Treachery goes further than guile: guile outwits strangers and rivals, while treachery betrays the very people who trusted you.

      Word Choice

      literary / evaluative

      Guile names the talent for cunning deceit, and writers reach for it when the deceit is elegant. Beguile, in this unit, is guile in action on a charmed victim; wily describes the person who owns the talent. When the cleverness is honest, the fair words are finesse or shrewdness.

      At forty-one, Ilona Vasquez gave away twenty years and a foot of reach to every opponent on the veterans' tour. She won anyway, and the how of it was the tour's favorite subject. Her matches ran on guile: a serve that looked identical whether it curved wide or straight, a slow ball disguised as a hard one until the last half-second, an exhausted slump between points that vanished the instant the ball was live. Younger players studied the film and swore nothing she did was illegal; nothing was. She simply made you certain of the wrong thing, point after point, until the set was gone.
