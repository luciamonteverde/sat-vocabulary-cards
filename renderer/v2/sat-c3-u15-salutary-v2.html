salutary

/SAL-yuh-tair-ee/ - the stress falls on SAL

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Salutary means producing a genuinely beneficial effect — often a shock or setback that does you good even though it stings at the time.

See the word in context

Losing the opening scrimmage 40–0 proved salutary: the team stopped coasting, rebuilt its defense, and never walked into another game overconfident.

The editor's brutal notes turned out to be salutary — the young reporter shed her habits of padding and hedging, and the leaner stories she filed afterward won her the beat she had wanted for years.

Notice the arc: the experience was unpleasant, but its effect was health-giving, and that is the signature of salutary. The word descends from Latin salus, 'health,' and it still grades experiences the way a doctor would — by whether the patient ends up better.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    beneficial: Beneficial means producing good results of any kind, pleasant or not; salutary narrows it — the benefit typically arrives through something unwelcome, a sting that turns out to be good for you.

    wholesome: Wholesome means good for health or character by its own nature — wholesome food, wholesome habits; salutary is judged by aftermath instead: even a defeat can be salutary if it leaves you better.

    corrective: Corrective means designed to fix a fault; a corrective step is engineered on purpose, while a salutary one may be pure accident — the scare nobody planned that finally makes everyone careful.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    harmful: leaving someone worse off than before

    ruinous: causing lasting damage or collapse

    Where it sits · how much lasting good it works

    agreeable → salutary → transformative

    agreeable vs. salutary: Agreeable is far milder than salutary: an agreeable afternoon merely pleases while it lasts, while a salutary one leaves you measurably better off after it ends.

    transformative vs. salutary: Transformative goes beyond salutary: a salutary setback improves your habits, while a transformative one rebuilds the whole enterprise into something new.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / approving

    Salutary praises outcomes, not intentions, and it gravitates to unpleasant subjects: salutary lessons, salutary shocks, salutary defeats. If the experience was enjoyable from the start, beneficial usually fits better — save salutary for the medicine that tasted terrible and worked.
