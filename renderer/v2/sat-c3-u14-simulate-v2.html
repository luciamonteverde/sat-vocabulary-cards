simulate

/SIM-yuh-layt/ - the stress falls on SIM

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Simulate means to reproduce the appearance or workings of something — to produce a convincing stand-in for the real thing without being it.

See the word in context

The hoverfly cannot sting, but its yellow-and-black bands simulate a wasp so convincingly that birds give it the same wide berth.

Before the crew ever touches the real spacecraft, they log four hundred hours in a mock-up that simulates it down to the switch guards — the cabin tilts, the instruments fail on cue, and the windows play footage of the turning Earth.

The mock-up is not a spacecraft and never pretends to leave the ground. To simulate is to reproduce the conditions and behavior of the real thing so faithfully that practice inside the copy transfers to the original — imitation built for use, not for deceit.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    mimic: To mimic is to copy the look or sound of something in the moment — a comedian mimics a senator's drawl, a lyrebird mimics a chainsaw. To simulate is to rebuild the thing's whole behavior: conditions, responses, consequences, usually so they can be studied or practiced.

    replicate: To replicate is to produce an exact duplicate — the same experiment, the same result, the same object again. To simulate is to produce a convincing stand-in: the flight never leaves the ground, and nobody needs it to, because behaving like the real thing is the entire job.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    undergo: to face the real event itself, stakes and all

    originate: to create something new rather than model the real

    Where it sits · how far the imitation is pushed toward passing for real

    mimic → simulate → counterfeit

    mimic vs. simulate: Mimic is lighter than simulate: a mimic copies the look or sound of a thing in the moment, while simulating rebuilds its whole behavior — conditions, responses, and all.

    counterfeit vs. simulate: Counterfeit goes further than simulate: a simulation is openly an imitation, but a counterfeit is built to be taken for the genuine article — imitation pushed all the way into fraud.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    technical / neutral

    Simulate is morally neutral machinery: flight crews, economists, and epidemiologists all simulate, and nobody blames them, because a simulation announces itself as an imitation. The deception words in this unit begin exactly where that announcement stops — feign is a person faking their own state, counterfeit is imitation built to be taken for the genuine article, and legerdemain hides the imitation altogether. Keep simulate for the honest copy: real behavior, openly reproduced, usually so something can be tested or practiced safely.

    On Tuesday afternoons, the medical school's east ward admits twelve patients who are not sick. They are trained actors, and the ward exists to simulate the worst hour of a residency: one 'patient' seizing, another quietly slipping into shock, a third demanding a diagnosis at the top of his voice. Monitors display vitals scripted in a control room; the crash cart is stocked; the clock is real. Students know none of it is genuine and sweat anyway, because the ward reproduces how an emergency behaves — its pacing, its noise, its decisions — without ever putting a life at risk. Errors made on Tuesday, the dean likes to say, are the cheapest ones the hospital sells.
