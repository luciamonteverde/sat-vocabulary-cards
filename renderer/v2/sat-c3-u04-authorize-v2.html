authorize

/AW-thuh-ryze/ - the stress falls on AW

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Authorize means to give official permission for something to be done — a go-ahead granted before the act, by someone whose position carries that power.

See the word in context

The city council authorized the night market for one trial summer, over the parking committee's objections.

For two years the observatory's staff asked to open the roof deck for public star nights, and the request died on desk after desk — until the new director finally authorized it, adding a single condition beside her signature: railings first.

Authorize is official through and through: not agreement, not encouragement, but formal permission granted by someone whose position holds that power. The director's signature is what turns star nights from a wish into a sanctioned program — the same Latin auctor, 'originator,' that gives us author: to authorize an act is to stand behind it as its official source.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    empower: To empower is to hand someone a lasting capacity to act — a law can empower an agency for a generation. Authorize is narrower and more transactional: it grants official permission for a particular act, and it can be as small as one signature clearing one purchase.

    sanction: To sanction is to give official approval with a blessing flavor, often to something already arranged — a league sanctions a tournament. Authorize stands at the front door: it is the advance permission that lets the act happen at all. (Watch the trap: sanction can also mean to penalize; authorize never flips.)

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    forbid: to order that something must not be done

    prohibit: to ban an act formally, by rule or law

    Where it sits · how much official force stands behind the go-ahead

    allow → authorize → mandate

    allow vs. authorize: Allowing something is weaker than authorizing it: anyone can allow by simply not objecting, while to authorize takes an office — a signature or a vote that actively grants the permission.

    mandate vs. authorize: A mandate goes further than authorizing: a board that authorizes repairs lets the crews proceed, but a board that mandates them makes the work compulsory — permission hardened into command.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / administrative

      Authorize is the paperwork verb of offices and agencies: budgets, permits, launches, missions. Two things travel with it. First, standing — only someone whose position holds the power can authorize, which is why a friend agrees to your plan while a board authorizes it. Second, timing — authorization comes first, clearing the road ahead; approval of a text already drafted and signed wants a different verb (treaties are ratified). People are authorized to act — 'authorized personnel only' — and the noun authorization names the grant itself.

      For thirty years, anyone wanting to drill a well in Harlan County has started at a single metal desk in the courthouse basement. That desk belongs to Ruth Okafor, the county clerk whose office must authorize every new well before a drill bit touches the ground. The power is not hers personally; it comes with the seal. State law names her office, not her, as the one that can grant permission. Farmers sometimes grumble that a neighbor's nod ought to be enough. Ruth points to the aquifer maps and shakes her head.
