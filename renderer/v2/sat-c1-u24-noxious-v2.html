noxious

/NOK-shus/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Noxious means harmful or poisonous, especially to breathe or be exposed to.

See the word in context

Never mix bleach with ammonia: the pair releases noxious fumes that can sear your lungs in seconds.

On the first day of chemistry lab, Mr. Okafor pointed to the glass-fronted cabinet along the wall. 'Anything that produces harsh vapors gets opened in there,' he said. He explained that the fume hood pulls air up and away, so that noxious gases never drift toward students' faces. The rule had no exceptions — even mildly irritating vapors went under the hood.

The gases are dangerous specifically to breathe — the direct, physical, toxic harm that noxious names.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    toxic: Toxic means poisonous to the body, and it is a property a substance carries wherever it sits — in a bottle, a bloodstream, or a landfill. Noxious adds the idea of exposure: fumes, gases, and vapors that harm people who breathe or contact them. Sealed mercury is toxic; the vapor escaping a broken seal is noxious.

    poisonous: Poisonous means capable of causing illness or death, and the harm usually travels by swallowing or touch — poisonous berries, a poisonous bite. Noxious harm arrives mostly on the air: fumes and gases that hurt you simply through exposure. A berry is poisonous on the tongue; a gas leak is noxious in the lungs.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    harmless: causing no damage at all

    fresh: clean and safe to breathe

    Where it sits · how much bodily harm it causes

    unpleasant → noxious → lethal

    unpleasant vs. noxious: An unpleasant smell merely offends the nose, while a noxious one can genuinely harm you.

    lethal vs. noxious: A lethal gas is the extreme beyond noxious — deadly outright, not just harmful.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    scientific, safety warnings

    Noxious is the most physical of this unit's harm words: unlike deleterious or pernicious, which often describe abstract or gradual damage, noxious almost always describes substances — fumes, gases, chemicals — that hurt you through direct exposure.
