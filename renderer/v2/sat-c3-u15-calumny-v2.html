Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Calumny means an attempt to ruin someone's reputation by spreading lies.

See the word in context

When the actor turned down the role, the studio fed gossip columnists a calumny about his drinking that trailed him for a decade.

The librarian's neighbors received an anonymous letter claiming she had been fired from her last job for stealing rare books; no such firing had ever happened, and the letter's author knew it. The calumny did its work anyway — dinner invitations dried up, and patrons began counting the volumes she handled.

Calumny is stronger and more deliberate than gossip or rumor: a rumor may be careless, but calumny is a knowing lie, manufactured for the specific purpose of wrecking someone's good name.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    smear: A smear is an informal word for a campaign to muddy someone's image — a run of insinuations and half-truths spread to make a person look dirty. Calumny is more surgical and more formal: one knowing, invented lie built to wreck a specific reputation.

    libel: Libel is the legal word for reputation-damaging lies set down in print or writing. Calumny is the older, literary word for the invented lie itself, whatever its medium — a calumny becomes libel the moment it is published.

    defamation: Defamation is the legal umbrella for any attack on a person's good name, spoken or written. Calumny stresses the lie's cold deliberateness — a story invented on purpose to destroy — where defamation only names the harm to the reputation.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    praise: words meant to lift a reputation rather than ruin it

    admiration: high regard expressed openly and sincerely

    Where it sits · how deliberately the talk aims to harm

    gossip → slander → calumny

    gossip vs. calumny: Gossip may be idle and half-believed even by the people repeating it; it is far milder than calumny, which is never idle.

    slander vs. calumny: Slander names the offense of spreading spoken lies, but it stops just short of calumny, which stresses the cold deliberateness of the lie and the intent to destroy.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Calumny is the formal noun for the lie itself — the invented story built to ruin a name. Its verb-side partner in this unit is vilify, which names the act of attacking a reputation; you commit calumny when you vilify someone with invented charges. In everyday contexts, 'slander' (spoken) or 'libel' (written) are the usual words.

    The story that the old composer had poisoned his young rival is a textbook calumny, and scholars can even name its author. A jealous secretary, passed over for a court post, invented the poisoning tale whole and planted it in three salons in a single week. He knew it was false; his own diary says so, in a line historians did not find for a century. The lie did its work. Commissions dried up, and the composer died believing his name was ruined for good. The diary's discovery cleared him, though the poisoning story still surfaces, retold by people who never learn it was built to destroy him.
