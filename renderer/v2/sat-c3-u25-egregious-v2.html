egregious

/ih-GREE-juhs/ - the stress falls on GREE

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Egregious means outstandingly, shockingly bad — a fault or offense so conspicuous it stands out from all the ordinary ones.

See the word in context

Even the arbitration lawyer winced at the gym contract's egregious fine print, which kept billing members for two full years after they canceled.

Continuity slips happen in every film, but the courtroom drama's was egregious: the defendant's plaster cast switched arms three times in a single scene, and by the third switch the audience had begun counting aloud.

Ordinary slips escape notice; this one had the whole theater keeping score. That is the claim egregious makes — not merely that something is wrong, but that its badness stands out from the ordinary run of faults by a margin everyone can see.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    flagrant: Flagrant is about openness: the offense is committed in plain view, almost defiantly, with no attempt to hide. Egregious ranks the offense itself — how shockingly bad it is — whether or not anyone saw it happen.

    appalling: Appalling names the reaction — it dismays and horrifies whoever looks at it. Egregious measures the offense against its ordinary kind: this error, this violation, stands out from all the routine ones.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    venial: minor and easily forgiven

    trifling: too slight to deserve notice

    Where it sits · how far past ordinary the badness stands out

    glaring → egregious → unconscionable

    glaring vs. egregious: Glaring is weaker than egregious: a glaring mistake is merely easy to spot, while an egregious one is shocking in how bad it is, not just in how visible.

    unconscionable vs. egregious: Unconscionable goes further than egregious — an egregious foul outrages the crowd, but an unconscionable one leaves the offender with no moral defense at all.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / condemning

      Egregious is a ranking word: it does not just call the thing bad, it certifies that the badness stands out from the ordinary run — an egregious error, violation, breach. The old approving sense ('an egregious talent') is dead; using it as praise today reads as a mistake. Its near-twin gregarious (met in the root check above) kept the flock's warmth instead.
