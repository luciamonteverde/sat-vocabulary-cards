promulgate

/PROM-uhl-gayt/ - the stress falls on PROM

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Promulgate means to formally and officially proclaim a law or rule to the public.

See the word in context

Universities promulgate their honor codes loudly during orientation week, printing them in every handbook and reading them aloud at convocation.

When the island nation adopted its first constitution, criers promulgated it the old way: read in full from every church step, printed on broadsheets, nailed to the harbor board — so that no fisherman could later claim the law was a secret.

Promulgate is official and outward-facing: an authority formally proclaims a law, doctrine or policy and puts it before the public. Announcing tells; promulgating tells with authority and on the record, which is why constitutions are promulgated while parties are merely announced.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    proclaim: To proclaim is to announce something publicly and grandly — from a balcony, in banner type. Promulgate adds legal machinery to the moment: an authority formally puts a law or rule on the record before the public. A love of trumpets is enough to proclaim; only office lets you promulgate.

    decree: To decree is to command by authority: the ruling itself, the order that something now be so. To promulgate is to spread the official word of a law or rule until the public is presumed to know it. A decree commands; promulgation broadcasts what was commanded.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    bury: to tuck something where no one will find it

    hush: to keep a matter quiet on purpose

    Where it sits · how official and far-reaching the notice

    post → announce → promulgate

    post vs. promulgate: To post a notice is the minimum gesture — one sheet on one board; it stops far short of promulgate.

    announce vs. promulgate: Announce goes further than post, actively telling an audience, but promulgate crowns the ladder: a formal, official proclamation pushed out until the whole public is presumed to know.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / official

    Promulgate belongs to governments, courts and churches: laws, doctrines and regulations are promulgated. For everyday news, announce; for informal spreading, circulate — a word in this unit's reference list. If no authority stands behind the message, promulgate will sound comically grand.

    The state's new boating-safety rule was approved in March, but approval was only the beginning. By law, the wildlife department could not enforce a rule the public had never been given; first it had to promulgate it. So the agency printed the rule in the official register, mailed a summary to every license holder, and placed notices at all two hundred boat launches. 'A rule kept in a filing cabinet binds no one,' the commissioner told reporters. 'Our job is to put this rule in front of every boater until no one can claim they never heard.' Only in June, when the campaign ended, did wardens begin writing tickets -- for a rule that was now, in every sense, public.
