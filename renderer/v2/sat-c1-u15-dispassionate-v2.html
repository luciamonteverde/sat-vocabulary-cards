Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Dispassionate means calm, objective, and not influenced by strong feeling.

See the word in context

A good judge must remain dispassionate, weighing the evidence carefully no matter how emotional the courtroom becomes.

After the warehouse accident, the safety investigator interviewed every witness in the same calm, methodical way, recording what each person saw without ever hinting at whom she blamed. Her dispassionate approach reassured workers that the final report would be fair.

The investigator sets aside emotion and personal opinion to weigh the facts evenly, which is exactly what makes her approach dispassionate.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    impartial: Impartial means favoring neither side - it is about the absence of bias between parties. Dispassionate is about the absence of emotion in yourself: judging on evidence with feeling set aside. A referee can be seething inside and still call the game impartially; a dispassionate referee has shut the seething out entirely.

    objective: Objective means grounded in facts that anyone could check rather than in personal feeling or opinion - it describes the basis of a judgment. Dispassionate describes the judge: a person who has set emotion aside to weigh things fairly. The evidence is objective; the investigator calmly weighing it is dispassionate.

    evenhanded: Evenhanded means treating all sides with equal fairness - the same rules and the same benefit of the doubt for everyone. Dispassionate is about temperature rather than balance: emotion has been set aside entirely. An evenhanded critic holds every film to the same standard; a dispassionate one keeps personal feeling out of the verdict.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    biased: unfairly favoring one side

    emotional: ruled by strong feelings

    Where it sits · how fully feeling is set aside

    composed → dispassionate → clinical

    composed vs. dispassionate: A composed person keeps their feelings under control, while a dispassionate person sets feelings aside entirely in order to judge fairly.

    clinical vs. dispassionate: A dispassionate reviewer stays fair and neutral, but a clinical one can seem so detached that no warmth comes through at all.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal

      Dispassionate is a compliment in contexts that demand fairness, as in 'a dispassionate analysis' or 'a dispassionate observer.' It means impartial and clear-headed, not cold or uncaring, and it should never be confused with compassionate, which means caring and sympathetic.

Colleagues describe her method as almost unsettlingly dispassionate: at a wreck she is all tape measure and notebook, no matter whose train it was. She insists the habit is learned, not natural.
