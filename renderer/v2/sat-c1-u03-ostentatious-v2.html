ostentatious

/os-ten-TAY-shus/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Ostentatious means designed to impress others through an excessive, showy display, especially of wealth or importance.

See the word in context

The billionaire's ostentatious yacht came complete with a helicopter pad, a private cinema, and gold-plated bathroom fixtures.

At his daughter's sixteenth birthday, the businessman rented an entire hotel ballroom, hired a headline pop star, and had the cake flown in from Paris — a display so ostentatious that several guests admitted feeling embarrassed on his behalf.

The party wasn't just generous; it was calculated to show off wealth in a way that made others uncomfortable — that showy excess is the heart of "ostentatious."

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    flashy: Flashy emphasizes surface glitter — chrome, sequins, gold trim — appearance doing all the work. Ostentatious is about motive as much as look: an excessive display staged to impress others with wealth or importance. A flashy jacket may just be loud; an ostentatious one is a boast.

    pretentious: Pretentious means claiming a status one may not deserve — cultured, brilliant, important. Ostentatious is about the show of wealth or importance itself, deserved or not. A billionaire's gold-plated yacht is ostentatious but not pretentious; the money is real, the display is just excessive.

    extravagant: Extravagant means spending or consuming far beyond what is reasonable, and it needs no audience — you can be extravagant entirely in private. Ostentatious display exists to be seen: excess staged to impress others. Every ostentatious purchase is extravagant; a secret one can't be ostentatious.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    understated: restrained, letting quality speak without display

    modest: deliberately unassuming

    Where it sits · how deliberately the display seeks attention

    noticeable → showy → ostentatious

    noticeable vs. ostentatious: A noticeable gold watch might catch someone's eye without seeming boastful — far short of ostentatious, which puts on a display designed to impress.

    showy vs. ostentatious: A showy gold watch draws deliberate attention to its own cost — one step short of ostentatious, which turns the whole display into a boast about wealth or importance.

      Word Choice

      critical, negative edge

      Ostentatious almost always carries a critical edge — calling something ostentatious suggests the display is excessive, tasteless, or meant to impress rather than simply enjoyed.
