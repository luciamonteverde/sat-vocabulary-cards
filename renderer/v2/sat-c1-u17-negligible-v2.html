negligible

/NEG-li-juh-bul/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Negligible means so small in amount or degree as to be not worth considering.

See the word in context

The engineers reported that the thicker insulation raised the car's total weight by a negligible amount, less than the weight of a water bottle.

The nutritionist reassured the worried caller that the trace of sodium in the sparkling water was negligible. At two milligrams per bottle, the amount was far too small to have any effect on even the strictest diet.

Two milligrams is a real amount, but it is so small in quantity that it can be safely ignored, which is exactly what negligible means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    slight: Slight means small in degree: a slight delay, a slight improvement. A slight thing can still deserve a mention. Negligible goes further: the amount is so small it can be safely ignored altogether. A slight cost still gets a line in the budget; a negligible one does not.

    minute: Minute means extremely small in size or amount, like a minute crack in a lens, yet minute things can still matter enormously. Negligible adds a verdict: the amount is too small to have any effect worth considering. Plenty of minute amounts are anything but negligible.

    insignificant: Insignificant means too small to have any real effect or leave any mark, and it can dismiss anything: a detail, an event, a person. Negligible is the more technical cousin, aimed at amounts and degrees. A negligible risk or increase is one you can drop from the calculation.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    substantial: large in amount or degree

    considerable: notably large in size or extent

    Where it sits · how close to nothing the amount is

    minor → negligible → nonexistent

    minor vs. negligible: A minor cost is small but still worth noting, while a negligible cost is so small it is not worth considering at all.

    nonexistent vs. negligible: A negligible amount is real but too tiny to matter, whereas a nonexistent amount is not there at all.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral

      Negligible describes a quantity or degree so small it can be safely ignored, as in 'a negligible risk,' 'a negligible increase in cost,' or 'a negligible amount of rainfall.' Trivial, also taught in this unit, describes something unimportant in a different way: it is about the significance of a matter or issue rather than the size of an amount. Do not confuse negligible with negligent, which describes a person who carelessly fails to take proper care of something.
