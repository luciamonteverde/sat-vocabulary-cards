lyrical

/LEER-ih-kuhl/

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Lyrical means expressing feeling in a beautiful, song-like, imaginative way.

See the word in context

The memoir's lyrical passages turn the desert into a living thing, describing dunes that "breathe slowly under the weight of the stars."

In her essay on tide pools, the biologist turned suddenly lyrical, calling each pool 'a small glass museum the sea forgets and refills twice a day.'

The word describes the imaginative, feeling-rich style of the writing—the poetic image of a 'glass museum'—not any actual music. That is the heart of lyrical: beautiful, song-like expression in language or art.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    poetic: Poetic is the closest cousin: language with the beauty and compression of poetry, whatever its mood or shape. Lyrical adds one specific quality — song. Lyrical writing flows with musical, feeling-rich grace, as if it could be sung. A bleak, jagged poem is still poetic; only the flowing kind is lyrical.

    expressive: Expressive covers any strong conveying of feeling — a scowl, a shout, or a plain blunt sentence can be expressive. Lyrical is a narrower compliment: the feeling comes out in beautiful, song-like language. An angry rant is expressive; a passage that sings its emotion is lyrical.

    evocative: Evocative writing calls up images, moods, and memories in the reader, whatever its style. Lyrical describes the style itself: flowing, musical, beautiful phrasing. One flat sentence can be deeply evocative if it summons the right memory; lyrical passages must sing, whatever they summon.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    clinical: Clinical writing is detached and technical, stripped of the feeling that lyrical writing pours in.

    dry: Dry prose delivers facts without color or emotion—the opposite of lyrical.

    Where it sits · how musically the emotion overflows

    heartfelt → lyrical → rhapsodic

    heartfelt vs. lyrical: Heartfelt writing is sincere in feeling but may be plain; lyrical shapes that feeling into beautiful, song-like language.

    rhapsodic vs. lyrical: Rhapsodic expression is so ecstatically lyrical it overflows, sometimes past the point of control.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    literary, appreciative

    Lyrical usually appears in praise of writing, film, or art with imaginative, flowing beauty—a critic's favorite word for prose that feels like poetry. It does not describe a literal tune: a catchy melody is melodic, while the sentences describing that melody can be lyrical.
