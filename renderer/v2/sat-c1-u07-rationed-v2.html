rationed

/RASH-und/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Rationed means strictly controlled and capped how much of something in short supply each person could receive, as governments do with food or fuel during a shortage or war.

See the word in context

Rationed means strictly controlled and capped how much of something in short supply each person could receive — as when wartime governments rationed gasoline and sugar, issuing coupon books that fixed every family's weekly share.

When the blizzard pinned the climbers in their tents for a week, the expedition leader rationed the remaining food, doling out one energy bar per person each morning.

The leader strictly controlled a supply that was running short — fixed, small shares handed out on a schedule. The one-bar-per-person rule is exactly the kind of hard cap that rationed describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    allotted: Allotted means given out as a fixed share — each speaker's allotted ten minutes, an allotted plot of land. It is neutral bookkeeping: shares assigned, no crisis implied. Rationed means shares were capped because supply ran short. Time is allotted at every meeting; food is rationed only in a shortage.

    apportioned: Apportioned means divided among parties according to some rule of fairness — costs apportioned among partners, seats apportioned by population. The focus is the formula for splitting. Rationed focuses on scarcity: a supply too small for normal use, doled out in strict, capped shares until it stretches.

    restricted: Restricted means held within strict bounds — restricted access, a restricted diet. It limits use without necessarily handing anything out. Rationed is restriction plus distribution: the scarce supply is still given to everyone, just in fixed, capped shares. A restricted door stays shut; rationed bread still reaches each table.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    lavished: poured out generously, without limit

    squandered: used up carelessly and wastefully

    Where it sits · how tightly the supply is restricted

    portioned → rationed → withheld

    portioned vs. rationed: Portioned just means divided into servings, with no scarcity implied — rationed adds strict caps forced by a shortage.

    withheld vs. rationed: Withheld goes further than rationed: a rationed supply is still handed out in small shares, but a withheld one isn't given out at all.

    Word Choice

    Historical & practical

    Rationed appears constantly in writing about wars, shortages, and expeditions — governments ration fuel, ships ration fresh water. You can also use it lightly ('I rationed my Halloween candy through November'), and the noun form is a ration: the fixed share itself.

    When the ferry workers went on strike in January, the village of Brinmore had no boat, no bridge, and one small shop. The shopkeeper, Mrs. Voss, counted her stockroom on the first morning and made a decision before opening the doors. Milk, eggs, and flour would be rationed: one carton, one dozen, one bag per family per week, marked in a ledger by the till. Nobody liked it, but nobody went without. The caps meant the last family through the door on Friday found the same shelves as the first. When the boats returned three weeks later, half a shelf of flour remained — proof that small, fixed shares had stretched a short supply exactly far enough.
