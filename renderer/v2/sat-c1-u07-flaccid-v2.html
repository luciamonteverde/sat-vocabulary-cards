flaccid

/FLAK-sid/ or /FLASS-id/

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Flaccid means completely loose and floppy — lacking the firmness or strength something is supposed to have.

See the word in context

Flaccid means limp and soft — lacking the firmness something should have, like a balloon three days after the party, hanging flaccid and wrinkled from its ribbon.

Distracted by a phone call, Nadia boiled the celery far too long, and the once-crisp stalks turned flaccid, drooping over the edge of the plate.

The stalks went from crisp to drooping — a complete loss of the firmness they were supposed to have. That physical limpness is exactly what flaccid describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    limp: Limp means without stiffness or firmness right now — a limp handshake, limp lettuce, limp noodles. It is the everyday word. Flaccid means the same physical looseness but sounds clinical and precise, the term a doctor or gardener might use for muscles or stems that have lost their firmness.

    droopy: Droopy describes things hanging or sagging downward weakly — droopy eyelids, a droopy houseplant. It is about posture: which way something sags. Flaccid is about internal firmness being gone entirely, whatever position the thing is in. A droopy flower bends over; a flaccid stem has no firmness left inside.

    floppy: Floppy means loose and bending easily — describing things that visibly flop about when moved, like a hat brim, a puppy's ears, or an old paperback's cover. It is casual and a little playful. Flaccid is its clinical cousin: the formal word for tissue or material that has lost the firmness it should have.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    firm: solid and holding its shape

    taut: stretched tight, with no slack

    Where it sits · how much firmness remains

    soft → flaccid → lifeless

    soft vs. flaccid: Soft things yield to pressure but can still hold their shape; flaccid things have lost their firmness completely and droop.

    lifeless vs. flaccid: Lifeless goes beyond flaccid — a flaccid stem still has some substance to it, but a lifeless one has nothing left at all.

      Word Choice

      Physical & slightly formal

      Flaccid is mostly literal — muscles, plant stems, balloons, dough that should be firm but isn't. Writers occasionally stretch it to arguments or prose ('a flaccid essay'), but in everyday speech 'limp' is the natural choice; flaccid sounds clinical.
