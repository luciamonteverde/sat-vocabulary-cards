Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Arduous means requiring great effort; difficult and tiring.

See the word in context

Restoring the overgrown orchard - clearing brambles and regrafting every tree by hand - was an arduous project that filled Ana's entire first year on the farm.

Training for her paramedic license while working night shifts made Priya's year arduous: every free hour went to practice drills and thick manuals, and she finished most evenings too drained to cook.

Arduous is sharper than plain 'hard' because it names effort that is both difficult and tiring over a long stretch - work that wears you down as it challenges you. A tricky puzzle is hard; a year of night-shift study is arduous.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    taxing: A taxing afternoon of interviews drains you, but taxing usually names a short, sharp demand - one exam, one shift, one steep climb. Arduous stretches the strain out: work that stays difficult and tiring over weeks or months, wearing you down as it goes.

    onerous: Onerous describes a duty or obligation that weighs on you as a burden - an onerous contract, a chore you never asked for. Arduous points at the effort itself: however the task landed on you, doing it is difficult and tiring over a long stretch.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    easy: requiring little effort at all

    painless: done without strain or discomfort

    Where it sits · how heavily the effort wears you down

    demanding → arduous → grueling

    demanding vs. arduous: A demanding class asks a lot of you each week; arduous goes further, naming work so difficult and tiring that finishing it feels like an ordeal.

    grueling vs. arduous: Grueling pushes past arduous: an arduous hike wears you out, while a grueling one punishes you until you wonder whether you can go on.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / neutral

      Arduous is the broadest of this unit's 'hard work' words: it says a task is difficult and tiring, whether the strain is mental or physical. Reach for laborious when the point is long, slow, careful toil, and for strenuous when the demand falls on the body's strength and stamina. In casual talk, 'tough' or 'exhausting' does the job; arduous adds a formal note of a long ordeal.

      In 1879, the lexicographer James Murray promised his publishers a complete dictionary of English within ten years. The work proved more arduous than anything he had imagined. Volunteers mailed in two million quotation slips, and each one had to be read, sorted, and filed by hand. Murray worked in an iron shed he called the Scriptorium, standing at his desk ten hours a day, six days a week, for decade after decade. Friends urged him to rest; he answered that the alphabet did not rest. When he died in 1915, thirty-six years in, the dictionary stood only at the letter T. Colleagues finished the final volume in 1928 -- forty-nine years of slow, grinding labor for a book its makers had once expected to finish in ten.
