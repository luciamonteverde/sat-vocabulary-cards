celebratory

/SEL-uh-bruh-tor-ee/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Celebratory means relating to or marking a celebration; festive in purpose.

See the word in context

The whole block turned out for a celebratory cookout the weekend the new park finally opened.

The robotics team had eaten in the same quiet corner of the cafeteria all year. But the Monday after their machine finally cleared the qualifying round, their table looked different: someone had brought a grocery-store cake, someone else had tied a single balloon to a chair, and the coach gave a toast with a juice box. It was a small lunch, but an unmistakably celebratory one — every detail existed to mark the win.

The cake, balloon, and toast all exist specifically to mark the team's win, which is what makes the lunch celebratory rather than just cheerful.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    congratulatory: Congratulatory means expressing praise for a specific achievement, and it is aimed at the achiever -- a congratulatory card, call, or handshake speaks directly to the person who did it. Celebratory belongs to the event: it marks the occasion with festivity. You send a congratulatory note; you throw a celebratory dinner.

    joyous: Joyous means full of happiness -- it names the emotion itself, wherever it appears. Celebratory is about purpose, not feeling: a thing is celebratory because it exists to mark a win, a launch, a milestone. A joyous morning needs no occasion at all; a celebratory toast without an occasion makes no sense.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    mournful: full of grief or sorrow

    somber: dark, serious, and grave in mood

    Where it sits · how firmly the joy marks an occasion

    cheerful → celebratory → jubilant

    cheerful vs. celebratory: Cheerful describes any pleasant mood, but celebratory ties the good mood to a specific occasion being marked.

    jubilant vs. celebratory: Jubilant is stronger than celebratory — not just marking an occasion, but exulting in it with open, triumphant joy.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral; describes events, gestures, and atmospheres

    Celebratory always points at a specific occasion being marked — a win, a launch, a milestone — while festive (also in this unit) can describe a merry, holiday-like atmosphere even when nothing particular is being commemorated.
