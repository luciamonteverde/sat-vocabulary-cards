buoyant

/BOY-uhnt/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Buoyant means cheerful and resilient in spirit — able to stay light and rise back up after a blow, the way a float refuses to stay under.

See the word in context

Even after the moving van broke down twice, Dad stayed buoyant, turning the second roadside wait into a picnic.

Halfway through the 400-mile charity ride, Tess's derailleur snapped, a headwind rose, and the rain started. She coasted into the rest stop grinning anyway, borrowed a wrench from a stranger, and by nightfall was leading the camp singalong. "You can't sink her," her teammate laughed — and it was true: every setback pushed Tess's mood under for a minute, and every time it bobbed right back up, buoyant as a cork.

Tess's spirits are not merely cheerful — they are repeatedly pushed down and keep surfacing. That refusal to stay sunk, the mood that rises back after every blow, is exactly what buoyant describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    lighthearted: Lighthearted means carefree and cheerful in the moment — a lighthearted joke or afternoon carries no weight at all. Buoyant adds the rebound: buoyant spirits have felt the weight and keep rising back anyway, the way a float pops up after every wave.

    optimistic: Optimistic means expecting things to turn out well — it is a judgment about the future. Buoyant describes the felt lift of the mood itself: you hear optimism in a forecast, but you see buoyancy in someone knocked flat at noon and humming again by two.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    despondent: sunk in low spirits, with no lift left

    gloomy: dark and downhearted in mood

    Where it sits · how surely the spirits rise back

    hopeful → buoyant → irrepressible

    hopeful vs. buoyant: Hopeful is weaker than buoyant: a hopeful mood looks for better days but can still sink, while buoyant spirits actually rise back every time something pushes them down.

    irrepressible vs. buoyant: Irrepressible goes further than buoyant: buoyant spirits dip and then recover, but irrepressible ones cannot be held down even for a moment.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      warm, admiring — praises resilience, not just cheer

      Buoyant praises a mood for its bounce, not its volume. Keep it apart from this unit's other bright words: vivacious is outward social sparkle that fills a room, and euphoric is a soaring high — buoyant is quieter and tougher, spirits that take a hit and rise anyway. It also differs from optimistic, which is a judgment that things will go well; buoyancy is the lift you can watch in someone's manner, whatever they predict.
