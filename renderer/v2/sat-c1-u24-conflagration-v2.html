conflagration

/kon-fluh-GRAY-shun/

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Conflagration means a large, destructive fire.

See the word in context

What began as a single overheated wire in the abandoned textile mill grew into a conflagration that lit the night sky for miles.

By August the prairie grass stood brown and brittle after two months without rain. A single lightning strike near the county road set it alight, and the wind did the rest. Within three hours, flames had crossed two ranches and were bearing down on the grain elevators at Dorsey. Firefighters from four towns could do little but dig lines and watch the conflagration roar past.

The fire's enormous, uncontrollable scale — crossing two ranches and outrunning crews from four towns — is what makes it a conflagration rather than an ordinary fire.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    inferno: An inferno is a huge, raging fire, and the word's weight falls on the furnace-like heat and roar of the flames themselves. A conflagration is equally huge, but the word names the event and its destruction — the disaster that eats through buildings and neighborhoods. Inferno says what the fire feels like; conflagration says what it does.

    wildfire: A wildfire is a big, fast-moving fire burning out of control through wildland — grass, brush, forest. A conflagration is any vast, destructive fire whatever it feeds on: a fleet at anchor, a grain harvest, or a forest can all be lost to one. A wildfire is defined by where it burns; a conflagration by sheer destructive scale.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    flicker: a tiny, unsteady flame

    ember: a small glowing remnant of a fire

    Where it sits · how vast the burning becomes

    blaze → conflagration → firestorm

    blaze vs. conflagration: A blaze is a strong, active fire, but a conflagration is far larger and more destructive — a fire that threatens whole neighborhoods.

    firestorm vs. conflagration: A firestorm is even more extreme than a conflagration: a fire so vast it generates its own winds and weather.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal, journalistic

      Reporters and historians reach for conflagration when a fire is huge and out of control. Do not confuse it with combustion (another word in this unit): combustion is the chemical process of burning, which can be as small as a match tip, while a conflagration is a massive, destructive fire you could watch consume a neighborhood.
