contiguous

kun-TIG-yoo-us

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Contiguous means sharing a common border or touching along an edge, with no gap in between.

See the word in context

Their two farms were contiguous, separated by nothing more than a low stone wall that both families had mended for generations.

The hotel gave us two contiguous rooms joined by a connecting door, so the kids could reach our room without ever stepping into the hallway.

Contiguous describes things that share a common boundary and touch with no gap in between. These two rooms are not merely close, or on the same floor — they meet along a shared wall, and the connecting door proves they are directly joined. Compare this with rooms that are only 'nearby': those might sit several doors apart, with other rooms wedged between them. The defining test for contiguous is direct contact — if you can fit anything at all between the two things, even a hallway or one empty room, they are close but not contiguous.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    adjacent: Adjacent means next to or very near, but it does not strictly require touching — two adjacent lots can sit side by side with a narrow gap between them. Contiguous is stricter: contiguous things actually touch, sharing a border with no gap at all.

    adjoining: Adjoining spaces are physically connected, usually through one shared wall or partition, like adjoining offices. Contiguous is the broader, more technical term for touching along any common edge — two contiguous counties share a border without sharing anything like a wall.

    abutting: Abutting is the technical word, common in law and architecture, for meeting exactly edge-to-edge at a boundary line, as when a driveway abuts the street. Contiguous covers the same no-gap contact but describes the whole relationship of bordering, not just the line where the edges meet.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    detached: Standing physically apart, separated by a gap or open space — a detached house sits on its own, sharing no wall with its neighbors.

    disconnected: Having no point of contact or link at all — where contiguous things meet along a shared edge, disconnected things have a clean break between them.

    Where it sits · how completely the gap has closed

    distant → nearby → contiguous

    distant vs. contiguous: Two distant islands are separated by miles of open water — nowhere near the direct, edge-to-edge contact that contiguous demands.

    nearby vs. contiguous: Two picnic blankets spread a few feet apart on the lawn are nearby, but only when their edges actually touch, with no grass showing between them, do they become contiguous.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      Neutral to formal.

      Contiguous is a precise, somewhat formal word common in geography, law, real estate, and even computing (as in 'contiguous memory'). Reach for it when you specifically mean 'touching with no gap'; in casual speech, 'next to' or 'side by side' usually sounds more natural.
