Meaning

What It Means

Prowess means extraordinary skill or ability.

See the word in context

Word of the young farrier's prowess traveled fast; ranchers hauled their worst kickers to her from three counties out.

The cave rescue hinged on one operator's prowess: reading the winch by sound alone, Basia lifted the stretcher through a shaft with a hand's width of clearance, pausing between gusts she felt before the spotters called them.

Prowess is the right word because it means extraordinary skill or ability — skill at the scale that gets witnessed, the kind crowds and captains write down. Talent is the raw gift; prowess is the gift proven in performance, and the word keeps a faint ring of its first meaning: bravery on a battlefield.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    mastery: Mastery means the craft is fully owned — every technique available, nothing left to chance — and it can stay entirely private; a master may never perform for anyone. Prowess is skill at the scale that gets witnessed and talked about. Mastery is the possession; prowess is the public display.

    expertise: Expertise is the knowledge side of skill — knowing what to do, built from study and experience — and it does its work quietly, in reports, diagnoses, and consultations. Prowess is skill out loud: extraordinary ability demonstrated where results are kept. The analyst has expertise; the record-breaker shows prowess.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    incompetence: the job beyond the hands assigned to it

    clumsiness: skill's opposite in the muscles

    Where it sits · how far the skill rises toward artistry

    competence → prowess → virtuosity

    competence vs. prowess: Competence does the job to standard and goes home — nobody buys a ticket to watch it — while prowess is the same skill risen far enough to draw the crowd.

    virtuosity vs. prowess: Virtuosity goes further than prowess — prowess wins the contest; virtuosity makes even the losing rounds look like performance.

      Word Choice

      elevated / admiring

      Prowess is grandstand vocabulary — athletic prowess, military prowess, technical prowess — for skill demonstrated where results are kept. Its unit-mates work smaller rooms: adept and deft describe the skilled person and hands; prowess names the quality the crowd came to see. Mastery says the same without the trumpets.

      For decades the regional fencing circuit was a quiet affair, its winners known only to one another. Then came Mirela Costa, and her prowess was the kind that pulls strangers into a gym: she once won a bout in eleven seconds against a national finalist. Coaches drove hours to film her footwork, and opponents lined up to shake her hand after losing to her. What set her apart was not preparation, since half the circuit trained as hard and several trained harder. It was the distance between what others could do and what she did in front of a crowd that made her name travel.
