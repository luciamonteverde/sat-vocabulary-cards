What It Means

Trite means not original; worn out by everyone's overuse.

See the word in context

By the fourth graduation speech, every 'chase your dreams' landed as trite as a fortune cookie.

The wedding videographer keeps a private tally of trite shots couples request anyway: rings resting on rose petals, a slow-motion sparkler exit, hands forming a heart around the sunset. None of it is wrong, she says — it's just that ten thousand couples reached the sunset first, and the freshness went with them.

Trite is the diagnosis because it means not original; overused — the shot still works mechanically, but repetition has rubbed all the surprise off it. Stale says the freshness is gone; trite adds the judgment that everyone else got there first, and that reaching for it was the lazy move.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    worn-out: Worn-out says the force is simply gone — used so often the words no longer move anyone, and no blame attached. Trite adds the accusation: everybody got there before you, and reaching for the phrase anyway was the lazy move. Worn-out describes the wear; trite condemns the choice.

    secondhand: Secondhand means borrowed rather than your own — the thinking was taken over from someone who got there first, whether one source or a hundred. Trite goes further: borrowed so often, by so many, that the freshness is gone. Secondhand faults the sourcing; trite faults the staleness too.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    fresh: newly minted, still surprising

    original: no one has said it before

    Where it sits · how far use has hollowed it out

    common → trite → meaningless

    common vs. trite: Common is neutral census-taking — it counts how widely a phrase turns up, well short of trite: a phrase can be common and still do honest work, while trite says the overuse has already rubbed the surprise off.

    meaningless vs. trite: Meaningless goes further than trite — a trite phrase still says something, tiredly; a meaningless one has been rubbed all the way down to noise.

      critical / judging

      Trite condemns: the expression was everybody's before it was yours, and choosing it was the lazy move. Stale mourns lost freshness without pointing fingers; corny faults cheap sentiment. Fresh and original are what the editor wanted instead.
