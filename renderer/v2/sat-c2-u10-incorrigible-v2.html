Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Incorrigible means incapable of being corrected or reformed.

See the word in context

Warnings, fines, and one towing later, the incorrigible double-parker was back astride the crosswalk by Friday.

Grandpa Lou has been caught improving his golf lie for thirty years — by caddies, by sons-in-law, once by a drone. Each discovery ends the same way: a solemn vow, a chastened Sunday, and a ball quietly toed out of the rough by Wednesday. The family stopped hoping years ago. He is incorrigible, and the vows are part of the act.

Incorrigible aims at the future, not the past: it doesn't count offenses, it promises that correction will never take. A habitual offender might yet be reached; calling someone incorrigible retires the project — every scolding from here on is ritual, not repair. The word usually lands with a sigh, half surrender and half affection.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unreformable: Unreformable spells the claim out in plain parts — this person will not be reformed — and it stays grim, said of hardened offenders and broken institutions. Incorrigible makes the identical promise but often makes it smiling: the incorrigible flirt or punster is past fixing and half-loved for it.

    hopeless: Hopeless means beyond help of any kind, and no misbehavior is required — you can be hopeless at math or a hopeless romantic. Incorrigible is narrower and always about conduct: someone has tried, repeatedly, to correct this behavior, and the corrections will never take.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    teachable: still able to learn better

    remorseful: sorry, and genuinely trying to change

    Where it sits · how far past fixing the behavior sits

    mischievous → wayward → incorrigible

    mischievous vs. incorrigible: A mischievous child makes playful trouble that one good scolding can end; incorrigible means the scoldings have stopped working and always will.

    wayward vs. incorrigible: The wayward drift off course but can still be steered back; incorrigible is the far rung — the fiftieth correction lands exactly like the first.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    wry / judging

    Incorrigible promises that correction will never take, and it often arrives smiling — an incorrigible flirt, punster, optimist. For genuine menace, hardened or remorseless hits harder. And keep it apart from this unit's implacable: the implacable can't be soothed; the incorrigible can't be reformed. One is about anger, the other about habits.
