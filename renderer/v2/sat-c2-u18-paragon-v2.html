Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A paragon is a model of excellence — a person or thing so perfect of its kind that everything else is measured against it.

See the word in context

Marisol's lab notebook circulated through the department as a paragon of record-keeping: every reagent, every timestamp, every failed run accounted for.

When the city audited its branch libraries, Elm Street emerged as the paragon: returns reshelved within the hour, waitlists cleared weekly, damaged spines mended and back in circulation within days. By the second week the auditors had stopped scoring the other branches against the manual and started scoring them against Elm Street.

Paragon is the exact word because a paragon is not merely excellent — it becomes the standard itself, the case everything else of its kind is measured against. Outstanding would only rank Elm Street near the top of the list; paragon captures the moment the branch stopped being a contestant and became the measuring stick.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    exemplar: An exemplar is a fine example held up for others to learn from — one good model among possibly many. A paragon stands alone: the peerless standard the rest of its kind is measured against. A course reader collects exemplars; a field has one paragon at a time.

    ideal: The ideal is perfection as a thought — the standard as it exists in the mind, which nothing real fully reaches. A paragon is perfection with a pulse: the actual person or thing that comes closest and so becomes the working standard. You reason from the ideal; you point at a paragon.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    disgrace: the case so bad it shames its whole kind

    mediocrity: a thoroughly middling example, setting no standard at all

    Where it sits · how close to perfection the standard sits

    exemplar → paragon → ideal

    exemplar vs. paragon: Exemplar is milder than paragon: an exemplar is one good model among many worth studying, while a paragon is the peerless case the whole field is measured against.

    ideal vs. paragon: Ideal goes further than paragon: a paragon is the closest real thing to perfection, but the ideal is perfection itself, existing in thought and never fully reached.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    elevated / evaluative

    Paragon is the measuring-stick word: name someone the paragon of a craft and you have made them the standard everyone else is scored against — a paragon of patience, a paragon of virtue. Keep its neighbors straight: an exemplar is one good model among many, the ideal is perfection as a thought, and the paragon is the real one that comes closest. It takes its quality in an of-phrase, and it always praises; there is no such thing as a minor paragon.
