What It Means

Euphemism means a mild or indirect expression substituted for a harsh or blunt one.

See the word in context

Real-estate listings run on euphemism: 'cozy' for cramped, 'vintage charm' for a furnace older than your grandmother.

The pediatric dentist keeps a whole vocabulary of euphemisms taped inside the supply cabinet: the drill is 'Mr. Whistle,' the needle is 'sleepy juice,' the extraction is 'letting the tooth graduate.' Parents smile at the code; four-year-olds stop gripping the armrests.

Euphemism is the precise term because each phrase is a mild or indirect expression substituted for a harsh or blunt one — the drill does not stop being a drill, but the word stops announcing it. A white lie changes the facts; a euphemism keeps the facts and swaps out their clothes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    understatement: An understatement admits the fact but shrinks it in the telling — 'a bit breezy' for a hurricane plays the size down. A euphemism keeps the fact at full size and swaps in kinder wording for it. Understatement cuts the thing down; a euphemism re-dresses it.

    code word: A code word is an insider's substitute, built to exclude outsiders — secrecy is its whole job. A euphemism is a substitute anyone present can decode; its job is to soften the blow, not to keep the topic from the uninitiated.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    plain talk: the thing said straight, no cushion

    frankness: openness that skips the softening entirely

    Where it sits · how much cover the harsh fact gets

    hedge → euphemism → whitewash

    hedge vs. euphemism: A hedge is milder than a euphemism — the speaker just goes vague ('we'll see,' 'at some point') and never offers the substitute phrase a euphemism supplies.

    whitewash vs. euphemism: A whitewash goes further than a euphemism — softening becomes covering: the unpleasant fact isn't re-worded, it's painted over and denied.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / analytic

      Euphemism names the softened phrase itself — use it when the wording is doing the cushioning. If the facts get shrunk, that's understatement; if they get inflated, you're in this unit's hyperbole territory; if they get denied outright, you've reached whitewash.

      Marisol first met the word in Ms. Grady's grading key, taped inside her sixth-grade folder. 'Developing,' the key explained, was the report card's euphemism for failing — a gentler dress on a fact the school preferred not to say plainly. Nobody was fooled, Ms. Grady admitted, least of all the students. But parents read 'developing' without flinching, and that, the district believed, kept the conversation open. Marisol tested the idea at home: the dog was not shedding everywhere, he was 'redistributing his coat.' Her mother laughed and made her vacuum anyway. The fact never changed size, Marisol noticed. Only the clothes it wore.

The descent rate had in fact been four times the certified limit, hard enough to crack two landing struts. And nothing was concealed — half the airfield had watched the jet slam down.
