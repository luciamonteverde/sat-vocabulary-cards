Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Rebuke means to scold sharply.

See the word in context

The archivist rebuked the visiting researcher in front of the whole reading room for uncapping a pen above the folios.

The flight instructor let the landing finish before she spoke, and then she rebuked him and did not soften it: skipping the crosswind checklist, she told him, was how licenses ended and how worse things began, and he would fly the pattern again until the checklist was reflex. Ten words of it carried into the next hangar. It stung — it was meant to.

Rebuke is the exact word because the scolding is sharp, direct, and delivered downward — an authority correcting a fault to its face. To reproach him would be gentler, more sorrow than sting; a lecture would take an hour. A rebuke is short, hot, and lands like a slap of cold water.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    scold: Scold is the everyday, kitchen-table word for angry correction — a parent over a broken curfew, a roommate over the dishes. A rebuke is a scolding with rank behind it: sharp, formal, and delivered downward by someone with the standing to correct you. Anyone can scold; a rebuke comes from authority.

    reprimand: A reprimand is the official version of a scolding — written up and kept in the file, in the measured language of an institution. A rebuke is hotter and more immediate: delivered to your face, in the moment, and done. The reprimand outlives the meeting; the rebuke ends with it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    congratulate: names what someone did right

    reassure: steadies instead of stings

    Where it sits · how much sting the response carries

    correct → rebuke → lambaste

    correct vs. rebuke: To correct someone fixes the error and moves on; a rebuke makes sure the error's owner feels its weight.

    lambaste vs. rebuke: Lambaste goes past rebuke: the rebuked pilot gets thirty stinging seconds, while the lambasted one gets the full verbal thrashing, at length and in front of witnesses.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      stern / formal

      Rebuke is the sharp, face-to-face scolding an authority delivers in the moment — judges rebuke attorneys, harbormasters rebuke captains. Its unit-mate reproach is the soft-spoken cousin: more disappointment than heat, often between equals or loved ones. If the scolding comes stamped and filed, reprimand is the bureaucrat's word for the same sting.
