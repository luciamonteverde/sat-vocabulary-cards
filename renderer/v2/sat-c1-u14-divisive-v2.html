divisive

/dih-VY-siv/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Divisive means causing disagreement or hostility between people; tending to split a group into opposing sides.

See the word in context

The school board's proposed dress code proved so divisive that parents who had been friends for years stopped speaking to one another after the vote.

When the three siblings inherited their grandmother's lakeside cottage, the question of whether to sell it became deeply divisive, splitting the family into two camps that argued at every holiday dinner.

The decision does not merely cause stress; it splits the family into opposing sides that argue with each other. Creating hardened, hostile camps out of one group is the heart of what divisive means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    contentious: A contentious issue sparks heated argument the moment it comes up: tempers rise and voices climb. Contentious is about the heat of the debate while it lasts. Divisive is about what remains afterward, a group split into camps that no longer trust each other.

    discordant: Discordant describes a group or scene already marked by conflict, clashing voices and colliding agendas, like a discordant meeting. It names the state of disagreement, not its cause. Divisive names the cause: the issue or figure that splits a once-united group into opposing sides.

    controversial: Controversial means attracting public disagreement and criticism from outside observers, the way a controversial ruling draws arguments everywhere. Divisive measures the damage inside one group: a divisive question turns colleagues, neighbors, or families against each other and leaves them in hostile camps.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unifying: bringing people together

    harmonious: marked by agreement and peace

    Where it sits · how far disagreement pushes people apart

    contested → divisive → polarizing

    contested vs. divisive: A contested issue is one people disagree about, but a divisive issue goes further, creating hostility and hardened sides.

    polarizing vs. divisive: A divisive question splits a group into camps, while a polarizing one drives those camps toward opposite extremes with almost no middle ground left.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      Neutral / common in news and politics

      Divisive describes anything that splits people into opposing sides: 'a divisive issue,' 'a divisive figure,' 'a divisive debate.' Keep it distinct from disruptive: a divisive policy creates disagreement between people, while a disruptive one interrupts the normal flow or functioning of an activity or system.
