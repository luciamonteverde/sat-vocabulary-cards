surreptitious

/sur-uhp-TISH-uhs/ - the stress falls on TISH

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Surreptitious means done secretly so as not to be noticed.

See the word in context

He perfected the surreptitious snack, easing the granola wrapper open inside his desk one silent millimeter at a time.

For two weeks the crew planned the captain's retirement surprise with surreptitious care: decorations came aboard in grocery boxes, rehearsals ran during her watch, and the banner was sewn in the engine room—the one compartment she never visited.

Surreptitious describes the manner of a single act: done under someone's nose, timed and placed to escape notice. The act itself can be innocent—a surprise party qualifies—which separates the word from plain 'sneaky,' with its whiff of guilt.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    stealthy: Stealthy is about the body: moving softly and slowly so as not to be heard or seen, like a cat crossing gravel. Surreptitious is about the plan of an act — timed and placed to escape notice — and it needs no movement at all; a surreptitious payment never makes a sound.

    sneaky: Sneaky is the informal cousin, and it always smuggles in a whiff of guilt or bad intent — the sneaky shortcut, the sneaky bite of someone else's dessert. Surreptitious judges only the manner of an act, not its morals: a surreptitious deed can be perfectly kind, so long as it is carried out to escape notice.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    open: done in plain view for anyone to see

    blatant: done so openly that it almost demands to be noticed

    Where it sits · the scale of what is being hidden

    discreet → surreptitious → undercover

    discreet vs. surreptitious: Discreet keeps a low profile out of tact—nothing needs hiding; surreptitious actively works to escape notice in the moment.

    undercover vs. surreptitious: Undercover goes beyond surreptitious: a surreptitious act hides one deed, while an undercover officer hides a whole identity for months at a stretch.

      Word Choice

      formal

      Surreptitious is the manner word in this unit's hiding cluster: it describes how a particular act is carried out—quietly, under watchful eyes, timed to escape notice. Conceal names what you do to the thing; secretive names a person's standing habit; evasive names the dodge when questions come. The act itself can be guilty or perfectly kind.
