heinous

/HAY-nus/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Heinous means shockingly wicked — describing an act or crime so evil that it provokes outrage.

See the word in context

Even veteran detectives were shaken by so heinous a crime.

The jury agreed that locking the workers inside the burning factory was a heinous act, far beyond ordinary negligence.

Heinous marks the specific act itself as so evil it shocks — the sentence contrasts it with mere negligence to show the moral outrage the word carries.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    atrocious: Atrocious marks acts and conditions as shockingly cruel or dreadfully bad — atrocious crimes, atrocious cruelty. Like heinous it attaches to the deed rather than the doer, but it has also gone soft in casual use (atrocious traffic, atrocious spelling); heinous never jokes, and always means real moral horror.

    abominable: Abominable centers on the disgust a thing provokes — it turns the stomach and invites moral revulsion. Heinous centers on the outrage: an act so shockingly wicked that people cry out against it. An abominable practice repels you; a heinous crime makes people demand justice.

    monstrous: Monstrous means outrageously evil or cruel, and it is the word that stretches to cover the doer as well as the deed — English speaks easily of a monstrous tyrant. Heinous stays pinned to the act itself: a monstrous man commits heinous crimes, not the other way round.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    admirable: deserving praise and respect

    noble: showing high moral character

    Where it sits · how horrifying the act is

    wrong → heinous → unspeakable

    wrong vs. heinous: Calling an act wrong notes a moral failure, but calling it heinous means it is so evil that it shocks people.

    unspeakable vs. heinous: Unspeakable is even stronger than heinous — the act is so horrifying that people can barely bring themselves to discuss it.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      Formal / legal

      Heinous describes a specific act or crime so evil it provokes shock — 'a heinous crime' is the classic collocation. It describes the act itself, not a person's ongoing character (for a person, a word like monstrous fits better). A related but distinct word in this unit, depravity, is a noun naming someone's underlying corrupt moral nature rather than any one shocking act.
