Meaning

What It Means

Sycophant means a person who flatters others for personal gain.

See the word in context

Every remark the heiress made at dinner was instantly the cleverest thing the table had ever heard — she traveled, her butler observed, with a full set of sycophants.

The kitchen's newest sycophant praised the head chef's burnt reduction as 'bold,' volunteered for his errands, and within a month held the sauce station over two better cooks.

A sycophant flatters as an investment. A flatterer may simply enjoy pleasing people, and an admirer's praise can be perfectly sincere — the sycophant's compliments are aimed up the ladder, priced, and waiting for the return.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    bootlicker: Bootlicker is the schoolyard insult for the sycophant — the same creature, cruder name. A bootlicker grovels openly to whoever holds power over him, and the word drips contempt. Sycophant says the same thing in a suit, polished enough for reviews, histories, and courtrooms.

    yes-man: A yes-man works one narrow trade: agreeing with every word the boss says, whatever he privately thinks. A sycophant runs a wider business — compliments, favors, and flattery aimed anywhere up the ladder, always priced for a return. Every yes-man is a sycophant; the reverse is not guaranteed.

    toady: A toady attaches himself to one important person and fawns in close attendance — running errands, laughing first, agreeing loudest. Sycophant is the broader, more formal word for anyone who flatters for personal gain; toady adds the picture of eager, hovering servility at a particular patron's elbow.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    critic: tells you what is wrong, whatever it costs

    straight shooter: speaks plainly and wants nothing for it

    Where it sits · how much the praise angles for gain

    admirer → flatterer → sycophant

    admirer vs. sycophant: An admirer sits far below a sycophant — admiration can be silent, sincere, and want nothing at all.

    flatterer vs. sycophant: Flatterer stops short of sycophant: a flatterer overpraises, perhaps just to be liked, while a sycophant runs flattery as a business aimed at the powerful.
