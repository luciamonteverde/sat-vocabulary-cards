Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Avarice means excessive greed.

See the word in context

What began as careful saving hardened into avarice: he charged his own sister interest on lunch money and logged every gift he gave as a debt owed back.

The heiress owned four empty mansions yet sued a neighbor over a strip of lawn no wider than a doormat; her lawyers called it principle, but the town called it avarice — wealth chasing more wealth long after need had ended.

Avarice is stronger than ordinary greed for a reason: greed can be a passing appetite, while avarice names greed grown into a ruling passion, a fixed hunger for money that crowds out everything else.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    covetousness: Covetousness is desire aimed at one particular thing that belongs to someone else — a colleague's corner office, a rival's title. Avarice is broader and colder: a general, consuming hunger for money and possessions themselves, whoever holds them. Covetousness has a target; avarice has an appetite.

    miserliness: Miserliness is the refusal to spend or share what you already have — the hoarder clutching a full purse. Avarice is the hungering side of the same vice: an endless drive to gain more wealth, whether or not any of it is ever enjoyed. The miser guards; the avaricious grasp.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    generosity: gives freely instead of grasping

    charity: turns money outward, toward those who need it

    Where it sits · how consuming the hunger for wealth

    greed → avarice → rapacity

    greed vs. avarice: Greed stops short of avarice — greed can be a moment's overreach at the buffet, while avarice is a settled, lifelong hunger for wealth.

    rapacity vs. avarice: Rapacity goes further than avarice — the avaricious hoard what comes to them, but rapacity reaches out and takes, plundering like a bird of prey.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / condemning

      Avarice is the moralist's word — at home in sermons, fables, and closing arguments. In casual speech say greed; choose avarice when you want greed to sound like a permanent vice that has taken charge of a life rather than a bad afternoon at the buffet.
