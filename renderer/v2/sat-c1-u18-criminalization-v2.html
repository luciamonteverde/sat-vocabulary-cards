criminalization

/krim-uh-nul-ih-ZAY-shun/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Criminalization means the act of making a formerly legal action a crime, typically through a new law.

See the word in context

The criminalization of sidewalk food vending in the 1920s turned hundreds of pushcart sellers into lawbreakers overnight.

For decades, musicians had played for tips in the downtown plaza without breaking any rule. Then the city passed an ordinance making unpermitted street performance a misdemeanor. Overnight, an activity that had been legal, and even celebrated, became grounds for a fine or arrest. Longtime performers protested that the criminalization of their art punished people who had harmed no one.

A formerly legal activity (street performance) was made illegal by a new law, which is exactly what criminalization means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    outlawing: Outlawing is the plain, everyday word for making an activity illegal. Criminalization is the formal legal term, and it stresses the change of status: an act that was legal yesterday becomes a crime today, written into the criminal code. Reporters may say the city outlawed a practice; lawyers describe its criminalization.

    prohibition: A prohibition is the official forbidding of something, and it can be enforced with any penalty, including ordinary civil fines. Criminalization goes further: it makes the act itself a crime, exposing people to prosecution and arrest. Every criminalization prohibits, but a prohibition only amounts to criminalization when breaking it lands you in criminal court.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    legalization: the act of making something lawful

    decriminalization: the removal of criminal penalties from an act

    Where it sits · how harshly the law targets the activity

    regulation → criminalization → persecution

    regulation vs. criminalization: Regulation places rules and limits on an activity that stays legal, while criminalization makes the activity itself a crime.

    persecution vs. criminalization: Criminalization makes an act punishable by law, but persecution goes further, relentlessly targeting the people who do it, often beyond what any fair law allows.

      formal, legal/political register

      Criminalization almost always appears with 'of' plus the activity being outlawed (the criminalization of vending, the criminalization of dissent). It names the change in legal status itself, not the arrests or enforcement that may follow.

      Putting it all together

      In 1910, crossing a street mid-block was nobody's legal business. Pedestrians had walked wherever they liked for as long as cities had existed. Then automobiles arrived, and with them a campaign to move walkers out of the roadway. The turning point was criminalization: by the late 1920s, dozens of cities had written mid-block crossing into their penal codes, complete with fines, court summonses, and, for repeat offenders, short jail stays. Defenders said the new offense saved lives. Critics answered that an ordinary act, legal for centuries, had been turned into a crime almost overnight. Either way, the change stuck. A century later, most Americans have never known a world where the street belonged to the walker.
