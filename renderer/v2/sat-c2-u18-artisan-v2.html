Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Artisan means a skilled craftsman.

See the word in context

Every railing scroll on the footbridge carries the initials of the artisan who forged it a century ago.

Ask the bindery why a repair takes a month and they will point to Amara, the artisan at the back bench: she saws the old spine free by hand, mixes wheat paste fresh each morning, and re-sews each signature with linen thread, so the mended book opens as its printer intended.

Artisan is the exact word because an artisan is a skilled craftsman — someone who makes or restores things by hand, to a standard machines don't reach. A laborer supplies effort and a technician follows procedures; the artisan owns a craft, start to finish, and answers for the result.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    craftsman: Craftsman is the plain, everyday word for a skilled maker, and most artisans would answer to it. Artisan adds a whiff of tradition and identity: not just someone good with tools, but someone whose handcraft answers to a standard and is owned start to finish — work an artisan would sign.

    artificer: Artificer is the antique word for the same skilled maker — met in old books and regimental titles, rarely on a shop door. Artisan is its living descendant, the current term for a hand-worker who owns a craft. Reach for artificer only when the prose itself is wearing costume.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    dabbler: tries the craft on weekends and owes it nothing

    bungler: hands that undo the work

    Where it sits · how fully one commands the craft

    hobbyist → artisan → master

    hobbyist vs. artisan: A hobbyist is milder than an artisan on this scale — making things for the joy of it, with no standard to answer to; the artisan answers for every piece, because the craft is the trade.

    master vs. artisan: A master goes further than an artisan — the artisan commands the craft; the master sets its standards and signs off on everyone else's.

      In a one-room shop behind her house, Marta Silva makes violin bows the way her teacher's teacher made them, from rough plank to finished silver winding. She is, by every measure, an artisan: the bow is her trade, her training runs back generations, and every stick that leaves the bench carries her brand. Factories can turn out a playable bow in an afternoon. Silva spends three weeks on one. The difference is not only patience. When a bow warps years later, players ship it back to her, because her name on the frog means she answers for it. Ask what she would do if the work stopped selling, and she shrugs: there is no other plan. The bench is the living.
