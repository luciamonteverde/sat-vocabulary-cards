digression

/dye-GRESH-un/ - rhymes with session

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Digression means a temporary departure from the main subject.

See the word in context

The planetarium show's single digression — ninety seconds on the smell of moon dust — was every kid's favorite part.

Uncle Bram's wedding speeches are famous for their digressions. Launching into the story of how the couple met, he will pause at the word 'ferry' and give you three minutes on the boats of his childhood, pull himself back with a little cough, and resume exactly where he left off. The bride's family times him now; the record digression is seven minutes, round trip.

Digression is the exact word for those wanders, because a digression is a temporary departure from the main subject — and the key word is temporary: he leaves the story and he comes back to it. Circumlocution never leaves the subject at all; it circles the point without touching it. A digression at least goes somewhere, just not where the story was going.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    tangent: A tangent is the casual word for the same wander off the subject, and it makes no promise about coming home — speakers fly off on tangents and often stay gone. A digression is the rounder trip: a temporary departure that means to return to the subject, and does.

    sidetrack: A sidetrack is what the wander does to you — you get sidetracked, tugged briefly off your task or your point by something more interesting. A digression is the departure itself, taken on purpose by a speaker or writer who means, all along, to come back.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    focus: staying on the one subject

    beeline: the straightest possible path to the point

    Where it sits · how completely the main thread is abandoned

    aside → digression → derailment

    aside vs. digression: An aside is far slighter than a digression — one sentence long, a wink to the audience, then straight back to business, while a digression wanders for whole minutes before finding its way home.

    derailment vs. digression: A derailment goes further than a digression — a digression steps off the path meaning to return; a derailment never finds the track again.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / bookish

    Digression suits essays, lectures, and long dinners — departures that mean to return. A tangent is the casual cousin and often never comes back. Keep it apart from this unit's circumlocution, which stays on the point precisely in order to avoid it.

    Putting it all together

    Read the passage, then answer Part A. Part B gives you a new passage.

    Everyone who took Mr. Okafor's history class remembers the map of Constantinople -- and the day he stopped mid-sentence to talk about his grandmother's spice cabinet. It sounded like a lost cause. For six or seven minutes he described cinnamon arriving by caravan, the smell of the tin she kept it in, the price a cook once paid for cloves. Then, without a pause, he tapped the map: every one of those spices had passed through this one city, which was why three empires had fought for it. The digression, the class realized, had never truly left the subject; it had been circling it the whole time. Years later, his students compared notes and found the pattern held: however far the story wandered, it landed back on the lesson, and the lesson stuck because of the trip.
