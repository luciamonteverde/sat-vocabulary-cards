Meaning

What It Means

Cloying means sickeningly sweet.

See the word in context

The wedding favors were chocolates filled with rosewater cream, pretty at first bite and cloying by the third.

Aunt Renata's fruit punch tasted like liquefied candy — two ladles in, the children abandoned their cups, and by the end of the picnic even the wasps seemed to find it cloying.

Cloying is more exact than 'too sweet' because it names the turning point where sweetness itself becomes repellent — the pleasure sours into something you push away, whether it's a dessert, a perfume, or an over-affectionate greeting.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    treacly: Treacly means gooily, heavily sweet — treacle is dark syrup — and it describes tone as often as taste: a treacly ballad, a treacly speech. Cloying goes one step further along the same road, marking the point where all that sweetness stops pleasing and begins to sicken.

    sickly-sweet: Sickly-sweet is the blunt, everyday compound for sweetness strong enough to turn the stomach. Cloying is its more precise cousin: it names the moment when a sweetness that began as pleasure tips over into distaste — the fourth forkful of fudge. Sickly-sweet labels the substance; cloying tells the story of the turn.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    tart: pleasantly sharp and acidic, the palate's relief from sweetness

    astringent: dry and puckering, the opposite of rich sweetness

    Where it sits · how unwelcome the sugariness becomes

    sweet → syrupy → cloying

    sweet vs. cloying: Sweet stops short of cloying — sweet is simply pleasant sugar, with nothing about it yet unwelcome.

    syrupy vs. cloying: Syrupy stops short of cloying — syrupy sweetness is heavy and thick, but cloying marks the point where the same sweetness starts to sicken.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      descriptive / critical

      Cloying and this unit's saccharine overlap but point at different failures: saccharine accuses sweetness of being fake — sugar substituting for real feeling — while cloying accuses it of being too much, real sweetness that has outstayed its welcome. A greeting-card rhyme is saccharine; the fourth spoonful of honey mousse is cloying.
