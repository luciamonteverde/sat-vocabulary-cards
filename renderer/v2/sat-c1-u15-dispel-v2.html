dispel

/dih-SPEL/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Dispel means to drive away or scatter completely, especially a feeling, doubt, rumor, or myth.

See the word in context

The museum guide was quick to dispel the popular myth that bats are blind.

Visitors kept skipping the old lighthouse tour because of rumors that it was haunted. The tour guide dispelled those fears by walking each group through every room and explaining the creaks and drafts one by one. Bookings doubled within a month.

The guide did not wait for the fears to fade on their own; she actively and completely drove them away with explanations, which is exactly what dispel means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    banish: To banish is to drive something away as if by decree - you banish a thought, a worry, or a courtier, forcing it out. What is banished still exists somewhere, just not here. To dispel is to scatter a doubt, fear, or myth until nothing of it remains anywhere, usually with facts and explanations.

    erase: To erase is to remove all trace of something recorded or visible - marks, files, footage. Dispel works on live, floating things: moods, doubts, rumors, myths. You erase what was written down; you dispel what hangs in the air. A false belief is dispelled by evidence, not rubbed out.

    eliminate: To eliminate is the general word for getting rid of anything entirely - errors, suspects, weeds - and it says nothing about how. Dispel is specific: it scatters intangible things like fear, doubt, or a myth, and it works through explanation or evidence. You eliminate a problem; you dispel the worry surrounding it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    instill: to plant a feeling or idea in someone

    confirm: to strengthen a belief as true

    Where it sits · how completely it is swept away

    ease → dispel → eradicate

    ease vs. dispel: Easing a worry only softens it, while dispelling a worry drives it away completely.

    eradicate vs. dispel: Eradicate is even stronger and more sweeping than dispel; you dispel a doubt or a myth, but you eradicate a disease or a deep-rooted problem.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Neutral to formal

    Dispel is active and complete: a person or a piece of evidence deliberately drives away a doubt, fear, rumor, or myth. Unlike dissipate, which describes something fading gradually on its own, dispelling is done on purpose. It is also distinct from disperse, which describes a crowd or group physically scattering in different directions; dispel is reserved for intangible things like feelings and beliefs, never for crowds.
