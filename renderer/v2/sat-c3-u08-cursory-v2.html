cursory

/KUR-suh-ree/ - the stress falls on KUR

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Cursory means so quick and surface-level that it stops at a glance — brief to the point of being superficial.

See the word in context

One cursory sweep of the flashlight satisfied the night guard, who signed the log and never saw the open window on the mezzanine.

Dad's cursory check of the twins' homework — a nod at each worksheet while stirring the sauce — missed that half the answers were in the same handwriting on both papers.

The check happened, but only at the surface: a glance in passing, attention mostly elsewhere, nothing examined closely enough to catch what a real look would have caught. That fast, shallow pass — brief to the point of being superficial — is exactly what cursory names.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    hurried: Hurried means done at speed because the clock demands it — a hurried job can still be careful in what it touches. Cursory adds the real charge: the attention itself never leaves the surface.

    passing: Passing describes a look that is brief because the attention is already on its way to something else — a passing glance takes in whatever it happens to catch. Cursory describes that same shallowness in a place where a real look was owed.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    thorough: examining every part with unhurried care

    painstaking: so careful that no detail goes unchecked

    Where it sits · how little of the attention actually lands

    quick → cursory → perfunctory

    quick vs. cursory: Quick is weaker than cursory: a quick check is merely fast and can still be sharp, while a cursory one is so shallow it never gets beneath the surface.

    perfunctory vs. cursory: Perfunctory goes further than cursory: a cursory glance at least tries to see something, while a perfunctory one is empty routine, performed only so the box can be ticked.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    critical / cautionary

    Cursory is a quiet criticism: it says the look was too brief to be worth much. Brief and quick are neutral about quality; cursory is not — a cursory reading is one that failed to really read. And don't confuse it with cursive, the flowing joined handwriting: the two share the Latin 'running' root and nothing else.
