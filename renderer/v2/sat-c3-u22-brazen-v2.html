brazen

/BRAY-zuhn/ - rhymes with raisin

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Brazen means excessively bold and without shame — daring done openly, as if unable to blush.

See the word in context

Even his own teammates laughed at the brazen dive: the striker went down clutching his shin before the defender had touched him.

The stowaway's plan was brazen in its simplicity: he boarded with the band, carried an empty trombone case, and waved to the purser twice a day for the whole crossing.

Brazen is bolder than bold, and it has lost the shame: a bold move takes a risk, while a brazen one takes the risk in the open and dares everyone watching to object. The word once meant 'made of brass' — brazen conduct wears a face of polished metal that cannot blush.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    brash: Brash is pushy boldness with no polish or patience — barging in loud, heedless of how it lands. Brazen keeps the nerve but adds shameless display: a brazen act is performed in the open and dares onlookers to object. Brash blunders forward; brazen performs.

    defiant: Defiant means openly resisting a rule or an authority — a defiant refusal, a defiant stare at the referee. Brazen needs no authority to resist: it names boldness that has lost its shame, offensive in itself even when no rule forbids it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    sheepish: embarrassed and apologetic about oneself

    timid: too fearful to act boldly at all

    Where it sits · how far past modesty the daring goes

    bold → brazen → shameless

    bold vs. brazen: Bold is milder than brazen: a bold plan takes a real risk, but it can still be modest about it.

    shameless vs. brazen: Shameless goes a step further than brazen — brazen defiance still performs for an audience it means to shock; shameless has stopped caring whether anyone watches at all.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / vivid

      Reach for brazen when the boldness itself is the offense — done in the open, without a flicker of shame. Bold can be praise; brazen never is. Its unit neighbors divide the ground: flagrant describes how glaring the violation looks, pert keeps sauciness small and charming, and brazen names the shameless nerve of the person acting.
