exogenous

/ek-SOJ-uh-nus/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Exogenous means caused by factors from outside a system rather than from within it.

See the word in context

The lake's sudden algae bloom was exogenous, fed by fertilizer washing in from farms miles upstream.

For decades, a mountain town's ski economy ran on steady snow and loyal visitors. Then a national airline canceled the only direct route into the regional airport, a decision made at a headquarters two thousand miles away. Bookings collapsed within a single season even though the snow, the lodges, and the service were as good as ever. The slump was exogenous: nothing inside the town's own economy had changed at all.

The downturn was caused entirely by an outside force, a distant airline's decision, not by anything within the town's own economic system, which is what exogenous means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    external: External means coming from the outside, and it fits anything at all — external walls, external pressure, an external hard drive. Exogenous is the analyst's word: it classifies a cause as originating beyond the system under study, usually in deliberate contrast with a homegrown one. Street noise is external; a currency shock is exogenous.

    extrinsic: Extrinsic means originating outside a thing's essential nature — extrinsic motivation comes from prizes and grades, not love of the work. Exogenous is about systems and causes: a force from beyond a system's borders driving change inside it. Extrinsic contrasts with what something is; exogenous contrasts with what a system does to itself.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    endogenous: caused by factors from within a system

    internal: existing or arising inside

    Where it sits · how actively forces beyond it intervene

    outside → exogenous → invasive

    outside vs. exogenous: Outside simply says where something is located, while exogenous makes a stronger claim: that a system's change was driven by forces beyond its own borders.

    invasive vs. exogenous: An exogenous factor comes from outside a system, but an invasive one goes further, pushing its way in and actively disrupting what it finds.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal, academic/scientific register

    Exogenous appears in economics, medicine, and ecology to label causes that originate outside the system being studied (an exogenous shock, an exogenous infection). Its direct antonym is endogenous, caused from within; asking whether a change is exogenous or endogenous is asking whether the trouble came from outside or was homegrown.
