Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Gregarious means fond of company — craving the crowd and hating to be alone for long.

See the word in context

Gregarious since kindergarten, Rafa turned his paper route into a rolling block party, collecting company at every corner.

The bakery's new apprentice is gregarious to her bones: she joined the dawn crew's card game on day one, eats lunch wherever the most aprons are gathered, and volunteers for the county-fair booth just for the crowds.

Gregarious names an appetite for company: the gregarious person seeks the group out, joins it, and is fed by it. It says nothing about warmth or charm — that is what separates it from affable and amiable — only that being alone is the one condition a gregarious soul can't stand for long.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    outgoing: Outgoing describes social confidence — the boldness to walk up to people you have never met and start the conversation yourself. Gregarious is about appetite rather than boldness: a gregarious person needs company around and seeks the group out. A shy person can crave the crowd yet never dare approach it.

    extroverted: Extroverted is the temperament word: energized by people rather than drained by them, recharging in company. Gregarious points at the behavior that follows — actively seeking the flock, joining every gathering, hating to be alone. Extroverted says where your energy comes from; gregarious says how hard you chase the crowd.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    solitary: preferring to live and work alone

    withdrawn: pulled back from contact, into oneself

    Where it sits · how actively one seeks out more people

    companionable → sociable → gregarious

    companionable vs. gregarious: Companionable sits well below gregarious — it enjoys the one friend already there, with no urge to go find more.

    sociable vs. gregarious: Sociable comes closer but stops short of gregarious — a sociable guest enjoys the party she's at, while a gregarious one arranged it, packed the guest list, and is already planning the next.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral

    Gregarious is about appetite for company, not warmth: a gregarious colleague fills her calendar with people. Affable and amiable (both in this unit) describe how pleasant someone is once you're talking; gregarious describes how badly they wanted the crowd in the first place. It also does honest work in biology — gregarious species live in herds and flocks.
