Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Expound means to lay out a subject in full, careful detail — to explain it thoroughly and at length, often with 'on' or 'upon.'

See the word in context

Give the ferry captain one question about the tides and he will expound for the whole crossing, charting currents on a napkin by the time you dock.

At the reunion, somebody asked the retired engineer how the old mill dam had actually worked, and she expounded happily for an hour: sluice gates, load curves, the winter the ice nearly won — a full course delivered over cooling coffee.

One fact would have answered the question; she instead set out the whole architecture, system by system, with evident command. That thorough, extended laying-out of a subject is what expound names — explanation at full length and full dress.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    elaborate: Elaborate (on) means to add more detail to something already said — a follow-up move that fills in a picture the audience has seen. Expound is the full performance from the start: setting out the whole subject, part by part, at length.

    explain: Explain simply makes something understandable, and a good explanation can take one sentence. Expound is explanation gone long-form — thorough, ordered, and usually delivered by someone with a whole theory to lay out.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    condense: to press an account down to its fewest essentials

    abbreviate: to cut a treatment down to a shorter form

    Where it sits · how fully the subject gets laid out

    outline → expound → anatomize

    outline vs. expound: Outlining is weaker than expounding: an outline hands you the skeleton in five bullet points, while to expound is to build the whole body of the subject in front of the audience.

    anatomize vs. expound: Anatomize goes further than expound: expounding lays a subject out generously, but to anatomize is to dissect it down to every last joint and fiber, sparing the listener nothing.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / expository

      Expound belongs to lecterns, long answers, and people glad you asked. Its unit-mate explicate unpacks a particular text or problem, piece by piece; expound sets out the speaker's own subject or doctrine at length. Keep it clear of the look-alike expand (to grow in size) and of its root-cousin propound: you propound a theory in a sentence and expound it for an hour.

      Every county fair has its corner of true believers, and ours belongs to Mrs. Okafor and her soil table. Ask a polite question about tomatoes and she will expound on the entire underground economy of a garden: fungi trading sugar for phosphorus, worms turning the subsoil, bacteria pulling nitrogen out of thin air. She does not skim. Each player gets its chapter, in order, with a jar of soil passed around as the exhibit. Listeners who wanted a quick tip sometimes edge away. Those who stay get the whole system, laid out end to end. By closing time, a question that could have been answered with 'add compost' has become an hour-long tour of everything happening under a garden bed.
