iridescent

/ir-ih-DESS-ent/

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Iridescent means showing shifting, rainbow-like colors that seem to change depending on the angle from which they're viewed.

See the word in context

Sunlight caught the iridescent sheen of the soap bubble just before it burst.

The bride chose an iridescent fabric that looked plain gray in the shop's fluorescent light but revealed flashes of purple, green, and gold the moment she stepped outside into direct sun.

The fabric wasn't dyed multiple colors — its surface simply bent light differently depending on the angle, which is precisely what makes something iridescent rather than just colorful.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    opalescent: Opalescent evokes the soft, milky glow of an opal — pale colors floating in a cloudy stone. Iridescent is sharper and more vivid: distinct rainbow colors that flip and change as the viewing angle moves. An opal glows gently; a soap bubble flashes.

    prismatic: Prismatic means splitting light into the full rainbow, the way a prism throws fixed bands of color on a wall. Iridescent colors also come from bent light, but they live on a surface and shift with every change of angle rather than fanning out in fixed bands.

    lustrous: Lustrous describes a rich, glowing sheen — polished wood, healthy hair, satin. The shine is steady and stays one color. Iridescent surfaces do the opposite: their whole character is change, showing different colors from every new angle.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    dull: reflecting no notable light or color at all

    matte: having a flat, non-reflective, single-toned surface

    Where it sits · how much the color shifts with light

    shiny → shimmering → iridescent

    shiny vs. iridescent: A shiny surface simply reflects light brightly, without necessarily changing color at all — far short of iridescent, whose rainbow colors shift with every viewing angle.

    shimmering vs. iridescent: A shimmering surface flickers as light moves across it, hinting at — but not yet splitting into — the full shifting colors of something iridescent.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      descriptive, literary

      Iridescent is a vivid, mostly descriptive word used for surfaces that visibly shift color with the light — soap bubbles, oil slicks, hummingbird feathers, certain fabrics and shells — not for objects that are simply multicolored or painted.
