emulate

/EM-yuh-layt/ - the stress falls on EM

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Emulate means to imitate someone in an effort to match and eventually surpass them.

See the word in context

The young cartoonist taped her favorite artist's strips above her desk, hoping first to emulate his comic timing and someday to out-draw him.

Teo emulated his older sister in everything: when she made the honor roll, he aimed for valedictorian; when she built a robot for the science fair, he built two. He wasn't copying her so much as racing her.

Emulate is more ambitious than imitate: both start by following a model, but emulate adds the goal of equaling or surpassing it. You imitate a birdcall; you emulate a hero.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    mirror: To mirror something is to reflect it exactly - the tribute set matches the original note for note, adding nothing of its own. Emulate follows a model with ambition attached: you copy in order to catch up, meaning one day to surpass. A mirror never overtakes what it reflects.

    rival: To rival someone is to match or challenge them - two bakeries can rival each other without either studying the other. Emulate starts from admiration: you take a model, follow it closely, and work to equal or surpass it. Rivals simply compete; emulators first apprentice themselves.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    shun: to deliberately avoid, rather than follow

    reject: to refuse to accept as a model

    Where it sits · how much ambition lies behind it

    copy → imitate → emulate

    copy vs. emulate: Copying reproduces the surface - the answers, the brushstrokes; emulate reaches past the surface toward the model's whole achievement.

    imitate vs. emulate: Imitating follows someone's example; emulate goes further, following it with the declared aim of matching or beating it.

      Word Choice

      formal / neutral

      Emulate is the word for imitation with ambition: you follow a model because you mean to match or surpass it. Plain imitate or copy stops at reproduction. Among this unit's drive words, emulate names where the drive points - at a person ahead of you - while resolute and zealous describe the firmness and heat you bring to the chase.

Critics noticed the homage first, then the divergence: her rye held Keita's crust but a darker crumb, her mornings ran earlier than his. Last spring, for the first time, a national guide ranked her shop above the one she had once crossed the city to admire.
