fortuitous

/for-TOO-ih-tus/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Fortuitous means happening by chance rather than design, often with a lucky result.

See the word in context

A fortuitous wrong turn led the hikers straight to the waterfall they had given up on finding.

A college student left her sketchbook on a city bus. The stranger who found it happened to be an art director at a children's publisher, and she flipped through the drawings while waiting for her stop. Two weeks later the student, who had planned none of it, signed her first illustration contract. The whole career began with one fortuitous mistake.

The lucky outcome came entirely from an unplanned accident, which is what fortuitous means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    accidental: Accidental means happening by chance, not on purpose, and it is neutral or even negative — an accidental fire, an accidental deletion. Fortuitous keeps the accident but adds a welcome result: the wrong turn that finds the waterfall. An accidental discovery might be a disaster; a fortuitous one is a gift.

    chance: Chance, used before a noun (a chance meeting, a chance remark), simply marks something as unarranged — it takes no side on whether the outcome was good. Fortuitous usually smiles: it describes the lucky accident, the one you would never undo. Any encounter can be a chance encounter; only the welcome ones read as fortuitous.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    deliberate: done on purpose

    engineered: brought about intentionally through planning

    Where it sits · how large a role luck plays

    unplanned → fortuitous → miraculous

    unplanned vs. fortuitous: An unplanned event simply was not arranged, while a fortuitous one adds a stroke of luck to the accident.

    miraculous vs. fortuitous: A fortuitous encounter is a lucky accident, but a miraculous one seems to defy the odds entirely, as if chance alone could not explain it.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral to formal register

      Fortuitous describes something that happens by accident, usually a welcome accident (a fortuitous discovery, a fortuitous meeting). Keep it distinct from propitious: fortuitous looks backward at a lucky accident that already occurred, while propitious looks forward, describing signs and conditions that favor what is about to happen.
