exult

/ig-ZULT/ - rhymes with result

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Exult means to rejoice openly and at full volume — triumphant joy let out in leaps and shouts.

See the word in context

The moment her visa approval loaded on the screen, Amara exulted — one enormous whoop that startled the whole internet cafe.

After six dense, doorstop failures, the seventh loaf rose — and Marco exulted, parading it around the kitchen on its cutting board like a championship belt.

To exult is to let triumph out — loud, physical, unmistakable rejoicing. It is intransitive: you exult, or exult in a victory, or exult over a rival's stumble (a shading that borders on gloating). Its look-alike exalt (this unit) aims praise at someone else; exult is joy aimed at the sky.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    rejoice: To rejoice is the plain, hymnal word for gladness — it can be quiet, inward, even solemn. To exult is rejoicing with the lid off: loud, physical, and unmistakable, triumph that leaps and shouts. A congregation can rejoice in silence; nobody exults in silence.

    crow: To crow is to exult a little too loudly and at somebody — boasting over a beaten rival with a rooster's tact. Exulting is joy aimed at the sky; crowing aims it at the loser. A team may exult all night with perfect grace and never crow once.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    mourn: to grieve a loss

    despair: to give up hope entirely

    Where it sits · how fully the joy breaks out

    smile → cheer → exult

    smile vs. exult: A smile is joy at its quietest — barely a ripple next to exulting.

    cheer vs. exult: A cheer is one burst of voiced joy; exulting is louder and longer than a cheer — whole-body celebration that refuses to sit down.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      literary / emphatic

      Exult when joy goes public — winning teams exult, rescued crews exult, grandmothers exult at spelling bees. It is grander than celebrate and louder than rejoice. Mind the twin: exult is your own leaping joy; exalt (this unit) lifts someone else onto the pedestal.

      For three rounds the Jefferson Middle robotics team had been the picture of composure, whispering over schematics while their robot, Beatrice, stacked blocks with surgical calm. Then the final buzzer sounded, the scores posted, and composure went out the window. The team exulted the way only twelve-year-olds can: leaping over pit tables, waving foam wrenches, roaring Beatrice's name until the announcer gave up talking over them. Notably, none of it was aimed at the second-place team, who got handshakes and, eventually, help carrying their crates to the bus. 'We weren't gloating,' the captain said later. 'We were just... loud. The ceiling seemed like the right audience.'
