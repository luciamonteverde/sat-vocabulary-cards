derelict

/DAIR-uh-likt/ - the stress falls on DAIR

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Derelict means abandoned and run-down.

See the word in context

Past the fairgrounds the derelict drive-in rots in the weeds, its screen peeled to the boards and its speaker posts leaning forty different ways.

Below Fourth Street there is a subway platform the trains pass without slowing. Closed in 1945, it has sat derelict ever since: tiles furred with soot, a shuttered fare window with its glass still cranked half open, an advertisement for a hat store three owners dead. Nobody maintains it; nobody even claims it.

Derelict is the right word because it needs both halves: abandoned and run-down. A locked summer cottage is merely empty — someone still cares for it. A shabby but crowded diner is merely worn. The platform has lost both its people and its upkeep, and that double desertion is what derelict certifies.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    abandoned: Abandoned says only that the owners left — it tells you nothing about the state of what they left behind. A freshly locked cottage can be abandoned and still spotless. Derelict needs more: the place was abandoned and then rotted, the neglect written all over it.

    dilapidated: Dilapidated judges condition alone — falling apart after years without upkeep — and says nothing about whether anyone is still there. A dilapidated bridge can carry traffic every day. Derelict makes both claims at once: no one uses the place anymore, and the decay has moved in behind them.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    maintained: kept in repair by steady care

    occupied: currently lived in or used

    Where it sits · how far the decay has run

    neglected → derelict → ruined

    neglected vs. derelict: A neglected building still has an owner who ought to show up; derelict means the caring stopped altogether and the rot moved in.

    ruined vs. derelict: Ruined goes past derelict: a derelict warehouse still stands to be photographed, while a ruined one has come down to rubble and sky.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral, with a bleak edge

    Derelict is for physical things — buildings, ships, piers — that have been abandoned and let rot; it makes the reader see the peeling paint. Its unit-mate defunct ends institutions, not structures: the ferry company goes defunct, and its terminal, if nobody buys it, goes derelict. (English also lets a person be 'derelict in duty' — neglectful — a related but separate use.)

A boarded bakery whose owner still pays taxes goes in one column; a sagging rowhouse with a family inside goes in another. The old glassworks is different.
