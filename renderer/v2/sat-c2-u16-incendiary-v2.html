Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Incendiary means deliberately designed to stir up open conflict or violence.

See the word in context

Stewards tore the incendiary poster off the canteen wall before the night shift arrived, but by then a hundred phones had photographed it.

Shakespeare stages it in the forum: Antony swears he has not come to stir anyone, then shows the crowd Caesar's torn cloak, reads them Caesar's will, and pauses in all the right places. He never says burn anything. By nightfall half of Rome is alight, which was the speech's whole incendiary design — each denial another armful of kindling, stacked where grief was already smoldering.

Incendiary is the exact word for that oration, because incendiary means deliberately designed to stir up anger or violence — the design is the point. Inflammatory language heats a room and may do it carelessly; incendiary language is aimed. Antony's speech was not a spark that happened to land badly. It was arson performed with sentences.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    inflammatory: Inflammatory language heats a room, and it can do so carelessly — a clumsy toast, one ill-chosen word at a dinner table, tempers rising with no plan behind them. Incendiary language is aimed: the heat is the design, chosen to end in real conflict. Every incendiary remark is inflammatory; the reverse is not true.

    rabble-rousing: Rabble-rousing is done out loud and in person — a speaker working a crowd's anger, feeding on the roar that comes back. Incendiary needs no stage and no voice: a printed page or an anonymous poster can be incendiary, so long as it is built to set off the explosion.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    soothing: cools the room back down

    placating: gives the anger something so it will lie down

    Where it sits · how much violence the words invite

    pointed → provocative → incendiary

    pointed vs. incendiary: A pointed remark is the mildest thing on this scale, far below incendiary — it has an edge, but it means to score a point, not to start a fire.

    provocative vs. incendiary: Provocative goes further than pointed — it is built to get a rise out of people; incendiary claims the most: language meant to end in broken glass, or worse.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / journalistic, always an accusation

      Incendiary is the strongest thing you can call language short of a criminal charge, and it accuses the author of design. Inflammatory may be an accident of tone; incendiary never is. The verb behind the effect is this unit's incite — incendiary words are the tool, inciting is the act — and a single act built to draw rage is a provocation.
