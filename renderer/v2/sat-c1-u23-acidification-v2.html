acidification

/uh-sid-ih-fih-KAY-shun/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Acidification means the process of becoming more chemically acidic, a term used especially of water chemistry.

See the word in context

Decades of sulfur emissions drove the acidification of the mountain lake until its pH fell from 6.8 to 4.9 and the trout eggs stopped hatching.

In 1968 a survey team recorded a pH of 6.9 in Cutter Lake and counted eleven fish species there. Coal plants upwind ran for another thirty years, and every rainfall carried sulfuric and nitric acid into the watershed. By 1998 the pH had fallen to 4.7, the crayfish were gone, and only two fish species remained. The report concluded that the lake's acidification had outpaced the granite bedrock's ability to buffer it.

A measured pH falling from 6.9 to 4.7 as acid rain accumulates year after year is exactly the chemical shift toward sourness that acidification names.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    souring: Souring is the everyday word for turning acid, the kitchen register of milk and vinegar. Acidification is the technical term for that change when it can be measured on the pH scale, tracked over a place and a period of time, and reported.

    acidulation: Acidulation is a deliberate act of making something slightly acid, the way a cook acidulates water with a squeeze of lemon. Acidification is the running process by which a whole system, such as a lake or a field's soil, grows steadily more acidic over time.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    alkalization: becoming more basic, the opposite of acidic

    neutralization: bringing a solution back toward a neutral pH

    Where it sits · how destructive the chemical change becomes

    tarnishing → acidification → corrosion

    tarnishing vs. acidification: Tarnishing is the mildest of these chemical changes, a thin surface film that dulls metal without harming what lies beneath, while acidification alters the chemistry of a whole lake or soil.

    corrosion vs. acidification: Corrosion goes further than acidification, naming the damage acid does once it is strong enough to eat into metal, stone, or shell.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    scientific and technical

    Acidification names a measurable process rather than a state, so it usually appears with a place and a period of time: the acidification of the lake since 1970. Keep it distinct from anthropogenic, taught in this unit, which identifies a cause (human activity); acidification identifies the chemical change itself, and that change can be driven by human emissions or by natural sources such as volcanic gas.

    When the river's freshwater mussels began disappearing in the 1980s, biologists first blamed dredging. The real culprit turned out to be quieter. Rain falling through polluted air had been washing acid into the watershed for decades, and the river's chemistry was slowly shifting. This gradual acidification did its damage invisibly: mussel shells are built from minerals that dissolve as water grows more acid, so each generation grew thinner shells than the last. No single year looked alarming. Only when researchers lined up forty years of water samples did the trend become impossible to miss. Once new air-quality rules cut the pollution, the watershed slowly recovered, and shell thickness began climbing again within a decade.
