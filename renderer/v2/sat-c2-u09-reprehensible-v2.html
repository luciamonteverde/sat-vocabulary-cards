reprehensible

/rep-rih-HEN-suh-buhl/ - the stress falls on HEN

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Reprehensible means deserving strong condemnation.

See the word in context

Charging survivors for their lifeboat seats was reprehensible even by the shipping line's standards.

For six winters, the heating-oil dealer shorted every delivery to the elderly customers least likely to check the gauge, then billed them for full tanks. When the scheme surfaced, nobody reached for mild words: cheating anyone is wrong, but engineering the cheat to land on the trusting made it reprehensible — conduct the whole town lined up to blame out loud.

Reprehensible is the exact word because it doesn't just register wrongdoing — it demands open blame. Regrettable conduct earns a wince; improper conduct breaks a rule. Reprehensible conduct is the kind decent people feel obliged to stand up and name, which is what the word's own Latin machinery does: it reaches back and seizes the wrongdoer.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    disgraceful: Disgraceful conduct brings public shame down on someone — the team, the family, the whole office winces in the open. Reprehensible aims at a different target: the deed itself deserves blame, whether or not anyone is watching or embarrassed. Disgrace needs an audience; reprehensible needs only the wrong.

    shameful: Shameful conduct is dishonorable, and the accent is on the blush — the hot embarrassment the deed ought to raise. Reprehensible keeps the accent on the judgment: the conduct deserves open condemnation, blush or no blush. A shameful act makes people look away; a reprehensible one makes them speak up.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    admirable: worth looking up to

    commendable: deserving open praise — the far shore from blame

    Where it sits · how gravely the conduct offends

    improper → reprehensible → monstrous

    improper vs. reprehensible: Improper conduct breaks the rules of the occasion — a ringing phone in the funeral row; reprehensible conduct breaks trust itself.

    monstrous vs. reprehensible: Monstrous goes past reprehensible: the reprehensible scheme deserves every word of blame it gets, while the monstrous one shocks the conscience so badly the words feel too small.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / moral

      Reprehensible is a judgment on conduct: it deserves open, public blame — not just an apology or an eye-roll. It describes the deed; its unit-mates denounce and decry describe what people then do about it. Keep it for real moral weight: calling a bad parking job reprehensible spends the word where 'careless' would pay.
