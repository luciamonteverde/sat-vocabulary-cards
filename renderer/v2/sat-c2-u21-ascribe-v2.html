What It Means

Ascribe means to assign or credit to a cause or source.

See the word in context

Fans ascribe the team's turnaround to its new goalkeeper, though the schedule softened at exactly the same moment.

The sourdough came out tangier every winter, and for years the baker ascribed the difference to her starter, a jar older than the shop itself. Then a friend boarded up the drafty cellar window as a favor, the tang mellowed in January for the first time, and the jar kept its reputation for nothing: the cold, not the culture, had been setting the flavor all along.

Ascribe is the exact word for what the baker did, because ascribe means to assign or credit to a cause or source — she wrote the tang down to the jar, rightly or not. Blame works only when the outcome is bad, credit only when it is good; ascribe stays neutral. And as the baker learned, an ascription is a claim, not a proof.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    attribute: Attribute is the everyday twin — to attribute is to assign an effect to a cause in plain conversation. Ascribe does the same assigning in a more scholarly register, where causes and sources get argued over, and it wears uncertainty comfortably: a painting ascribed to a workshop may yet be reassigned.

    credit: Credit assigns a cause too, but only for outcomes worth having — you credit the striker with the win, never with the loss. Ascribe holds the scales level: good, bad, or merely odd, an effect can be ascribed to any source without praising or blaming anyone.

    impute: Impute assigns a cause or motive with a finger pointed — faults, motives, and blame get imputed. Ascribe keeps the accusation out of it: a result can be ascribed to any source, innocent or not, while an imputed motive always carries a suspicion of wrongdoing.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    deny: refuse to grant the link at all

    dissociate: pull the effect and its supposed cause apart

    Where it sits · how firmly the cause is fixed

    suspect → ascribe → pin

    suspect vs. ascribe: Suspect is much fainter than ascribe — a suspected cause is merely wondered about, nothing assigned yet, while to ascribe is to write the effect down to its cause openly, on the record.

    pin vs. ascribe: Pin goes further than ascribe — an ascribed cause can still be argued; a fault pinned on someone is treated as settled.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      formal / scholarly

      Ascribe is the historian's and critic's verb, at home where causes and sources are argued over. Attribute, met among this unit's reference words, is its everyday twin; credit leans positive and blame negative, while ascribe holds the scales level. It also suits shaky assignments — paintings ascribed to a workshop, sayings ascribed to the wrong sage — wherever the assignment itself might be wrong.
