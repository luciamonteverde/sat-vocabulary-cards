strenuous

/STREN-yoo-uhs/ - the stress falls on STREN

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Strenuous means requiring great physical energy and stamina.

See the word in context

Moving day proved more strenuous than the couple expected; by noon the stairs had beaten them, and they hired two teenagers to finish the job.

The scallop divers' season is short and strenuous: forty pounds of gear, four dives a day, and currents that fight every kick. Rookies assume swimming skill will carry them; by the second week, most discover that no gym could have readied their legs and lungs.

Strenuous keeps its focus on the body: a strenuous task demands muscle power and stamina, whatever else it asks. Homework can be hard, but only the paper route is strenuous.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    exhausting: Exhausting means draining all your energy, of any kind — a day of interviews or grief can be exhausting without your lifting a thing. Strenuous is narrower: it names what a task demands of muscle and stamina, not what it leaves behind. A strenuous climb taxes the legs; an exhausting week can empty you at a desk.

    demanding: Demanding says a task asks a great deal, and it does not care of what — patience, brainpower, or sweat. Strenuous pins the demand to the body: great physical energy and stamina spent in the doing. A proof can be demanding from an armchair; nothing is strenuous from an armchair.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    gentle: easy on the body

    restful: restoring rather than draining

    Where it sits · how much it taxes the body

    brisk → strenuous → punishing

    brisk vs. strenuous: A brisk walk raises your heart rate pleasantly; strenuous effort makes real demands on muscle and breath.

    punishing vs. strenuous: Punishing goes beyond strenuous: a strenuous climb taxes your strength, while a punishing one damages it, leaving the body wrecked for days.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral

      Strenuous is the body's word: use it when the cost is muscle, breath, and stamina. Arduous covers difficulty and tiredness of any kind, and laborious the long slow grind; vigorous - also in this unit - describes the energy someone brings, not the energy a task demands. A vigorous swimmer enjoys a strenuous workout.
