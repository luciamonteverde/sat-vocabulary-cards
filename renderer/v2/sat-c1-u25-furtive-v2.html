furtive

/FUR-tiv/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Furtive means done secretly or stealthily, in a way that suggests hidden or guilty intent.

See the word in context

He slid the report into his bag with a furtive glance toward the supervisor's office.

Halfway through the exam, the proctor noticed a student's odd rhythm: write, pause, drop the left hand below the desk, glance sideways, write again. Each movement was small, quick, and furtive, timed for the moments the proctor turned toward the window. When she finally circled behind him, she found a strip of formulas taped inside his sleeve. It was the furtiveness of the gestures — not the gestures themselves — that had given him away.

The quick, concealed movements timed to escape the proctor's eye suggest hidden, guilty intent — that sneaky manner is exactly what furtive describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    stealthy: Stealthy means quiet and careful to escape notice — a technique of silent movement, whatever the purpose. Furtive adds the look of guilt: a furtive glance is not just quiet but shifty, as if its owner fears being caught at something. Stealth is a method; a furtive manner is a tell.

    sneaky: Sneaky means acting slyly and deceptively in secret, usually to get away with something — it judges the behavior. Furtive describes how that secrecy looks from the outside: the darting glance or half-hidden movement that suggests guilty intent. A sneaky plan is the scheme; the furtive manner is what betrays it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    open: done in plain view

    forthright: direct and honest in manner

    Where it sits · how guilty the secrecy looks

    discreet → furtive → clandestine

    discreet vs. furtive: Discreet is weaker than furtive: a discreet person keeps things quiet out of tact, with nothing to hide, while a furtive one moves as if caught halfway through a guilty act.

    clandestine vs. furtive: Clandestine is stronger than furtive — clandestine describes whole operations kept deliberately hidden, while furtive describes the darting, guilty manner of a single glance or gesture.
