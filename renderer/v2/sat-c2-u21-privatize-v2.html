privatize

/PRY-vuh-tize/ - rhymes with size

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Privatize means to transfer from government to private ownership.

See the word in context

The kingdom privatized its postal service in stages, selling the parcel routes first.

For sixty winters the town ran its own skating rink at a cheerful loss — dollar skates, cocoa at cost, hours set at the annual meeting. Last March the council privatized it, selling the rink, ice plant and all, to a family recreation company. The rink still opens at four; what changed is the deed. Season-pass prices now answer to the buyers' ledger, and the annual meeting votes on nothing colder than sidewalks.

Privatize is the exact word for that sale, because privatize means to transfer from government to private ownership — the deed moved, not just the work. Had the town merely hired the company to run its rink, that would be outsourcing, with the public still owner and the council still boss. Privatizing hands over the thing itself.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    sell off: Sell off is the plain-English version of privatize — the town sells its rink, the state sells the railway. But anyone can sell off anything, heirlooms included. Privatize is the narrower, official word: the seller is a government, and the deed crosses to private hands.

    denationalize: Denationalize is the technical twin of privatize — undo state ownership, reversing an earlier nationalization. It lives in treaties and ministry papers and usually covers whole industries. Privatize is the everyday policy word and fits any scale, a national railway or a town skating rink.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    nationalize: the state takes the deed the other way

    collectivize: fold it into shared public ownership

    Where it sits · how much ownership leaves public hands

    subcontract → outsource → privatize

    subcontract vs. privatize: Subcontract hands over the least on the way to privatize — one narrow job under the owner's own contract, with the public owner still running the show.

    outsource vs. privatize: Outsource stops short of privatize — an outsourced service is run by a private firm while the public keeps the deed; privatizing sells the thing itself.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / policy

      Privatize is policy vocabulary, and its test is the deed: only when ownership itself crosses from state to private hands is the word earned. Outsource keeps the deed public and hires out the work; deregulate loosens the rules and moves nothing; nationalize is the same journey in reverse. Editorials load the word with cheers or alarm, but the mechanism it names is neutral paperwork.

      The town council did not arrive lightly at its decision to privatize the aging ice rink. For forty years the rink had been public: town crews patched the roof, the recreation office set the skate fees, and losses came out of the general budget. Last spring the deed finally crossed to a family-run sports company, along with the keys, the debts, and the hiring. Supporters point out that the roof is fixed and the lights stay on past nine. The town no longer votes on skate fees, though, because it no longer owns the ice. That trade — control surrendered along with the costs — is the whole bargain in a single building.
