intractable

/in-TRAK-tuh-buhl/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Intractable means so stubborn that attempts to manage, control, or solve it keep failing.

See the word in context

The young colt proved so intractable that he bucked off every saddle and ignored every command the ranch hands tried.

The community garden's bamboo patch proved intractable: volunteers cut it, dug out the roots, and smothered the bed with tarps, yet new shoots kept spreading into the neighboring plots.

The bamboo resists every method the volunteers use to control it and keeps spreading anyway. Something that stubbornly defies all attempts to manage or contain it is exactly what intractable describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unmanageable: Unmanageable means impossible to handle or control; the word announces that management has failed. It is the plainest twin of intractable and often interchangeable with it. Intractable adds a sense of active, continuing stubbornness: every new method gets tried, and every method gets defeated.

    unruly: Unruly means hard to control and disorderly, and it describes behavior: an unruly crowd, an unruly mane of hair. Unruly things can usually be settled by a firmer hand. An intractable problem or person defies every method of control anyone applies, firm or gentle.

    obstinate: Obstinate describes a person digging in, stubbornly refusing to change course or cooperate on some particular point; it is willful by definition. Intractable reaches wider, covering problems, conflicts, and pain as well as people, and it points at the failure of every attempt to manage them.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    manageable: able to be handled or controlled

    docile: easily led, taught, or controlled

    Where it sits · how completely it resists control

    stubborn → intractable → ungovernable

    stubborn vs. intractable: A stubborn problem resists a first attempt or two; an intractable one defies every method anyone throws at it.

    ungovernable vs. intractable: Ungovernable is stronger than intractable, describing something so far beyond control that no authority can restrain it at all.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Formal / academic

    Intractable commonly pairs with problem, conflict, and pain, and it can also describe a person or animal that refuses to be guided. Keep it distinct from insoluble, which applies only to a problem or dispute that has no solution at all: an intractable situation resists every attempt to manage or control it, even if a solution might exist in theory.
