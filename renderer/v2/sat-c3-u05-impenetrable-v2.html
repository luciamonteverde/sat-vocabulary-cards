Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Impenetrable means impossible to pass through, enter, or pierce — and, of writing or ideas, impossible to understand.

See the word in context

Behind its moat, the citadel's outer wall proved impenetrable to every battering ram the invaders dragged against it.

Reviewers admired the physicist's results but not her writing: one editor called the book's final chapter an impenetrable wall of jargon that no reader outside her own lab could enter.

Impenetrable is an absolute. The chapter is not merely difficult — no outside reader gets in at all. A barrier a determined effort can still get through is dense or thick, not impenetrable; the word says the effort fails. And the barrier need not be physical: walls, forests, codes, and prose can all be impenetrable when they shut everything and everyone out.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    impassable: Impassable is the traveler's word: a flooded road or a snowed-in pass that cannot be crossed. Impenetrable is wider and stricter — it seals a thing against entry of every kind, and it extends to jargon, codes, and expressions, where impassable never goes.

    airtight: Airtight means sealed so completely that no air slips in or out — said of jars and cabins and, figuratively, of alibis and arguments. Impenetrable needs no seal at all and blocks everything, not just air: a forest, a fortress wall, or a page of legal boilerplate can be impenetrable.

    inscrutable: Inscrutable means impossible to read or interpret — said of faces, motives, and silences. Impenetrable can cover baffling writing too, but it starts physical: the wall or thicket nothing gets through, and only then the prose that keeps every reader outside.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    porous: letting things seep through its many tiny openings

    accessible: easy to enter, reach, or understand

    Where it sits · how completely it blocks the way through

    thick → dense → impenetrable

    thick vs. impenetrable: Thick is the mildest of the three: a thick crowd or thick fog slows your progress without ever quite stopping it, while impenetrable means the way through simply does not exist.

    dense vs. impenetrable: Dense is stronger than thick but still weaker than impenetrable: a dense stretch of forest makes every step a struggle, yet a determined effort gets through — impenetrable means that effort fails.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral

    Impenetrable is an absolute — reserve it for barriers that truly admit nothing, and reach for thick or dense while a way through still exists. It moves freely between the physical and the figurative: an impenetrable vault, an impenetrable contract, an impenetrable expression that gives nothing away. Test writers lean on the figurative use, so expect it attached to prose and plans as often as to walls.
