Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Irascible means easily angered.

See the word in context

The irascible crossing guard keeps three whistle blasts: one for cars, two for bicycles, and a long, furious third for anyone who rolls their eyes.

The community garden's plot map is really a seating plan built around Mr. Petrescu. Nobody borrows his hose. Nobody's vines stray across his string. A wheelbarrow once parked at the mouth of his row earned the entire garden a twenty-minute lecture on boundaries, delivered to a jury of nodding sunflowers. He is irascible — the fuse is short, and everyone gardens accordingly.

Irascible describes the fuse, not the explosion: how little it takes, not how bad it gets. The trap in the spelling is the ir- — it looks like the 'not' of irreversible, but it is Latin ira, anger, hiding in plain sight. Irascible means prone to anger, not immune to it; the irascible may cool in minutes, but they light at a spark.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    short-tempered: Short-tempered is the everyday report: quick to snap, no patience to burn. Irascible is the formal, character-study cousin — it makes the short fuse a fixture of the personality, the sort of thing biographers note. Your neighbor is short-tempered on moving day; the legendary editor was irascible for forty years.

    testy: Testy is a milder charge: prickly over small things, good for a snappish word or a curt reply that passes in seconds. Irascible means genuinely easy to anger — the flare is real anger on a hair trigger, not mere prickliness, even if it cools quickly.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    easygoing: slow to bother, quick to shrug

    unflappable: impossible to rattle at all

    Where it sits · how volatile the temper is

    grumpy → irascible → explosive

    grumpy vs. irascible: Grumpy is a standing, low-grade gripe with the world; irascible adds a trigger — real anger, on a hair spring.

    explosive vs. irascible: Explosive goes past irascible: the irascible captain flares ten times a day and cools; the explosive one detonates and takes the whole room with him.

    Every apprentice at the boatyard got the same warning on day one: measure twice before you ask Old Halvorsen anything. The foreman was irascible in a way the yard had come to schedule around, and his anger, though real, never lasted past the next tide. A crooked plank could send him from calm to roaring in the space of a breath. Yet the same men who braced for the roar stayed for decades, because the fury was never personal and never lingered. Ask them about Halvorsen and they would laugh: a hair trigger, they said, wired to a very short blast.
