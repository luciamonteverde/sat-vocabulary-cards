posit

/POZ-it/ - rhymes with deposit

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Posit means to put forward as a claim or possible explanation.

See the word in context

Rather than blame bad luck, the report posits a simpler cause for the failed harvest: the seed stock itself.

Where do the bay's young eels come from? No net had ever caught one being born, so the aquarium's director posited a spawning ground far offshore in the floating weed — a claim she offered to explain the yearly arrivals, and one her critics could now go and test.

Posit is the precise verb because the director put the claim forward for examination, not as settled truth. Declare would overstate her confidence; posit — literally “to place” — sets an explanation on the table where an argument can inspect it. Postulate, its cousin on the next card, goes one step further and assumes the claim as a base to build on.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    propose: To propose is to put a plan before a group so it can be adopted — a route, a budget, a hire. You posit an idea for an argument to examine; you propose an action for people to decide on. Posit hands a claim over for testing, while propose asks for a decision.

    advance: To advance a claim is to push it into the discussion with real force behind it — the speaker wants it to win ground. Posit is cooler: a posited claim is laid out for inspection, urged on no one. Advance presses; posit places and steps back.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    withdraw: to pull a claim back off the table

    recant: to formally take back what you once maintained

    Where it sits · how much is staked on the claim

    float → posit → declare

    float vs. posit: To float an idea is weaker than to posit it — floating just tosses a thought out to see who reacts, with nothing staked on it.

    declare vs. posit: Declare is stronger than posit: a posited claim is offered for testing, while a declared one is announced as settled fact.

      academic

      Posit is for essays and seminars: you posit a claim or an explanation so an argument can examine it. Its neighbors in this unit divide the work — postulate assumes a starting point for reasoning, and surmise draws a hesitant conclusion from scraps.

      Something keeps tugging at the outermost objects of the solar system. Their orbits cluster oddly, tilted the same way, as if herded by a gravity nobody has located. In 2016 two astronomers posited a ninth planet -- unseen, far beyond Neptune -- as the simplest explanation for the clustering. They did not claim to have found it. The paper laid the idea out with its own weak points attached: here is the mass that would do it, here is where it might hide, and here is what would prove us wrong. A decade of telescope time later, the planet remains an offer on the table -- a candidate explanation, standing exactly where its authors set it, waiting to be confirmed or knocked down.
