Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Domesticate means to bring a wild species under human control over many generations.

See the word in context

Cats may have half domesticated themselves, biologists argue, drifting toward the first grain stores after the mice and staying for the saucers.

The silk moth is the extreme case in every textbook. Fifty centuries of people choosing which caterpillars to keep have domesticated it so completely that the adult moth cannot fly, barely eats, and would not last a week outside its tray of mulberry leaves. There is no wild silk moth to return it to; the species itself has been reshaped, era by era, into a creature whose whole world is human hands.

Domesticate is the exact word because it means to bring a wild species under human control over many generations — the plural matters. No one moth was ever won over; breeders selected parents for age upon age until tameness was born in, not taught. That species-deep change is what separates domestication from anything one patient keeper can do.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    breed: To breed is to choose which animals or plants leave offspring — the tool, applied one generation at a time. To domesticate is what ages of that choosing add up to: a wild species remade until tameness itself is inherited. Breeding is the means; the finished, species-deep change is domestication.

    cultivate: To cultivate is to grow and tend a plant on purpose — any gardener cultivates tomatoes, wild varieties included. To domesticate goes far deeper than a season's tending: generations of selection until the species itself changes, like wheat that can no longer scatter its own seed.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    wild: never brought under human control

    untamed: still resisting handling, even when kept

    Where it sits · how deeply the change is bred in

    befriend → tame → domesticate

    befriend vs. domesticate: You can befriend a wild crow with peanuts — it trusts you and no one else, and nothing about the crow changes — which leaves befriending at the far, shallow end of the scale from domesticate.

    tame vs. domesticate: Taming goes further than befriending — one animal learns to accept handling within its own lifetime, though its offspring are born as wild as ever; to domesticate claims the most, a whole species reshaped over generations until calmness is inherited rather than learned.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / scientific

      Domesticate is a species-scale word: biologists use it for the long, inherited change that turned wolves toward dogs and wild grasses toward wheat. For one animal won over in one lifetime, the honest word is tame — a raccoon can be tamed by autumn, but no keeper alive will domesticate one. Used of people or rivers, it is a picture borrowed from the barnyard, and it keeps the same core: brought under control, made at home.
