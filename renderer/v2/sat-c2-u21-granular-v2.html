What It Means

Granular means broken down into fine detail.

See the word in context

The sleep app's granular log slices each night into five-minute bands of light, deep, and dreaming.

Grandfather's orchard ledger gave one line to each season: good year, fair year, hard frost. His grandson keeps the same ledger on a laptop, and his granular records run to forty columns — yield by row, by rootstock, by picking day, even by which crew carried the sacks. When blight crept in, the old book could only have said a bad year; the new one showed it starting in row nine, a fortnight before anyone saw a spot.

Granular is the exact word for the grandson's records, because granular means broken down into fine detail — the harvest ground down to its grains. Detailed praises thoroughness loosely; granular names the structure itself: information split into units so small that patterns show up long before they reach the surface.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fine-grained: Fine-grained is the literal image granular also draws — detail carried down to the individual grain. The two are nearly twins; granular is the analyst's preferred word for data, budgets, and logs, promising that the information arrives already split into its smallest working units.

    itemized: An itemized bill lists every charge on its own line so each entry can be checked. Granular goes past listing: it promises resolution, data milled into units so small that hidden patterns surface. Itemized is about accountability, line by line; granular is about how finely the whole is split.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    coarse: big lumps, little resolution

    big-picture: one broad stroke over everything

    Where it sits · how finely the information is split up

    broad → detailed → granular

    broad vs. granular: Broad sits at the opposite end from granular — one number for the month, and no way to see inside it.

    detailed vs. granular: Detailed stops short of granular — a detailed report covers many points; a granular one splits the data down to its smallest grains.

      Word Choice

      technical / modern

      Granular is the analyst's adjective — data, budgets, logs — and it promises structure, not just abundance: the information comes pre-milled into small units. Detailed is the loose everyday cousin; fine-grained the literal twin. Its opposite number in this unit is aggregate: aggregate rolls everything up into one figure, granular grinds the figure back down into grains.
