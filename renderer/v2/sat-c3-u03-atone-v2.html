atone

/uh-TOHN/ - rhymes with alone

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Atone means to make amends for a wrong; to repent.

See the word in context

After skipping his sister's graduation for a poker weekend, he spent months trying to atone — every airport pickup, every moving day, he was there first.

The coach who benched the wrong boy over another player's insult did not just say sorry at practice. To atone, he stood up at the awards banquet, named his mistake in front of every parent, and handed the sportsmanship trophy to the student he had shamed.

Atone is stronger than apologize because it means to make amends — to pay something real toward the wrong, not only to regret it. The public correction and the trophy are the payment; words alone would not have atoned for a public shaming.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    redeem: To redeem is to win back your standing or worth after a failure — you redeem yourself, in your own eyes or others'. Atone points the other way, at the wrong itself: you atone for the deed by repairing the harm it caused, whatever your reputation does afterward.

    make up for: Make up for is the everyday, low-stakes version: you make up for a forgotten call with coffee and a sheepish apology. Atone is morally loaded — it assumes a real wrong and demands real, costly repair, something paid toward the damage before the debt feels settled.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    offend: to commit the wrong in the first place

    gloat: to enjoy the harm done instead of repairing it

    Where it sits · how fully the wrong is made good

    regret → apologize → atone

    regret vs. atone: Regret is the faintest step and stops well short of atoning — a private ache over the wrong that no one else ever sees, with nothing yet said and nothing repaired.

    apologize vs. atone: An apology puts the regret into spoken words, but it stops short of atoning, which requires repairing the harm itself.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / weighty

    Atone is a morally loaded verb: it assumes a real wrong and demands real repair, and it always travels with for — you atone for something. For small stuff, apologize or make up for is the natural register; save atone for wrongs that cost something to put right.

    For forty years the paper mill on the Weller River paid its fines and kept dumping, and the trout runs thinned to nothing. When the founder's granddaughter took over, she told the town the company would not merely apologize for its history -- it would atone for it. The distinction, she said at the town meeting, was cost: an apology is words, but atonement is repair paid for by the one who did the harm. The mill funded the dredging of six miles of riverbed out of its own profits, not out of a settlement it was forced into. Some residents stayed skeptical, but even they conceded that stocking the river each spring, year after year, was repair aimed at the damage itself rather than at the company's image.
