Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Appalling means causing shock, horror, or disgust.

See the word in context

The star's appalling behavior at the awards banquet — mocking the hosts, tipping over a chair — cost him three sponsors by morning.

When the relief ship finally docked, the newsmen who went aboard came down the gangway gray-faced. Wounded men lay two to a berth; the water casks were green; the surgeon had been rationing bandages for a month. The stories printed that week called the whole scene appalling, and enlistment in the port towns fell by half.

Appalling is the exact word for such a scene, because it means causing shock, horror, or disgust — the sight does not merely surprise you; it turns your stomach and drains the color from your face. Shocking stops at the jolt. Dreadful is loose and general. Appalling says the thing itself is a disgrace to look at.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    shocking: Shocking covers the jolt of pure surprise — a result no one saw coming — and stops there. Appalling goes past surprise into moral disgust: the sight is not just startling but a disgrace to look at. News can be shocking without anyone being at fault; appalling says the thing itself is shameful.

    dreadful: Dreadful is loose, all-purpose badness — dreadful weather, a dreadful commute — with no verdict attached. Appalling is specific and moral: what you are looking at shocks and disgusts, and someone is answerable for it. Dreadful complains; appalling condemns.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    admirable: worth looking up to

    heartening: giving hope instead of dismay

    Where it sits · how deep the moral shock cuts

    disappointing → appalling → monstrous

    disappointing vs. appalling: Disappointing is far milder than appalling — the results merely fell short of hopes, and everyone sighs and moves on; nothing here turns a stomach.

    monstrous vs. appalling: Monstrous goes further than appalling — appalling conditions disgust whoever reads the report; monstrous cruelty makes them question the humanity of whoever caused it.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    strong / condemning

    Appalling judges what you see: conditions, conduct, findings. Its unit-mate harrowing faces the other way, weighing what an ordeal does to the people inside it — a report is appalling to read; the escape it describes was harrowing to live. Below both sit shocking (mere surprise) and dreadful (vague badness).

    In 1908 the photographer Lewis Hine began slipping into coal towns with a hidden agenda and a camera. What he recorded was appalling: boys of eight and nine hunched over coal chutes ten hours a day, their fingers bleeding from sorting rock, their lungs full of dust. Mine owners knew the boys' ages. Paymasters wrote false birth dates into the ledgers, and inspectors were steered past the breaker rooms. Hine's photographs left viewers no room to look away, and no one to excuse. The shame belonged to the men who profited. Within a decade, the pictures had done what pamphlets could not, and the first serious child labor laws began to pass.
