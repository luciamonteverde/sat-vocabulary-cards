Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Raucous means loud and boisterous.

See the word in context

Dawn at the rookery is raucous — ten thousand gulls contesting every ledge at full volume.

The county fair saves the hog-calling contest for dusk, when the midway is packed: forty contestants bellowing in turn, judges wheezing with laughter, a grandstand of farm families pounding the boards and howling scores nobody asked for. You could find the event from the parking lot with your eyes shut. Raucous is not a complaint here — it is the whole point.

Raucous is the exact word because it needs both volume and rowdiness: loud, and loud in an unruly, laughing key. A jackhammer is merely loud; a library after a prank is merely disorderly. The raucous grandstand is both at once — noise with jostling life in it, usually more fun to be inside than beside.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    rowdy: Rowdy is disorderly high spirits — shoving, chanting, spilling over the rails. Raucous needs that unruliness plus the sheer volume of it: a scuffling hallway can be rowdy at a whisper, but a raucous one is heard three classrooms away. Rowdy names the behavior; raucous adds the roar.

    uproarious: Uproarious means convulsed with loud laughter — the comedy club at the night's best punchline. Raucous is broader: loud and unruly in any key, laughing or not. An uproarious room is always laughing; a raucous one may be cheerfully arguing every call with no joke anywhere in it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    hushed: held to a whisper

    restrained: kept deliberately in check

    Where it sits · how much disorder joins the volume

    noisy → raucous → riotous

    noisy vs. raucous: Noisy measures decibels and nothing else; raucous adds rowdy, laughing disorder to the volume.

    riotous vs. raucous: Riotous goes past raucous: the raucous party rattles the windows, while the riotous one has somebody's fence in the bonfire — disorder graduated from sound to deed.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    vivid / often fond

    Raucous is for noise with rowdy life in it — crowds, gulls, locker rooms, fairs — and it often carries a grin: the raucous reunion was probably a good one. When the sound is mechanical or joyless, a plainer word like thunderous will serve; raucous insists that somebody is having a loud, unruly time.

    Visitors expect the exchange floor to be busy; nothing prepares them for the sound of it. By the opening bell the pit is raucous — traders bellowing bids over one another, jackets grabbed, tickets flung, the roar carrying two floors up. A first-timer once asked our guide whether a fight had broken out. Nothing was wrong, she said; that was just Tuesday. The din is not chaos for its own sake. Every shout is an order, every shove a path to a counter-party; the disorder is the market working. At the closing bell it stops all at once, and the sudden quiet feels louder than anything that came before it.
