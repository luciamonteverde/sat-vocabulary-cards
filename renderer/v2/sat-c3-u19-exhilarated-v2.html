Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Exhilarated means filled with a rush of energizing happiness — thrilled, wide awake, and charged with fresh energy.

See the word in context

Exhilarated by her first solo landing, Noor taxied in grinning and talking at twice her normal speed.

The crew came off the whitewater exhilarated — soaked, breathless, half-shouting — and stood in the parking lot replaying every rapid before their life jackets were even unclipped.

Exhilaration is joy with voltage in it. The rafters aren't calmly satisfied; the thrill has flooded them with energy — racing pulses, loud voices, the itch to go again — and that electric, wide-awake charge is exactly what exhilarated adds to plain happy.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    thrilled: Thrilled is the sudden jolt of excitement — the spike when the news lands. Exhilarated is the jolt plus the current that follows: energy still surging after the moment has passed, mind wide awake and body ready for more.

    invigorated: Invigorated means refreshed and restored to energy — a cold shower invigorates without any joy attached. Exhilarated runs on the same fresh energy but is unmistakably happy about it: the charge and the delight arrive together.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    listless: drained of energy and interest

    weary: worn down and wanting rest

    Where it sits · how much voltage the happiness carries

    pleased → exhilarated → euphoric

    pleased vs. exhilarated: Pleased is far milder than exhilarated: a pleased person smiles and carries on, while an exhilarated one is charged — loud, quick, and hunting for the next hill.

    euphoric vs. exhilarated: Euphoric is stronger than exhilarated: exhilaration is a vivid, energizing rush that stays tethered to its cause, while euphoria floods everything, a high so total it can float free of reason.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    vivid / kinetic

    Exhilarated is the body's word for joy: pulse, breath, and voltage. Elated, taught earlier in this unit, climbs high but can sit still; exhilaration never sits — it wants motion, another run, a colder wind. Use it when the happiness is physical and freshly caused. For quiet good moods it overclaims, and for a high so total it floats free of its cause, English usually reaches for euphoric instead.
