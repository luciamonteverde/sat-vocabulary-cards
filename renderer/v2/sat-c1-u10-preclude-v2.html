Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Preclude means to prevent something from happening — to make it impossible in advance.

See the word in context

A clause in her contract precludes her from working for a competitor for two full years.

Because the two required classes meet at exactly the same hour, taking chemistry this semester precludes taking orchestra.

The schedule conflict doesn't merely make orchestra harder — it shuts off the possibility entirely, in advance. That is the force of preclude: the door is closed before you ever reach it.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    prevent: Prevent is the everyday, all-purpose word for keeping something from happening — a fence prevents accidents. Preclude is its formal relative with a sharper edge: something is made impossible in advance, shut off before it can even start, and it appears mostly in legal and official prose. Anything precluded is prevented, but preclude insists the door never opened at all.

    bar: To bar something is to block it by rule or authority — a person or institution does the barring: the league bars ineligible players. Preclude works impersonally: a schedule conflict or an injury can preclude something with no authority involved. Bar needs someone wielding a rule; preclude needs only circumstances that close the door.

    rule out: To rule out is to eliminate a possibility, usually by deciding or reasoning — a doctor rules out one diagnosis, a planner rules out a venue. Someone weighs an option and strikes it from the list. Preclude does the striking without anyone deciding: conditions themselves make the thing impossible in advance.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    permit: allow to happen

    enable: make possible

    Where it sits · how completely the door is closed

    hinder → preclude → ban

    hinder vs. preclude: To hinder something is only to slow it down — it can still happen. To preclude it is to shut the door on it entirely, before it can even start.

    ban vs. preclude: A ban adds force and authority on top of what preclude does: something is formally forbidden, with penalties for anyone who tries anyway.

      Formal

      Preclude appears mostly in legal, academic, and official prose: a rule precludes an action, or precludes someone from doing something. It signals true impossibility, not mere discouragement — if bad weather merely makes a picnic unpleasant, it hasn't precluded it.
