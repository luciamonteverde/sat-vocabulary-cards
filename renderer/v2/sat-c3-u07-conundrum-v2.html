What It Means

Conundrum means a puzzle or difficult problem.

See the word in context

Scheduling the twins' recitals is this spring's conundrum: both fall on Saturday at noon, in towns an hour apart.

Casting the school play is Mr. Abara's yearly conundrum: the strongest singer can't act, the strongest actor can't sing, and the show needs both in a single lead.

A conundrum is more than a question — it's a problem whose options undercut one another, so that every straightforward answer opens the next difficulty.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    riddle: A riddle is a puzzle posed as a game, with a clever answer waiting for whoever thinks sideways enough. A conundrum is no game: it is a real problem whose options undercut one another, and cleverness alone may never crack it.

    dilemma: A dilemma is a forced choice between exactly two bad options — you must walk through one door or the other. A conundrum is wider: any stubbornly hard problem, with two paths or ten, that resists every clean answer.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    cinch: something so easy it needs no thought

    certainty: a thing already settled beyond question

    Where it sits · how stubbornly the problem resists solving

    snag → puzzle → conundrum

    snag vs. conundrum: A snag is a passing hitch, fixed by the time lunch is over; it sits far below a conundrum.

    puzzle vs. conundrum: A puzzle stops short of a conundrum — it has an answer waiting once you're clever enough, while a conundrum resists even its cleverest readers.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral

    Conundrum names a problem that resists every clean answer — budgets, ethics, scheduling. Its unit-mate paradox is different: a paradox may be true exactly as it stands, while a conundrum sits there demanding a solution nobody has.

    The town of Marrow Point has argued about its lighthouse for eleven years. The cliff beneath it loses a little ground to every winter storm, and engineers agree the tower will eventually fall if nothing is done. Moving the lighthouse inland would save the structure but cost more than the town's entire yearly budget; leaving it in place means losing the coast's oldest landmark; building a seawall would protect the cliff while starving the beach below of sand. Every option the council studies weakens the case for another, which is why the mayor has taken to calling the lighthouse the town's standing conundrum. This year's meeting ended like the last ten: with a vote to keep studying.
