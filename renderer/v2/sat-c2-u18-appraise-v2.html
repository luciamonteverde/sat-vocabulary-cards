Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Appraise means to assess the worth or value of something.

See the word in context

The county sends an assessor every five years to appraise each farm, and tax bills rise or fall with her figure.

When the city debated selling its oldest firehouse, it first hired a valuer to appraise the building. Her report weighed the cracked foundation against the copper roof and the corner lot, and the number she signed became the floor for every offer that followed.

Appraise is the precise verb because it means to assess the worth or value of something — not admiring it, not selling it, but arriving at a defensible number or judgment of quality. You can also appraise without a price tag: a coach appraises a recruit, a general an enemy line — always the cool, weighing look.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    gauge: To gauge something is to estimate its measure — depth, mood, risk — with or without numbers, and nobody expects you to sign the result. To appraise is to gauge for the record: worth assessed carefully enough that someone will act on the figure. You gauge the crowd's patience; you appraise the estate.

    size up: Size up is the quick, informal read — a bouncer sizes up the line, a scout sizes up a rival, all in seconds and nothing written down. Appraise is the slow, accountable version: worth weighed carefully enough to defend, often with a signature under it. Sizing up ends where appraising begins.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    guess: a number named with nothing behind it

    overlook: to pass by without weighing at all

    Where it sits · how thorough the examination is

    eyeball → appraise → audit

    eyeball vs. appraise: To eyeball is milder than to appraise — value judged from across the room, no records pulled, no signature checked; appraising crosses the room, pulls the records, and stands behind the number.

    audit vs. appraise: An audit goes further than appraising — the appraiser signs one careful number; the auditor combs every receipt behind it.

    Word Choice

    formal / commercial

    Appraise is the professional's verb: a signed, defensible judgment of value — houses, jewels, damage, talent. Its unit-mates keep their own lanes: prudence is the caution that orders the appraisal, criteria are the standards it scores against. For a casual once-over, size up; save appraise for the judgment you'd put your name to.

    When the Vasquez family decided to sell their grandmother's emerald ring, the auction house would not set a reserve price on a guess. It sent the ring to a certified gemologist, who spent two days with it: testing the stone, tracing the setting's hallmarks, pulling sale records for comparable pieces. Her job was to appraise the ring -- to fix its worth carefully enough that buyers, sellers, and the insurer could all act on the same number. The figure she signed, $46,000, went into the catalog and the insurance binder alike. A friend at a dinner party had once glanced at the ring and guessed twice that. Nobody printed his number anywhere.
