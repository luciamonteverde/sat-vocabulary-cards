Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Capricious means subject to whim; changing unpredictably.

See the word in context

Three-year-olds are capricious diners — yesterday's beloved noodles are today's outrage, and no menu survives the week.

The house cat has decided, this Tuesday, that the blue armchair is the only acceptable throne; by Thursday the armchair no longer merits a glance and only the laundry basket will do. Offer a reason and you'll be wrong by the weekend. Her affections are capricious — governed by whim, and by nothing else anyone can name.

Capricious is stronger than changeable: plenty of things change on schedule or for reasons — tides, moods before coffee. A capricious mind changes without pattern or cause, so no one can prepare for it; that whiff of power over everyone waiting on the whim is the word's signature.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    flighty: Flighty describes a person who is scattered and easily distracted — attention fluttering off mid-sentence — and it stays light, almost fond. Capricious is heavier: the changes follow no reason at all, and someone is usually stuck paying for the whim — a capricious ruler, a capricious referee.

    arbitrary: Arbitrary means decided without any reason — the cutoff was forty because someone said forty. What it does not require is motion: an arbitrary choice can be made once and never revisited, while capricious decisions keep changing, whim after whim, each reversing the last.

    fickle: Fickle is about likings and loyalties that switch fast — a fickle fan drops the team after one lost game. Capricious is wider and harder to live under: not just affections but rulings and demands shifting on pure whim, with no pattern anyone can learn.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    steady: holding the same course day after day

    consistent: matching itself — same input, same answer, every time

    Where it sits · how little pattern the shifts follow

    changeable → temperamental → capricious

    changeable vs. capricious: Changeable weather shifts often, but often for reasons a forecaster can map; capricious cuts deeper — change with no pattern or cause at all.

    temperamental vs. capricious: A temperamental engine acts up when it's cold, and you learn its moods; capricious goes further, swinging on pure whim that no one can learn.

      Word Choice

      literary / judging

      Capricious passes judgment: it says the changes have no reason and everyone else pays for the whim — capricious rulings, capricious weather. Its neighbor in this unit, dynamic, treats ceaseless change as life and energy; call a market dynamic and you praise it, call it capricious and you warn people off.
