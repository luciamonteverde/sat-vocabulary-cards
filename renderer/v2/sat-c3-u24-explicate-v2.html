What It Means

Explicate means to explain in careful detail.

See the word in context

The planetarium guide paused the star show to explicate one constellation myth, tracing how each pictured star earned its place in the story.

It took the volunteer an hour to explicate the community garden's watering schedule to the new members: which beds drink daily and why, how the rain barrels feed the drip lines, what the colored flags on the taps actually signal. By the end, the whiteboard was full and so were the notebooks.

Explicate promises more than explain: it is explanation in careful, systematic detail, the kind that unfolds a dense text or system piece by piece. You explain a joke in a sentence; you explicate a sonnet, a statute or a wiring diagram.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unpack: To unpack is the casual classroom verb for opening an idea up so a group can see inside it. To explicate is its formal, full-dress cousin: a systematic working-through of something dense, carried out in careful detail with nothing skipped.

    interpret: To interpret is to say what something means — the verdict on its meaning. To explicate goes further: it shows, step by step, how the text builds that meaning, unfolding each image and turn in order.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    muddle: to tangle a clear thing into confusion

    garble: to scramble a message until it cannot be followed

    Where it sits · how fully the subject is unpacked

    mention → explain → explicate

    mention vs. explicate: A mention merely points at the topic in passing and moves on; it stops far short of explicate.

    explain vs. explicate: Explain goes further than mention — the listener actually understands — but explicate outruns explain by unfolding every detail in order, leaving nothing fuzzy.

    Word Choice

    formal / academic

    Explicate is for full-dress explanation of something dense — a poem, a contract, a proof — carried out in systematic detail. Explain covers everyday clarifying; annotate, this unit's margin-note verb, adds explanations beside a text rather than building one long account; and expound, in this unit's reference list, stresses holding forth at length.

    When the world championship went to a seventh game, the broadcast booth brought in a retired grandmaster to explicate the decisive endgame for viewers at home. She refused to rush. Move by move, she rebuilt the position on the demonstration board, naming what each piece defended and what each square threatened. A colleague had already called the game 'a masterpiece of patience.' That verdict, she said, was true but useless on its own. Understanding meant watching the win get built: why the knight retreated on move forty, what the quiet pawn push prepared, how the trap closed six moves later. By the end, viewers who had never studied chess could follow the whole design. Nothing had been skipped, and nothing had to be taken on faith.
