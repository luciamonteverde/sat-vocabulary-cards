incarnate

/in-KAR-nuht/ - the stress falls on KAR

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Incarnate means existing in bodily or human form.

See the word in context

To the harbor crews the old pilot was reliability incarnate, first aboard in any weather for forty years.

The novelist's editor kept one note pinned above her desk: the detective must be skepticism incarnate — a man who double-checks the tide tables against the tide.

Incarnate usually stands after its noun — greed incarnate, patience incarnate — and it claims more than resemblance: in- ('in, into') + caro, carnis ('flesh') says the quality itself has taken on a body. Symbolic and personified stop at representation; incarnate walks in the door.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    embodied: Embodied is the cool, general word for an idea given concrete form — a policy embodied in a document, a value in a ritual — and no flesh is required. Incarnate makes the stronger, stranger claim: the quality itself has taken on a living body.

    corporeal: Corporeal simply means having a physical body — flesh rather than spirit — and any creature qualifies. Incarnate runs the claim the other way: not that a being has a body, but that an abstraction like greed or patience has stepped into one.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    ghostly: present only as a bodiless spirit

    intangible: untouchable, existing only as an idea

    Where it sits · how physically present the idea becomes

    symbolic → personified → incarnate

    symbolic vs. incarnate: Symbolic is the faintest form — a thing merely stands for the idea — and stops far short of incarnate, where the idea walks around in a body.

    personified vs. incarnate: Personified gives the idea a human face in art or speech, but stops short of incarnate, which claims the idea is actually present in flesh.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / literary

      Incarnate is the dramatic word — an abstraction present in flesh, usually placed after its noun: chaos incarnate. Embodied and personified are cooler and stop at representation. Keep incarnate for beings; a tidy spreadsheet is not order incarnate, but your aunt might be.

They did not mean she followed the safety rules. They meant something stranger — that the rules seemed to have taken on a body and gone walking. Ropes she passed tightened themselves, the joke went; trapdoors latched at the sound of her step. Watching her cross the fly floor, you were not watching a woman think about care.
