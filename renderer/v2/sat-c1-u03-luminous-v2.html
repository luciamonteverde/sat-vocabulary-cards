luminous

/LOO-mih-nuhs/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Luminous means giving off or reflecting a steady, glowing light.

See the word in context

The old clock's luminous hands glowed faintly green in the darkened bedroom.

Campers setting up late at night noticed dozens of small luminous specks blinking on and off across the meadow — fireflies signaling to each other in the dark, each one glowing on its own without any outside light source.

The fireflies weren't reflecting a flashlight or the moon; they generate their own glow, which is exactly what "luminous" describes: something that shines with its own steady light.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    radiant: Radiant describes light — or warmth, or joy — streaming outward strongly from its source: a radiant sun, a radiant smile. Luminous is quieter: a steady glow, often soft, that a thing gives off or holds. Radiance pours out; luminous things simply shine.

    incandescent: Incandescent technically means glowing from heat, like a metal filament, and it usually implies intense, hot brightness. Luminous makes no claim about heat or intensity: watch hands and fireflies are luminous — cool, faint, and steady.

    shining: Shining is the everyday word for giving off or reflecting light — the sun, a polished car, wet streets. Luminous is more particular: a steady glow that seems to come from the thing itself, often visible in the dark.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    dim: giving off very little light

    opaque: blocking light entirely rather than producing or passing it

    Where it sits · how steadily the light is given off

    bright → glowing → luminous

    bright vs. luminous: Something bright simply gives a strong, clear light, without necessarily implying the soft, steady glow of something luminous.

    glowing vs. luminous: Something glowing gives off a steady, gentle light, and luminous goes one step further: the glow seems to come from the thing itself, holding steady even in the dark.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    descriptive, literal or figurative

    Luminous is most often literal — describing an actual glow, like paint, a screen, or a firefly — but it can also be used figuratively for an especially bright or vivid quality, as in 'a luminous performance.'
