What It Means

Distend means to swell out or expand from pressure building inside.

See the word in context

As the crew pumped in more helium, the giant parade balloon began to distend until its cartoon cheeks looked ready to pop.

The veterinarian frowned as she examined the cow, whose left side had begun to distend alarmingly. Gas trapped in its stomach was pushing outward, and she needed to relieve the pressure before the swelling grew any worse.

The cow's side is swelling outward because of pressure building inside its stomach, which is a textbook example of what it means to distend.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    swell: To swell is the everyday word for growing larger, from a sprained ankle to a rising river, and it says nothing about the cause. To distend is technical and specific: internal pressure pushes a surface outward, usually past its normal limit. An ankle swells; a stomach filling with trapped gas distends.

    bulge: To bulge is to stick out in a rounded lump at one spot - a wallet bulges in a pocket, a wall bulges after a leak. To distend is for the whole container: pressure from inside stretches the entire surface outward. One overstuffed pocket bulges; an overinflated balloon distends all over.

    inflate: To inflate is to fill something with air or gas on purpose so it expands - you inflate tires and balloons, and the process is controlled. To distend is what happens when pressure builds past that point: the surface swells tautly outward, often alarmingly. You inflate a raft; a blocked stomach distends.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    shrink: to become smaller

    deflate: to collapse as air or gas escapes

    Where it sits · how close to giving way it comes

    expand → distend → burst

    expand vs. distend: Almost anything can expand a little, but something that distends swells tightly outward from pressure trapped inside it.

    burst vs. distend: A distended surface is stretched near its limit, and if the pressure keeps building it may finally burst.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral, often technical

    Distend is common in medical and scientific writing, especially in the collocations 'distended stomach' and 'distended abdomen.' It almost always implies pressure from inside pushing a surface outward, not simple growth in size.
