Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Self-reinforcing means strengthening itself so that each round makes the next more likely.

See the word in context

Confidence at the free-throw line is self-reinforcing; each make steadies the hands for the next.

Watch a hillside pasture and you can see why every cattle path is self-reinforcing: hooves wear the grass to bare dirt, rain finds the bare line and softens it, and soft ground pulls the next day's herd into exactly the same groove. Within a few seasons the path is a trench no cow chose and no cow can avoid.

Self-reinforcing is the precise label because each round strengthens the conditions for the next — the loop's output becomes its own input, and the effect grows. A merely repeated event lacks this: a weekly market recurs without deepening. The trench is different; every pass makes the following pass more certain.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    snowballing: Snowballing is the informal picture of a self-reinforcing process: a rolling mass growing bigger with every turn, the avalanche already in view. It belongs in headlines and conversation; self-reinforcing is the analyst's term, precise about the mechanism — output feeding back in as input.

    compounding: Compounding is the accountant's version of self-reinforcing growth: adding gains on top of previous gains, the way interest earns interest. It lives among quantities and rates. Self-reinforcing is broader and causal — each round strengthens the conditions for the next, whether anything is being counted or not.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    self-correcting: pulling itself back toward balance

    self-defeating: undoing itself with every round

    Where it sits · how much momentum the cycle builds

    recurring → self-reinforcing → runaway

    recurring vs. self-reinforcing: Recurring is the weaker fact — a recurring bill merely comes back; a self-reinforcing one comes back bigger because of the last round.

    runaway vs. self-reinforcing: Runaway goes further than self-reinforcing: a self-reinforcing loop still creeps, gathering strength turn by turn, while a runaway process has snapped every brake.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    analytic

    Self-reinforcing is the analyst's word for feedback loops that grow — panics, streaks, ruts, booms. Its unit-mate self-perpetuating claims only continuation: a process that keeps itself going. When the loop also gains strength each round, self-reinforcing is the sharper choice; when it merely never needs outside fuel, say self-perpetuating. Snowballing says the same thing informally, with the avalanche already in view.

    Rangeland ecologists describe a dry hillside as a system that can tip. Grass holds soil; soil holds water; water grows grass. Lose a little grass, and the loop runs the other way. Each rain now strips soil the roots no longer hold, so less water soaks in, so less grass returns the following year. The degradation is self-reinforcing: every round of loss makes the next round of loss easier. That is why crews reseed after a single bad season instead of waiting -- once the feedback settles in, no one planting grass can outpace it.
