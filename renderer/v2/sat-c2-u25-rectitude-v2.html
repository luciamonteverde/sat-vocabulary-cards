Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Rectitude means moral uprightness; strict honesty in one's dealings.

See the word in context

Everyone trusted the scorekeeper's rectitude: she once erased her own daughter's point total and re-added it in front of witnesses.

Halfway through a routine review, Dr. Lin found a miscount buried in her own most-cited study. Nobody else would ever have caught it; the conclusion barely moved. She emailed the journal that afternoon asking to publish the correction under her own name. Colleagues called her rectitude reputation damage. She called it arithmetic.

Rectitude is the exact word for what she showed, because it means moral uprightness and strict honesty — conduct held straight when bending would cost nothing. Integrity is the broad everyday term; rectitude is sterner and more formal, a straightness you could audit.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    integrity: Integrity is wholeness of character — the broad, warm, everyday word for a person whose actions match their values. Rectitude is sterner and more formal: strict, auditable straightness in one's dealings, every rule kept whether or not anyone is watching or asking.

    uprightness: Uprightness is the same straightness of conduct said plainly — the ordinary word for someone who deals fair and keeps their hands clean. Rectitude is its formal, slightly starched register, the term a citation or a court opinion reaches for, carrying an extra shade of strictness.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    crookedness: bent dealings, corners quietly cut

    dishonesty: plain lying and cheating

    Where it sits · how high a moral bar is kept

    honesty → rectitude → saintliness

    honesty vs. rectitude: Honesty is a much milder claim than rectitude — the truth told when asked, the change counted right — while rectitude means a whole career of dealings held straight, rule by rule.

    saintliness vs. rectitude: Saintliness goes further than rectitude — it gives away the coat no rule required; rectitude keeps every rule to the letter, asked or not.

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / admiring

    Rectitude praises conduct that runs straight when bending would be easy — a career of rules kept and corners refused. Integrity is the broad everyday word; rectitude is more formal and a shade sterner, uprightness you could audit. It carries no re- prefix and no 'again' anywhere in it — see this card's word-building panel — and no unit-mate shares its lane, though a reprobate is precisely the person rectitude never describes.

    When the province needed someone to run its new lottery, it passed over livelier candidates for Odile Marchand, a retired bank examiner. Her rectitude was already a byword: in thirty years of audits she had never accepted so much as a cup of coffee from a bank under review. She brought the same strictness to the lottery office -- every drawing filmed, every rule published, her own tickets forbidden by a rule she wrote herself. Critics called the regime cold. Marchand agreed, cheerfully. Warmth, she said, could be counterfeited; a record in which every rule is kept to the letter, watched or unwatched, cannot.
