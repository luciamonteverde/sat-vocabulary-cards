anachronistic

/uh-nak-ruh-NIS-tik/ - the stress falls on NIS

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Anachronistic means out of its correct place in time — belonging to a different period, earlier or later, than the one in which it appears.

See the word in context

The mural's one anachronistic touch is beloved anyway: a steam locomotive puffing along behind the medieval harvest, four centuries ahead of the rails.

Halfway through the Civil War film, a critic spotted a jet's contrail crossing the sky behind the cavalry charge — one anachronistic streak of white that no amount of costume research could excuse.

Anachronistic is the exact word because it names something sitting outside its proper time: contrails belong to the age of jet engines, and the film's sky belongs to 1863. The detail is not wrong in itself — only wrongly placed in time, and that misplacement is precisely what anachronistic points to.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    incongruous: Incongruous means out of place in its surroundings, whatever the reason — a mismatch of kind, style, or mood. Anachronistic is the special case where the mismatch is one of time: the thing belongs to a different era than the scene around it.

    antiquated: Antiquated means so old-fashioned it feels quaint or outmoded, judged against the present's own standards. A thing can be antiquated without ever leaving its era; anachronistic says it has crossed into the wrong one — in either direction, too early or too late.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    contemporary: belonging to the same era as its surroundings

    current: fully in step with the present moment

    Where it sits · how badly it is out of its time

    dated → anachronistic → fossilized

    dated vs. anachronistic: A dated phrase is merely a step behind the times — a lighter charge than anachronistic, which says the thing belongs to another era altogether.

    fossilized vs. anachronistic: Fossilized goes further than anachronistic: not just astray in the wrong era, but frozen so completely in a past form that all life and change have left it.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral - the historian's and critic's word

      Reach for anachronistic when the problem is placement in time: the thing belongs to a different period than the scene around it, whether it shows up too early or lingers too late — a wristwatch in a Roman epic, a town crier on a modern payroll. If something is simply behind the times in its own present, antiquated is the honest word, and if the mismatch has nothing to do with time — wrong style, wrong setting — incongruous covers it. Keep it apart from this unit's ephemeral and transitory, which measure how long a thing lasts, not whether it landed in the right era.
