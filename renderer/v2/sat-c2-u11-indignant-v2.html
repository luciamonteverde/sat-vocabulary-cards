Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Indignant means feeling or showing anger at something unjust or unfair.

See the word in context

Maribel came back to a ticket on the one car on the block with a valid permit, and stood indignant in the street, waving it after the departing scooter.

Mrs. Okoye's rhubarb pie was disqualified twenty minutes before judging, for a rule about store-bought crusts that appears nowhere in the printed booklet. She did not cry and she did not shrug. Indignant, she stood at the officials' table with the booklet open, reading the rules aloud, slowly, page by page, and asked the head judge to point to the one she had broken.

Indignant is the exact word because it names anger at unfairness in particular. She isn't merely disappointed at losing, and this isn't a tantrum; the heat comes from the injustice — a rule invented after the fact. Anger says how she feels; indignant says why the feeling is earned.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    outraged: Outraged is the same anger at unfairness that indignant names, but at full boil and usually at full volume — a public eruption. Indignant keeps its control: the anger has a case to make and is still choosing its words. Outrage erupts; indignant argues.

    offended: Offended is the sting of a personal slight — a rude remark, a snub, a joke at your expense — and it is about hurt feelings. Indignant is about injustice: something unfair has been done, and the anger can point to it and argue. You are offended by an insult; you are indignant at a wrong.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unruffled: calm, feathers unstirred

    gratified: pleased at how things went

    Where it sits · how much wrong fuels the anger

    annoyed → indignant → incensed

    annoyed vs. indignant: Annoyed is the small coin of irritation, spent on traffic and slow elevators; indignant is anger with a wrong at the bottom of it.

    incensed vs. indignant: Incensed goes past indignant — the anger has caught fire and stopped choosing its words.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral

      Indignant is anger with a case to make: something unfair happened, and the feeling knows it. Plain angry needs no reason; indignant supplies one. Its noun is this unit's indignation, and the pair travel together — an indignant customer, full of indignation. If no injustice is anywhere in sight, the honest word is just annoyed.
