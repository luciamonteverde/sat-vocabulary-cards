What It Means

Implicit means understood or meant without being directly stated.

See the word in context

Neither roommate ever said it out loud, but there was an implicit agreement that whoever cooked dinner never had to wash the dishes.

When the landlord remarked, 'Good tenants usually stay for years,' the message to the young couple was implicit: he expected them to choose the two-year lease rather than the six-month one.

The landlord never directly asked them to sign the longer lease — the expectation was folded into his remark and understood without being stated. That unstated-but-understood quality is exactly what implicit means.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unspoken: Unspoken means never said aloud, though both sides know it — an unspoken rivalry, an unspoken worry. It covers anything left in silence, however it arose. Implicit is more specific: the meaning is folded inside something that was expressed — a remark, a contract, an action — waiting to be read out of it.

    tacit: Tacit describes agreement given silently: no discussion happens, and consent is shown by not objecting. Implicit meaning rides inside words or actions that did occur — a landlord's remark carrying an unstated expectation. When silence itself does the agreeing, the word is tacit; when a statement hints beyond itself, it is implicit.

    understood: Understood means accepted without ever being put into words — 'it's understood that you call if you'll be late.' It points to standing arrangements everyone already accepts. Implicit points instead to meaning carried inside a particular statement or act, which a listener must draw out. An understood rule simply exists; an implicit message gets read.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    explicit: stated fully, directly, and clearly

    stated: put into words outright

    Where it sits · how deeply the unstated idea sits

    hinted → implicit → ingrained

    hinted vs. implicit: A hinted idea is lightly suggested once and might easily be missed — fainter than an implicit one, whose meaning is genuinely built in even though it is never stated.

    ingrained vs. implicit: An ingrained assumption sits even deeper than an implicit one — buried so far down that people follow it without ever noticing it exists.

      Word Choice

      neutral, academic

      Implicit is common in SAT reading passages about arguments — an implicit assumption, an implicit bias, implicit trust. Be careful: implicit does not mean 'obvious.' It means present but never directly stated; its direct opposite is explicit.
