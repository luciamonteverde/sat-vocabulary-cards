Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Expedite means to speed up the progress of something.

See the word in context

A note from the surgeon expedited the insurance approval that had been idling in someone's inbox for a month.

The journal expedites papers on disease outbreaks: reviewers get seventy-two hours instead of six weeks, the editor reads overnight, and typesetting starts before the last review lands. Nothing about the science is skipped. The same checks happen — they simply happen closer together, because a finding about a spreading illness ages by the day.

Expedite is the right verb because it means to speed up the progress of something: the paper still travels its whole road, just faster, with the friction cleared away. Rushing would cut corners; expediting cuts waiting. The word always takes an object — you expedite a process, a file, a shipment.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    accelerate: Accelerate means to make anything go faster — a car, a heartbeat, a trend. Expedite is narrower and lives in offices: it moves a file, order, or request through its official steps sooner by clearing the waiting away. You accelerate motion; you expedite paperwork.

    streamline: To streamline is to redesign a process so it runs leaner for everyone, permanently — fewer forms, fewer steps, for every future user. To expedite is to hurry one particular item through the process as it stands. Streamlining fixes the road; expediting speeds a single traveler down it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    delay: to hold something back to a later time

    stall: to bring progress quietly to a halt

    Where it sits · how much special handling it gets

    hasten → expedite → fast-track

    hasten vs. expedite: Hasten is a milder push than expedite — it hurries a thing along somewhat, without touching the path it travels, while to expedite is to clear the waiting out of the route itself.

    fast-track vs. expedite: Fast-track goes further than expedite: to expedite a file is to clear friction from its normal route, while to fast-track it is to lift it over every other file in the building.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      bureaucratic / businesslike

      Expedite lives in offices: files, permits, orders, reviews. It promises speed without carelessness, which is why officials love it — rush suggests corners cut, while hasten suits events and people rather than paperwork. Its mirror in this unit is prolong: prolong stretches a thing's duration out; expedite clears its path so the same journey takes less time.
