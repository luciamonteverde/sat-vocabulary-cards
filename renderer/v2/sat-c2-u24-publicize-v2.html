Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Publicize means to make something widely known to the public.

See the word in context

The trick isn't building the night market — it's publicizing it until three neighborhoods can't imagine Friday without it.

When the observatory's roof fund stalled at half, the director realized she had built the telescope nights but never publicized them: no sign on the highway, no school flyers, nothing in the paper. Six months of steady noise later — star parties in the calendar listings, a comet named on the local news — the fund closed out.

Publicize is the working verb because it means to make something widely known to the public — the telescope was always there; the town's awareness wasn't. Advertise usually means buying the attention; publicize covers every way of earning it, paid or not.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    promote: To promote something is to push it toward success — ticket sales, votes, a career — with public awareness only a means to that end. Publicize stops at the awareness itself: make the thing widely known and the job is done, whatever happens next.

    broadcast: Broadcast implies the airwaves — one sender scattering the message wide in a single transmission. Publicize is indifferent to the channel: posters, calendar listings, a reporter's favor all count, and the spreading can take months. Broadcast names a method; publicize names only the goal.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    hush up: work to keep it from getting out

    sit on: hold the news back and let it quietly age

    Where it sits · how insistently the news is spread

    announce → publicize → trumpet

    announce vs. publicize: Announce is a single act — one statement, one audience, done — where to publicize is to keep spreading the word until the public at large has heard.

    trumpet vs. publicize: Trumpet goes further than publicize — the spreading turns boastful, volume standing in for information.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / practical

    Publicize is the plain workhorse for making things widely known, and its plainness is the point: it doesn't say how. This unit's advertise implies paying for the attention and broadcast implies the airwaves; trumpet adds a boast. When you only mean 'get the word out,' publicize is exact.

She started six weeks out: index cards on the laundromat corkboard, a note in the parish bulletin, a reminder read aloud at two school assemblies. She asked the hardware store to tape a flyer by the register and got the county paper to run three lines in its weekend listings. None of it praised the event or promised anything; every piece just said what, where, and when. On the day, one hundred forty people came — most of them saying they had seen it somewhere twice.
