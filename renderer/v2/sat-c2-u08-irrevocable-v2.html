Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Irrevocable means incapable of being taken back or withdrawn.

See the word in context

The dean was plain about expulsion: the decision is irrevocable, reopened by no appeal, apology, or later good conduct.

Before the first lot, the auctioneer gives the room its one warning: 'A raised paddle is an irrevocable bid.' Wave at a friend, stretch, change your mind while your arm is still in the air — the bid stands, and the vase is yours. Nothing you say afterward reaches back to unmake the offer you made.

Irrevocable is the precise word because it targets the taking-back: a commitment its own maker cannot recall. Final says only that nothing came after; binding says you must honor it while it stands. Irrevocable welds the escape hatch shut — there is no 'on second thought' with any power to act.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    final: Final says the matter is closed and nothing comes after it — a final score, a final answer. It stays silent about whether anyone could reopen it. Irrevocable speaks to exactly that question: the maker of the act cannot take it back, however much regret arrives later.

    permanent: Permanent describes how long something lasts — a state meant to continue indefinitely, like a permanent stain or a permanent post. Irrevocable describes an act of will the maker cannot recall: a vow, a bid, a ruling. A permanent arrangement might still be revoked someday; an irrevocable one never can be.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    retractable: able to be formally taken back

    negotiable: open to revision by agreement

    Where it sits · how firmly the commitment holds

    offhand → binding → irrevocable

    offhand vs. irrevocable: An offhand promise is barely fastened — either side can shrug it away; irrevocable is fastened forever.

    binding vs. irrevocable: A binding deal must be honored, yet lawyers dissolve binding deals every week by mutual consent; irrevocable goes further — no consent, court, or regret can take it back.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / legal

      Irrevocable belongs to acts of will — gifts, vows, bids, rulings — and says the maker cannot take them back. Its unit-mate irreversible belongs to processes and damage: you revoke a promise but reverse a trend. If the thing that can't be undone is something someone did on purpose, irrevocable is usually the truer word.

Most of an application stays soft until spring: essays can be swapped, majors changed, financial forms amended. The early-decision pledge is different.
