Meaning

What It Means

Choreography means the art of designing and arranging movements for performers — dancers most of all, but also actors, marching bands, or anyone whose moves are planned step by step.

See the word in context

The heist film's most famous scene owes its tension to choreography: seven actors crossing a crowded casino floor on precisely timed paths.

For the play's climactic sword fight, the fight director spent two weeks on choreography, plotting every lunge, parry, and stumble so that the duel looked reckless while remaining perfectly safe.

Choreography is more specific than 'planning' or 'staging': it names the art of designing the movements themselves — who travels where, on which beat — whether the movers are dancers, actors, or a marching band.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    routine: A routine is the finished sequence a performer learns and repeats — a floor routine, a tap routine. Choreography is the art of composing that sequence in the first place: the routine is the product, the choreography is the design behind it.

    orchestration: Orchestration is the arranging of music for an ensemble's instruments — and, figuratively, the behind-the-scenes coordination of any complex effort. Choreography composes the movement itself: who travels where, on which beat, one step at a time.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    freestyle: performance that follows no set sequence

    spontaneity: acting in the moment, with nothing worked out in advance

    Where it sits · how finely the movement is planned

    staging → blocking → choreography

    staging vs. choreography: Staging settles only the broad picture — where the set pieces sit and which side performers enter from; it stops far short of choreography, which designs every step they take.

    blocking vs. choreography: Blocking maps actors' positions and crossings scene by scene, but it stops short of choreography, which fixes each movement to a count.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral

      Choreography is the standard word wherever designed movement is the point — dance, stage combat, even camera work. Use 'staging' or 'blocking' for rough positions; say choreography when every step, count, and pathway has been composed in advance.
