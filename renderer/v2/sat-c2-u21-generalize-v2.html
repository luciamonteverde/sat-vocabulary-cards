What It Means

Generalize means to draw a broad conclusion from limited examples.

See the word in context

One rude cabbie is thin grounds to generalize about a city of nine million.

Three months into her exchange year, Priya's classmates back home wanted verdicts: what is breakfast like there, are the trains really silent, is everyone so formal? She refused to generalize, typing the same reply — I have lived with one family, on one street, in one town. She could describe her street perfectly; the whole country was a claim her one street could not carry.

Generalize is the exact word for what Priya refused to do, because generalize means to draw a broad conclusion from limited examples — stretching one household over sixty million people. To infer is simply to reason from evidence, and can be modest; generalizing is the specific stretch from few cases to a whole class, and the smaller the sample, the thinner the stretch.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    infer: To infer is to reason to a conclusion from evidence, and the conclusion can be modest — one clue, one careful step. Generalize names one specific inference: stretching a small sample over a whole class. Every generalization is an inference; only the sample-to-class stretch earns the word generalize.

    universalize: To universalize is to extend a claim to absolutely everyone — no exceptions, no borders. Generalize stops earlier: it stretches limited examples over some larger class, a school or a city, and the claim can still be tested and revised. Universalize is generalize pushed past every limit.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    narrow down: shrink the claim to the cases actually seen

    itemize: treat each case on its own, one line at a time

    Where it sits · how far the claim outruns the evidence

    notice → generalize → stereotype

    notice vs. generalize: Notice claims far less than generalize — one case, seen and reported, with nothing extended anywhere; to generalize is to stretch that single case over a whole class.

    stereotype vs. generalize: Stereotype goes further than generalize — a generalization can be tested and revised; a stereotype hardens into a fixed label that no new case can shake.

      Word Choice

      neutral / analytic

      Generalize is the word for the move itself — sample to class — and it turns critical the moment the sample is thin: don't generalize is a warning about evidence, not manners. Infer is broader and blameless; extrapolate, its technical cousin, extends a trend beyond the data. When the stretch hardens about people, stereotype is the uglier word waiting one rung up this card's ladder.
