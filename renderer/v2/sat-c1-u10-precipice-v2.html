precipice

/PRESS-uh-pis/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Precipice means the face of a steep cliff — a sheer drop where the ground falls away almost straight down; used figuratively for the very edge of disaster.

See the word in context

The old lighthouse stood just a few meters from a precipice that plunged into the crashing surf below.

After three quarters of losses and a failed merger, the software company found itself on the precipice of bankruptcy.

Here precipice is figurative: the company is standing at the edge of a sheer drop, one step from falling into ruin. The phrase 'on the precipice of' almost always signals that something feared is about to happen.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    cliff: A cliff is the everyday word for a high, steep rock face — sea cliffs, cliff edges on a map. A precipice is a cliff at its most extreme and threatening: the ground falls away almost straight down, and the word itself carries danger. Every precipice is a cliff; only the sheerest ones earn the stronger word.

    crag: A crag is a steep, rugged mass of rock jutting upward — something climbers scale. It names the rock formation that rises. A precipice names the drop: the sheer face where the ground falls away beneath you. You climb up a crag; you fall from a precipice.

    brink: The brink is the extreme edge itself — the last inch of ground before the drop — and it is the usual choice in figurative phrases like 'on the brink of collapse.' A precipice is the whole sheer face below that edge. You stand on the brink; the precipice is what plunges away under it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    flatland: level ground with no steep drops

    basin: a low, bowl-shaped stretch of land

    Where it sits · how steeply the ground gives way

    slope → precipice → chasm

    slope vs. precipice: A slope slants gently enough that you could walk down it; a precipice drops away so steeply that one more step means a fall.

    chasm vs. precipice: A chasm is even more dramatic than a precipice — not just a sheer drop in front of you, but a gaping split in the earth with depth on every side.

    Word Choice

    Literal and figurative

    In literal use, a precipice is the sheer face or drop itself — never a gentle hill or a flat overlook. In figurative use, 'on the precipice of' nearly always introduces something feared — bankruptcy, war, collapse — not something hoped for.
