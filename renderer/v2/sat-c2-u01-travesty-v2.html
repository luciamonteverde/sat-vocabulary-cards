travesty

/TRAV-uh-stee/ - the stress falls on TRAV

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Travesty means a debased and grossly inferior imitation.

See the word in context

Critics called the outdoor 'tennis exhibition' a travesty of the sport—sponge balls, a sagging rope for a net, and scores invented by the announcer.

The historic village promised 'a faithful window on 1750.' Visitors found a travesty: vinyl 'log' cabins, a blacksmith selling phone cases, and costumed guides reciting events that never happened. One historian wrote that the past was not being shown so much as insulted.

Travesty is harsher than 'bad copy': it says the imitation is so debased that it wrongs the original. That is why the idiom 'a travesty of justice' stings—the proceeding wears the costume of a trial while betraying everything a trial is for.

    Accuracy

    “Does this word fit the meaning exactly, or does a neighbour fit better?”

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    mockery: A mockery is a version so botched or hollow that it ridicules the very thing it claims to be — a hearing where no one listens makes a mockery of justice. A travesty gets there through imitation: some performance actually exists, and it is so debased it insults its original. Both words need real conduct to point at.

    sham: A sham is a fake passed off as the real thing, and it requires no performance at all — the deception can live entirely in the claim. A travesty must actually be carried out: an imitation exists, and it degrades its original. Where nothing was ever done, no imitation exists to judge.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    masterpiece: a work of the highest quality—everything a travesty is not

    original: the real thing itself, which a travesty can only degrade

    Where it sits · how harshly the original gets treated

    copy → spoof → travesty

    copy vs. travesty: A copy just reproduces the original, well or badly, with no attitude toward it; a travesty degrades what it imitates.

    spoof vs. travesty: A spoof pokes affectionate fun and can even flatter its target; a travesty goes further down the same road, debasing the original until the imitation insults it.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / critical

      Travesty passes judgment: the imitation is so inferior or debased that it wrongs its original. Mimic, earlier in this unit, is the neutral craft of close imitation—a mimic can honor the original; a travesty never does. In set phrases like 'a travesty of justice,' the word measures an institution against its own ideal and finds an insult.
