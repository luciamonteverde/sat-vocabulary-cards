consign

/kuhn-SYNE/ - rhymes with sign

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Consign means to hand something over into another's care.

See the word in context

Rather than run the spring sale herself, the sculptor consigned her early bronzes to an auction house in the city.

When the touring company folded, its wardrobe crates were consigned to the drama school across the street — the school stores and mends the costumes, and the receivers may take them back if the company ever re-forms.

Consign is more precise than give or send because it marks a formal transfer of custody: the crates changed keepers, on the record, without necessarily changing owners at all.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    entrust: To entrust is to hand something into another's care while spotlighting your confidence in that person — trust is the whole point. Consign is cooler and transactional: custody changes hands formally, often with paperwork, and says nothing about affection. You entrust a child to a friend; you consign goods to a broker.

    transfer: Transfer is the flat, neutral word for moving something from one holder to another — money, titles, employees. Consign is narrower and more formal: a recorded handover of custody, the register of auction houses and archives. Every consignment is a transfer, but not every transfer is a consignment.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    reclaim: take something back into your own hands

    retain: keep something in your own keeping

    Where it sits · how formal and lasting the handover

    lend → deposit → consign

    lend vs. consign: Lend is the lightest handover, far milder than consign — you stay the owner, expect the thing back shortly, and sign nothing, while consigning puts the transfer of custody on paper.

    deposit vs. consign: Deposit is still milder than consign: you place a thing with an institution for safekeeping and can take it back at will, while consigning hands over real custody — recorded, receipted, and often for good.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / transactional

    Consign is formal and transactional — the register of auction houses, brokers, and archives. It stresses the handover of custody, often with paperwork, and says nothing about affection; entrust is the warmer choice when your confidence in the keeper is the point. Consign also has a darker idiom — to consign something to the flames, or to oblivion — meaning to hand it over to a fate.
