diaphanous

/dahy-AF-uh-nuhs/ - the stress falls on AF

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Diaphanous means so light and thin as to be almost transparent.

See the word in context

A diaphanous scarf of cloud slid across the moon without dimming it.

The jellyfish drifted past the aquarium glass like a floating lamp, its diaphanous bell so fine that the children could count the lights of the far tank straight through it.

Diaphanous goes beyond 'thin' or 'see-through': it names material so light and delicate that it barely interrupts light at all—mist and veils—where 'sheer' still suggests ordinary fabric.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    gauzy: Gauzy compares a material to gauze — loosely woven, light, slightly hazy to look through — and it stays a homely, fabric-shop word. Diaphanous is the literary cousin: material so fine it seems almost not to be there, with a shimmer of glamour gauzy never claims.

    translucent: Translucent states an optical fact: light passes through, but detail blurs — wax paper over a jar, a paper lampshade. The word says nothing about weight or delicacy. Diaphanous describes the material itself: fabric or mist so light and fine it barely interrupts the light crossing it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    thick: built up with substance no light passes through

    heavy: weighty material that hangs solid and blocks the view

    Where it sits · how close the fabric comes to nothing

    sheer → diaphanous → gossamer

    sheer vs. diaphanous: Sheer stops short of diaphanous — sheer curtains thin the light, while diaphanous fabric seems hardly to be there at all.

    gossamer vs. diaphanous: Gossamer goes further than diaphanous — gossamer is spider-silk lightness, cloth you could pull through a ring.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      literary

      Diaphanous is a literary word for fabrics, wings, mist — anything so fine it barely interrupts the light. In plain speech, 'sheer' or 'see-through' does the job; reach for diaphanous when you want the shimmer of the thing on the page.
