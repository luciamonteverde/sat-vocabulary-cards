rejuvenate

/rih-JOO-vuh-nayt/ - the stress falls on JOO

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Rejuvenate means to make someone or something fresh and young again.

See the word in context

One afternoon thunderstorm rejuvenated the wilted garden, and by dusk every stem stood upright again.

The graying retriever had spent two years asleep under the porch, rising only for meals. Then the puppy arrived and rejuvenated him inside a month: the old dog was soon patrolling fences, inventing games with a stolen glove, wading into the creek he had ignored since his muzzle went white. The vet found nothing changed on paper. Everything else about him was five years younger.

Rejuvenate is the exact word for what the puppy did, because it means to make fresh, young, or energetic again — the years themselves seem to run backward. Revive is for bringing something back from the edge of stopping; rejuvenation starts from merely tired and old, and hands back youth.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    revive: Revive means to bring someone or something back from the edge of stopping — the fainting runner, the stalled campaign — a moment's rescue rather than a transformation. Rejuvenate starts from merely tired or old, never from nearly gone, and it claims far more: youth and freshness handed back.

    renew: Renew means to make something like new again, and it attaches most naturally to things, supplies, and promises — a lease, a stock of firewood, a vow. Rejuvenate works on whatever is alive, or spoken of as if it were, and it specifically hands back youth.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    drain: empty the energy out of

    age: carry it the other way — toward old

    Where it sits · how big and lasting the boost is

    refresh → invigorate → rejuvenate

    refresh vs. rejuvenate: Refresh is a much lighter lift than rejuvenate — a splash of cold water, a ten-minute break, energy topped up for an hour — while rejuvenate hands back youth itself.

    invigorate vs. rejuvenate: Invigorate goes further than refresh — real vigor poured in, a whole afternoon's worth — but it stops short of rejuvenate, which claims the years themselves roll back.
