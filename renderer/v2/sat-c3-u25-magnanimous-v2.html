magnanimous

/mag-NAN-uh-muhs/ - the stress falls on NAN

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Magnanimous means great-souled: generous and forgiving toward a rival or wrongdoer, especially when you hold the upper hand and have every right to be petty.

See the word in context

In a magnanimous gesture nobody expected, the winning architect asked the jury to publish her rival's design alongside her own.

After the tennis final, the seventeen-year-old champion was magnanimous at the net and afterward: she told reporters the tournament's best shot had been her opponent's, and she asked the club to split her exhibition fee with the runner-up's academy.

Magnanimity is generosity exercised from the winner's seat. She holds every advantage and owes her rival nothing — the moment invites gloating — and she spends it lifting the person she just beat. That upward gift from a position of strength is what separates magnanimous from ordinary kindness.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    generous: Generous is the everyday word for giving freely — money, time, second helpings — to anyone at all. Magnanimous narrows and elevates it: generosity of spirit shown to a rival or a wrongdoer, from the position where pettiness would have been easy.

    forgiving: Forgiving lets an injury go, and anyone can do it from any position. Magnanimous adds the seat of power: the winner or the wronged party who could exact a price chooses instead to be openhanded about it — forgiveness with a gift on top.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    petty: small-minded about slights and scorekeeping

    vindictive: bent on paying every injury back

    Where it sits · how far generosity rises above the grudge

    gracious → magnanimous → saintly

    gracious vs. magnanimous: Gracious is milder than magnanimous: graciousness is warm good manners toward anyone, while magnanimity forgives a real injury from a real rival — it gives up something the giver was owed.

    saintly vs. magnanimous: Saintly goes further than magnanimous — the magnanimous winner forgives the rival standing in front of them, while the saintly forgive everyone, everywhere, and keep no ledger at all.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / warmly admiring

      Magnanimous is praise with a precondition: the speaker believes you had the power to punish and the right to gloat, and you chose neither. Call an everyday giver generous; save magnanimous for the winner's gesture toward the loser, or the wronged party's grace toward the offender. Its root-cousin unanimous (met in the root check above) shares only the spirit — animus — not the size.
