Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Flagrant means offensive, egregious; glaringly bad.

See the word in context

The judges ruled the bakery sticker still on the 'homemade' pie a flagrant breach of the bake-off's one rule.

Parking in the ambulance bay is not a gray area, and the tow report described a flagrant case: cones dragged aside, a note on the dash reading 'ten minutes,' and the bay blocked through an entire shift change.

Flagrant marks an offense so open and so bad that no one could miss it or excuse it. It is stronger than blatant, which only says a thing is unconcealed — a blatant hint is loud; a flagrant foul is loud and indefensible. The word judges the deed, where its unit neighbor brazen judges the offender's shameless nerve.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    glaring: Glaring means impossible to overlook — a glaring error announces itself from across the room. It measures visibility alone. Flagrant measures visibility and guilt at once: a flagrant violation is both unmissable and inexcusable, while a glaring one may be an innocent mistake.

    egregious: Egregious means conspicuously, shockingly bad — an egregious blunder, an egregious abuse of trust. It grades the badness alone. Flagrant grades badness and openness together: a flagrant offense happens in plain view, while an egregious one may shock only once it is uncovered.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    subtle: quiet and easy to miss

    veiled: deliberately half-hidden

    Where it sits · how much offense the openness carries

    overt → blatant → flagrant

    overt vs. flagrant: Overt is far milder than flagrant — it just means done in the open, with no judgment attached.

    blatant vs. flagrant: Blatant stops a step short of flagrant: both are impossible to miss, but flagrant adds the offense — a blatant hint is loud, a flagrant foul is loud and wrong.

      Word Choice

      formal

      Flagrant condemns an offense that is both glaring and indefensible — flagrant foul, flagrant violation, flagrant disregard. If the thing is merely obvious, blatant is enough. Within this unit, flagrant judges the deed, brazen judges the doer, and an affront is the insult a particular person receives.
