Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Contextualize means to place something within its surrounding circumstances so it can be understood.

See the word in context

The pediatrician contextualized the growth chart before the parents could worry: their daughter was small, but exactly as small as she had always been.

A quarter for a burger sounds like paradise until Mr. Osei contextualizes the price for his economics class: a 1955 factory wage was about two dollars an hour, gasoline cost pennies, and a house cost less than a modern pickup. Set inside its own decade, the quarter stops sounding cheap.

Contextualize is the precise verb because the number is not being corrected or translated — it is being surrounded with the circumstances that let it make sense. Explain would be vaguer; situate would only park it in 1955; contextualize builds the whole frame.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    situate: Situate places a thing in its time and place — a treaty in 1648, a speech on a courthouse step — and stops there. Contextualize keeps going: it builds the surrounding circumstances that make the thing intelligible, the whole picture rather than just the pin on the map.

    frame: Frame sets the border around how something is seen, and the border can carry a slant — the same layoff framed as trimming fat or as gutting a team. Contextualize is more neutral and more complete: it supplies the surrounding circumstances rather than an angle on them.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    detach: to cut a thing loose from its surroundings

    uproot: to tear something out of the very setting that explains it

    Where it sits · how much background gets filled in

    caption → contextualize → recreate

    caption vs. contextualize: A caption is far weaker than contextualize — a date and a name beneath a photo, where contextualizing builds the whole surrounding picture.

    recreate vs. contextualize: Recreate goes further than contextualize: instead of explaining a thing's surroundings, you rebuild them entirely, room by room or scene by scene.

      Word Choice

      academic

      Contextualize belongs to classrooms, museums, and journalism — building the surroundings that make a fact intelligible. Unlike construe, its neighbor in this unit, which works out what the words themselves mean, contextualize supplies what sits around them.

      Putting it all together

      Read the passage, then answer Part A. Part B gives you a new passage.

      The headline said shark encounters off the county's beaches had doubled in a single summer. Town forums lit up; a petition demanded nets. The number was true, and on its own it was terrifying. Then the marine institute published a short note that did nothing but contextualize the figure. Encounters had gone from two to four -- in a season when beach visits had tripled after the new ferry opened, and when 'encounter' included any sighting within a hundred yards. Warmer currents had also shifted the sardine run, which sharks follow, closer to shore for a few weeks. Nothing in the note said the number was wrong. It said the number had been alone -- and that, surrounded by its circumstances, it stopped meaning what the headline implied.

        When the anthology reprints a soap advertisement from the eighteen-nineties, the editors refuse to let it stand alone on the page. Dates and cities are the easy part; a full page of notes goes on to help readers ____ the ad — what a nickel bought, which fevers buyers dreaded, how rare indoor plumbing still was — until its old promises make sense. The notes take no side and push no reading, and they run far past a label.
