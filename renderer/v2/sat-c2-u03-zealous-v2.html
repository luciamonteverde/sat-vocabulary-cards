zealous

/ZEL-uhs/ - rhymes with jealous

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Zealous means filled with intense eagerness or enthusiasm for a cause.

See the word in context

The new hall monitor proved so zealous about the no-running rule that teachers began taking the long corridor just to avoid her clipboard.

No campaign volunteer out-knocked the zealous Silas: he wore through two pairs of shoes, memorized the walking lists, recruited his barber and both grandmothers, and treated every porch conversation as if the whole election hung on it. Even the candidate begged him to take one Sunday off.

Zealous names enthusiasm with a cause attached - eagerness burning hot enough to fill weekends and wear out shoes. It usually admires, though it stands one rung short of fanatical, where devotion stops listening to reason.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    avid: Avid describes sheer appetite for an activity, enjoyed for its own sake — the avid reader devours novels by the stack. Zealous adds a cause and a crusading edge: the enthusiasm serves a mission and pulls others in. An avid gardener loves her roses; a zealous one lobbies the city for community plots.

    passionate: Passionate says strong feeling drives you, whatever the object — a passionate cook, a passionate defense of a friend. Zealous narrows the feeling to a cause and turns it outward into tireless public action. Passion can burn quietly at home; a zealous believer is out signing people up.

    keen: Keen marks eager interest at its mildest — keen on chess, a keen student of maps. Zealous is that same eagerness at full boil and attached to a cause, spilling into evenings and weekends. A keen supporter shows up to the rally; a zealous one organized it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    lukewarm: barely interested at all

    halfhearted: giving only partial effort

    Where it sits · how consuming the excitement becomes

    eager → zealous → fanatical

    eager vs. zealous: Eager wants to start; zealous has already started, recruited three friends, and printed the handouts.

    fanatical vs. zealous: Fanatical pushes past zealous and loses the compliment: the zealous fan paints her face, while the fanatical one keys the rival team's bus.

Chen was zealous in a way the city had not seen: she spoke at six churches a Sunday, signed up shopkeepers by the hundred, and turned petition drives into street festivals. Reporters noted that she did not especially enjoy gardening; the trees were never the point. The cause was everything, and by 1920 her volunteers had planted eleven thousand elms. Asked to rest, she famously answered that she would rest when the last block had its shade.
