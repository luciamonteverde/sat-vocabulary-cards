superfluous

/soo-PER-floo-uhs/ - the stress falls on PER

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Superfluous means exceeding what is necessary.

See the word in context

With both concerts already sold out, the third week of radio ads was superfluous.

The house already had the camera, the motion lights, the deadbolt app, and the dog. At some point the 'Beware of Dog' sign became superfluous: the yard announced itself from the sidewalk, and the sign added nothing the yard didn't already declare.

Superfluous doesn't accuse the sign of being wrong — only of exceeding the need. Take it down and nothing changes; that removability is the test. The root says it plainly: super (over) + flu (flow) — the supply has overflowed the need, and the extra is running over the rim.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unnecessary: Unnecessary is the plain, everyday verdict: not needed at all, now or later, and better left out. Superfluous is more precise — the supply was right in kind but overflowed the need, like a fourth proof after three had settled the theorem. Superfluous points at the overflow; unnecessary just says no.

    excess: Excess is the amount past the required amount — the noun for the overflow itself: the third egg when the recipe asked for two. Superfluous is the judgment passed on that overflow, the adjective that says the extra serves no purpose. You measure an excess; you dismiss the superfluous.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    indispensable: cannot be done without

    needed: required, plainly and simply

    Where it sits · how little good the extra does

    spare → superfluous → burdensome

    spare vs. superfluous: A spare key exceeds today's need on purpose and earns its keep next month; superfluous extras exceed any need at all.

    burdensome vs. superfluous: Burdensome sits past superfluous: the extra has stopped being harmless and started costing you — weight in the pack, noise in the text.

      Word Choice

      formal

      Superfluous measures against need: on-topic, in kind, but past the required amount — the fourth proof, the second signature line. Extraneous, its unit-mate, measures against the subject: imported from outside it altogether. And when the excess is specifically a repeat, this unit gives you a third tool — redundancy. Overflow, outsider, echo: three different flaws.

      Before the six-day traverse, the guide emptied every pack onto the lodge floor and weighed each item against the route ahead. Two ropes stayed: the second was insurance the glacier could genuinely demand. The third rope she set aside as superfluous — sound equipment, the right kind exactly, but more of it than any pitch on the mountain could use. A camp stove with a cracked valve she binned for a different reason: broken gear is not extra, it is useless. What the team carried at dawn was lean — everything needed, nothing merely additional, every kilogram earning its place.
