vacuous

/VAK-yoo-us/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Vacuous means empty of ideas or intelligence; lacking substance, especially in speech or expression.

See the word in context

The talk-show guest smiled for the cameras but gave such vacuous answers that the host finally gave up and cut to a commercial break.

Running for student council president, Bryce plastered the hallways with posters and ended every speech with the slogan 'Dream big, win bigger.' When a classmate asked what he would actually change about the lunch schedule, his vacuous reply contained plenty of enthusiasm and not a single concrete idea.

Bryce's answer sounds energetic but is completely empty of real thought or substance, which is exactly what makes it vacuous.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    inane: Inane means silly to the point of making no sense: an inane joke, inane chatter. It points at foolishness you can hear. Vacuous points at absence instead: a vacuous answer or stare is empty of thought or substance, and it can sound perfectly smooth while containing nothing.

    mindless: Mindless means done or said without engaging thought, the way people describe mindless scrolling or a mindless reply; the process is switched off. Vacuous judges the content: whatever produced it, a vacuous remark simply has nothing inside, no idea and no substance, however confidently it is delivered.

    empty-headed: Empty-headed is a blunt insult aimed straight at a person, saying they show no intelligence or seriousness. Vacuous usually aims at what a person produces, a vacuous smile or a vacuous answer, and it is the sharper, more formal criticism of emptiness of thought.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    insightful: showing deep understanding

    thoughtful: full of careful, serious thinking

    Where it sits · how little thought lies behind it

    shallow → vacuous → brainless

    shallow vs. vacuous: A shallow comment simply lacks depth, while a vacuous one is empty of thought altogether.

    brainless vs. vacuous: A vacuous remark shows an absence of ideas, but calling something brainless is an outright insult to the speaker's intelligence.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal, often critical

    Vacuous most often describes expressions, remarks, and stares, as in 'a vacuous smile' or 'a vacuous answer.' Do not confuse it with vacant: a room can be vacant, but calling a person's expression vacuous is a pointed criticism of their lack of thought.
