catalyze

/KAT-uh-lyze/ - the stress falls on KAT

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Catalyze means to set a process in motion or speed it up.

See the word in context

The new rail stop catalyzed a wave of construction, and within three years the empty lots around it had become a neighborhood.

The library's seed exchange began with one shoebox of envelopes, but it catalyzed something larger: a garden club, then a farmers' stall, then a course at the community center, each started by people who first met over that box.

Catalyze is borrowed from chemistry, where a catalyst speeds a reaction without being used up. That is the nuance to keep: whatever catalyzes a change is a spark and an accelerator, not the raw material of the change itself.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    spark: To spark something is to supply the first flash that starts it, and the word's work ends there. To catalyze goes further: whatever catalyzes a change both starts it and speeds it, staying behind the process like an engine rather than a single match.

    trigger: To trigger something is to set it off when it was already primed and waiting, like a tripwire — the release happens at once. To catalyze a change is different: the change is a running process, and the catalyst makes it run faster than it would alone.

    kindle: To kindle something is to start it small and feed it slowly, the way a first flame is coaxed along with twigs. To catalyze is brisker — a catalyzed process visibly accelerates, gaining speed it could never have found on its own.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    stifle: to smother a process before it can develop

    stall: to bring progress to a standstill without ending it

    Where it sits · how powerfully it drives things forward

    nudge → catalyze → unleash

    nudge vs. catalyze: A nudge is gentler than catalyzing — it inches something forward once, while catalyzing sets a whole process running faster than it could run on its own.

    unleash vs. catalyze: Unleash is stronger than catalyze — a catalyzed process still runs in an orderly way, but an unleashed one bursts out past anyone's control.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / journalistic

    Catalyze is the writer's word for a spark with an engine behind it — a grant, an event, or a person that gets change moving faster. Prefer trigger for something primed to go off anyway, and spark for the bare first flash; catalyze earns its place when the process visibly accelerates.
