Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

A grievance is a wrong that someone feels justifies complaint — a real or perceived injustice, and, in formal settings, the complaint itself, stated and pursued through channels.

See the word in context

The tenants kept a running list of every grievance — the dead elevator, the vanishing deposits — and read it aloud, item by item, at the September board meeting.

When the airline quietly cut the crews' contractual rest hours, the union filed a formal grievance within the week, and the arbitration that followed restored every hour it had claimed.

The word is doing double duty here. A grievance is first the felt wrong itself — the cut rest hours — and then, in unions, schools, and workplaces, the formal complaint that carries it: a document you can file, hear, and settle. Either way it claims more than displeasure; a grievance says an injustice was done and something is owed.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    complaint: A complaint can be about anything — cold soup, a slow app — and claims nothing beyond displeasure. A grievance asserts an injustice: something owed, denied, or wrongfully done, which is why institutions build procedures for grievances and not for gripes.

    grudge: A grudge lives inside the person holding it — private, nursed, often petty, and rarely spoken. A grievance points at the wrong itself and survives daylight: you air a grievance, file it, argue it; a grudge you only harbor.

    injustice: Injustice names the wrong from the outside, as a fact about the world that anyone might observe. A grievance is the wrong as carried by the person wronged — felt, remembered, and raised in protest by whoever holds the claim.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    satisfaction: the state of having nothing left to complain of — a wrong made right

    boon: a benefit or blessing, gladly received

    Where it sits · how heavy the claimed wrong weighs

    gripe → grievance → outrage

    gripe vs. grievance: A gripe is far lighter than a grievance: you gripe about the parking or the coffee and forget it by noon, while a grievance claims a wrong serious enough to raise, argue, and press.

    outrage vs. grievance: An outrage goes beyond a grievance: a grievance is an injustice worth filing and hearing out, while an outrage is a wrong so flagrant it scalds everyone who so much as hears of it.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / procedural

      Grievance is the word institutions reach for when a complaint claims real injustice: employees file grievances, contracts set out grievance procedures, and revolutions begin with lists of them. Mind the family split — grief, from the same heavy root, took the sorrow and left grievance the protest; you grieve a loss but air a grievance. And where umbrage (also in this unit) is the bristling feeling at a slight, a grievance is the wrong itself, stated plainly enough to be examined, argued, and set right.

      The night nurses' grievance did not begin as paperwork. It began with a whiteboard where someone tallied, shift by shift, how many beds each nurse was covering alone. When the number hit twelve, the union filed. The document was specific: the hospital's own staffing plan promised a ceiling of eight. This was the difference the arbitrator kept returning to. Complaining about hard work is just complaining; a broken written promise is a claim. The hearing took nine months. The nurses kept the whiteboard going the entire time, photographing it nightly. When the ruling came, the tally stopped at eight — the number the plan had promised all along.
