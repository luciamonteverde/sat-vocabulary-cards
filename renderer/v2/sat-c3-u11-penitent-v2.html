Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Penitent means remorseful about a wrong and moved to confess or make amends.

See the word in context

The penitent shoplifter returned to the store every Saturday for a year, working off what she had taken shelf by shelf.

In the old monastery, penitent monks copied out by hand the books they had damaged; the abbot liked to say that sorrow which costs nothing mends nothing. One friar spent a whole winter regilding a single scorched page.

Penitent is remorse with a direction: sorrow for a wrong that turns toward confession and amends. It is the word behind penance and penitentiary, and it often keeps that religious depth — regret that intends to repair.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    regretful: Regretful is the mildest of the sorry words: you wish it had gone otherwise, and there it ends — no confession required, sometimes no fault at all. Penitent is regret with weight and direction: sorrow over a real wrong that moves toward confession and repair.

    repentant: Repentant stresses the turn: the wrongdoer renounces what they did and changes course, the word of sermons and courtrooms. Penitent walks beside it but leans toward the debt still owed — confession made, amends paid, sorrow turned into long repair rather than a single turning point.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    unrepentant: will not admit the wrong, let alone atone for it

    hardened: past feeling sorry about anything

    Where it sits · how far the regret is acted on

    embarrassed → remorseful → penitent

    embarrassed vs. penitent: Embarrassed is far milder than penitent — an awkward flush at being caught, with no real sorrow in it.

    remorseful vs. penitent: Remorseful is weaker than penitent — remorse feels the sorrow, while a penitent person carries it forward into confession and amends.

    Word Choice

    formal, often religious

    Penitent is remorse with a long tail: confession, amends, a changed life — the word behind penance and penitentiary. Its unit-mate contrite is the immediate, visible sorrow that begs forgiveness on the spot. A contrite email goes out within the hour; a penitent donor spends a decade rebuilding what he burned.
