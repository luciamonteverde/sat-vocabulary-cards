Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Conduit means a channel through which something passes — a pipe for water or cable, or, figuratively, a person or route through which information, money, or influence flows.

See the word in context

The night janitor became the strike's conduit: notes from the day shift crossed the building in his cart and reached the negotiators by morning.

During the merger talks the two teams refused to meet, so every offer moved through a retired judge they both trusted: proposals went in from one suite, crossed the hallway in her folder, and came out unchanged in the other. She added nothing and kept nothing — a pure conduit.

That is the essence of a conduit: it neither originates what it carries nor keeps or alters it. Water mains, fiber-optic lines, discreet go-betweens, and shell companies can all be conduits — the word cares only that something flows in one end and out the other.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    channel: A channel is any route along which something flows, and the word floats easily into the abstract — 'channels of communication.' A conduit is the concrete carrier itself, pipe or person, and it stresses pure passage: what enters, leaves, unchanged.

    intermediary: An intermediary is a person standing between parties who usually acts — negotiating, filtering, advising. A conduit just transmits: calling someone a conduit insists that things move through them untouched, and often denies they added anything at all.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    barrier: a block that stops the flow instead of carrying it

    endpoint: the place where a thing stops moving and stays

    Where it sits · how freely what enters passes through

    checkpoint → conduit → thoroughfare

    checkpoint vs. conduit: A checkpoint passes things through more grudgingly than a conduit: everything is stopped, inspected, and sometimes turned back, while a conduit carries its cargo straight through without interference.

    thoroughfare vs. conduit: A thoroughfare is a stronger, wider-open passage than a conduit: a conduit is one dedicated channel moving one kind of cargo, while a thoroughfare is a broad public route with traffic streaming through in every direction.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / technical, with an investigator's edge in news prose

      In a wiring diagram, conduit is pure hardware. In news prose it carries an edge: call a foundation a conduit for foreign money and you have accused it of being a pass-through. The nearby words divide the labor — a channel is any route, an intermediary acts and negotiates, a courier physically carries. A conduit only transmits, which is exactly why investigators love the word.

      At nine, Amaya became the family's conduit. Landlord notices, school forms, the pharmacist's warnings — everything entered in English and left in Tagalog, and her parents insisted it pass through her with nothing added and nothing left out. She learned early that her job was passage, not judgment: when the rent letter was harsh, the harshness was delivered too. Years later she wrote that she had been less a member of those conversations than the pipe they flowed through. Only at eighteen, signing her own lease, did she finally speak in a conversation instead of carrying one.
