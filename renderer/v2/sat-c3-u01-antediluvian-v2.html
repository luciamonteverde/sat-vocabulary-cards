Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Antediluvian means extremely old, belonging to a very distant age.

See the word in context

Uncle Ray still mows with an antediluvian push mower that squeals like a kettle and predates the transistor.

When Priya produced her flip phone at the reunion, her nephews gathered around as if she had unearthed a fossil; one asked, quite sincerely, whether it had belonged to her grandmother. "It's not old," she said, thumbing out a message with three slow, defiant clicks, "it's antediluvian."

Antediluvian literally means "from before the Flood" — the one in the story of Noah — so calling the phone antediluvian is deliberate exaggeration: not just old, but so old it seems to predate the modern world entirely. That wink of hyperbole is what separates it from plain words like old-fashioned.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    ancient: Ancient means genuinely from the distant past — Rome, Egypt, millennia gone — and it is said in earnest. Antediluvian makes the same reach as a joke, teasing that a flip phone or a filing system seems older than the modern world. Ancient dates a thing; antediluvian mocks it.

    immemorial: Immemorial means older than memory or record can reach — customs from time immemorial have no known starting point, and the word is solemn and literal. Antediluvian plays the same vastness for a smile, mocking a thing as so outdated it seems to predate the modern world.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    brand-new: fresh from the box, no history at all

    cutting-edge: at the newest edge of its field

    Where it sits · how far back in time it seems

    old-fashioned → age-old → antediluvian

    old-fashioned vs. antediluvian: Old-fashioned is far milder than antediluvian: handwritten thank-you notes are only a generation behind today's taste, while calling a habit antediluvian jokes that it belongs to the age before the Flood.

    age-old vs. antediluvian: Age-old stops short of antediluvian: an age-old custom has real centuries behind it, while antediluvian teases that a thing seems older than the modern world itself.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    humorous / literary

    Antediluvian is exaggeration worn openly — reach for it when you want the reader to smile at how old a thing seems, not to date it. Inside this unit, keep the lanes: antiquated is the sober complaint that something no longer serves; antediluvian mocks sheer age; and an anachronism is not old at all, merely stranded in the wrong period.

    When Priya joined the payroll office, her supervisor apologized for the computer system with a phrase she took for a joke. "It's antediluvian," he said gravely. The screen was black with green letters, and the printer sounded like a woodpecker. The system was installed in 1994. That is old for software, but the word was not a measurement; it was a tease. Calling the terminal antediluvian mocked it as something from before the modern world entirely -- older, the joke ran, than the office, the building, the city. Priya learned the register quickly. When the elevator stalled that winter, she patted its brass panel and pronounced it fit for the ark.
