compile

/kum-PYLE/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Compile means to gather material from many separate sources and assemble it into one organized whole.

See the word in context

Every January the harbor master compiles the year's tide readings, storm logs, and shipping counts into a single bound report.

After her grandfather died, Nadia spent a summer compiling the family's scattered history — census records from three countries, photographs from six shoeboxes, and voice memos from every living cousin — into one organized archive that now sits on a single shelf.

Dozens of separate sources, gathered and then assembled into one organized whole: that patient double work of collecting and arranging is exactly what it means to compile.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    assemble: Assemble means to fit parts together into a working whole — you can assemble furniture, a cast, or a committee. Compile is assembling done with information and documents: what gets fitted together is material drawn from many sources.

    collate: Collate means to gather things and arrange them in proper order, page by page or entry by entry. Collating is about sequence; compiling is the larger job of building a whole out of many sources, of which putting them in order is one step.

    amass: Amass means to heap up a great quantity over time — a collector amasses stamps, a miser money. Amassing stops at accumulation; compiling goes on to organize what was gathered into something usable.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    scatter: to fling apart what had been brought together

    disperse: to spread in every direction until nothing remains collected

    Where it sits · how organized the gathering becomes

    gather → compile → codify

    gather vs. compile: Gather is looser than compile: gathering just brings things into one place, while compiling goes on to organize what was gathered into a single usable whole.

    codify vs. compile: Codify goes further than compile: a compiled collection is organized, but a codified one has hardened into a formal, numbered system of rules.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral — diligent, workmanlike

      Compile emphasizes many sources becoming one thing: you compile statistics, records, evidence, playlists. If the material comes from a single source, you are copying or editing, not compiling; if you gather without organizing, you are merely collecting. Programmers use the word too — a compiler assembles human-written code into a machine-ready program — the same many-into-one idea wearing a lab coat.
