Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Petulance means childish rudeness, irritability.

See the word in context

Grandpa met the ruled-out move with open petulance, flicking the dice off the table and demanding the rulebook be read aloud.

The under-12 coach kept a special bench for petulance: any player who kicked a bottle or flung a shin guard after a substitution sat there, in full view, until the pout wore off.

Petulance names the display, not the feeling: the huff, the slammed door, the flounce that says a grown person has briefly gone twelve again. Anger can be justified; petulance, by definition, is out of all proportion to its cause. Its unit cousin puerile judges an act as immature — petulance is the tantrum's small, sulky sibling.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    crankiness: Crankiness is a general bad temper — grumbling, short answers, complaints about anything at hand. Petulance is narrower and more theatrical: a childish display staged over one small disappointment. A cranky person is unpleasant all afternoon for no particular reason; a petulant one performs a sulk about the thing just lost.

    peevishness: Peevishness is fretful irritability over minor annoyances — a whiny readiness to complain that never quite becomes a scene. Petulance goes public: the same childish temper turned into a visible display, sighs and slammed doors included. Peevishness is a mood someone is in; petulance is a show someone puts on.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    patience: taking small setbacks without fuss

    graciousness: meeting disappointment with courtesy

    Where it sits · how showy the bad temper gets

    sulkiness → petulance → tantrum

    sulkiness vs. petulance: Sulkiness is quieter than petulance — the pout without the performance.

    tantrum vs. petulance: A tantrum goes further than petulance: petulance sighs and slams one door, while a tantrum is the full floor-pounding storm.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / wry

    Petulance is the noun for grown-up sulking — snappish, disproportionate, faintly ridiculous. Use it for the display itself; use its unit neighbor puerile to judge behavior as immature, and save tantrum for the full eruption. The adjective is petulant.
