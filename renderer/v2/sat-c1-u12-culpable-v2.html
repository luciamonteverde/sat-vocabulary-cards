culpable

/KUHL-puh-buhl/

Meaning

What It Means

Culpable means deserving blame for a wrongdoing, whether in a courtroom or in everyday life.

See the word in context

The inquiry found the factory culpable for the equipment failure because required safety inspections had been skipped for months.

After the bikes were stolen from the garage, Priya admitted she was culpable, since she had left the door unlocked overnight.

No court is involved here — this is an everyday, moral use of the word. Priya's carelessness helped the wrong happen, so she deserves a share of the blame, which is exactly what culpable describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    blameworthy: Blameworthy says, plainly, that someone deserves blame. Culpable says the same thing in a legal-moral register with a grammar of its own — a person is culpable for a specific failure, and courts speak of culpable negligence. In everyday judgment the two overlap; culpable is the formal one.

    at fault: To be at fault is to be the one who caused the mishap — insurers assign fault to the driver whose error produced the crash. Culpable reaches past causation to desert: it says the person deserves blame for the wrong, not merely that their action set events in motion.

    liable: Liable is the civil law's word: a person or company that is liable must answer for harm, usually by paying for it, even when no one thinks them wicked. Culpable is about deserving blame itself — a moral verdict that can attach with no lawsuit anywhere in sight.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    blameless: deserving no blame at all

    innocent: not guilty of the wrongdoing

    Where it sits · how firmly blame attaches

    careless → culpable → guilty

    careless vs. culpable: Careless is weaker than culpable — it points to sloppiness without saying anyone deserves blame or punishment for a wrong.

    guilty vs. culpable: Guilty is stronger than culpable — it declares that the wrongdoing has been formally established, as when a court convicts someone.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    Formal

    Culpable works in legal settings ('culpable negligence') and in ordinary moral ones — a company, a committee, or a forgetful roommate can all be culpable. It usually takes 'for' ('culpable for the failure'). Don't confuse it with the sound-alike capable, which means able to do something and carries no blame at all.

    When the customer records appeared for sale online, the bank's first statements blamed sophisticated attackers no firm could have stopped. The regulator's inquiry told a different story. Its final report found the bank culpable for the breach: the flaw had been reported by the bank's own auditors twice, and both warnings were filed away unread by managers who deserved, the report said, a full share of blame. The report was careful about what it was not saying. Whether customers could recover damages was left to the civil courts, and the attackers, not the managers, had done the actual stealing. The judgment was about desert: the bank had failed in a duty it understood, and the failure merited blame.
