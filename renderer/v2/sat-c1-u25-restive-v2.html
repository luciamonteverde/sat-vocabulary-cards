restive

/RES-tiv/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Restive means restless and impatient under restraint, straining against control or delay while still being held in check.

See the word in context

The colts grew restive in the starting gate as the third false start was announced.

The documentary crew had promised the villagers a ten-minute ceremony before filming began. An hour later, cables still snaked across the square and the director kept calling for one more lighting check. Children broke from their parents to chase pigeons; two elders stood and stretched pointedly; a low murmur ran through the rows of folding chairs. The longer the delay stretched, the more restive the crowd became — not angry yet, but visibly done with waiting.

The murmuring, pointed stretching, and children drifting off show a crowd growing restless and resistant under an imposed delay — the exact condition restive names.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fidgety: Fidgety means unable to stay still — tapping, shifting, checking the clock — restless energy with no particular target. Restive gives that energy a grievance: restless impatience straining against the specific restraint or delay causing it. A fidgety child squirms anywhere; a restive one squirms against the seatbelt.

    uneasy: Uneasy means unsettled and vaguely anxious, a discomfort that may have no clear cause at all. Restive is sharper and points outward: restless impatience straining against a known restraint or delay. An uneasy audience worries quietly; a restive one starts pushing at whatever holds it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    patient: able to wait calmly

    settled: calm and at rest

    Where it sits · how close to open revolt it comes

    impatient → restive → mutinous

    impatient vs. restive: Impatient is weaker than restive: an impatient person merely wants the wait to end, while a restive one is visibly beginning to strain against whatever is holding things up.

    mutinous vs. restive: Mutinous is stronger than restive — a mutinous crowd is on the edge of open revolt, while a restive one is still contained, just barely and unhappily.

      Word Choice

      formal; journalistic (crowds, legislatures, animals)

      Restive describes impatience under restraint or delay — a held crowd, a waiting horse, a stalled legislature — which distinguishes it from this unit's combative: a restive group strains against being made to wait, while a combative one is actively spoiling for a fight or argument.
