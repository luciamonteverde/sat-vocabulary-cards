Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Lithe means flexible and graceful in movement — a body that bends and flows with ease.

See the word in context

Otters are lithe enough to turn a full circle inside their own length.

In the tango final, the oldest couple on the floor won on lithe economy: no lunges, no strain, just long supple turns that seemed to have no corners at all.

Lithe says more than 'flexible' — it bundles bend and grace into one word. A gymnast's stretched hamstring is flexibility; movement that flows soft and effortless through every joint is lithe, and the word always compliments.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    nimble: Nimble is about speed and lightness — quick feet, quick fingers, quick wits. Lithe is about how the body bends: flexibility carried with grace, the flowing ease of a dancer or a cat. A nimble mover darts; a lithe mover flows.

    agile: Agile is athletic quickness — changing direction fast, in control, like a point guard. Lithe cares less about speed than about grace: a body that bends and flows softly through a motion. An agile mover is quick and sure; a lithe one is supple and beautiful to watch.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    stiff: barely able to bend

    ungainly: awkward and graceless in motion

    Where it sits · how elegantly the body bends

    limber → supple → lithe

    limber vs. lithe: Limber means loose enough to move and stretch — it stops well short of lithe.

    supple vs. lithe: Supple goes further than limber — it bends softly, without resistance — but stops short of lithe, which adds grace to the bending.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      admiring

      Lithe is for living bodies in motion — dancers, cats, willows — and it always admires. Choose supple when you mean pure soft bendability, nimble when you mean quickness, and lithe when flexibility moves with grace. It rarely fits objects or ideas: a lithe argument sounds as odd as a graceful doorknob.
