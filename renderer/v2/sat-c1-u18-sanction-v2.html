sanction

/SANGK-shun/

Meaning

What It Means

Sanction has two nearly opposite meanings: (1) to officially approve or permit something, and (2) a penalty imposed to enforce a rule.

See the word in context

Sanction has two nearly opposite meanings: it can mean to officially approve something, or a penalty imposed to enforce a rule. In the approval sense, the school board voted to sanction the new tutoring program, giving it official permission to begin.

When the nation of Corvia kept fishing in protected waters despite repeated warnings, its neighbors acted together. They imposed economic sanctions, banning Corvia's seafood exports from their markets until the treaty was honored. Corvia's fishing industry lost millions within months. Facing the mounting penalty, its government finally returned to the negotiating table.

Here sanction is used in its punishment sense: the export ban is a penalty imposed to force Corvia to follow the treaty.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    authorize: Authorize means to officially approve or give permission for something — a board authorizes spending before a dime moves. Sanction, in its approval sense, is the same official blessing, but sanction is double-edged: the very same word can mean a penalty imposed to enforce a rule. Authorize only ever opens the door; sanction may bless you or punish you.

    penalty: A penalty is a punitive measure imposed for breaking a rule, anywhere from a parking fine to lost yardage. Sanction, in its punishment sense, is the institutional version: a formal penalty imposed under a rule or treaty, most often by governments, to force compliance. And unlike penalty, sanction can flip meanings and signify official approval.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    prohibit: to forbid officially (opposite of sense 1, approval)

    reward: a benefit given for good behavior (opposite of sense 2, punishment)

    Where it sits · how severe and sweeping the penalty is

    warning → sanction → blockade

    warning vs. sanction: A warning merely cautions a rule-breaker, while a sanction, in its punishment sense, imposes a real penalty with real costs.

    blockade vs. sanction: A sanction targets specific trade or conduct, while a blockade cuts off nearly all of a country's commerce at once.

      Word Choice

      formal, legal and diplomatic register

      Sanction carries two nearly opposite meanings, so context decides everything. As a verb it usually means to officially approve, as in the league sanctioned the tournament. As a noun, especially in the plural, it usually means a penalty, as in economic sanctions imposed on a country that breaks international rules.

      The international chess federation both blesses and punishes, often in the same afternoon. In the morning it voted to sanction the Riga open — official approval that puts the tournament on the world calendar and makes its results count for ratings. In the afternoon it turned to discipline: a grandmaster caught consulting a hidden phone mid-game. The measure it imposed was harsher than any caution — a two-year ban from rated play and forfeiture of his prize money, imposed under the federation's anti-cheating rules. Delegates joked that a newcomer reading the minutes would need a glossary: the same body had spent one day approving a tournament and penalizing a cheater, wielding one word both ways.
