adulation

/aj-uh-LAY-shun/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Adulation means excessive, gushing, often fawning praise or admiration.

See the word in context

The young gymnast found the adulation after her first gold medal more exhausting than the routine itself.

By the third city of her book tour, Naomi's signings had turned into something else entirely. Readers pressed gifts into her hands, a man read aloud a poem he had written about her, and one woman burst into tears just reaching the front of the line. Naomi smiled through the adulation, but that night she told her editor she missed being asked hard questions about the book.

The gifts, the poem, and the tears show praise swollen far past ordinary appreciation — that flood of gushing, fawning admiration is adulation.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    hero-worship: Hero-worship is a lasting attitude of idolizing one person as a larger-than-life hero -- someone the admirer looks up to and tries to imitate over time. Adulation is the outpouring itself: gushing praise and admiration showered on someone, often on a single occasion. Hero-worship lives in the admirer's head; adulation spills out in public.

    fawning: Fawning is over-eager, servile praise aimed at pleasing someone -- the flatterer bows and gushes to stay in a powerful person's good graces. Adulation, by contrast, is genuinely felt: the admirers gain nothing and mean every word; the praise is simply swollen far past reason. Fawning performs; adulation overflows.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    scorn: open contempt

    indifference: a complete lack of interest

    Where it sits · how far admiration exceeds reason

    praise → adulation → worship

    praise vs. adulation: Praise is measured approval; adulation is praise turned up past reason, gushing and uncritical.

    worship vs. adulation: Worship goes one step beyond adulation — the admirer no longer just gushes but treats the person as something sacred.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal; often faintly critical

      Adulation is gushing and usually uncritical — more than earned renown, less official than a commendation, and unlike idolatrous devotion it names the over-the-top praise itself rather than god-like worship; note too that adulation is genuinely felt by the admirers, whereas flattery is calculated praise with an angle behind it.
