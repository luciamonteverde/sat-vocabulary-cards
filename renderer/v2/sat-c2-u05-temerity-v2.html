temerity

/tuh-MAIR-ih-tee/ - the stress falls on MAIR

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Temerity means reckless boldness that defies rank, rules, or warnings; audacity.

See the word in context

Fresh from her first sale, the junior agent had the temerity to ask the firm's founding partner for his corner office when he retired - three decades early, as he saw it.

The new deckhand untied the harbor master's own skiff to run a forgotten lunch out to the fleet, returning it with an emptied fuel tank and a cheerful wave. The harbor master, speechless, finally produced the only word that fit: temerity - boldness so heedless of rank and consequence it looped past bravery into recklessness.

Temerity is not courage, though it borrows courage's costume: courage weighs the risk and accepts it; temerity never does the weighing. The word always carries a raised eyebrow - the speaker thinks the boldness was rash, presumptuous, or both.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    nerve: Nerve is informal boldness — the cheek to try it, said over the back fence and often half in admiration. Temerity is its formal, frowning cousin: boldness so heedless of rank, rules, or warnings that the speaker plainly disapproves.

    gall: Gall is brazen presumption that offends — demanding what decency forbids, with no visible shame. Temerity is wider and less self-serving: it covers overstepping toward danger as well as toward rank, and it stresses the heedlessness of the boldness rather than its shamelessness.

    recklessness: Recklessness is pure disregard of danger, and it needs no boldness at all — you can be reckless out of sheer inattention, texting through a red light. Temerity needs the bold gesture: an overstep against rank, rules, or warnings, made without ever weighing the cost.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    caution: careful weighing of risk

    timidity: shrinking from any risk at all

    Where it sits · how heedless the risk-taking becomes

    boldness → daring → temerity

    boldness vs. temerity: Boldness takes the initiative and may be admirable; temerity has already left admiration behind.

    daring vs. temerity: Daring runs real risks with open eyes; temerity goes further - risk-taking so heedless of consequence and rank that onlookers wince.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Temerity always disapproves a little: use it when boldness overstepped - toward danger or toward rank. You met audacious in Unit 3; audacity can still be admiring, but temerity rarely is. Informally, nerve and gall live in the same neighborhood; caution and timidity are its opposites.
