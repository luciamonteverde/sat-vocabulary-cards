stringent

/STRIN-jent/ - starts like 'string'

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Stringent means strict and tightly binding — said of rules, standards, and requirements that leave little slack and demand real effort to satisfy.

See the word in context

Visa rules for the fellowship were stringent: original documents only, four proofs of residence, and a filing window nine days wide.

To sell a single jar across state lines, the honey co-op had to meet stringent labeling standards — batch numbers, moisture content to the tenth of a percent, an allergen panel in two languages — and its first three label designs failed.

Stringent lives up to its root: the requirements bind tight. Notice what carries the word — standards, rules, limits. The inspectors enforcing them may be strict or even harsh, but stringent belongs to the requirements themselves.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    strict: demanding that rules be followed — the everyday word, and the one for people: a strict proctor, a strict parent. Stringent stays with the rules themselves — stringent codes, stringent limits — and adds that they bind tight and cost real effort to meet.

    rigorous: thorough, exact, and leaving nothing loose — often admiring, as in rigorous scholarship. Stringent is felt from below: a stringent requirement is one you must strain to satisfy.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    lax: loose and careless about enforcing standards

    lenient: mild, inclined to let things pass

    Where it sits · how tightly the rule binds

    firm → stringent → draconian

    firm vs. stringent: Firm is milder than stringent: a firm rule holds steady and expects compliance, while a stringent one binds tight — exacting, detailed, and costly to satisfy.

    draconian vs. stringent: Draconian is harsher than stringent: stringent standards are demanding but defensible, while draconian ones are so severe that they read as punishment rather than rigor.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      regulatory / precise

      Stringent is the auditor's adjective: it attaches to codes, standards, deadlines, and requirements, and it certifies that they bind tight. Keep people out of its reach — a demanding teacher is strict, not stringent — and keep your grip on the spelling: astringent, its cousin, is a different word for a different job. Between lax and draconian, stringent marks the tight-but-defensible stretch of how hard a rule can press.
