gratuitous

/gruh-TOO-ih-tuhs/ - the stress falls on TOO

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Gratuitous means uncalled for, unwarranted.

See the word in context

The referee let the hard tackle go but flagged the gratuitous shove that came three seconds after the whistle.

The best man's toast ran warm and funny for four minutes. Then came the gratuitous crack about the groom's old girlfriend — unasked for, connected to nothing, answered by the loudest silence a banquet hall can hold. He had the room; the jab bought him nothing and cost him most of it.

Gratuitous is precise because the line had no cause and no purpose: nothing invited it, no joke needed it, no point was served. Harsh words can be earned — a review can owe its subject some pain. A gratuitous jab is the kind nothing owed.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unprovoked: Unprovoked means that nothing set the attack off — no act invited it, no offense came first. Gratuitous reaches further: a gratuitous act is uninvited and also serves no purpose. Unprovoked looks backward at the missing cause; gratuitous adds that nothing useful lies ahead either.

    needless: Needless describes what serves no purpose a need could explain — the trip that could have been skipped, the third reminder. A gratuitous act is needless with an edge: nothing needed it and nothing even invited it. Needless faults wasted effort; gratuitous faults the act's very existence.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    justified: backed by good reason

    deserved: earned by the receiver's own actions

    Where it sits · how little purpose the act serves

    unasked-for → gratuitous → spiteful

    unasked-for vs. gratuitous: Unasked-for advice merely arrives without an invitation; a gratuitous remark goes further — nothing invited it and nothing is served by it.

    spiteful vs. gratuitous: Spiteful passes gratuitous on the ladder: the uncalled-for remark now has a motive, and the motive is to wound.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal, disapproving

    Gratuitous faults a thing for its cause: nothing called for it. Frivolous, its unit-mate, faults a thing for its weight: too light for the moment. A frivolous question wastes the panel's time; a gratuitous insult had no business existing. When reviewers flag gratuitous scenes, they mean the story owed us none of that.

    The manuscript's first readers kept snagging on chapter nine. Mid-heist, the story halts for two pages while a henchman is dangled from a bridge — a scene no one demands and no later chapter remembers. The editor's margin note was blunt: gratuitous. Nothing in the plot calls for it, and nothing downstream is paid off by it. The author protested that thrillers need menace; the editor agreed, then pointed out that this menace threatens no one the reader knows and changes nothing anyone does. In the revision the bridge is gone, and chapter nine, two pages lighter, reads as if nothing was ever missing.
