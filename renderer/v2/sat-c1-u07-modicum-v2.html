modicum

/MOD-ih-kum/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Modicum means a small amount of something — a little but noticeable bit, often of an abstract quality like respect, privacy, or sense.

See the word in context

Modicum means a small amount of something — sharing a bedroom with two younger brothers, Sam treasured even a modicum of privacy, like ten quiet minutes with the door closed.

After months of fetching coffee and stacking chairs, the rookie finally earned a modicum of respect from the veteran players when she sank the winning shot.

She earned a small but real amount of respect — not admiration or fame, just a first noticeable bit after months of nothing. That small-but-countable quantity is precisely a modicum.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    shred: A shred is a tiny scrap of something, and it lives mostly in negative company — not a shred of evidence, without a shred of doubt. A modicum is its positive twin: a small amount that genuinely exists and counts. You deny someone even a shred; you grant them a modicum.

    trace: A trace is a barely detectable amount — a trace of an accent, traces of a chemical — something an instrument or a sharp eye just registers. A modicum is more substantial: small, but solid enough to do something or mean something. A trace is noticed; a modicum is used.

    sliver: A sliver is a thin, small piece cut or split from something larger — a sliver of glass, of cake, of moonlight. It stays concrete and physical. Modicum is the abstract counterpart, taking nouns like respect, privacy, and sense: a small but real portion of a quality.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    abundance: a plentiful, more-than-enough supply

    surplus: an amount beyond what is needed

    Where it sits · the sheer amount present

    hint → modicum → heap

    hint vs. modicum: A hint is fainter than a modicum — barely there at all; a modicum is small but solid enough to count.

    heap vs. modicum: A heap dwarfs a modicum: if you have a heap of patience, you have far more than the bare minimum a modicum implies.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      Formal, often ironic

      Modicum nearly always appears in the pattern 'a modicum of' + an abstract noun — a modicum of respect, decency, privacy, sense. It often carries an edge: saying someone lacks 'even a modicum of sense' means they fall short of the bare minimum.
