Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Vicarious means felt or enjoyed through another person's experience rather than firsthand.

See the word in context

Too seasick for boats, Ines takes a vicarious delight in her sister's regattas, grilling her for every detail the moment the launch docks.

The bookshop's 'armchair adventure' shelf sells vicarious experience by the pound: readers who will never leave the county cross the Gobi by camel, round Cape Horn in a gale, and walk into Timbuktu at dusk — all between dinner and bed.

Vicarious experience arrives through another person: their risk, your racing pulse. The word covers borrowed joys and borrowed sorrows alike — a parent's vicarious nerves at an audition, a fan's vicarious triumph — and it always keeps one honest distance: someone else lived it first.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    secondhand: Secondhand describes what gets passed along — information, goods, an account relayed through other hands before it reaches you. Vicarious is about feeling: an experience you live through another person, their risk raising your pulse. A secondhand report reaches your ears; a vicarious thrill reaches your nerves.

    borrowed: Borrowed means taken temporarily from someone else, with the understanding it goes back — a coat, an umbrella, a phrase. Vicarious experience is not on loan and is never handed back: you keep the feeling of a race someone else ran. Borrowed marks a loan; vicarious marks a distance.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    firsthand: from your own eyes and hands

    direct: without anything standing in between

    Where it sits · how far someone else's story becomes yours

    interested → invested → vicarious

    interested vs. vicarious: Interested sits far below vicarious — an interested viewer follows the story but feels none of it as her own.

    invested vs. vicarious: Invested comes closer but stops short of vicarious — an invested fan cares how it ends, while vicarious excitement makes the finish feel like yours.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / reflective

    Vicarious marks experience at one remove: the feeling is real, but the living was someone else's. It pairs naturally with emotions — vicarious thrill, pride, embarrassment. Use 'secondhand' for relayed information; keep vicarious for borrowed feeling, the slot English has no plainer word for.

But by October her kitchen wall held a bus map of Valparaiso, and she was living the year at a distance that somehow had no distance in it -- her week rising and falling with his. When he wrote that he had frozen during a class presentation, she lost a night's sleep; when he finally aced one, she baked as if the grade were hers. Her daughter called the whole performance vicarious and meant it kindly: Ruth had found a way to travel while never leaving her stove.
