grievous

/GREE-vus/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Grievous means very serious and severe, especially describing harm, injury, or damage.

See the word in context

One misplaced decimal in the bridge calculations was a grievous error, forcing engineers to tear out a year of work.

The night the freighter missed the channel, Keeper Anders rowed out through six-foot swells. He pulled three sailors from the water, but the second engineer had been thrown against the rocks. The doctor's telegram to the shipping company did not soften the news: the man's injuries were grievous, and he would never sail again.

Injuries severe enough to end a sailor's career — reported in the gravest possible terms — are what grievous conveys.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    severe: Severe means very serious in degree, and it is the standard word across registers: severe weather, a severe sprain, severe delays. Grievous matches that seriousness but is formal and a touch literary, reserved for harm, injury, or wrongdoing described in grave language. A forecast warns of severe storms; a court records grievous bodily harm.

    dire: Dire looks forward: dire warnings, dire straits, a dire shortage — the word for a situation threatening disaster ahead and demanding urgent action. Grievous looks back at harm already done and weighs how severe it is: a grievous wound, a grievous error. Dire signals what is coming; grievous measures what has happened.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    minor: small and not serious

    slight: barely noticeable in degree

    Where it sits · how severe the harm is

    serious → grievous → catastrophic

    serious vs. grievous: A serious injury demands attention, but a grievous one is graver still — severe and possibly permanent.

    catastrophic vs. grievous: Catastrophic harm goes beyond even grievous — total disaster on the largest scale.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal, somewhat literary

    Grievous measures the severity of harm rather than naming a specific injury: a deep laceration (another word in this unit) might be grievous, but so can a betrayal or an error — no cut required. Grievous is also formal and a touch literary; in casual speech, 'serious' usually fits better.
