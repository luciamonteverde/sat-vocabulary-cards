What It Means

Enigmatic means mystifying — hinting at a meaning yet staying puzzling and hard to interpret.

See the word in context

The grandmaster's enigmatic smile after the draw kept commentators theorizing for a week.

For sixty years the town's unknown donor mailed the library a typed envelope of cash every March. The stamps changed, the amounts grew, and the note inside never said more than 'For the shelves.' She - or he - remains enigmatic: openhanded, exact, and unread.

Enigmatic fits because the donor resists every interpretation the town attempts: present in effect, absent in person. Nothing is coded - there is no message to crack - so cryptic would miss. What defeats the librarians is the person, not the note.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    mystifying: Something mystifying actively bewilders whoever confronts it — it throws a puzzle at you and demands an answer on the spot. Enigmatic is quieter and more magnetic: an enigmatic person or painting gives off hints and presence, inviting theory after theory without ever settling one.

    inscrutable: An inscrutable face or manner is closed to reading: it offers no signs at all to interpret. Enigmatic works the other way — an enigmatic person gives off signals and hints that invite reading and then defeat it. The inscrutable give you nothing to start from.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    open: shows everything and holds back nothing

    forthcoming: readily offers facts about itself

    Where it sits · how long it resists being read

    puzzling → enigmatic → unfathomable

    puzzling vs. enigmatic: A puzzling detail trips you up for an afternoon; enigmatic goes further - it resists interpretation year after year and invites theory after theory.

    unfathomable vs. enigmatic: Unfathomable is the last rung: an enigmatic painting still tempts you to read it, while an unfathomable one lies beyond any reading's reach.

      Word Choice

      literary

      Enigmatic suits people, faces, and works of art that invite interpretation and defeat it - there is presence, aura, an unanswered question. A two-word note is cryptic (this unit's word for messages hidden on purpose), and a person who simply keeps facts back is guarded, not enigmatic. Save enigmatic for what keeps you theorizing.

      In 1908, excavators on Crete lifted a palm-sized clay disc stamped, on both faces, with spirals of tiny pictures: a walking man, a fish, a crested head. The disc has been enigmatic ever since -- not blank or silent, but generous with hints that refuse to add up. Every few years a new decipherment is announced; each wins headlines, then collapses under the next scholar's review. The signs are crisp and deliberate, clearly meant to be read, which is exactly what keeps the theories coming. After a century of attempts, the little disc still draws more readers than almost any text that can actually be read.
