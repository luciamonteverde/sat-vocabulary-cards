/kuh-PICH-uh-layt/ - the stress falls on PICH

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Capitulate means to surrender and stop resisting.

See the word in context

Nine moves from checkmate and an hour behind on the clock, the grandmaster still refused to capitulate.

For thirty years the orchard's owner turned down every offer from the resort next door. The winter a late frost killed half her trees, she capitulated at last, signing over the land on the buyers' original terms.

Capitulate says more than 'give up' or 'quit' — it means the resistance itself ends, usually after a long fight and usually on the other side's terms. You quit a hobby; you capitulate to an opponent who has worn you down.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    surrender: To surrender is to give up, often physically handing yourself or a position over — the white flag moment. Capitulate is the fuller word: not just handing something over but ending the resistance itself, usually after a long fight and on the other side's terms.

    submit: To submit is to place yourself under someone else's authority and stop opposing it — it can be quiet, even routine. Capitulate marks a sharper moment: a real fight was fought, the holdout was long, and the surrender is complete and usually on the winner's terms.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    resist: to keep pushing back against pressure

    defy: to refuse openly and boldly

    Where it sits · how much of the resistance ends

    waver → yield → capitulate

    waver vs. capitulate: Wavering is far milder than capitulating — your resolve trembles for a moment, but nothing has been given up yet.

    yield vs. capitulate: Yielding is weaker than capitulating — you give ground on a point or a moment, while capitulating means the whole resistance ends.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal

      Capitulate is the formal word for the moment resistance ends — sieges, standoffs, long negotiations. For small everyday moments, 'give in' sounds more natural; reach for capitulate when the fight was real, the holdout was long, and the surrender is complete. Yield, by contrast, can be partial or temporary — you can yield an inch without capitulating.
