sardonic

/sar-DON-ik/ - the stress falls on DON

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Sardonic means grimly mocking and scornful — humor with dark disdain running underneath it.

See the word in context

The tow-truck driver kept a sardonic sign bolted above the winch — 'Your shortcut, my salary' — and waved pleasantly at every car in the ditch.

After the third round of budget cuts, the lab director's memos turned sardonic: 'Good news — headquarters believes we can cure the disease with two beakers and the interns' optimism.'

Sardonic is more precise than 'sarcastic' because sarcasm is a technique — saying the opposite of what you mean — while sardonic names the temperature: cold, scornful amusement, usually aimed at fate, folly, or the whole absurd situation. A sardonic speaker isn't erupting in anger; the joke is how little he expects anymore.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    mocking: Mocking is the broad word for making fun of something, from playground teasing to open ridicule — the mockery can even be affectionate. Sardonic is mockery served cold: the laugh has scorn in it, and the speaker sounds half-finished with the world he's laughing at.

    cynical: Cynical describes a belief — that people act from self-interest and every promise is for sale. Sardonic is what that belief sounds like when it smiles: the dry, dark joke a cynic makes instead of an argument.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    earnest: sincere and serious, meaning every word straight

    good-humored: amused in a warm way, with no scorn in the laugh

    Where it sits · how much scorn darkens the humor

    wry → sardonic → sneering

    wry vs. sardonic: Wry is milder than sardonic — a small, crooked smile at life's ironies, affectionate as often as not, with none of sardonic's scorn.

    sneering vs. sardonic: Sneering goes further than sardonic: the humor drains away entirely and open contempt is all that's left on the face.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      dark / literary

      Sardonic is the reviewer's word for humor that scorns what it describes — a sardonic narrator, a sardonic aside. Two unit-mates patrol the same block: acerbic stings with a sour, witty bite, and vitriolic has stopped joking altogether. Sardonic is the coolest of the three — it mocks darkly and keeps its seat.
