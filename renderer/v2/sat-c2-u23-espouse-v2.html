espouse

/ih-SPOWZ/ - rhymes with vows

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Espouse means to take up and publicly support a cause.

See the word in context

The chef has espoused nose-to-tail cooking since her first restaurant, printing the philosophy right on the menu.

Nobody expected the quiet cartographer to espouse open data. But when the county tried to lock its maps behind fees, she gave talks, wrote letters, and put her name on the lawsuit — the cause was publicly hers from then on.

Espouse is stronger than support and more public than believe in: you take the cause up as your own and say so where it costs you something. The word began as 'to marry' — and a trace of the wedding survives: espousing a cause means being joined to it in front of witnesses.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    embrace: To embrace an idea is to take it fully to heart — genuine, warm, and possibly entirely private; you can embrace a philosophy for years without telling a soul. To espouse a cause is to take it up in public, your name and voice attached where the commitment shows.

    uphold: Uphold means to defend and maintain something already established — an appeals board upholds a decision, a family upholds a tradition. Espouse points the other way: you take up a cause, often new or out of favor, and publicly argue for standing it does not yet have.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    renounce: to give a cause up formally and publicly

    oppose: to work against it

    Where it sits · how much of yourself the cause claims

    applaud → espouse → crusade

    applaud vs. espouse: Applauding a cause stops far short of espousing it — applause costs one evening and both hands, approval from a seat in the audience, while an espoused cause carries your name and your voice in public.

    crusade vs. espouse: Crusade goes further than espouse — the espoused cause has your name and your voice, but a crusade takes the rest of the calendar too.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / public

    Espouse belongs to public life: causes, doctrines, reforms — things you adopt and argue for where it shows. Support is milder, and embrace can stay private. One warning: despite the wedding in its history, modern English does not use espouse for marrying — a sentence about espousing a person will read as a mistake.
