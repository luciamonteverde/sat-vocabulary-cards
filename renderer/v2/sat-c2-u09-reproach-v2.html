reproach

/rih-PROHCH/ - rhymes with coach

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Reproach means to scold; to express disapproval.

See the word in context

He would gently reproach the borrowers who returned his chisels dull — and then lend them again the next weekend.

When Amos finally visited the workshop after missing his oldest friend's farewell party, old Herz didn't raise his voice. He set two cups of coffee on the bench and said only, 'Forty years, and I looked for your face.' Amos would have preferred shouting. To reproach someone is to hand them their fault wrapped in disappointment — quiet, personal, and heavier than anger.

Reproach is the exact word because the disapproval is spoken, but gently and from close range — between friends, family, people who looked for better from each other. A rebuke comes down from authority and stings on impact; a reproach rises out of hurt and settles in slowly. The word does its work in a lowered voice.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fault: To fault someone points out the error, cool and clinical — the inspector faults the wiring and files her report. To reproach carries personal hurt with it: the fault matters because the two of you mattered to each other. Fault keeps its distance; a reproach closes that distance.

    chasten: To chasten aims to humble and improve — discipline heavy enough to bend someone toward better behavior, the way a crushing loss chastens a boastful team. A reproach is far lighter machinery: one quiet, personal word of disapproval. Chastening reshapes a person; a reproach simply reaches one.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    excuse: lets the fault pass unmentioned

    console: comforts the person instead of weighing the fault

    Where it sits · how forcefully the fault is aired

    hint → reproach → castigate

    hint vs. reproach: A hint leaves the fault unspoken and hopes you find it yourself; a reproach says it aloud, however softly.

    castigate vs. reproach: Castigate goes far past reproach: the reproached apprentice hears quiet disappointment, while the castigated one is scoured — corrected at length, in heat, and often in public.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    soft / personal

    Reproach is disapproval at close range and low volume — the word for friends, family, and mentors more than for courtrooms. Its unit-mate rebuke is the sharp, downward, in-the-moment scolding. A reproach can even go unspoken, carried in a look; and English lets the noun stand for the state of deserving blame — conduct 'beyond reproach' — a fixed phrase worth recognizing on test day.
