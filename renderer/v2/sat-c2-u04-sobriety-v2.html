Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Sobriety means calm, grave seriousness of manner.

See the word in context

Even the class clown read his half of the war diary with sudden sobriety.

Four hundred meters below the summit, the expedition leader gathered the climbers and spread the weather charts on the snow. She cracked none of her usual jokes and rushed no one; she laid out the numbers, named the risks, and asked each climber in turn. Her sobriety told them more than the forecast did — they voted to turn back.

Sobriety is stronger than seriousness because it adds weight and composure: she is not merely unjoking but unhurried, calm, and equal to the gravity of the decision. Note the word's other everyday sense — not being drunk — which context alone rules out here.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    gravity: Gravity is the felt weight of a matter or a manner — the room registers that something serious is underway, and the situation itself can have it. Sobriety belongs to a person's bearing and adds self-command: calm, unhurried, clear-headed. A crisis has gravity; the leader who meets it steadily shows sobriety.

    solemnity: Solemnity is gravity dressed for ceremony — the hush of a funeral, an oath, a state occasion — dignity performed in ritual form. Sobriety needs no occasion at all: it is one person's grave, composed manner, as much at home in a weather briefing as at a memorial.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    levity: lightness or joking where weight is expected

    giddiness: bubbling, unserious excitement

    Where it sits · how heavy the manner grows

    seriousness → sobriety → grimness

    seriousness vs. sobriety: Seriousness only says the joking has stopped; sobriety goes further, adding calm weight and self-command to the manner.

    grimness vs. sobriety: Grimness goes past sobriety into darkness: the sober speaker is grave but steady, while the grim one has let bleakness into her voice.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal

      Sobriety names a manner: calm, grave, weighed. Because it also commonly means not being drunk, let context do the disambiguating — 'the sobriety of the eulogy' is safe, 'his sobriety' alone may mislead. Its cluster-mate stoic faces inward at hardship endured without complaint; sobriety faces outward, the gravity an audience can see.

Captains remember the moment a pilot takes the bridge. The wind may be screaming, but the pilot speaks quietly, asks for coffee, and studies the charts as if the night were calm. One veteran, Elena Vidal, is famous for her sobriety in the strait's narrowest passage: orders given in a low, even voice, no wasted words, hands folded behind her back. Crews say the manner is contagious. Panic, one captain wrote, cannot survive ten minutes of watching her. The strait stays as dangerous as ever. What changes, when a pilot like Vidal is aboard, is the temperature of the people facing it.
