Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Cosmopolitan means drawing on many cultures and at ease among them.

See the word in context

Years of crewing on cargo ships had made the cook cosmopolitan, as ready with a Manila breakfast as with a Marseille stew.

Raised in three capitals by diplomat parents, the translator moves through a reception without a false step — bowing at the right depth to one guest, switching to the familiar form of address with the next, declining a dish so graciously that the refusal reads as a compliment. Colleagues call her cosmopolitan, and mean that no room of strangers is foreign to her.

Cosmopolitan is more precise than 'well-traveled' because it means drawing on many cultures and being at ease among them — the ease is the point. A suitcase full of stamps proves only movement; cosmopolitan describes what the moving made of you.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    worldly: Worldly means experienced in the ways of the world — money, motives, how deals really get done — sometimes at the cost of innocence. Cosmopolitan is warmer and more particular: shaped by many cultures and at ease among them. A worldly person is hard to fool; a cosmopolitan one is hard to make feel foreign.

    international: International describes things that span nations — treaties, airports, agencies — and is said of organizations and undertakings more than of a person's temperament. Cosmopolitan describes the person or place itself, drawing on many cultures and moving easily among them. A firm can be international while every manager in it has never felt at home abroad.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    provincial: limited to one region's narrow outlook

    parochial: narrowly local, as if nothing beyond the parish mattered

    Where it sits · how at home among other cultures

    open-minded → well-traveled → cosmopolitan

    open-minded vs. cosmopolitan: Open-minded says you welcome unfamiliar ways when they arrive; it stops short of cosmopolitan, which has gone out and lived among them.

    well-traveled vs. cosmopolitan: Well-traveled goes further than open-minded — the stamps and the miles are real — but it stops short of cosmopolitan: miles prove motion, not ease among the cultures they pass through.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / slightly formal

      Cosmopolitan praises range and ease across cultures — of a person, a city, a menu. Do not stretch it to mean merely large or busy. In this unit it sits beside pluralism: cosmopolitan describes a person or place at home among many cultures, while pluralism names the arrangement that lets many groups keep their own ways side by side.
