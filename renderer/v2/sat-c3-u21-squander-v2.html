squander

/SKWON-der/ - the stress falls on SKWON

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Squander means to waste something valuable recklessly — to spend money, time, or a chance on nothing worth having.

See the word in context

The team squandered a three-goal lead in the final period, giving the puck away at center ice again and again.

Historians still wince at how the expedition squandered its head start: three weeks of perfect weather spent renaming landmarks and staging photographs, while the pass ahead quietly filled with snow.

Squander is harsher than 'waste' because it adds blame and scale: something genuinely valuable — three weeks of perfect weather — was thrown away recklessly by the very people who held it, and it will not come back.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    fritter: To fritter something away is to waste it in small nibbles — a dollar here, ten minutes there — until it is quietly gone. Squander moves in bigger, more reckless strokes: the fortune, the lead, the once-in-a-lifetime chance, thrown away in full view.

    dissipate: To dissipate money or energy is to scatter it until nothing is left, often in idle pleasure. Dissipate watches the resource thin out and vanish; squander points at the person — it is an accusation of reckless, blameworthy waste.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    conserve: protects a resource and spends it carefully so it lasts

    safeguard: guards something valuable against loss or harm

    Where it sits · how recklessly the resource burns

    waste → squander → hemorrhage

    waste vs. squander: Waste is weaker than squander: any slack afternoon can waste an hour, but to squander is to burn through something precious with both hands, and the loss stings.

    hemorrhage vs. squander: Hemorrhage goes further than squander — a squandered budget was thrown away choice by choice, while a hemorrhaging one is bleeding out faster than anyone can even track.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    critical / accusing

    Squander comes with the blame built in: it accuses someone of recklessly throwing away something precious — a fortune, a lead, a one-time chance. Deplete just tracks a supply going down, with no judgment attached, and waste covers even trivial losses. Reach for squander when the loss was valuable, avoidable, and someone's fault.
