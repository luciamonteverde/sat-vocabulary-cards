Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Jubilant means overflowing with triumphant joy — happiness that celebrates out loud, usually over a victory or a long-awaited success.

See the word in context

When the inspectors finally cleared the dam, the jubilant town rang the firehouse bell until well past midnight.

When the final buzzer confirmed the upset, the jubilant bench emptied — players piling into a heap at center court, seniors hugging the equipment manager, somebody spraying water like champagne.

Jubilant joy is triumphant and loud: it follows a win, and it must celebrate. Notice there is nothing quiet in the scene — piling, hugging, spraying — because jubilation without an outlet stops being jubilation. That victorious, out-loud quality is what separates it from mere happiness.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    triumphant: Triumphant centers on the victory itself — the winner's stance over a beaten challenge; a team can be triumphant in grim silence. Jubilant centers on the joy: noisy, overflowing celebration of whatever has been won.

    overjoyed: Overjoyed says the happiness has exceeded its container — but it can stay private, a hand pressed over the mouth. Jubilant joy goes public: it cheers, rings bells, and pulls a crowd into the celebrating.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    despondent: sunk in hopeless gloom

    crestfallen: visibly deflated by a defeat or letdown

    Where it sits · how loudly the joy celebrates

    glad → jubilant → ecstatic

    glad vs. jubilant: Glad is far milder than jubilant: gladness is a warm inner yes that may never reach the face, while jubilant joy is louder and fuller — it cheers, it celebrates, it draws a crowd.

    ecstatic vs. jubilant: Ecstatic is stronger than jubilant: a jubilant crowd is still celebrating a win it can name, while ecstasy is joy so intense and overwhelming that the moment itself dissolves — beyond celebrating, beyond words.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    celebratory / vivid

    Jubilant is victory's adjective: it wants a scoreboard, a verdict, a reopened road — some named success to celebrate. Its unit-mates split the neighborhood: elated is the altitude of the feeling, exult is the act of letting it out, and ebullient bubbles by temperament, win or no win. If nothing has been won, jubilant is the wrong word — and if the joy is silent, it isn't jubilant yet.
