ominous

/OM-uh-nuhs/ - the stress falls on OM

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Ominous means serving as a sign that trouble is coming — foreshadowing evil the way a dark omen does.

See the word in context

The letter's ominous closing line — 'we will speak of this again soon' — kept the inspector awake half the night.

For three days the seismographs traced an ominous pattern under the volcano — small quakes arriving closer and closer together — and on the fourth morning the observatory told the villages below to pack.

Nothing has erupted; no ash has fallen. Ominous belongs precisely to that interval — it names the sign, not the disaster, and claims only that the sign points toward trouble. The moment the mountain actually blows, the pattern stops being ominous and becomes a catastrophe's first chapter.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    foreboding: Foreboding most often names the feeling — the heavy inner sense that something bad is coming. Ominous belongs to the sign itself: the sky, the silence, the letter that carries the warning.

    threatening: Threatening implies an actor: someone or something intends the harm and shows it. Ominous needs no intender — a barometer reading can be ominous; it merely predicts, the way an omen does.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    auspicious: promising success — a good omen

    reassuring: easing worry rather than raising it

    Where it sits · how clearly the sign points to coming harm

    unsettling → ominous → menacing

    unsettling vs. ominous: Unsettling is milder than ominous: an unsettling detail stirs a vague unease, while an ominous one points to specific trouble on the way.

    menacing vs. ominous: Menacing is stronger than ominous — the ominous merely signals danger to come, while the menacing is the danger itself, present and aimed at you.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      dark / literary

      Ominous attaches to things that are read — skies, silences, footsteps, closing lines — and claims they predict trouble. Where a person means you harm, reach for threatening or menacing; where the signs simply point the wrong way, ominous is the word. Its bright opposite is auspicious, the omen read for good.

      The observatory's job was to read a mountain that could not speak. For three quiet years the instruments recorded almost nothing: a tremor here, a wisp of steam there, the ordinary breathing of a sleeping volcano. In March the readings changed. The lake on the crater floor warmed by two degrees, and the tremors began arriving in rhythmic swarms. None of this endangered anyone yet, and the mountain intended nothing — mountains never do. But to the monitoring team the pattern was ominous, an unmistakable sign that an eruption was assembling itself below. They did not wait for smoke. The evacuation notices went out that same week, drafted calmly, before the danger itself had even arrived.
