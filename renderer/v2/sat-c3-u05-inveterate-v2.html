inveterate

/in-VET-ur-it/ - the stress falls on VET

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Inveterate means firmly fixed in a long-standing habit.

See the word in context

An inveterate eavesdropper, Aunt Bettina chose café tables the way generals choose hills.

My grandfather haggles at the fixed-price supermarket, at the toll booth, and once — successfully — at a parking meter. Sixty years of market stalls made him an inveterate bargainer, and no checkout screen will unmake him.

Inveterate is for habits that have grown into the person — in- ('into') + vetus ('old'): grown old in it. It sits before the noun (an inveterate liar, an inveterate optimist) and resists reform by definition. Frequent counts occurrences; inveterate counts decades.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    habitual: Habitual is neutral bookkeeping: the thing is done by settled habit, and that is all it says -- a habitual jogger may have started last spring. Inveterate counts decades, not occurrences, and signals a habit fused into the person's identity, well past reforming.

    confirmed: Confirmed says a person has settled into a role and is unlikely to leave it -- a confirmed bachelor, a confirmed skeptic. It marks the state as fixed. Inveterate adds the long history behind that state: the decades of repetition that fused the habit into the person.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    occasional: happening now and then, no habit at all

    reformed: the habit renounced and left behind

    Where it sits · how deeply the habit has set

    recurring → ingrained → inveterate

    recurring vs. inveterate: Recurring only says a thing comes back — it stops well short of inveterate, a habit grown into the person.

    ingrained vs. inveterate: Ingrained habits run deep, yet even they stop short of inveterate, which has hardened over years past any real hope of reform.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal / literary

    Inveterate always modifies the doer (an inveterate punster) and always signals decades — habit fused into identity, past reforming. Habitual is neutral bookkeeping; chronic belongs to conditions. It usually smiles wryly: inveterate collectors are teased, not condemned.
