pervasive

/per-VAY-siv/ - the stress falls on VAY

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Pervasive means spread through every part of something — present wherever you look within it.

See the word in context

Two years after the fire, the smell of smoke is still pervasive in the archive — in the boxes, the bindings, even the carpet glue.

No one at the station ever ordered anyone to stay late. The pressure was pervasive anyway: it lived in the joking about 'half-days' at six p.m., in the glances at the first coat pulled off its hook, in the schedule that quietly assumed evenings. New hires absorbed it within a week without being told a thing.

Pervasive is the right word because the pressure has soaked through the whole place — the jokes, the glances, the schedule. Nothing about it is loud or official; its power is exactly that there is no corner of the station where it isn't.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    rampant: spreading fast and unchecked, still gaining ground — rampant is growth caught in motion. Pervasive says the spreading is finished: the thing is already in every part of the whole.

    entrenched: dug in so firmly that removal is nearly impossible — entrenched is about grip, not reach. A pervasive attitude fills every corner; an entrenched one refuses to leave the corner it holds.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    contained: held within limits, kept from spreading

    scarce: thin on the ground — met rarely, if at all

    Where it sits · how completely it fills the whole

    recurring → pervasive → suffocating

    recurring vs. pervasive: Recurring is weaker than pervasive: a recurring worry keeps coming back in particular moments, while a pervasive one has soaked into every moment and doesn't need to return.

    suffocating vs. pervasive: Suffocating is stronger than pervasive: pervasive means present in every part, but suffocating means so thick in every part that it crowds the air out of the room.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral, often wary — the word leans toward what you'd rather not find everywhere

    Pervasive spreads through one whole: a mood pervades an office, damp pervades a cellar, doubt pervades a report. Its unit-mate ubiquitous turns up everywhere you go — many places, one world. The smell in a house is pervasive; the coffee chain on every corner is ubiquitous. And though the word is technically neutral, it keeps low company — bias, odors, and rumors are pervasive more often than joy is.
