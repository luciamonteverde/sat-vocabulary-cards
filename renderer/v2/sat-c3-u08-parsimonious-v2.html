Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Parsimonious means extremely unwilling to spend money; stingy.

See the word in context

Parsimonious to the end, old Mr. Voss padlocked the sugar tin and issued firewood by the stick, even in January.

The team's new owner proved parsimonious in ways the fans felt at once: broken seats stayed broken, star players walked away over nickels, and the scoreboard flickered through two more seasons because a replacement bulb 'could wait.'

Parsimonious is stinginess taken to a fault: not care with money but a refusal to release it, even when spending is plainly needed. Where frugal earns respect, parsimonious earns resentment — the word almost always arrives as an accusation.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    miserly: Miserly is stinginess with a picture attached: the hoarder counting coins by candlelight. Parsimonious means the same refusal to spend, dressed for print — the word a report or biography uses about a landlord or a board. Call the neighbor miserly; call the institution parsimonious.

    penny-pinching: Penny-pinching stays small: it grudges the little expenses — tips, bus fare, the second scoop. Parsimonious scales the same reflex up into a whole character, a settled refusal to spend even where spending is plainly needed. A penny-pinching manager skimps on coffee; a parsimonious one lets the roof leak.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    openhanded: giving freely and gladly

    free-spending: parting with money without a second thought

    Where it sits · how completely the wallet stays shut

    thrifty → tightfisted → parsimonious

    thrifty vs. parsimonious: Thrifty sits at the healthy end, far from parsimonious — thrift manages money well and still buys what's needed.

    tightfisted vs. parsimonious: Tightfisted comes close but stops just short of parsimonious — a tightfisted uncle resists opening his wallet, while a parsimonious one has built his whole life around never opening it.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    critical / formal

    Parsimonious is the dressed-up accusation of stinginess — the word reports and biographies use when 'cheap' would sound cheap. Frugal (also in this unit) sits on the honorable end of the same scale: careful, waste-hating, but willing to spend where it counts. Praise the frugal treasurer; indict the parsimonious one.

    Histories of the Calder Valley line rarely dwell on its engineering. They dwell on its accounts. The railway's board was parsimonious on a scale that startled even Victorian critics: stations went unheated, lamps were rationed, and track inspections were cut to save the inspectors' wages. This was not poverty. The line paid handsome dividends every single year of its operating life. Nor was it economy in any useful sense — the money saved on inspections was spent tenfold on the derailments that followed. One shareholder's letter survives: 'We do not run a railway. We run a purse that happens to own trains.'
