preponderance

/prih-PON-der-ens/

Meaning

What It Means

Preponderance means a superiority in number, weight, or importance — the greater part or share of something.

See the word in context

In the civil trial, the jury needed only a preponderance of the physical evidence to side with the plaintiff.

When the committee tallied the responses, the preponderance of the surveys — nearly four out of five — favored keeping the community pool open year-round.

Four out of five responses is clearly the greater share, so preponderance fits: it names the side with superior numbers or weight.

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    predominance: Predominance is the condition of being the most numerous or influential — the predominance of oaks in a forest. It names the state itself, not the people or votes that hold it. Preponderance likewise names superior weight or share, but it is the standard term of formal analysis, especially 'a preponderance of the evidence.'

    bulk: The bulk of something is its main or largest part, with a physical, mass-like feel — the bulk of the cargo, the bulk of the work. Preponderance adds judgment to the arithmetic: the greater share outweighs the rest and tips a decision, as when a preponderance of the evidence decides a civil case.

    lion's share: The lion's share is the informal term for the biggest cut of something being divided — the lion's share of the credit or the budget. It belongs to casual talk. Preponderance does formal, analytical work: it names the side whose weight or numbers outbalance the rest, at home in courtrooms and reports.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    minority: the smaller share

    handful: a very small number

    Where it sits · how decisively one share outweighs the rest

    majority → preponderance → dominance

    majority vs. preponderance: A majority is simply more than half; a preponderance suggests a weightier, more decisive share that clearly outweighs the rest.

    dominance vs. preponderance: Dominance is stronger than preponderance — not just outweighing everything else, but actively controlling it.

    Word Choice

    Formal / legal

    Preponderance is at home in legal and analytical writing — 'a preponderance of the evidence' is the actual standard of proof in U.S. civil trials. In everyday talk, 'most' or 'the majority' does the same job. It is always a noun: a preponderance of something.
