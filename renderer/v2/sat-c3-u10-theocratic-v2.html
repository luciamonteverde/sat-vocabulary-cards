Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Theocratic means governed by religious authority or leaders.

See the word in context

The strategy game's theocratic faction is the fan favorite: temples collect the taxes, and a council of oracles can veto the king's wars.

Medieval Durham came close to theocratic rule in practice — its prince-bishop minted coins, raised an army, and judged criminals, holding the crozier in one hand and the county in the other.

Theocratic is more precise than 'religious' because a nation full of believers is religious; it becomes theocratic only when religious authority holds the levers of state — writing the laws, staffing the courts, crowning or canceling the king.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    priestly: Priestly describes what belongs to or befits priests — priestly robes, priestly duties, a priestly calm. It says nothing about power. Theocratic is a claim about the state itself: the clergy do not just serve at the altar, they govern — writing laws, judging cases, collecting taxes.

    church-run: Church-run is the plain, literal label for the arrangement theocratic names: religious officials hold the levers. Use church-run for a school or a hospital; when a whole government answers to the temple, theocratic is the formal word historians and political scientists reach for.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    secular: keeps religion and government in separate rooms

    worldly: concerned with earthly affairs rather than sacred ones

    Where it sits · how directly faith runs the state

    religious → clerical → theocratic

    religious vs. theocratic: A religious society is full of faith — far short of theocratic, since believers can keep their courts and their altars in different buildings.

    clerical vs. theocratic: Clerical influence puts clergy in the corridors of power, advising and administering, but it stops short of theocratic until the clergy stop advising the rulers and become them.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      formal / historical

      Theocratic classifies governments, real and fictional — reach for it when religious authority actually rules, not merely inspires. A devout country with secular courts isn't theocratic. Its -cratic unit-mates differ only in the driver's seat: wealth (plutocratic), a small circle (oligarchic), earned ability (meritocratic), or the temple (theocratic).

After a plague carried off its royal line, Verath's council of clerics did not appoint a new king. They took the ledgers themselves. What made Verath different was not the depth of its faith but the address of its government: every writ was signed in the temple.
