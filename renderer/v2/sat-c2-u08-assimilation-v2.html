What It Means

Assimilation means the process of being absorbed into a larger group or culture.

See the word in context

Linguists date the loanword's full assimilation to the 1960s, when English printers quietly stopped setting it in italics.

The Andersens moved to the island in October, mainlanders to their boots. By the second winter the children answered in the harbor accent, knew which tides meant chores, and stood with the island crews at the ferry dock; a visiting cousin said she could no longer hear where the family ended and the island began. That erasing of the seam is assimilation.

Assimilation is the precise word because it names being absorbed into a larger group or culture — not visiting it, not copying it for a night. Integration would leave the family recognizably mainland while taking full part; assimilation goes further, folding them in until the difference stops showing at all.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    absorption: Absorption is being taken in by anything at all — a sponge absorbs water, a firm absorbs a rival, a crowd absorbs a stranger — with no cultural claim attached. Assimilation is absorption of one particular kind: a person or group taken into a larger culture until the seam stops showing.

    naturalization: Naturalization is the legal act of becoming a citizen — forms, a test, an oath, a certificate with a date on it. Assimilation is the slower cultural change no office can grant: taking on the larger group's ways until the difference fades. Many people are naturalized years before they are ever assimilated.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    exclusion: being kept out of the group altogether

    separateness: remaining apart and distinct, by choice or by wall

    Where it sits · how much the newcomer changes to match

    acceptance → integration → assimilation

    acceptance vs. assimilation: Acceptance means the group lets you in and leaves you as you are; assimilation reaches deeper — the group's ways become your ways.

    integration vs. assimilation: Integration is full membership with your own colors kept; assimilation goes further, blending you in until newcomer and native can't be told apart.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral / academic

    Assimilation is the social-science word for being absorbed into a larger group or culture, and it can carry real weight — gain and loss at once — so use it where that depth belongs. For everyday joining, 'settling in' is lighter. If the newcomers keep their own identity while taking full part, the honest word is integration, not assimilation.

Her assimilation took far longer, and no office ever issued a paper for it. The certificate made her a citizen in an afternoon; becoming indistinguishable from her neighbors was the work of thirty years.
