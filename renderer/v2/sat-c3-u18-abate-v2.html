abate

/uh-BAYT/ - rhymes with await

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Abate means to become less intense; to ease off.

See the word in context

Campers waited in the ranger station until the wind began to abate before rowing back across the lake.

The protest noise outside the courthouse did not stop all at once; it abated through the afternoon, from chanting, to scattered shouts, to a murmur the clerks could ignore.

Abate is more precise than simply 'stop' because it names a gradual easing of force or intensity — the thing is still there, only weaker. It is the word for winds, pain, noise, and anger that die down by degrees rather than ending in an instant.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    subside: Subside means to sink to a quieter or lower level — floods subside, swelling subsides, an uproar subsides. Abate is about intensity easing off; subside is about a level dropping. A storm's fury abates, while the floodwater it leaves behind subsides.

    let up: Let up is the informal, everyday phrase for pressure or rain easing off, and it usually means a pause you can act in. Abate is the more formal word and points to a gradual, continuing decline in force, not just a momentary break.

    die down: Die down is the casual phrase for noise, wind, or excitement fading toward quiet, and it looks ahead to the finish. When a force abates, the word measures the easing itself — it may still be strong, just weaker than before it began to abate.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    worsen: to grow more severe rather than less

    mount: to build up steadily, as tension or losses do

    Where it sits · how completely the force dies down

    dip → abate → cease

    dip vs. abate: A dip is milder than abating — a dip is a brief, shallow drop, while a force that abates is genuinely and steadily draining away.

    cease vs. abate: Cease is stronger than abate — a storm that abates is still blowing, only less fiercely, but one that ceases has stopped altogether.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral / slightly formal

      Abate is the standard word in forecasts, medicine, and law for force that eases by degrees — storms, pain, noise, a nuisance. In casual talk, 'let up' or 'die down' sounds more natural. Note that unlike attenuate, which needs an agent doing the weakening, abate usually describes a thing easing on its own.
