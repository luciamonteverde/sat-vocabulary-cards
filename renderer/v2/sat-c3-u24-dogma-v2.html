Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Dogma means a belief or set of beliefs laid down by an authority as unquestionably true — principles to be accepted, not examined.

See the word in context

The magazine's dogma — never end a review with a question — outlived four editors-in-chief, though none of them could say who had made the rule.

For decades the studio ran on dogma: animation is for children, a musical needs a villain, sixty minutes is the ceiling. Nobody could say where the rules came from, only that questioning them in a pitch meeting ended careers — until one film broke all three and became the studio's biggest hit.

That is the mark of dogma: the beliefs sit above argument. Evidence is never offered because none is thought needed — the authority of the rule is the whole case, and a doubter is treated as disloyal rather than curious.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    doctrine: Doctrine is the neutral word for a body of teachings — a church, party, or school can state its doctrine and happily debate it. Dogma is doctrine with the door closed: laid down as beyond question, so that doubt itself becomes the offense.

    orthodoxy: Orthodoxy is whatever opinion a community currently treats as the approved, correct one — the pressure to conform comes sideways, from the group. Dogma comes down from authority as fixed truth; you can quietly drift from orthodoxy, but dogma is enforced.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    open inquiry: questioning freely and following evidence wherever it goes

    skepticism: the habit of doubting claims until they earn belief

    Where it sits · how firmly the belief is sealed against question

    conviction → dogma → taboo

    conviction vs. dogma: Conviction is weaker than dogma: a conviction is firmly held, but it is still yours — argument and evidence can reach it. Dogma has been sealed by authority, and challenging it is treated as betrayal rather than debate.

    taboo vs. dogma: Taboo goes further than dogma: dogma forbids doubting the belief, while a taboo forbids even raising the subject — the question itself becomes unspeakable.

      Section 3

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      critical / intellectual

      Dogma is almost always an accusation now: call a belief dogma and you are saying it survives on authority instead of evidence. Doctrine and creed can be neutral self-descriptions; orthodoxy names the group's approved line; dogma is the hard case — fixed, enforced, and closed. Science keeps one famous exception, the 'central dogma' of molecular biology, a deliberately wry name for a rule that did, in the end, get revised.

It was not offered as a hypothesis; it was taught as settled fact, and researchers who suggested an infection might be to blame struggled to get a hearing.
