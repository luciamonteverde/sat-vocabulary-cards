Meaning

Hear It

What It Means

Elude means to evade or escape.

See the word in context

The counterfeiter eluded auditors for years by keeping two sets of books in two different languages.

The winger eluded three defenders along the touchline, each lunge closing on the space she had just left.

Elude is more exact than escape or outrun because it means slipping away again and again — from pursuers, from nets, even from memory — through quickness or cunning rather than raw speed.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    evade: Evade is elude's near twin — slipping away by cunning — but it leans toward duties, taxes, and questions: you evade responsibility or a direct answer. Elude is the broader escape-artist's word: pursuers, nets, cures, even a name on the tip of your tongue can elude you.

    outrun: To outrun is to beat pursuers by sheer speed — a footrace you win. Elude wins by quickness and cunning instead: slipping away, doubling back, staying out of sight. A fugitive can elude a manhunt for years without ever once outrunning anybody.

    shake: To shake is to break free of pursuers who are actively on your tail — the cruiser behind you, the reporter at your heels. Elude is wider and quieter: you elude searchers who never even find you. You can only shake what has found you.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    confront: turn and face something head-on

    surrender: stop running and give yourself up

    Where it sits · how thoroughly you slip out of reach

    dodge → elude → vanish

    dodge vs. elude: Dodge is a smaller act than elude — a single sidestep around one grab, one question, one puddle — while eluding means slipping away again and again, for as long as the hunt lasts.

    vanish vs. elude: Vanish goes further than elude: the quarry is not merely uncaught but gone without trace, as if it had never been there.

      Apply

      “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

      Word Choice

      neutral

      Elude suits sustained, successful slipping-away — a fugitive eludes a manhunt, a cure eludes researchers, a word eludes your memory. Evade leans toward duties and questions; dodge is a single quick move. You met elusive earlier in this course — it is the adjective for whatever keeps eluding you.
