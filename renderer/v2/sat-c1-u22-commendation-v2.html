commendation

What It Means

Commendation means a formal, official statement or act of praise, such as an award.

See the word in context

The museum's trustees voted unanimously to issue a written commendation to the conservator who saved the fire-damaged murals.

When the ransomware attack froze the hospital's systems, night-shift pharmacist Ellis Okafor hand-checked every medication order against paper records for eighteen straight hours, and not one dose went out wrong. Two months later, the hospital board did something it had done only four times in its history: it entered a formal commendation into the official record, praising Okafor by name and describing exactly what his diligence had prevented.

The board's formal, on-the-record statement of praise — voted on, written down, and entered into the official record — is what makes this a commendation rather than a simple thank-you.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    citation: A citation is a formal written mention that can serve many purposes -- honoring a soldier, penalizing a driver, or crediting a source. A commendation is narrower: it is official praise and nothing else. Every commendation honors; a citation might just as easily fine you or footnote you.

    tribute: A tribute is a public act or expression of honor -- a speech, a dedicated song, a moment of silence -- and anyone who wants to show respect can offer one. A commendation comes from an authority: official praise issued formally and entered on the record. A fan can pay tribute; only the board issues a commendation.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    condemnation: a formal statement of disapproval

    criticism: fault-finding disapproval

    Where it sits · how official the praise becomes

    compliment → commendation → medal

    compliment vs. commendation: A compliment is casual, everyday praise; a commendation is official praise made formally and on the record.

    medal vs. commendation: A medal goes one step past a commendation — the official praise becomes a physical decoration pinned on for all to see.

      Apply

      Word Choice

      formal, institutional; an act or document, not a feeling

      A commendation is a specific, official act of praise — a certificate, resolution, or statement read into the record — which keeps it in a different lane from renown (general earned fame), adulation (gushing unofficial admiration), and idolatrous devotion (worship that has gone too far); it names paperwork and ceremony, never an emotion.
