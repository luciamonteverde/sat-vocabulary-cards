illicit

/ih-LIS-it/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Illicit means forbidden by law, rules, or custom; not permitted.

See the word in context

Border agents discovered a hidden compartment packed with illicit goods that smugglers hoped to slip past inspection.

Every Friday night, cars slipped quietly behind the old cannery on Dock Street. Inside, an illicit gambling operation ran until dawn, with card tables, cash boxes, and lookouts posted at every door. The organizers never advertised, because everything about the operation violated state law. When police finally raided the building, they seized thousands of dollars in untraceable bets.

The gambling operation is called illicit because it runs in secret and violates state law, which is exactly what the word describes.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    unlawful: Unlawful means against the law: a statute, ordinance, or legal code has been broken. Illicit sweeps wider — anything forbidden by law, rules, or strong custom, often with a whiff of secrecy about it. An unlicensed casino is both unlawful and illicit, but an affair that breaks no statute can still be illicit.

    forbidden: Forbidden means not allowed by rules or authority — a parent, a posted sign, a code — and it is the plainest of these words. Illicit is more loaded: forbidden by law, rules, or custom, with a strong hint of secrecy and wrongdoing. Dessert before dinner may be forbidden; a smuggling run is illicit.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    legal: permitted by law

    permissible: allowed under the rules

    Where it sits · what level of rule it breaks

    improper → illicit → criminal

    improper vs. illicit: Improper describes behavior that breaks social expectations, while illicit describes what rules or laws actually forbid.

    criminal vs. illicit: Illicit covers anything forbidden by law or custom, while criminal means serious enough to be prosecuted and punished in court.

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    formal, often legal or journalistic

    Illicit describes activities banned by law or strong social rules, such as illicit trade or an illicit scheme. Do not confuse it with the sound-alike elicit, which means to draw out a response.
