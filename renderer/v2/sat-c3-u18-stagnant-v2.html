Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Stagnant means still to the point of going stale — not flowing, not moving, not developing.

See the word in context

By August the attic office turned stagnant — no vent, no fan, the same warm air hanging over the desks from morning to night.

Three years into the job, Marisol admitted her career had gone stagnant: the same title, the same tasks, and a skills list she had not added to since her training week.

The word begins at the pond's edge — water that stops flowing soon clouds and sours — and English carries the picture to anything that stops moving and starts to stale: careers, wages, whole economies. Stagnant is not merely slow; it asserts that motion has stopped entirely, and that the standstill has begun to do damage.

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    motionless: simply not moving — a parked car, a held breath. Stagnant adds the sourness of it: stillness that has lasted long enough to begin going stale.

    sluggish: still moving, just heavily and slowly — sluggish traffic crawls, a sluggish stream creeps. Stagnant goes further: the movement has stopped altogether.

    inert: unreactive — failing to respond or act when something touches it, the way inert gases sit out every reaction. Stagnant blames the stillness itself and watches the decay that follows it.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    flowing: moving along in a steady current

    thriving: growing strongly and healthily

    Where it sits · how far the stillness has gone toward decay

    sluggish → stagnant → putrid

    sluggish vs. stagnant: Sluggish is weaker than stagnant — a sluggish stream still creeps forward, while a stagnant one has stopped moving entirely and begun to sour.

    putrid vs. stagnant: Putrid is stronger than stagnant — stagnant water is stale from standing still, but putrid water has finished the job and rotted outright.

    Section 3

    Apply

    “Can I recognize and use it correctly?”

    Word Choice

    neutral, mildly disapproving — reporting, science, and business writing

    Stagnant always carries a verdict: the stillness has lasted too long and something is spoiling for it. A pond can be still and lovely; call it stagnant and you have said it has begun to sour. The same charge rides along in figurative use — stagnant wages, a stagnant career — where the word asserts both that growth has stopped and that the standing still is doing harm.
