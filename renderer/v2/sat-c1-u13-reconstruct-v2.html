reconstruct

/ree-kun-STRUKT/

Section 1

Meaning

“What does this word mean?”

Hear It

What It Means

Reconstruct means to build again after damage, or to piece together an understanding of past events from evidence.

See the word in context

Engineers worked through the winter to reconstruct the bridge the flood had washed away.

Using time-stamped receipts, doorbell-camera clips, and a parking stub, the detectives reconstructed the suspect's movements on the night of the theft.

The detectives never witnessed that night themselves. They took separate scraps of evidence and fit them together into one coherent account of what happened, which is the evidence-based sense of reconstruct: rebuilding the past piece by piece.

    Accuracy

    Compare the words

    Synonyms · words with a similar meaning

    rebuild: Rebuild means to build a physical thing again after damage or destruction — you rebuild a bridge or an engine. Reconstruct covers that and one thing more: piecing together an account of past events from evidence, the way detectives reconstruct a timeline. You rebuild structures; you can reconstruct a night.

    restore: Restore means to return something to its original condition, often keeping the original materials — restorers work on paintings and old houses. Reconstruct means building the thing again, sometimes entirely from new parts, or assembling a picture of past events from clues rather than preserving anything original.

    reassemble: Reassemble means to fit existing pieces back together — the parts are all on the table and go back where they belong. Reconstruct starts with less: the original is damaged or gone and gets built again, or the past is pieced together from whatever scraps of evidence remain.

    What it isn’t · antonyms and near-opposites

    demolish: to knock down completely

    dismantle: to take apart piece by piece

    Where it sits · how much of it gets redone

    repair → reconstruct → overhaul

    repair vs. reconstruct: Repair is weaker than reconstruct, repairing fixes the damaged parts of something, while reconstructing builds the whole thing again.

    overhaul vs. reconstruct: Overhaul is stronger than reconstruct, reconstructing rebuilds what was damaged or lost, while an overhaul strips something down and redoes every part of it, even the parts that still worked.

      Word Choice

      Neutral / everyday

      Used both literally, as in reconstruct a building, and for evidence work, as in reconstruct events or reconstruct a timeline. Distinguish it from restore: restoring returns something to its original condition, often keeping the original materials, while reconstructing builds it again, sometimes entirely from new parts or from scattered clues.
