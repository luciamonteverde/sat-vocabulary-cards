Chronological

/kro-nuh-LOJ-i-kul/ — rhymes with biological · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Chronological means arranged in the order in which events occurred in time.

Show it in context

The curators arranged every exhibit in strict chronological order, so that each step down the corridor carries the visitor a few million years later than the last.

Here's how to decide when chronological is the exact word.

Worked Example

The historian organized the ancient manuscripts by the order they were written, from earliest to latest, so readers could trace the civilization's development step by step through time.

Chronological means arranged in the EXACT ORDER EVENTS OCCURRED IN TIME. It's not just any organized arrangement (that could be by size, alphabet, color, etc.); it's specifically organized by TIME ORDER. The manuscripts from earliest to latest is the definition of chronological.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
sequential

Sequential means in order, one after another. Chronological is more specific: it means arranged by TIME, not just any sequence (alphabetical, by size, etc.).

consecutive

Consecutive means following one after another without gaps. Chronological specifically refers to TIME order; consecutive just means unbroken sequence.

temporal

Temporal means relating to time in general. Chronological is more specific: it means arranged by the exact time events happened.

Antonyms · opposites

random — Random means without order or pattern. Chronological is the opposite: arranged in a specific, time-based order.

Build the Word

chron- Greek: 'time'

The chron- root means 'time' — chronological order arranges things by when they happened in time, showing how time flows from one event to the next.

The root connection →

Chronological and anachronism share the root chron- ('time'). In chronological, that root gives us ordering things by time; in anachronism, it gives us something out of place in time — both relate to how things fit (or don't fit) within a timeline.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / formal

Chronological is the precise term for arranging things in time order, especially in history, science, and literature. In everyday speech, say 'in order' or 'from beginning to end,' but chronological is the exact word when you want to emphasize arrangement by time.
