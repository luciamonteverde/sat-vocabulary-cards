Exaggeration

/ig-zaj-uh-RAY-shun/ noun

Hear It
What It Means

Exaggeration means a statement that represents something as larger, better, or worse than it really is

Show it in context

Every fishing story Grandpa told ended in Exaggeration: the trout barely longer than his hand grew, with each retelling, into a monster that nearly pulled him off the dock.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

When Marcus said the cafeteria line was 'a thousand miles long,' everyone understood the exaggeration — the line was long, but it reached only to the salad bar.

An exaggeration represents one thing as larger, better, or worse than it really is. Marcus takes a single true detail — a long line — and inflates it far past the facts. It is a playful overstatement, not a whole comic performance.

Build the Word · tap each piece to reveal its meaning
Prefixex-'out, up, thoroughly,' from Latin
Rootagger-'heap, pile,' from Latin agger; the verb aggerare meant 'to pile up'
Suffix-ation'act or result of,' Latin noun-forming suffix

Put together, exaggeration means, at its roots, 'the act of heaping it up and out' — piling more onto a claim than the plain facts can support.

Use the root in a new word: exclaim

'That pumpkin is enormous!' she exclaimed, though it was merely average for the county fair.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Neutral; common in everyday speech, journalism, and test passages about advertising and argument.

Use exaggeration for a single claim or detail that outgrows the facts — a fish that gets bigger with each retelling, a wait called 'forever.' It can be playful, careless, or misleading, and it is not automatically funny. (A parody, by contrast, is a whole comic send-up of a work or style, and it often uses exaggeration as one of its tools — see this unit's word Parody.)
