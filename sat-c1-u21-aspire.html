Aspire

/uh-SPYRE/ verb

Hear It
What It Means

Aspire means to direct one's hopes or ambitions toward achieving something.

Show it in context

Aspire means to direct one's hopes or ambitions toward achieving something. Even as a stagehand sweeping between scenes, Nora aspired to direct plays of her own.

Worked Example

The junior park ranger spent her lunch breaks reading wildfire-science journals. She saved for two summers to attend a fire-ecology field course three states away. On her locker she taped a photo of the federal research station in Missoula. When anyone asked why she volunteered for every controlled burn, she said the same thing: one day she would lead a fire-research team of her own. Everything she did pointed toward that single hope — she aspired to it.

Every choice the ranger makes — the reading, the saving, the volunteering — is aimed at one hoped-for future, which is what it means to aspire to something.

Prefixa-toward (from Latin ad-)
Rootspirto breathe (Latin spirare)

Aspire comes from Latin aspirare: ad- (toward) plus spirare (to breathe). To aspire was originally to breathe toward something — to pant after it, straining upward. The breathing root spirare fills English: inspire (breathe into), respiration (breathing), even spirit, the breath of life.

Use the root in a new word: respiration

The nurse checked the patient's respiration — respiration is breathing itself, from the same Latin spirare that pictures someone who aspires as breathing toward their goal.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral-to-elevated; takes 'to' + noun or infinitive

Aspire is the verb, and ambition (also in this unit) is the matching noun — to aspire to something is to hold an ambition for it. Aspire always points upward at a goal not yet reached; you cannot aspire to something you already have.
