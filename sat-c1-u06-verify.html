Worked Example

Before running the story, the reporter spent two days working to <strong style="color:var(--accent);">verify</strong> every date and dollar figure against bank records and public files.

The reporter doesn't just trust her notes — she checks each fact against reliable outside sources (bank records, public files) to make sure it's accurate. That checking-against-a-trusted-standard is what verify means.

Rootver-true (Latin verus)
Suffix-ifyto make, to cause to be (from Latin facere, 'to make')

Verify is literally 'to make true' — the Latin root verus ('true') plus -ify ('make') builds a verb meaning to establish that something really is true.

Use the root in a new word: veritable

Veritable means 'true to the name' — after the snowstorm, the backyard was a veritable mountain range of drifts.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Neutral / Procedural

You verify facts, figures, identities, sources, and accounts — always against something reliable. It's a step more formal than 'check': checking can be a quick glance, but verifying implies comparing against a trusted standard. You can verify a thing ('verify the address') or verify that something is so ('verify that the name matches').
