Comma After Introductory Elements

The Rule

Place a comma after an introductory word, phrase, or dependent clause that comes before the main independent clause of a sentence.

Watch Out For

Students often skip the comma after short introductory phrases like "In 1945" or "During the experiment" because the phrases feel too brief to need punctuation, but the rule applies regardless of the length of the introductory element — even a single introductory word like "However" or "Furthermore" requires a comma after it when it precedes the main clause.

Quick Pattern

Ask yourself: Does this sentence begin with a word, phrase, or clause that comes before the main subject and verb? If the sentence opens with any element that precedes the independent clause — whether it is a dependent clause, a prepositional phrase, a transitional word, or a participial phrase — then place a comma immediately after that introductory element and before the main clause begins.
