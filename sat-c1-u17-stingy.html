Stingy

/STIN-jee/ adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Stingy means unwilling to spend or give money, holding tightly to every cent.

Show it in context

Stingy means unwilling to spend or give money, holding tightly to every cent. Everyone enjoyed dinner with Uncle Ray until the check arrived, because he was famously stingy and would study the bill for ten minutes rather than leave a fair tip.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

The office manager was so stingy with supplies that employees had to sign a form to receive a single new pen, and he kept the box of paper clips locked in his own desk drawer.

Rationing pens and locking up paper clips shows an unwillingness to spend or hand out even the tiniest amounts, which is exactly what makes the manager stingy.

Stingy is a homegrown English word of uncertain origin. One popular theory ties it to a dialect word meaning 'sharp-tempered, stinging,' from Old English stingan, 'to sting,' picturing someone prickly and sharp about money. Because the connection is unproven and no reliable root family exists, there is no root quiz for this word.

Use the root in a new word: sting

Stingy's true origin is unproven, but the favorite theory ties it to sting, from Old English stingan — picturing someone so sharp and prickly about money that parting with a single cent stings.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

informal, conversational

Stingy is an everyday, mildly insulting word for someone unwilling to spend or give. It describes habits with money and generosity, not physical sensations; a wasp's sharp jab is a sting, never stingy. In formal writing, miserly or parsimonious strikes a more elevated tone.
