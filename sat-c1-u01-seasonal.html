Seasonal

/SEE-zun-ul/ — rhymes with reasonable · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Seasonal means occurring only at a particular time of year, tied to the seasons.

Show it in context

The little shop only sells seasonal produce, switching its shelves from strawberries to pumpkins as the year turns.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when seasonal is the exact word.

Worked Example

The store's seasonal decorations transformed from snowflakes and silver in December to shamrocks and green in March.

Seasonal makes clear that these decorations are specifically tied to the seasons, changing as spring arrives. 'Temporary' or 'changing' wouldn't capture the exact sense of being connected to the natural seasons themselves.

Your Turn

The ski resort is only open when there is enough snow, which happens from December through March.

Is seasonal the right word here, or would temporary fit better?

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
periodic

Recurring on a regular schedule. Seasonal adds the specific sense of being tied to the natural seasons (spring, summer, fall, winter).

cyclical

Repeating in cycles. Seasonal specifies that the cycle is specifically the yearly seasons, not just any repeating pattern.

temporary

Lasting only for a time. Seasonal adds the idea that the timing is specifically tied to natural seasons, not just random duration.

Antonyms · opposites

year-round — Available or happening all the time—the opposite of seasonal, which is tied to specific times of year.

How Strong Is It?
permanent→temporary→periodic→seasonal

Seasonal — the strongest of the group: specifically tied to the natural seasons of the year, not just any regular cycle or limited duration. (our word)

Build the Word

sect- Latin: 'to cut' or 'to divide'

Seasonal comes from 'season,' which uses the root sect- ('to cut'). A season is a time when the year is 'cut' or divided into natural periods. Dissect uses the same root: dis- means 'apart,' so dissect means 'to cut apart' in order to examine something carefully.

In science class, students had to dissect a flower to observe its different parts and understand how it grew.

to cut apart carefully in order to examine something in detail

to see or observe something from a distance

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / everyday

Use seasonal for anything tied to the natural seasons—foods that grow at certain times, weather patterns tied to specific months, or products only sold during certain times of year. For something that simply lasts a limited time unrelated to seasons, use temporary instead.
