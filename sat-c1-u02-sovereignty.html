Sovereignty

/SOV-rin-tee/ · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Sovereignty means supreme, independent power to govern oneself or a nation without control from any outside authority.

Show it in context

The small island nation guarded its sovereignty fiercely, refusing every offer of foreign aid that came with even a hint of control over its laws.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when sovereignty is the exact word.

Worked Example

By keeping every customer record on servers physically inside its own country, the software firm protected its data sovereignty, so that no foreign government or overseas cloud provider could ever compel access to that information under laws written elsewhere.

Here, sovereignty names the firm's SUPREME, INDEPENDENT power to govern its own data with NO outside authority able to override that control. Notice how much stronger this is than a looser word like independence, which can be casual or personal (you might value your "independence" simply because you like doing things your own way). It is also sharper than jurisdiction, which only describes the SCOPE of a legal authority — which court or country gets to make a ruling — not the ultimate, self-ruling power itself. Sovereignty is the top of the chain: the authority that answers to nothing above it.

Your Turn

A city council sets its own parking rules, school-bus schedules, and park hours — but the state legislature can overturn any city ordinance it dislikes, and often does. A reporter is drafting a headline and needs the precise word for the city's power to manage its own local affairs.

Which word accurately describes the city's power over these local matters?

Choose the word that fits — then tap it to check.

autonomy

✓ Correct — Correct. The city governs a limited set of local matters but remains under the state's higher authority — the legislature can overturn any ordinance. That is autonomy: real but delegated, limited self-governance, not supreme rule.

sovereignty

✗ Not quite — Not quite. Sovereignty means SUPREME power with no authority standing above it. Because the state can strike down any city ordinance, the city is clearly not the final word — so it has autonomy, not sovereignty.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
autonomy

The power to govern one's own affairs — but often within limits set by a larger body. Sovereignty is stronger: it implies there is no higher authority at all.

self-rule

Governing your own affairs rather than being ruled by outsiders. It captures the same idea but is plainer and less absolute than sovereignty's formal 'supreme authority' sense.

independence

Freedom from outside control. It is broad and can be personal or casual, whereas sovereignty specifically names the supreme legal and political authority to govern a person, group, or nation.

Antonyms · opposites

subjugation — The state of being forced under the domination and control of another power — the direct opposite of holding supreme authority over oneself.

How Strong Is It?
dependency→autonomy→sovereignty→hegemony

This spectrum tracks the degree of self-governing power. At dependency, a body is governed by others; with autonomy it manages some of its own affairs but still answers to a higher authority; at sovereignty it holds full, supreme self-rule with nothing above it. Hegemony goes one step further — not only ruling oneself but dominating other states as well.

Build the Word

super- (also seen as sover-/supr-) From Latin super meaning "above," which passed through Vulgar Latin *superanus ("that which is above; chief, principal") into Old French soverain and then into English.

At its core, sovereignty traces back to the idea of being ABOVE all others — the supreme power that sits at the very top, with no authority higher than it.

The Supreme Court is the highest authority in the land, and its rulings stand above those of every lower court.

In the word supreme, what does the root super-/supr- contribute to the meaning?

the sense of "above" or "highest"

✓ Correct — Correct. Supreme comes from Latin supremus, "highest," built on super ("above") — the supreme thing is the one positioned above all the rest.

the sense of "under" or "lesser"

✗ Not quite — That is the opposite. That meaning belongs to the root sub- (as in submarine or subordinate). Super- points upward, to what is above.

the sense of "across" or "between"

✗ Not quite — Not this one — "across/between" belongs to roots like trans- or inter-. Super- specifically means "above."

The root connection →

Both sovereignty and supreme climb from the same Latin root, super — "above." A sovereign power sits ABOVE all others, with no authority over it, exactly as a supreme court's word stands above every lower court's. Once you hear "super = above," the meaning of sovereignty follows naturally: the topmost, self-ruling authority.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal / political / legal

Sovereignty Sovereignty is a formal word at home in law, government, and international relations. In everyday speech it sounds out of place — you would say you have "control" of your own schedule, not "sovereignty" over it. Writers do occasionally borrow it figuratively for effect (a chef's "sovereignty" over the kitchen), but that is a deliberate, elevated flourish, not casual usage.
