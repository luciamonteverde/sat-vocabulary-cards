Interval

/IN-tur-ul/ — three syllables, stress on first · noun

Hear It
What It Means

An interval is a gap or pause in time between two events.

Show it in context

That interval felt endless as she stood alone beneath the humming lights, listening to water drip somewhere in the tunnel.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when interval is the exact word.

Worked Example

The concert's second act began after the *interval*, when the orchestra returned to the stage and musicians retook their seats.

Interval is more precise than the plain word 'break' because it specifically names the gap *between* two separate events (the end of the first act and the start of the second), not just any pause. The context here — two performances separated by time — is exactly where interval shines.

Build the Word

inter- Latin: 'between'

Interval comes from 'inter-' (between). Romans noticed this root everywhere: the space between fortifications on a wall, the distance between places. 'Between' is the core idea of every interval.

The moss grew in the interstices between the old bricks, filling every tiny space in the wall.

The root connection →

Interval and interstice share the root inter- ('between'). In interval, that root gives us a gap between events; in interstice, it gives us tiny spaces between physical objects.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / timekeeping

Interval emphasizes the gap *between* two separate events or things, while 'pause' is just a stop within one activity. Use interval in formal or technical contexts (like music, sports, or astronomy) when the separation between two distinct things matters. For a simple stop, 'pause' or 'break' is clearer.
