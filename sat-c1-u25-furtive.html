Furtive

/FUR-tiv/ adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Furtive means done secretly or stealthily, in a way that suggests hidden or guilty intent.

Show it in context

Furtive means done secretly or stealthily, in a way that suggests hidden or guilty intent. He slid the report into his bag with a furtive glance toward the supervisor's office.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Halfway through the exam, the proctor noticed a student's odd rhythm: write, pause, drop the left hand below the desk, glance sideways, write again. Each movement was small and quick, timed for the moments the proctor turned toward the window. When she finally circled behind him, she found a strip of formulas taped inside his sleeve. It was the furtiveness of the gestures — not the gestures themselves — that had given him away.

The quick, concealed movements timed to escape the proctor's eye suggest hidden, guilty intent — that sneaky manner is exactly what furtive describes.

Rootfurttheft (Latin furtum, from fur, thief)
Suffix-ive(fused inside Latin furtivus — not added in English)

Furtive is this unit's clearest false friend. It comes whole from Latin furtivus, 'stolen; thief-like,' built on furtum ('theft') from fur ('thief'). The '-ive' you see was already fused inside the Latin adjective — it was never added to an English or Latin base the way corroborate builds corroborative or instruct builds instructive. And the root fur left no common English family to check against (its likeliest descendant, ferret, is a fun rarity, not a pattern). So treat furtive as a single sealed unit: a furtive movement is 'thief-like' — done the way a thief moves.

Use the root in a new word: ferret

A fun aside: ferret likely descends from Latin furittus, 'little thief' — the same fur ('thief') behind furtive, which is why ferreting something out means slyly digging up what was hidden.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral to formal; implies guilt or concealment

Furtive describes a manner — a glance, a movement, a gesture that suggests something to hide. Distinguish it from this unit's connive, which means actively scheming with others in secret, and reclusive, which means avoiding company altogether, with nothing hidden or guilty about it.
