Discern

/dih-SURN/ verb

Hear It
What It Means

Discern means to perceive or recognize something, especially something subtle or hard to notice

Show it in context

Discern means to perceive or recognize something, especially something subtle or hard to notice. Standing on the dark porch, Leo could barely discern the outline of the mountains against the night sky.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

An art restorer examined the painting under a bright lamp for nearly an hour. Slowly she began to discern tiny irregularities in the brushstrokes, details invisible to a casual viewer, that revealed the canvas was a forgery.

The restorer perceives something subtle only through careful, sustained attention, which is the core of discern: detecting what is not immediately obvious.

Prefixdis-apart (not negation in this word)
Rootcernto sift, separate, distinguish (Latin cernere)

Watch out: in this word dis- means 'apart,' not 'not' or 'opposite of,' even though that is this unit's headline pattern. Discern comes from dis- (apart) plus Latin cernere (to sift, to separate), literally 'to sift apart,' which is why it means telling things apart clearly.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Formal

Discern suits careful, effortful perception of subtle things; when something is obvious, plain words like notice or tell sound more natural. Also watch a classic confusable: discern (to perceive or detect) is unrelated to discreet (careful and tactful about what you say or do), despite the similar look and sound.
