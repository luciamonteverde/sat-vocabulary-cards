Rare

/RAIR/ adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Rare means not occurring often; uncommon; found in small numbers

Show it in context

Rare means not occurring often or found only in small numbers — like a bird so rare that even lifelong birdwatchers may never spot one.

See How It Works · a worked example, then try one yourself
Worked Example

Halley's Comet makes a rare appearance in Earth's skies, swinging close enough to see only once every 75 years or so.

An event that comes around once in 75 years is about as infrequent as sky-watching gets. Rare captures exactly how seldom something happens or how few of it exist.

Rootrarusthinly spread; loosely packed; infrequent (Latin)

Rare comes straight from Latin rarus, "thinly spread" — picture items scattered so thinly across the world that you seldom come across one.

Use the root in a new word: rarefied

Climbers move slowly in the rarefied air near the peak, where the oxygen is thinly spread.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Neutral / everyday — near-synonym alert

Rare and its unit-mate scarce overlap but are not identical: rare means few exist or it seldom happens (a rare coin, a rare visit), while scarce usually means something is temporarily hard to get relative to demand (water grew scarce during the drought). Rare also has an unrelated cooking sense — a rare steak is one cooked only lightly.
