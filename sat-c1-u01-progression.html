Progression

/pro-GRESH-un/ — rhymes with fashion · noun

Hear It
What It Means

A progression is a step-by-step development from one stage to the next.

Show it in context

Watching this progression from egg to caterpillar to winged insect, students could hardly believe that all four forms belonged to a single living thing.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when progression is the exact word.

Worked Example

Over the spring, Maya's artwork showed an amazing progression: her first painting was muddy and flat, her second had better colors, and her third showed real perspective.

Progression emphasizes the step-by-step, orderly nature of the change—each stage leads logically to the next. If we just said 'improvement,' we'd miss the sense that each stage is a necessary building block.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
sequence

Just the order of things. Progression adds the idea that each step develops from the last.

advancement

Progress or moving forward. Progression emphasizes the methodical, step-by-step nature of that movement.

evolution

Change over time, especially gradual change. Progression is more about deliberate stages where each builds purposefully.

Antonyms · opposites

regression — Moving backward through stages—the opposite of forward progression.

How Strong Is It?

Build the Word

gred- Latin: 'to step'

Progression uses the root gred- ('to step'). The prefix pro- means 'forward,' so progression is 'stepping forward' through connected stages. Digress uses the same root: di- means 'aside' or 'away,' so digress means 'stepping away' from your main topic.

When the teacher started to digress about her childhood, the students had to remind her to return to the algebra lesson.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / academic

Use progression when describing a step-by-step, orderly change where each stage is necessary and builds toward the next. For a simple list of things in order with no development between them, use succession instead.
