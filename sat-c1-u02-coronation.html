What It Means

Coronation means the formal ceremony in which a new king or queen is invested with royal authority.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
crowning

The plainest, most concrete synonym: it names the physical act of placing the crown on the monarch's head. It highlights the visible gesture rather than the full legal and religious rite.

enthronement

Emphasizes formally seating the ruler on the throne as the act of installation. It stresses taking the seat of power and can occur even in monarchies that do not perform a full crowning.

investiture

The broadest term: the formal conferral of an office, rank, or authority together with its insignia. It need not involve a crown or even a monarch, so it is the most general and least specifically royal of the three.

Antonyms · opposites

abdication — A coronation is the ceremony that grants a monarch royal authority; an abdication is the formal act of giving that authority up and stepping down from the throne. One begins a reign, the other ends it.

How Strong Is It?
accession proclamation→enthronement→coronation→imperial coronation

These four royal events are arranged by ceremonial weight and formality rather than by "intensity," since a coronation is a single specific event, not a matter of degree. An ACCESSION PROCLAMATION is the lightest: a herald or official simply announces publicly that a new sovereign has succeeded, with little ritual. An ENTHRONEMENT adds real ceremony by formally seating the ruler on the throne, but it need not include the crowning rite. A CORONATION (index 2) is the full sacred rite in which the monarch is crowned with regalia and invested with royal authority — heavier and more elaborate than a mere proclamation or seating. An IMPERIAL CORONATION sits above it: the crowning of an emperor, historically the grandest of all, often performed by the Pope and claiming authority over many kingdoms rather than one. Coronation belongs at index 2 because it is the definitive investiture of a national monarch, more weighty than announcement or enthronement alone, yet below the imperial rite in scope of claimed authority.

Build the Word

corona Latin

"Coronation" comes from Latin corona, meaning "crown, garland, or wreath" (itself borrowed from Greek korōnē). The literal idea inside the word is the crown that is placed on the new ruler's head, so a coronation is quite literally a "crowning."

corona (crown) + -ation (the act or process of) → the act of crowning

✓ Correct — Correct. Corona means "crown," and -ation turns it into the name of the act, so a coronation is literally "the act of crowning."

coro (body) + nation (a country) → the governing body of a nation

✗ Not quite — Incorrect. This is a false split. "Coronation" does not contain "nation"; it is built on Latin corona, "crown."

coron (time) + -ation → the timing of a royal event

✗ Not quite — Incorrect. There is no root meaning "time" here. The base is corona, "crown," not a word for time.

The root connection →

Here is the surprising link: "coronation" and "coronary" share the exact same Latin root, corona, "crown." A coronation places a crown on a monarch's head, and the coronary arteries earned their name because they encircle the top of the heart like a crown or wreath. So the ceremony that crowns a king and the vessels that ring your heart are, etymologically, both about crowns.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

formal, dignified, and historical

Coronation "Coronation" is a formal noun tied to monarchy, history, and journalism, so it fits ceremonial, news, or scholarly writing. Keep it for the literal crowning of a king or queen. Using it loosely for any big promotion or triumph — "his coronation as CEO" — is figurative and can sound grandiose or lightly ironic, so choose that extended sense deliberately.
