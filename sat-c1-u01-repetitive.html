Repetitive

/ri-PET-i-tiv/ — stress on PET · adjective

Hear It
What It Means

Repetitive means repeating the same action or pattern again and again, often tediously.

Show it in context

Hour after hour, the work was so repetitive that his mind drifted far from the fluorescent-lit floor while his hands moved on their own.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when repetitive is the exact word.

Worked Example

The assembly line worker performed the same three welding motions hour after hour, and after the first week, she no longer needed to think about what her hands were doing — it had become fully repetitive.

Repetitive is more precise than just saying 'boring' or 'the same action' because it names both the physical fact that the action repeats and the wearying quality that comes from endless repetition — something tedious and mind-numbing by nature.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
redundant

Unnecessary duplication or repetition; repetitive just means repeating, whether necessary or not.

monotonous

Lacking variety and interest; repetitive focuses on the repeating pattern itself, not always on the feeling of boredom.

tedious

Dull and tiring; repetitive describes the repeating pattern, while tedious describes how it feels to endure it.

Antonyms · opposites

varied — Different each time; the opposite of repetitive's sameness.

Build the Word

petere Latin: 'to go, to seek, to aim'

Petere means 'to go' or 'to seek' — add re- (again) and you get repetere, 'to go/seek again.' The same root gives us 'petition' (seeking something) and 'compete' (seeking together with others).

The root connection →

Repetitive and centripetal both share the root pet- ('to go, to seek'). In repetitive, that root gives us the idea of going/seeking again and again; in centripetal, it gives us motion directed toward the center point.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

neutral / everyday

Repetitive is the word to use when something repeats the same action or pattern over and over. It usually suggests tedium or weariness but focuses on the pattern itself. When you want to emphasize how boring or tiring it is, try 'tedious' or 'monotonous' instead — they emphasize the feeling rather than the pattern.
