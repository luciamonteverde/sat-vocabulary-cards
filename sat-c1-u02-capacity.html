Capacity

/kuh-PAS-uh-tee/ — rhymes with capacious · noun

Hear It
What It Means

Capacity means the largest amount that something can hold or produce, or a person's ability to do a particular thing.

Show it in context

The stadium's capacity was ninety thousand, yet the organizers sold tickets as though the seats could somehow stretch to fit twice that number.

See How It Works · follow the thinking, then try one

Here's how to decide when capacity is the exact word.

Worked Example

Years of mediating bitter disputes had given her an extraordinary capacity for patience, letting her stay composed through hours of shouting that would have worn anyone else down.

Here, capacity is a NOUN naming an ABILITY: the MAXIMUM AMOUNT of patience she is able to sustain. It doesn't describe a room or a container; it NAMES the invisible "how much she can hold or do" itself. Don't confuse it with CAPACIOUS, the ADJECTIVE meaning "roomy, able to hold a lot" (a capacious closet). CAPACIOUS DESCRIBES a thing; CAPACITY NAMES the amount or ability. You could praise a capacious mind, but you would measure the capacity of that mind. They are different PARTS OF SPEECH and are never interchangeable.

Your Turn

A tech reviewer is comparing two phone batteries. The newer one is physically slimmer, yet it stores far more charge before it needs recharging. She wants the single precise word for the MAXIMUM AMOUNT OF CHARGE a battery can hold.

Which word precisely names the maximum amount of charge the battery can hold?

Choose the word that fits — then tap it to check.

capacity

✓ Correct — Correct. Capacity names the MAXIMUM AMOUNT the battery can hold, which is exactly the storage limit being measured, regardless of the battery's physical dimensions.

size

✗ Not quite — Not precise. "Size" refers to the battery's physical dimensions, and this battery is actually slimmer while holding more charge. Size says nothing about how much charge fits; capacity names the holding limit itself.

Synonyms · words that mean the same — tap one to see how it differs
volume

Overlaps when capacity means a physical amount that can be held (a tank's volume vs. its capacity), but volume just measures the space, while capacity stresses the MAXIMUM that space can take in.

ability

Matches the personal sense: a capacity to learn is an ability to learn. But ability is broad and neutral, while capacity often points to the upper LIMIT or full extent of what one can hold or sustain.

capability

Close for the ability sense; capability stresses possessing the skill or power to do something, whereas capacity emphasizes HOW MUCH of it a person or system can hold or handle.

Antonyms · opposites

incapacity — The absence of the room or ability to hold or do something. A legal ruling of incapacity, for example, means a person lacks the capacity to make binding decisions.

How Strong Is It?
empty→half-full→at capacity→over capacity

This spectrum measures how full something is relative to its limit. Capacity sits at index 2 as the FULL POINT: the exact maximum a container or system can take. Below it there is still room (empty, half-full); the moment you push past it you are OVER capacity (overflowing, oversubscribed). Capacity marks the boundary line itself, not a partial state, which is why it lands at the top of the filled range rather than in the middle.

Build the Word

capere Latin

"to take, hold, or contain." Capacity descends from Latin capacitas, from capax ("able to hold"), from capere ("to take, hold"). At its core, capacity is about how much can be TAKEN IN or HELD.

After a year of intensive training, the junior engineer was fully capable of running the project on her own.

Which breakdown shows how "capable" is built from the same root as "capacity"?

cap- (from capere, "to take, hold") + -able ("able to") — literally "able to take hold of" a task

✓ Correct — Correct. Capable shares the capere root with capacity: someone capable can "take on" and handle what is required.

cap- (from caput, "head") + -able — "able to lead from the top"

✗ Not quite — Tempting, because "cap" does come from caput ("head") in some words, but capable traces to capere ("to take, hold"), the same root as capacity, not to "head."

ca- + -pable (from pabulum, "food") — "able to feed"

✗ Not quite — No. This invents a root. Capable is cap- (capere, "to take, hold") + -able.

The root connection →

Both capacity and capable flow from Latin capere, "to take or hold." A person of great capacity can hold or take in a lot (of charge, of knowledge, of patience); a capable person can take on and manage a task. Interesting connection: the sibling card CAPACIOUS ("roomy") comes from the very same capax/capere family. Capacious DESCRIBES the roomy thing, while capacity NAMES the amount that thing can hold. Same root, different jobs.

Word Choice · its tone, and when to use it

Neutral; works in both everyday and formal writing.

Capacity Capacity is at home everywhere: casual ("the trunk's capacity"), technical ("processing capacity," "battery capacity"), and formal or legal ("legal capacity to sign"). It reads as precise rather than fancy, so it is safe in academic and professional writing where a vaguer word like "size" or "amount" would blur the meaning.
